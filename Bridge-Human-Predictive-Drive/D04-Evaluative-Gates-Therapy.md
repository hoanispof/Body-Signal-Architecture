## §2 — Human-Predictive-Drive CONCEPT: Evaluative Gates Direct-State

### §2.1 The 5-Step Insula Gradient Process

Human-Predictive-Drive describes a SINGLE mechanism that applies to BOTH reward and dissonance
directions (Reward-Signal-Architecture.md §3.1, Dissonance-Signal-Architecture.md
§3.1):

```
STEP 1 — POSTERIOR INSULA: Direct-State arrives (~0-50ms)
  → Hardware signal arrives RAW and UNMODULATED
  → CT → lamina I → posterior insula (reward direction)
  → Nociception → lamina I → posterior insula (dissonance direction)
  → SAME signal regardless of WHO causes it, WHY, or WHERE
  → 🟢 Craig 2002: posterior insula = primary interoceptive cortex

STEP 2 — EVALUATIVE ASSESSMENT fires in parallel (~50-200ms)
  → Context chunks activate: WHO/WHAT/WHERE
  → Social schemas: friend? stranger? threat?
  → Amygdala: rapid threat assessment (LeDoux fast path ~12ms)

STEP 3 — ANTERIOR INSULA: Integration (~100-300ms)
  → Direct-State signal PROPAGATES anteriorly
  → Evaluative context arrives TOP-DOWN
  → Anterior insula INTEGRATES both = EXPERIENCED signal
  → 🟢 Craig 2009: anterior insula = "seat of subjective awareness"

STEP 4 — ACC: CONFLICT CHECK (~200-400ms)
  → Direct-State positive + Eval positive → silent (smooth)
  → Direct-State positive + Eval negative → FIRES (flags conflict)
  → Direct-State negative + Eval negative → silent (amplified pain)
  → Direct-State negative + Eval positive → FIRES (suppress pain)
  → 🟢 Botvinick et al. 2004: ACC conflict monitoring

STEP 5 — OFC/vmPFC: VALUE ASSIGNMENT (~300-500ms)
  → Final value computation integrating all signals
  → 🟢 Damasio 1994: vmPFC somatic marker
```

This process runs for EVERY body-level signal, every moment. In body therapy,
it runs CONTINUOUSLY throughout the session. 🟡

### §2.3 Placebo/Nocebo as Empirical Proof

```
PLACEBO = Evaluative SUPPRESSES Direct-State Dissonance:
  → Patient BELIEVES treatment works (compiled positive evaluation)
  → Direct-State pain arrives at posterior insula
  → Evaluative context: "being treated" → SUPPRESS
  → NET: less pain experienced
  → 🟢 Wager et al. 2004: PFC reduces pain response (rostral ACC + PAG)
  → 🟢 Zubieta et al. 2005: Placebo activates ENDOGENOUS OPIOID system
    = Evaluative assessment LITERALLY activates opioid suppression of pain

NOCEBO = Evaluative AMPLIFIES Direct-State Dissonance:
  → Patient BELIEVES treatment will hurt (compiled negative evaluation)
  → Direct-State pain arrives → Evaluative "this will hurt" → AMPLIFY
  → NET: more pain experienced
  → 🟢 Colloca & Benedetti 2005: Nocebo hyperalgesia
  → 🟢 Benedetti et al. 2007: Nocebo activates cholecystokinin (CCK)

SOCIAL CONTEXT x PAIN:
  → 🟢 Eisenberger et al. 2006: Social support reduces pain neural activity
  → 🟢 Master et al. 2009: Photo of loved one reduces pain
  → 🟢 Younger et al. 2010: Romantic love reduces pain (fMRI)
```

These are NOT "just psychology" — they produce MEASURABLE neurochemical changes.
Zubieta 2005 shows placebo activates the SAME opioid system that pharmacological
analgesics target. The Evaluative Gate produces REAL biological effects. 🟢

### §2.4 Asymmetric Transition Timing

Human-Predictive-Drive identifies a critical asymmetry (Dissonance-Signal-Architecture.md §7.5):

```
REWARD → DISSONANCE: FAST (~milliseconds to seconds)
  → Schema firing: 0ms (compiled pattern match)
  → Amygdala fast path: ~12ms (BYPASSES PFC, LeDoux 1996)
  → NE spike: ~500ms
  → Opioid deactivation: seconds (reward CAN stop almost instantly)
  → Cortisol: 5-20 min (arrives AFTER, then SUSTAINS)

DISSONANCE → REWARD: SLOW (~minutes to hours)
  → Cortisol clearance: half-life ~60-90 minutes
  → NE clearance: minutes
  → Opioid RE-activation: all 5 Body-Feedback-Preconditions must RE-VERIFY
  → PFC must RE-ASSESS: "is it actually safe now?" → costs time + budget

WHY THE ASYMMETRY:
  → Missing a threat = DEATH (max cost)
  → False alarm = wastes energy (low cost)
  → "Better safe than sorry" = BUILT INTO HARDWARE
  → 🟢 Baumeister et al. 2001: "Bad is stronger than good"
  → 🟢 Kahneman & Tversky 1979: Loss aversion ~2x
  → 🟢 Rozin & Royzman 2001: Negativity dominance
```

This asymmetry resides in the EVALUATIVE layer. Direct-State is relatively
symmetric (pain onset ≈ touch onset speed). The Evaluative layer is STRONGLY
asymmetric: threat detection FAST, threat clearance SLOW. 🟡

### §2.6 Six Edge Cases

Human-Predictive-Drive identifies cases where the Evaluative/Direct-State boundary is a spectrum,
not a clean line (Reward-Signal-Architecture.md §3.4):

1. **Spicy food**: Direct-State INPUT (pain) → Evaluative REWARD (compensatory endorphin)
2. **Social touch**: Direct-State (CT) + Evaluative (social evaluation) → friend AMPLIFIES,
   stranger SUPPRESSES, infant = RAW
3. **Music + movement**: Evaluative (music) + Direct-State (body rhythm) + Social
4. **Massage**: Direct-State (CT) + Evaluative (technique evaluation) → bad massage:
   Evaluative negative → overrides Direct-State
5. **Sexual pleasure**: Direct-State + Evaluative + Social + Relief → compound
6. **ASMR**: Evaluative gate decides WHETHER Direct-State-like response fires → not universal

Edge case ④ (massage) directly applies to body therapy and confirms that
Evaluative Gates are ACTIVE during therapy sessions. 🟡

### §3.4 Expectation-Specificity Inverse (C05 §4.5)

"The MORE mechanistically specific a therapy is, the LESS its outcomes depend on
expectations. The LESS specific, the MORE expectations dominate" (C05 §4.5).

```
HIGH EXPECTATION CONTRIBUTION              LOW EXPECTATION CONTRIBUTION
B14, B06, B12 ... B05, B13 ... B02, B03 ... B09, B04 ... B08, B01
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────→
         ← Mechanism specificity INCREASES →
```

Clinical implication (C05 §4.5): For mechanistically specific therapies,
individual variation is BIOLOGICALLY driven (genetics, anatomy, dose-matching).
For non-specific therapies, individual variation is PSYCHOLOGICALLY driven
(expectations, therapeutic alliance, cultural alignment). 🟡

**Implication:** Sham-controlled trials test layer 3. Since layer 3 is where

mechanism-specific effects BEYOND the gate contribution.

sham ≈ verum because layers 1-2 (both gate-heavy) dominate. The "failure" to
outperform sham is not evidence the therapy "doesn't work" — it is evidence that
the therapy works THROUGH gates rather than through specific mechanisms. 🟡

**CH1. The non-specific floor is NOT purely Direct-State unmodulated by

**Implication for D01/D02:** Frame 2 needs REVISION. The non-specific floor is

universally (all therapies provide SOME physical contact AND SOME evaluative
context) and why it cannot be reduced to zero by either debunking scaffolding
(Direct-State component persists) or removing physical contact (encounter-only trials

**CH2. Human-Predictive-Drive's 4 modulation outcomes may be insufficient for body therapy —

```

This LAYERED state is invisible to Human-Predictive-Drive's 4-outcome model. The patient

dissonance (heavy, aching, spreading), but the TCM framework REFRAMES it as

different Therapy Reward Depth Gradient layers, held together by a meta-level

simultaneously activate multiple depth layers (D03 §4.2 F2). 🔴

**CH3. Human-Predictive-Drive's development trajectory assumes gradual gate FORMATION but does

```

```

Human-Predictive-Drive's Evaluative Gates concept needs THREE temporal scales to fully account for

### §4.5 Remaining Gaps

**G1. Evaluative Gates MAGNITUDE has never been measured in isolation for
body therapy.** 🔴

We know expectation accounts for up to 69.8% of pain variance (Vase 2013) and
the non-specific floor is SMD 0.2-0.4, but no study has isolated the GATE
contribution specifically — the proportion of the clinical effect that comes
from Evaluative modulation of Direct-State signals vs from Direct-State signals
themselves. The challenge: the gate and the signal are processed SIMULTANEOUSLY
(Step 3 integration), making isolation difficult.

**G2. The LAYERED gate state (CH2) has no direct neural evidence.** 🔴

The proposed 5th outcome (simultaneous different gate settings at different
depth layers) is inferred from clinical observation ("hurts good") and the
multi-layer Therapy Reward Depth Gradient (D03). No neuroimaging study has
examined whether the insula processes multiple gate outcomes simultaneously
vs sequentially vs integrating them into a single output.

**G3. Gate COMPILATION across therapy sessions is inferred, not measured.** 🔴

The prediction that Evaluative Gates become more robust with repeated positive
sessions (HF3) is consistent with therapeutic alliance literature but has not
been directly measured via gate-specific neural markers. No study tracks
insula activation patterns across a series of body therapy sessions.

**G4. Prediction error DIRECTION in body therapy is assumed, not measured.** 🔴

The prediction error model (F4) assumes positive prediction error for therapeutic encounters.
But the prediction error direction depends on the patient's prediction: if expectation is very
HIGH and actual experience is moderate, prediction error is NEGATIVE despite positive absolute
experience. Over-promising may produce negative prediction error → SUPPRESS. This has been
studied for pharmaceutical placebo but not for body therapy specifically.

**G5. The asymmetric transition timing has not been measured within body
therapy sessions.** 🔴

Human-Predictive-Drive's reward→dissonance FAST / dissonance→reward SLOW is from general
neuroscience (LeDoux, Baumeister, Kahneman). Whether the same timing applies
DURING body therapy sessions — where the physical stimulus continues throughout
— is unknown. The continuous Direct-State input during therapy may ACCELERATE recovery
from gate-suppress events compared to non-therapy contexts.

  → More robust gate → LESS vulnerable to momentary disruption
  → Result: experienced patients → better outcomes → partly from
    gate compilation, not just technique accumulation
```

This explains an otherwise puzzling finding: why body therapy outcomes improve
with repeated sessions EVEN for therapies with no plausible dose-response
mechanism at the physical level (B14, B06). The improvement is not from
accumulating physical stimulus — it is from GATE COMPILATION. Each positive
session compiles a stronger AMPLIFY gate for the next session.

**Cross-reference to D05 (Interoception-Training):** Gate compilation may also
improve interoceptive accuracy — patients become better at READING the Direct-
State signals that gates modulate, creating a virtuous cycle: better signal
reading → more accurate prediction → more precise prediction error → more efficient gate
activation.

## §6 — PREDICTIONS & TESTABLE HYPOTHESES

**P1. Removing scaffolding reduces outcomes proportionally to Evaluative Gates
contribution.** 🔴

Statement: If scaffolding is removed from B14 craniosacral (replaced with
"structured compassionate touch, no theoretical framework"), outcomes will
decrease by 40-60%. If removed from B01 acupuncture-EA, outcomes will decrease
by <15%.

Confirm: B14 scaffolding-removed shows large outcome decrease; B01 shows small
decrease. The decrease ratio matches the Evaluative Gates contribution
estimates (§3.3).

Refute: Both therapies show similar outcome decreases when scaffolding is
removed, OR B14 shows no decrease (scaffolding isn't the mechanism).

**P2. Negative events early in session reduce outcomes MORE than negative
events late in session.** 🔴

Statement: An unexpected painful stimulus in Phase 1 (first 5 minutes, before
gate establishment) will reduce total session outcomes by 30-50% more than the
same stimulus in Phase 2 (after gate establishment at 15+ minutes).

Confirm: Same painful stimulus produces significantly different outcome impacts
depending on session timing.

Refute: Timing of negative events has no differential impact on outcomes.

**P3. Gate-setting warm-up phase improves outcomes for ALL therapies.** 🔴

Statement: Adding a standardized 5-minute warm-up protocol (warm environment,
gentle initial contact, calm verbal framing) before ANY body therapy technique
will improve outcomes by SMD 0.1-0.2 compared to immediate technique onset.

Confirm: Warm-up consistently improves outcomes across therapy types.

Refute: Warm-up has no systematic effect, OR effect size varies dramatically
by therapy type in a way not predicted by the leverage gradient.

**P4. Experienced patients show LESS outcome variation than novice patients
within the same therapy type.** 🔴

Statement: Patients with 10+ prior sessions in a given therapy type will show
outcome variance 30-50% lower than first-time patients, reflecting gate
compilation (more robust, pre-set gate).

Confirm: Experienced patient outcomes cluster more tightly around the mean.

Refute: Variance is equal regardless of experience, OR experienced patients
show MORE variation (habituation or expectation inflation effects).

**P5. Cultural alignment predicts outcomes for scaffolding-dominant therapies
but not for mechanism-dominant therapies.** 🔴

Statement: Cultural alignment with therapy framework (e.g., Chinese patient +
TCM acupuncture vs Western patient + TCM acupuncture) will predict outcomes for
B07 cupping and B08 moxibustion (scaffolding-moderate) but NOT for B03 massage
or B04 deep tissue (mechanism-dominant).

Confirm: Cultural alignment × outcome interaction is significant for high-
scaffolding therapies and non-significant for low-scaffolding therapies.

Refute: Cultural alignment predicts outcomes equally across therapy types, OR
has no predictive value for any therapy type.

**P6. Practitioner confidence affects B14 outcomes more than B01 outcomes.** 🔴

Statement: Practitioner confidence level (measured via self-report scale) will
explain >40% of outcome variance for B14 craniosacral but <15% for B01
electroacupuncture, reflecting the Evaluative Gates leverage gradient.

Confirm: Practitioner confidence × therapy type interaction matches leverage
gradient predictions.

Refute: Practitioner confidence has similar effects across therapy types.

## §7 — LIMITATIONS & GAPS

**L1. The Evaluative Gates Leverage Gradient (§4.2 F2, §5.1) is entirely
theoretical.** No study has measured gate leverage at different therapy depth
layers. The gradient is inferred from the expectation-specificity inverse (C05)
and the Therapy Reward Depth Gradient (D03), but direct neural evidence is
absent.

**L2. Gate COMPILATION (§5.5) extrapolates from general therapeutic alliance
literature.** No body therapy study has tracked insula activation across a
series of sessions to confirm that gate settings change with repeated positive
experiences.

**L3. The LAYERED gate state (§4.3 CH2) is inferred from clinical
observation.** "Hurts good" and de qi are subjective reports consistent with
simultaneous gate settings but could also be explained by temporal alternation
(rapid switching between amplify and suppress) rather than true simultaneity.

**L4. Prediction error as gate activation (§4.2 F4) transfers from
pharmaceutical placebo to body therapy by analogy.** The prediction error
mechanism is well-established for pharmaceutical placebo (Wager 2004, Zubieta
2005) but has not been directly measured in body therapy contexts where
continuous physical stimulation occurs throughout the prediction-confirmation
cycle.

**L5. Session design principles (§5.3) are DERIVED not TESTED.** The Gate
Management Protocol is a logical synthesis from Human-Predictive-Drive's asymmetric transition and
Body-Signal-Architecture's therapy structure data. No RCT has compared gate-managed vs non-managed
session designs.

**L6. The Evaluative Gates contribution percentages (§3.3) are estimates with
WIDE uncertainty ranges.** C05 §3.3.2 acknowledges these are synthesis products,
not measured values. The exact fraction is unknown for any therapy.

## §8 — OPEN QUESTIONS

**Q1 — CRITICAL: Can Evaluative Gates be measured in REAL-TIME during body
therapy?** If anterior insula activation could be tracked during therapy (fMRI
too constrained; fNIRS possible?), the gate leverage gradient could be directly
tested — does anterior insula activation increase when techniques move from
Direct-State dominant to Evaluative dominant within a single session?

**Q2: Does the LAYERED gate state (simultaneous amplify + conflicted) have
distinct neural signatures?** If so, body therapy reveals a gate mode that
general neuroscience hasn't identified. If not, the LAYERED state may reduce
to rapid temporal alternation.

**Q3: Is gate compilation TRANSFERABLE across therapy types?** If a patient
compiles a robust AMPLIFY gate from 20 massage sessions, does this gate transfer
to their first acupuncture session? If yes, gate compilation is GENERAL (about
therapeutic encounters). If no, it is THERAPY-SPECIFIC (about specific
frameworks).

**Q4: Does over-promising CREATE negative prediction error?** If scaffolding
generates predictions STRONGER than what the therapy can confirm (e.g., "this
will completely eliminate your pain"), the prediction error may be negative → SUPPRESS. This
would mean scaffolding has an OPTIMAL LEVEL — strong enough to generate positive
prediction error but not so strong that reality disappoints. Has this been studied?

**Q5: How does the asymmetric transition interact with CONTINUOUS Direct-State input?**
Human-Predictive-Drive's timing (cortisol clearance ~60-90 min) applies in non-therapy contexts.
During body therapy, continuous Direct-State input may ACCELERATE recovery from suppress
events. Is gate recovery faster during therapy than outside therapy?

**Q6: Can gate DAMAGE (from negative therapeutic experiences) be quantified
and reversed?** Patients who have had painful, unconsented, or traumatizing body
therapy experiences may have COMPILED suppress gates that generalize to all body
therapy. How long do these negative compilations persist? Can they be
systematically reversed through graded positive exposure?
