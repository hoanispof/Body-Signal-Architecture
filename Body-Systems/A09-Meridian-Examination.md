# A09 — Meridian Examination: Data vs Theory

> **Position:** Track A file #9 — FINAL file in Phase 1. Intentionally placed last
> because it requires the full foundation from A01-A08 to evaluate.
>
> **Core Question:** Do meridians exist as anatomical structures? If not, what IS
> the physical basis for the clinical observations that meridian theory encodes?
>
> **Approach:** This file is EVALUATIVE, not merely descriptive. It examines each
> proposed physical substrate against evidence, using data already established in
> A01-A08 plus new meridian-specific research. Traditional theory is recorded as
> DATA (empirical observations accumulated over millennia), not endorsed or
> dismissed as mechanism.
>
> **Design Principle #4 reminder:** "Dữ liệu lâm sàng hàng nghìn năm = data thực.
> Không dismiss vì 'chưa giải thích được.' Nhưng cũng không chấp nhận giải thích
> truyền thống như mechanism — phải tìm mechanism riêng."

## §1 — PHYSICAL ARCHITECTURE

### §1.1 — Traditional Meridian Theory: What Is Claimed

```

  The meridian (jing-luo 经络) system as described in classical TCM:

  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  12 PRINCIPAL MERIDIANS (Jing Mai 经脉):                         │
  │    Bilateral paired channels, each associated with one organ.    │

  │                                                                   │

  │      Lung (LU), Spleen (SP), Heart (HT),                        │
  │      Kidney (KI), Pericardium (PC), Liver (LR)                  │
  │    YANG meridians (run lateral/posterior):                        │
  │      Large Intestine (LI), Stomach (ST), Small Intestine (SI),  │
  │      Bladder (BL), Triple Burner (TB), Gallbladder (GB)         │
  │                                                                   │
  │  8 EXTRAORDINARY VESSELS (Qi Jing Ba Mai 奇经八脉):              │
  │    Ren Mai, Du Mai, Chong Mai, Dai Mai,                          │
  │    Yin Wei, Yang Wei, Yin Qiao, Yang Qiao                       │
  │    = "Reservoirs" regulating overflow from 12 principal          │
  │    Only Du Mai and Ren Mai have their own acupoints              │
  │                                                                   │
  │  15 COLLATERALS (Luo Mai 络脉):                                  │
  │    Branch from principal meridians + countless smaller branches   │
  │                                                                   │
  │  12 DIVERGENT CHANNELS (Jing Bie 经别):                          │
  │    Travel deep to connect surface → organs → rejoin principal    │
  │                                                                   │
  │  12 SINEW CHANNELS (Jing Jin 经筋):                              │
  │    Follow musculotendinous distributions                         │
  │                                                                   │
  │  12 CUTANEOUS REGIONS (Pi Bu 皮部):                              │
  │    Skin zones corresponding to each meridian                     │
  │                                                                   │
  │  361 CLASSICAL ACUPOINTS on the 14 main channels                 │
  │  (12 principal + Ren Mai + Du Mai)                               │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  KEY TRADITIONAL CLAIMS:
    ① Qi flows through these channels as a substance/energy
    ② Each meridian connects to its named organ via internal pathway
    ③ Disease = blockage or imbalance of qi flow
    ④ Stimulating specific points restores balance
    ⑤ Qi flows in specific directions along each meridian
    ⑥ 24-hour circadian cycle (Chinese clock) governs qi flow
    ⑦ Five Elements (Wu Xing) govern inter-meridian relationships

    The meridian system was codified in Huangdi Neijing (黄帝内经,
    ~200 BCE). The modern standardized map (361 points) was significantly
    revised by Cheng Dan'an in the 1930s, who moved treatment locations
    away from blood vessels and introduced fine needles. The WHO
    standardized nomenclature in 1989.

  🔴 Mechanism claims: untested as described
```

### §1.2 — Candidate Substrate 1: Neural Pathways

```
🟢 NEURAL HYPOTHESIS — THE STRONGEST CANDIDATE:

  CORE CLAIM: Meridians represent TCM's empirical mapping of
  the peripheral nervous system's distribution.

  EVIDENCE FROM THIS PROJECT:

  ① ACUPOINTS = NERVE-RICH SITES:
    → Shanghai College (1960): 323/324 acupoints (99.7%) supplied
      by nerves — BUT this is nearly universal for any body point
    → A01 §1: all sensory pathways travel through PNS
    → A05 §1: higher mast cell density at acupoints (nerve-enriched)
    → A06 §1: local chemical milieu at acupoints contains
      neurally-derived signaling molecules

  ② MERIDIAN PATHS ≈ PERIPHERAL NERVE DISTRIBUTIONS:
    → Dorsher 2009-2018 (Medical Acupuncture): systematic comparison
      of meridian paths with peripheral nerve anatomy
    → Spleen Channel follows femoral/saphenous nerve trajectories
      (odds against chance: ~1 in 34 million)
    → 9/12 Back-Shu points occupy identical spinal levels as
      corresponding organ's autonomic innervation
      (odds against chance: ~1 in 5 billion)
    → 🟢 Dorsher 2022 (Longhua Chinese Medicine): comprehensive review

  ③ TRIGGER POINT-ACUPOINT CORRESPONDENCE:
    → Melzack 1977: 71% anatomic correspondence
    → Dorsher 2008: 93.3% (238/255) of most common trigger points have
      anatomically corresponding acupoints
    → 94% of these share pain indications
    → 91% of referred-pain patterns follow meridian distributions
    → A08 §4: 70% trigger point-acupoint overlap, motor endplate basis
    → Validated at University of Munich (Irnich, 2006-2007)
    → 🟢 Dorsher 2009 (The Journal of Pain)

  ④ PROKR2 — MOLECULAR BASIS FOR ACUPOINT SPECIFICITY:
    → A02 §3.5: Liu et al. 2021 (Nature)
    → PROKR2+ sensory neurons in deep fascia = ESSENTIAL for
      electroacupuncture at ST36 → vagal-adrenal axis
    → PROKR2 distribution PREDICTS which body regions produce
      specific autonomic effects
    → PROKR2 ablation → complete loss of effect
    → 🟢 Liu et al. 2021 (Nature) — landmark paper

  ⑤ SEGMENTAL CONVERGENCE:
    → A07 §1: dermatome-viscerotome convergence explains ~60-70%
      of acupoint-organ effects
    → A07 §4.2: Beissner 2011 — Head zones = Mu/Shu acupoints
    → A07 §4.3: Kim 2017 — neurogenic inflammatory spots at
      acupoint locations = PHYSICAL PROOF acupoints are real
    → Segmental model leaves ~30-40% NON-SEGMENTAL gap

  WHAT NEURAL HYPOTHESIS EXPLAINS:
    ✓ Acupoint locations (nerve-rich sites)
    ✓ Segmental organ effects (convergence)
    ✓ Trigger point correspondence
    ✓ Molecular specificity (PROKR2)
    ✓ De Qi sensation (nerve fiber activation)

  WHAT IT DOES NOT EXPLAIN:
    ✗ Non-segmental effects (ST36→gastric, L4-L5→T6-T9 gap)
    ✗ Why meridians run longitudinally (nerves run segmentally)
    ✗ Complete meridian pathway continuity

  🟢 Research: strong foundation
  🟡 Synthesis: "meridians = nervous system" is inference
```

### §1.3 — Candidate Substrate 2: Fascial Connective Tissue

```
🟢 FASCIAL HYPOTHESIS — STRONG ANATOMICAL CORRELATE:

  CORE CLAIM: Meridians correspond to the body's fascial
  connective tissue network.

  EVIDENCE FROM THIS PROJECT:

  ① LANGEVIN & YANDOW 2002 (The Anatomical Record):
    → Mapped 24 acupoints / 6 meridians on Visible Human arm
    → >80% of acupoints at intermuscular/intramuscular CT planes
    → ~50% of meridian pathway intersections at CT planes
    → Statistical significance: p < 0.001
    → A03 §3.4: detailed analysis
    → LIMITATIONS: single cadaver, arm only

  ② ANATOMY TRAINS CORRESPONDENCE:
    → A03 §3.3: Myers' 12 myofascial meridians
    → Dorsher 2009 (Medical Acupuncture): 8/9 lines (89%)
      substantially overlap with TCM meridian distributions
    → SBL ↔ Bladder, SFL ↔ Stomach/Spleen, LL ↔ Gallbladder,
      DFL ↔ Kidney/Liver
    → Myers himself cautions against this mapping — explicitly
      stated he "did not want to shoehorn" his system into TCM

  ③ CADAVER STUDY (Maurer et al. 2019):
    → 4 cadavers + 2 lower legs, 100 acupoints evaluated
    → Fascial fibers show directional patterns along meridian paths
    → Stomach meridian: characteristic "Z-form" pattern
    → BUT: VNBs found at only 2/100 acupoints (2%)
      = CONTRADICTS Heine's widely-cited 80% VNB claim
    → Forefoot/thigh: NO correspondence found
    → 🟢 Maurer et al. 2019 (eCAM, PMC6448339)

  ④ NEEDLE GRASP = FASCIAL MECHANISM:
    → A03 §3.2: Langevin's mechanotransduction chain
    → Needle rotation → 167% increase in pullout force
    → Collagen winding, NOT muscle contraction
    → Fibroblast spreading via Rho/Rac pathways
    → Effects spread SEVERAL CENTIMETERS from needle

  ⑤ FASCIAL INNERVATION:
    → A03 §1.5: Fede 2021 — hidden neural network in deep fascia
    → Free nerve endings throughout fascial layers
    → Mechanoreceptors (Ruffini, Pacini) in deep fascia

  WHAT FASCIA EXPLAINS:
    ✓ Acupoint locations (~80% at CT planes)
    ✓ Needle grasp / De Qi mechanism
    ✓ Longitudinal connectivity (fascial lines run head-to-toe)
    ✓ Non-segmental distant effects via force transmission
    ✓ Mechanical signal propagation

  WHAT FASCIA DOES NOT EXPLAIN:
    ✗ Only ~50% of meridian PATHWAYS follow fascial planes
    ✗ Organ-meridian specificity (fascia ≠ organ connection)
    ✗ Directionality of qi flow (fascial planes have no direction)
    ✗ Five Elements relationships
    ✗ "Fascia is everywhere" problem — nearly unfalsifiable

  ⭐ CRITICAL LIMITATION — THE 80%/50% SPLIT:
    80% of POINTS but only 50% of PATHWAYS correspond.
    This means acupoints are real fascial structures,
    but the CONNECTIONS between them are less clear.

  🟢 Research: acupoint-fascia correspondence well-established
  🟡 Synthesis: "fascia = meridians" is overstated
```

### §1.4 — Candidate Substrate 3: Neurovascular Bundles

```
🟡 NEUROVASCULAR HYPOTHESIS — WEAKENED BY RECENT DATA:

  CORE CLAIM: Acupoints overlie sites where neurovascular bundles
  (nerve + artery + vein) penetrate the superficial fascia.

  ① HEINE 1987-88:
    → Claimed >80% of acupoints have perforating VNBs
    → 3-9 mm thick bundles with arteriole, venules, nerve
    → Widely cited for 30+ years

  ② MA SX — NITRIC OXIDE AT ACUPOINTS:
    → A04 §3.3: Ma 2003 — NO levels 43-51% HIGHER at acupoints
      vs non-meridian controls (CV, BL, PC meridians)
    → nNOS specifically elevated (not eNOS)
    → Ma 2017: manual acupuncture further increases NO release
    → A04: acupoints = neurovascular bundle sites

  ③ CRITICAL CHALLENGE — MAURER 2019:
    → VNBs found at only 2/100 acupoints (2%)
    → DIRECTLY CONTRADICTS Heine's 80% claim
    → Fascia, not discrete VNBs, is the primary substrate
    → 🟢 This is the most rigorous recent test of the VNB hypothesis

  ASSESSMENT:
    → Individual acupoints DO overlie neurovascular structures
    → But a DEDICATED perforating VNB at each point is NOT confirmed
    → NO elevation at acupoints is real but may reflect nerve density
      + neurogenic inflammation rather than unique VNB anatomy

  🟡 Synthesis: partially supported, weakened by Maurer 2019
```

### §1.5 — Candidate Substrate 4: Primo Vascular System (PVS)

```
🔴 PVS HYPOTHESIS — UNCONFIRMED, CONTROVERSIAL:

  HISTORY:
    → Kim Bong-han (North Korea, 1962-1965): claimed discovery of
      a novel vascular system = physical meridians
    → "Bonghan corpuscles" at acupoints, "Bonghan ducts" = meridians
    → Used undisclosed "blue dye" — methods never fully described
    → Kim disappeared ~1966, institute closed abruptly
    → Only partial replication (Fujiwara, Japan) before 40-year gap

  MODERN REVIVAL:
    → Kwang-Sup Soh (Seoul National University, 2002-present)
    → Renamed "Primo Vascular System" in 2010
    → ~50 papers from Soh's group
    → Claimed structures: threadlike vessels (20-50 μm), multi-lumen,
      containing "primo fluid" with DNA-bearing granules ("sanals")
    → Found on organ surfaces, inside lymphatic/blood vessels,
      in CNS, on skin surface (4 subtypes)

  EVIDENCE STATUS (2026):
    → Fewer than 5 independent labs have observed PVS structures
    → Vodyanoy (Auburn University): partial confirmation
    → Nan et al. (Huazhong, China): partial confirmation
    → Staining methods: trypan blue, alcian blue, acridine orange
    → The sub-skin subtype (most relevant to surface meridians)
      is the LEAST verified

  CRITICAL PROBLEMS:
    → Limited independent replication outside Korean groups
    → Artifact concerns: structures may be fibrin strands,
      lymphatic fragments, or pathological deposits
    → Sanal-cell cycle (granule → cell differentiation) NEVER
      demonstrated
    → No functional evidence that PVS operates as circulatory system
    → PVS structures are MOBILE (floating in fluid), not FIXED
      like meridian pathways
    → Hall 2016 (Science-Based Medicine): compared PVS to N-rays
      — a famous case of pathological science

  RADIOACTIVE TRACER STUDIES:
    → De Vernejoul et al. 1985/1992: injected technetium-99m at
      acupoints → tracer migrated along meridian-like pathways
    → Simon et al. 1988: DECISIVE REPLICATION FAILURE
      → Identical patterns at CONTROL (non-acupoint) sites
      → Venous blockade eliminated all trajectories
      → Conclusion: vascular drainage, NOT meridians
    → Trajectories didn't extend along full meridian length
    → Target organs of Tc-99m (thyroid) always visualized
    → = Following venous drainage, NOT meridian channels
    → 🟢 Refuted — scientific consensus clear

  🔴 Hypothesis: interesting but unconfirmed
  🔴 NOT recognized in standard anatomy textbooks or by major
     anatomical societies as of 2026
```

### §1.6 — Candidate Substrate 5: Interstitial Fluid Channels

```
🟡 INTERSTITIAL HYPOTHESIS — EMERGING:

  ① BENIAS ET AL. 2018 (Scientific Reports):
    → Discovered body-wide network of fluid-filled,
      collagen-supported interstitial spaces
    → Previously invisible because standard fixation collapses them
    → Found below skin, lining digestive/urinary tracts, around
      vessels, between muscles
    → ~20% of body's fluid volume
    → Functions as "pre-lymphatic" space
    → Authors did NOT propose meridian connection

  ② ZHANG WEI-BO — LOW HYDRAULIC RESISTANCE CHANNELS:
    → Zhang WB et al. 2008-2015 (eCAM, JAMS)
    → Some interstitial regions have higher permeability forming
      "low hydraulic resistance points"
    → Adjacent low-resistance points connect into channels
      following meridian trajectories
    → Minipig experiments: hydraulic resistance at meridian points
      DECREASED during acupuncture; no change at controls
    → Blocking these channels → hyperalgesia in animals
    → Fluid movement follows Darcy's law (porous media)
    → Most experimentally grounded meridian research

  ③ FASCIAL-INTERSTITIAL SYSTEM (2026):
    → Xia et al. 2026 (Frontiers in Physiology): proposed the
      fascial-interstitial system as analogous to TCM's Sanjiao-
      Mocou system
    → Ubiquitous, fluid-transporting, cavity-containing CT system
    → 🔴 Hypothesis — analogy-based, not empirically validated

  WHAT INTERSTITIAL HYPOTHESIS EXPLAINS:
    ✓ Body-wide connectivity (continuous interstitial network)
    ✓ Potential for fluid-mediated signal transport
    ✓ "Qi flow" as interstitial fluid dynamics

  WHAT IT DOES NOT EXPLAIN:
    ✗ Specific meridian pathways (interstitium is everywhere)
    ✗ Acupoint specificity (same "everywhere" problem)
    ✗ Organ-meridian connections

  🟡 Zhang's LHRC work is the strongest element (measurable data)
  🔴 Full interstitium = meridians claim is speculative
```

### §1.7 — Candidate Substrate 6: Bioelectrical Properties

```
🟡 BIOELECTRICAL HYPOTHESIS — MIXED EVIDENCE:

  ① SKIN CONDUCTANCE AT ACUPOINTS:
    → Niboyet 1957-1963: original claim of lower skin resistance
    → Becker, Reichmanis, Marino 1976: confirmed electrical
      conductance maxima at some acupoints (p < 0.05)
    → Ahn et al. 2008 (Bioelectromagnetics): SYSTEMATIC REVIEW
      → 16 articles (18 studies) included
      → 5/9 point studies showed positive association
      → 7/9 meridian studies showed positive association
      → Quality: "generally poor, limited by small sample size
        and multiple confounders"
      → CONCLUSION: "evidence does not conclusively support"
        electrical distinguishability
    → Kelvin Probe (Gow et al. 2012): noncontact measurement
      → >60 mV maximum at LI-4 (2mm diameter) in 2/3 subjects
      → No maximum at PC-6
      → Intriguing but tiny sample, inconsistent across points

  ② METHODOLOGICAL PROBLEMS (Ahn & Martinsen 2007):
    → Electrode polarizability artifacts
    → Stratum corneum thickness variation
    → Sweat gland density confound (acupoints often at sweat-rich
      areas → lower resistance from moisture, NOT meridian)
    → Contact medium variation, electrode geometry
    → High short-term but low long-term reproducibility

  ③ COLLAGENOUS BAND CONNECTION (Ahn et al. 2010, PLOS ONE):
    → Echogenic collagenous bands (ultrasound) associated with
      lower impedance along SOME meridians
    → PC meridian: lower impedance; Spleen: NOT
    → LI meridian: lower impedance; Liver/Bladder: NOT
    → = Inconsistent across meridians

  ④ ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION:
    → Kim 2018: SP + CGRP → vascular dilation + leakage →
      increased subskin tissue water at acupoints →
      explains electrical differences WITHOUT semiconductor theory
    → A07 §4.3: neurogenic inflammation at acupoints = biological
      explanation for electrical properties

  ⑤ BECKER'S DC SEMICONDUCTOR MODEL:
    → Robert Becker (1960s-1980s): proposed perineural DC system
    → Injury currents: 🟢 real, replicated
    → Body-wide semiconductor network: 🔴 never independently
      validated as a coherent system
    → Not to be confused with Michael Levin's modern bioelectric
      work (legitimate but does NOT validate meridian claims)

  ⑥ COLLAGEN PIEZOELECTRICITY:
    → Fukada & Yasuda 1957: bone/collagen IS piezoelectric
    → 🟢 Real physics — ~300 μV during normal walking
    → Too small for physiologically significant signaling
    → 🔴 In vivo meridian signaling via piezoelectricity: undemonstrated

  ⑦ PROTON SEMICONDUCTOR (Ho 1998):
    → Proton hopping along hydrated collagen: 🟢 demonstrated in vitro
    → "Proton superconduction" at body temperature: 🔴 contradicted
      by thermal decoherence arguments
    → Conductivity (~10⁻³ S/m at 90% RH) orders of magnitude below
      neural signaling

  OVERALL ASSESSMENT:
    → Some acupoints show electrical differences in some studies
    → But these differences are inconsistent, confounded by anatomy
      (sweat glands, tissue water, nerve density), and do not
      demonstrate a coherent bioelectrical meridian system
    → Best explanation: neurogenic inflammation (Kim 2018, A07 §4.3)

  🟡 Synthesis: electrical differences exist but are NOT evidence
     for a dedicated electrical meridian system
```

### §1.8 — Candidate Substrate 7: Propagated Sensation Along Channels (PSC)

```
🟡 PSC — REAL PHENOMENON, DEBATED MECHANISM:

  WHAT PSC IS:
    → During acupuncture, some patients report sensation traveling
      ALONG the classical meridian trajectory
    → Sensations: numbness, pressure, heaviness, warmth, tingling,
      radiating paraesthesia
    → Called "propagated sensation along channels" or "de qi
      propagation" (distinct from local de qi)
    → Prevalence: ~10-30% of patients experience clear PSC

  PROPOSED MECHANISMS:

  ① AXON REFLEX / NEUROGENIC:
    → Antidromic activation of C-fibers spreading from needle site
    → CGRP/SP release → neurogenic inflammation along nerve path
    → A01 §3.4: axon reflex mechanism established
    → Explains LOCAL spread but not long-distance propagation

  ② FASCIAL WAVE:
    → Mechanical signal propagating through fascial planes
    → Langevin: fibroblast cytoskeletal response spreads
      several centimeters from needle
    → A03 §3.3: Anatomy Trains = potential wave guides

  ③ VOLUME TRANSMISSION IN PERIPHERAL TISSUE:
    → Li et al. 2013 (Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine):
      signal spread through interstitial fluid
    → Slow, diffuse, non-synaptic

  ④ NO-MEDIATED SIGNALING:
    → Ma SX: NO elevated at acupoints; acupuncture further
      increases release
    → NO-ergic molecules + neuropeptides contribute to signal
      transduction via dorsal medulla-thalamic pathways
    → A04 §3.3: NO signaling at acupoints

  ⑤ REFERRED SENSATION (SIMPLEST EXPLANATION):
    → A07 §3.3: referred pain follows dermatome/myotome patterns
    → PSC may = referred sensation along neural pathways
    → Pattern determined by nerve distribution, not meridian channels

  ⭐ PSC follows the general DIRECTION of meridians in many cases
    BUT has not been proven to follow a dedicated physical channel.
    Multiple conventional mechanisms (axon reflex, referred
    sensation, fascial propagation) can explain the phenomenon
    without requiring meridian-specific anatomy.

  🟡 Synthesis: PSC is real but explainable by known mechanisms
```

### §2.2 — What Acupoints Actually ARE

```
🟡 ACUPOINTS — MULTI-SYSTEM CONVERGENCE ZONES:

  Integrating evidence from A01-A08, acupoints are sites where
  MULTIPLE body systems physically converge:

  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
```

### §3.2 — Non-Segmental Effects: The Remaining Gap

### §3.3 — fMRI Evidence for Meridian Specificity

```
🟡 NEUROIMAGING — PARTIAL SPECIFICITY, NO MERIDIAN CONFIRMATION:

  ① SAME-MERIDIAN SHARED ACTIVATION:
    → Bai et al. 2009: LR3+LR6 (Liver) → ipsilateral BA7;
      ST36+ST43 (Stomach) → ipsilateral BA10
    → Some overlap within meridians BUT not exclusive
    → Could reflect shared segmental innervation

  ② ADJACENT POINTS, DIFFERENT MERIDIANS (2025):
    → ST36 (Stomach) vs GB34 (Gallbladder) — anatomically
      adjacent, overlapping dermatomes (L4-S2), different meridians
    → DISTINCT brain networks: ST36 → visual network, correlated
      with noradrenaline/acetylcholine maps; GB34 → subcortical/
      basal ganglia, correlated with dopamine/serotonin
    → = Some specificity beyond simple somatotopy
    → 🟡 Single study, needs replication

  ③ CHO ET AL. RETRACTION:
    → 1998 PNAS: BL67 → visual cortex (seemed to confirm
      traditional vision-related acupoint claim)
    → 2006: RETRACTED by 5/8 authors — methodological artifact
    → Beissner 2011: identified specific flaws (eyes-closed
      baseline, no attention control, fixed-effects analysis)
    → 🟢 Retraction is documented fact

  ④ META-ANALYSES (2023-2025):
    → Huang et al. 2012 (PLoS ONE): acupuncture activates
      somatosensory + limbic regions consistently
    → 2025 umbrella review (PMC12401896): NO CONSENSUS on
      acupoint specificity. "There do not appear to be specific
      mechanisms unique to each acupoint"
    → 2025 multimodal ALE meta-analysis (39 studies, 723 subjects):
      core regions = bilateral inferior parietal lobule + thalamus
    → Most studies rated "very low" quality by GRADE

  ⑤ DE QI NEURAL CORRELATES:
    → Napadow et al. 2009-2010: De Qi WITHOUT sharp pain →
      DEACTIVATION of DMN (medial PFC, amygdala, posterior
      cingulate, parahippocampus)
    → + ACTIVATION of salience network (insula, thalamus, ACC)
    → = One of the most replicated findings in acupuncture fMRI
    → De Qi necessary for therapeutic effect? UNRESOLVED
      (Zhang 2013 systematic review: insufficient evidence)
    → 🟢 De Qi-limbic deactivation pattern

  ⑥ SHAM VS REAL:
    → Vickers et al. / Acupuncture Trialists' Collaboration
      (2012-2018): ~17,922 patients for chronic pain
    → Acupuncture > sham > no treatment
    → BUT: real-sham difference is "relatively modest"
    → Sham at SAME points ≈ verum; sham at NON-indicated
      points < verum = POINT LOCATION MATTERS but effect is
      mainly from needle insertion + context
    → 🟢 Largest individual patient data meta-analysis
    → ATLAS NOTE: "modest" describes the GENERAL real-sham
      chronic pain gap, not ALL acupuncture effects. PC6 for
      PONV = equivalent to ondansetron (pharmaceutical) per
      Cochrane (Lee & Fan 2009). Point-specific efficacy can
      exceed the pooled average significantly.
      → Atlas: AP-Mechanism-Summary §3.0 Pattern 2

  ⑦ PARADIGM SHIFT:
    → Field moving from "which region lights up" →
      "how do brain networks reconfigure"
    → DMN modulation as key mechanism (sustained changes
      after needle removal)
    → Network neuroscience > single-region activation
    → All evidence is CORRELATIONAL, not causal

  🟡 Synthesis: some acupoint specificity exists beyond simple
  somatotopy, but meridian-level organization is NOT confirmed
  by neuroimaging. Point effects likely reflect local innervation
  patterns rather than traditional meridian assignment.
```

```
    → Some patients rarely feel PSC
    → Individual variation in nerve fiber density at acupoints

  ⑤ GENETIC VARIATION:
    → PROKR2 expression levels (individual differences unknown)
    → A06 §7: OPRM1/COMT (opioid), FAAH/CNR1 (endocannabinoid system) genetics
    → TRPV1 polymorphisms affect De Qi threshold

  ⑥ INFLAMMATORY STATUS:
    → A06 §1.1: peripheral opioid receptors UPREGULATED by
      inflammation → more inflamed tissue responds MORE strongly
    → Kim 2017: acupoints are dynamic, appearing with disease
    → = Patient's current pathological state changes which
      points are "active"

  IMPLICATIONS:
    → Personalized acupoint mapping may improve outcomes
    → "One-size-fits-all" point prescriptions are approximations
    → TARA project (Langevin 2025) attempting standardized atlas
      but individual variation remains a fundamental challenge
```

## §8 — OPEN QUESTIONS

```
🔴 WHAT IS NOT YET UNDERSTOOD:

  Q1: WHY DO ACUPOINTS CLUSTER AT MULTI-SYSTEM CONVERGENCE ZONES?
    Is there a developmental/embryological reason why nerves,
    fascia, vessels, and immune cells concentrate at specific
    sites that traditional medicine independently identified?
    → Candidates: neurovascular bundle development patterns,
      somite segmentation, fascial node formation
    → Status: no systematic developmental study exists

  Q2: CAN THE NON-SEGMENTAL GAP BE FULLY CLOSED?
    PROKR2-vagal pathway explains ST36→gastric. But do ALL
    non-segmental acupoint effects have identified ascending
    neural pathways, or do some require additional mechanisms?
    → Status: PROKR2 is established for one pathway; systematic
      mapping of others is needed

  Q3: DOES POINT PRECISION MATTER CLINICALLY?
    Network meta-analyses show verum ≈ sham at same points.
    Is the EXACT point location clinically important, or is
    hitting the general convergence zone sufficient?
    → Implications for: training standards, device development
    → Status: active debate, data favor "zone not point"

  Q4: WHAT IS THE MECHANISM OF PSC?
    Propagated sensation follows meridian trajectories in
    ~10-30% of patients. Is this axon reflex, fascial wave,
    referred sensation, or something else?
    → Status: multiple candidates, none confirmed as primary

  Q5: ARE THERE UNDISCOVERED PHYSICAL CONNECTIONS?
    The sub-skin PVS, interstitial fluid channels, and
    bioelectrical pathways are all poorly characterized.
    Could improved imaging reveal connective structures
    not visible with current methods?
    → Benias 2018: standard fixation collapses interstitial
      spaces → some structures may be invisible to conventional
      histology
    → Status: open — absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence

  Q6: HOW DO DYNAMIC ACUPOINTS WORK?
    Kim 2017: neurogenic spots appear only with organ disease.
    What is the temporal dynamics? How quickly do they appear
    and resolve? Can they be predicted?
    → Status: very limited data. Only animal studies.

  Q7: WILL TARA RESOLVE THE DEBATE?
    Langevin's TARA project (standardized 3D atlas with MRI)
    is the most systematic attempt to empirically map acupoints.
    Will this data resolve the competing hypotheses?
    → Status: under development, results forthcoming

  Q8: IS "MERIDIAN" A USEFUL SCIENTIFIC CONCEPT?
    Should the field retain "meridian" as a concept with
    revised mechanistic content, or abandon it entirely in
    favor of neuroanatomical/fascial terminology?
    → Dry needling practitioners already work without it
    → But meridian theory organizes clinical relationships
      that pure neuroanatomy hasn't replicated
    → Status: philosophical/practical question, no consensus
```

> *"Traditional Chinese medicine identified treatment-relevant body sites with remarkable
> accuracy — acupoints are real multi-system convergence zones confirmed by modern anatomy
> (80% at fascial planes, 93% overlapping trigger points, neurogenic inflammatory spots
> at predicted locations). However, the CONNECTIONS between these points — meridian channels
> carrying qi — have not been found as anatomical structures. The strongest evidence suggests
> that 'meridians' represent the spatial overlap of neural, fascial, vascular, immune, and
> chemical systems creating functionally connected corridors, not a single dedicated channel
> system. The clinical map is validated; the explanatory mechanism requires revision.
> As Langevin (2025) acknowledged: 'although acupuncture points have been used for thousands
> of years, their exact biological basis remains uncertain.' The question is no longer WHETHER
> acupoints work, but HOW — and the answer involves multiple body systems operating
> simultaneously, not a single meridian pathway."*
