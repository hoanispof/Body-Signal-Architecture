# Common-Mechanisms

## §1 — PURPOSE & METHODOLOGY

### §1.1 — Why This Cross-Reference Exists

Individual Track B files each propose their own mechanism model (§5 in each file). These models use different organizing principles: B01 organizes by anatomical spatial scale (6 levels from local tissue to systemic), B07/B09 by Track A system dominance (vascular-first), B10 by processing level (proprioceptive-first), B12 by cognitive-first logic. Each model is internally coherent but exists in isolation.

**This cross-reference answers three questions no individual Track B file can:**

1. **What mechanisms are SHARED across therapies?** Gate control appears in ~13/16 therapies — does that make it trivial or fundamental?
2. **What mechanisms are UNIQUE to specific therapies?** HSP is B08-only, PROKR2 is B01-EA-only — what does exclusivity reveal about therapeutic specificity?
3. **Can the different mechanism models be UNIFIED?** B01's 6-level and B11's 4-level describe the SAME building from different doors (B11 §5.3) — what does the unified building look like?

### §1.2 — Data Sources

- **Mechanism models:** All 16 Track B §5 (Proposed Mechanisms) sections, read in full
- **System mappings:** All 16 Track B §6 (Body System Pathways) via C01
- **Reality Check findings:** 7 RCs from Phase 2 (~15 additions, ~4 corrections)
- **C01 patterns:** 10 emergent patterns, particularly System Dominance Fingerprints (§4.1), Therapeutic Scaffolding Cluster (§4.9), and Endocannabinoid System Pressure Threshold (§4.6)

### §1.3 — Methodology

**Mechanism extraction.** Every mechanism mentioned in every Track B §5 was cataloged with: mechanism name, confidence level, Track A system mapping, and therapy occurrence. Total: ~250+ mechanism entries across 16 files, reducible to ~51 distinct mechanism types.

**Frequency counting.** Each mechanism type was scored for presence/absence across 16 therapies. "Present" requires the mechanism to be identified at 🟢 or 🟡 confidence in the therapy's §5; inferred mechanisms (🔴) are noted separately.

**Level unification.** Different therapy-specific level models were mapped onto a common spatial-scale framework, preserving the insight that each therapy's model reflects its PRIMARY mechanism domain.

**Shared/unique classification.** Mechanisms were classified into four tiers:
- **Universal** (≥10/16): fundamental to body-based therapy
- **Common** (5-9/16): shared across significant subset
- **Narrow** (2-4/16): restricted to specific therapy families
- **Unique** (1/16): therapy-exclusive mechanisms

## §3 — MAIN ANALYSIS

### §3.1 — Mechanism Level Unification

All therapy-specific models map onto a common **Dual-Axis Framework** with two orthogonal dimensions:

**Axis 1 — Spatial Scale** (WHERE the mechanism operates):

```
L1  LOCAL TISSUE        At stimulation site. Seconds to minutes.
L2  SEGMENTAL/SPINAL    Spinal cord dorsal horn processing. Milliseconds to seconds.
L3  SUPRASPINAL         Brainstem (PAG, NTS, LC, DRN). Seconds to minutes.
L4  CORTICAL/CENTRAL    Higher brain (limbic, DMN, cortex). Minutes to sustained.
L5  SYSTEMIC            Whole-body effects. Minutes to hours to sustained.
L6  COGNITIVE/BEHAV     Mind-mediated effects. Immediate to sustained.
```

### §3.3 — Unique Mechanism Analysis

For each therapy with unique mechanisms, a brief analysis of what the unique mechanism ADDS beyond shared pathways:

**B01 Acupuncture (EA)** — PROKR2 provides the first molecular basis for WHY specific anatomical locations produce specific systemic effects. Han's frequency-dependent opioids enable chemical prescribing via parameter selection. Goldman's adenosine pathway provides a tissue-penetration-specific analgesic mechanism. Together, these make B01 the most mechanistically specified therapy in Track B. Without EA, B01 would lose PROKR2 and Han — reducing to shared mechanisms plus needle-specific L1 effects. 🟡

**B08 Moxibustion** — TRPV1 direct activation is molecularly unique: TRPV1 is a convergence hub for heat + endocannabinoid system (AEA) + eicosanoids (PGE2) + opioid sustain (prevents MOR desensitization). HSP provides cytoprotective function via thermal stress response. These make B08 mechanistically ISOLATED from all mechanical therapies — the ONLY therapy operating through a non-mechanical primary channel. 🟡

**B07 Cupping** — Fascial decompression is the ONLY therapy applying force away from the body surface. Blood redistribution creates unique hemodynamic patterns. These explain cupping's narrow but real clinical profile (pain only, no function/psychology): vascular mechanisms without higher neural engagement. 🟡

**B09 Gua Sha** — HIF-1α pathway is independent from HO-1, giving B09 TWO distinct vascular mechanisms. This dual pathway may explain B09's broader clinical profile than B07 (pain + hepatoprotection + breast engorgement + perimenopausal). The HIF-1α finding has causal evidence (DMOG reversal, Ge 2025) — one of the strongest causal demonstrations in Track B. 🟢

**B10 Chiropractic** — Velocity-dependent receptor profile hypothesis suggests HVLA activates qualitatively different proprioceptors than slow mobilization. Joint gapping is biomechanically unique. These explain B10's distinctive Track A profile (A08 PRIMARY — only therapy where musculoskeletal is dominant). 🟡

**B16 Yoga** — GABA hypothesis (Streeter) is the most yoga-specific mechanism in Track B. Activating pranayama is unique: no other therapy deliberately induces sympathetic activation as therapeutic tool (autonomic FLEXIBILITY training). Bower's transcriptional anti-inflammatory operates at a deeper molecular level than exercise myokines, potentially explaining effects at low intensity. 🟡

**B12 EFT** — Exposure/habituation is unique within Track B but is well-established in cognitive therapy (CBT, exposure therapy, EMDR). B12's uniqueness is in COMBINING cognitive protocol with somatic stimulus — whether the somatic component adds anything beyond the cognitive is the central unresolved question. 🟡

#### §3.4.1 — Definition

**Therapeutic scaffolding** = an elaborate theoretical framework (maps, point systems, rhythms, energy flows) that provides protocol structure, patient narrative, and enhanced therapeutic context — BUT whose stated mechanism is either **unsupported** or **contradicted** by evidence. The scaffolding has therapeutic VALUE (via Evaluative Gates) without having mechanistic VALIDITY.

#### §3.4.3 — What Scaffolding DOES (Positive Functions)

Despite mechanistic invalidity, scaffolding serves real therapeutic functions:

1. **Protocol structure** — gives practitioner a systematic procedure (B06: 60-min foot coverage; B14: 10-step protocol; B05: 30+ maps for any complaint). Without scaffolding, treatment becomes unstructured touching. 🟡

2. **Patient narrative** — gives patient an explanation ("your kidney zone needs work" = comprehensible, even if anatomically wrong). Understanding → sense of control → reduced anxiety → better outcomes. B16 self-efficacy data: narrative mediates 36% of disability effect (PMC9510936). 🟢

3. **Practitioner confidence** — confident practitioner = better outcomes (therapeutic alliance research). B14 practitioners who believe they detect CSR are MORE confident and WARMER than skeptical ones. 🟡

4. **Expectation enhancement** — elaborate theory → enhanced Evaluative Gates → larger placebo component. B14's "The Theory IS the Placebo" finding (B14 §5.4). 🟡

5. **Dose consistency** — scaffolding ensures minimum treatment coverage (B13: all major muscle groups; B06: all foot zones). Without scaffolding, treatment is ad hoc and potentially inadequate. 🟡

#### §3.4.4 — The Scaffolding Paradox

**The paradox:** Scaffolding IMPROVES outcomes via mechanisms 1-5 above, BUT it also MISLEADS about why the therapy works. B06 practitioners who believe they are treating kidneys via foot zone 3 are getting real clinical results (anxiety↓, sleep↑) — but the mechanism is general foot massage + therapeutic context, not zone-organ reflexes.

**Implications for clinical practice:**
- Dismantling scaffolding might REDUCE effectiveness (removing narrative → removing meaning → reducing Evaluative Gates)
- BUT maintaining scaffolding perpetuates mechanistic misconceptions
- Resolution: acknowledge scaffolding AS scaffolding — it has therapeutic value BECAUSE of its psychological functions, not DESPITE them

**Implications for research:**
- Sham controls that preserve scaffolding (sham reflexology at non-indicated zones) test zone specificity
- Sham controls that remove scaffolding (no foot chart, random touching) test entire therapeutic encounter
- These are DIFFERENT questions requiring DIFFERENT study designs
- Most negative sham-controlled trials in B06/B14 used scaffolding-preserved shams → conclude scaffolding is NOT mechanism-specific → correct

### §3.6 — Key Cross-Therapy Mechanism Patterns

Six cross-cutting patterns emerged from the mechanism inventory that are not visible in any individual Track B file:

#### Pattern 1: The Gate Control Floor 🟢

Gate control (Melzack & Wall 1965) is the MINIMUM mechanism for any body-based therapy applying mechanical stimulus. It requires only Aβ fiber activation — achievable with light to moderate pressure. It provides TEMPORARY pain reduction (during stimulation + minutes after) but NO lasting therapeutic change.

**Implication:** Gate control is necessary but NOT sufficient. Therapies that ONLY engage gate control (B06, B14, B12) have the weakest clinical profiles. Therapies that build ABOVE gate control (B01 adds L3-L6; B04 adds the endocannabinoid system; B10 adds proprioceptive reset) have stronger profiles. Gate control is the floor, not the ceiling.

#### Pattern 2: The Depth-Mechanism Cascade 🟡

Increasing stimulus depth (surface → subcutaneous → deep fascia → muscle → bone/joint) activates progressively more mechanisms in a predictable cascade:

```
DEPTH                    MECHANISMS ADDED                THERAPIES
───────────────────────  ────────────────────────────── ────────────────
Surface skin (<5N)       CT afferents, gate control      B14, B03-light
                         (Aβ only), oxytocin
Subcutaneous (5-40N)     Somatovisceral reflex, vagal    B02, B03-mod,
                         shift, thixotropy               B05, B06, B13
Deep fascia (40-65N)     endocannabinoid system ACTIVATION (threshold),     B03-deep, B04,
                         mechanotransduction (Crane)      B01 (needle reaches)
Muscle (65-110+N)        Descending modulation (PAG),    B04-deep, B03-deep,
                         trigger point deactivation, DNIC          B11 (needle reaches)
Bone/Joint               Proprioceptive reset, joint     B10 (HVLA)
                         gapping, periosteal effects
```

This cascade explains B03's Pressure-Dependent Pathway Model (§5.3): different pressure levels activate qualitatively different mechanisms, not just quantitative differences. The endocannabinoid system threshold at ~40-65N (C01 §4.6) is the most clinically important transition point. 🟡

#### Pattern 3: Penetration Bypass 🟢

Needle penetration (B01, B11) BYPASSES the depth-mechanism cascade entirely. A 0.25mm needle at 20mm depth reaches deep fascia and muscle directly, engaging:
- Collagen winding (L1, unique)
- Goldman adenosine 24x (L1, unique)
- Micro-wound/DAMPs cascade (L1, unique)
- LTR/ACh depletion (L1, B11)
- Deep fascia endocannabinoid system (via physical reach, not pressure magnitude)

This explains the 8,000× pressure paradox (B02 §5.3): acupuncture needle tip exerts ~600 MPa vs acupressure thumb ~75 kPa, yet BOTH produce clinical effects — because retained mechanisms (gate control, somatovisceral reflex) are NOT pressure-magnitude-dependent, while lost mechanisms (adenosine, PROKR2, LTR) are PENETRATION-dependent. 🟡

#### Pattern 4: The HO-1 Convergence 🟢

Two therapies with DIFFERENT force vectors (B07 vertical suction, B09 horizontal shear) converge on the SAME biochemical endpoint (HO-1). This convergence reveals that HO-1 activation requires:
- Controlled capillary microtrauma (sufficient for extravasated RBCs)
- Macrophage CD163 phagocytosis
- Heme catabolism → CO + biliverdin + ferritin

Force DIRECTION is irrelevant — force CONSEQUENCE (capillary disruption) determines pathway activation. This distinguishes microtrauma therapies from ALL other mechanical therapies (B03 massage, B04 deep tissue produce NO HO-1 because they don't disrupt capillaries).

B09 adds a SECOND vascular pathway (HIF-1α, Ge 2025) independent of HO-1, giving it broader clinical profile than B07 (pain + hepatoprotection + breast + perimenopausal vs pain only). 🟢

#### Pattern 5: The Active Therapy Emergence 🟡

B15 and B16 share a cluster of mechanisms UNAVAILABLE to passive therapies (B01-B14):
- Voluntary ANS access (breathing = Zone D Bridge)
- Myokine anti-inflammatory (exercise IL-6)
- Self-efficacy (patient as agent, not recipient)
- Motor learning → neuroplastic adaptation compounding over years
- Highest safety profile (no external risk)

These mechanisms explain why active therapies show CUMULATIVE effects (12+ weeks in positive RCTs) rather than the ACUTE effects of passive therapies (single session measurable). The underlying difference: passive therapies provide INPUT (signal to be processed); active therapies build CAPACITY (system that generates its own signals).

B16 adds three unique mechanisms (GABA, activating pranayama, transcriptional anti-inflammatory) that B15 lacks, but B15 has stronger clinical evidence for its primary indication (fall prevention). The GABA hypothesis (Streeter) remains the most yoga-specific mechanism in Track B — if confirmed, it would move B16 significantly higher on the specificity spectrum. 🟡

#### Pattern 6: The Cognitive-Somatic Boundary 🟡

B12 EFT sits at the boundary between body-based therapy and cognitive therapy. Its mechanism analysis reveals that:
- Cognitive component (exposure, reframing) = 🟢 established CBT mechanisms
- Somatic component (tapping) = 🔴 near-zero Track A engagement (C01 §4.9)
- Combined = 🟡 clinical effects exist but likely from cognitive, not somatic

This raises the GENERAL question: how much of ANY body-based therapy's effect comes from cognitive/contextual factors? Even B01 acupuncture (the most mechanistically specified therapy) faces the verum vs sham challenge: specific acupuncture > sham > no treatment, but verum-sham difference is often small compared to sham-no treatment difference.

The cognitive-somatic boundary is not sharp — it's a CONTINUUM from B14 (nearly 100% cognitive/contextual) to B01-EA (most somatic-specific). Every therapy sits somewhere on this continuum, and the EXACT position for most therapies is unknown. 🔴

## §4 — EMERGENT PATTERNS

Patterns discovered from the cross-reference that are NOT visible in individual Track B files.

### §4.1 — The Mechanism Inequality Distribution 🟡

Mechanism occurrence across 16 therapies follows a power law, not a normal distribution:

```
Occurrence    # of mechanism types    Examples
────────────  ────────────────────── ────────────────────────
≥10/16          7 types (14%)         Gate control, parasympathetic,
                                      evaluative gates, opioids
5-9/16         13 types (25%)         Mechanotransduction, endocannabinoid system,
                                      DNIC, vagal anti-inflammatory
2-4/16         16 types (31%)         HO-1, collagen winding,
                                      voluntary ANS, LTR
1/16           15 types (29%)         PROKR2, HSP, GABA, HIF-1α
```

**14% of mechanisms account for the majority of therapeutic coverage.** The top 7 mechanisms (gate control, parasympathetic shift, evaluative gates, cortisol, opioids, descending modulation, vasodilation) are engaged by almost all therapies. This means the BASE therapeutic effect is similar across therapies — differentiation comes from the TAIL of specific mechanisms.

**Implication:** Most clinical trials comparing different therapies will find small or non-significant differences, because the shared mechanism base dominates. Detecting therapy-specific effects requires targeting conditions where unique mechanisms matter (e.g., B10 for proprioception, B07/B09 for HO-1-responsive conditions). 🟡

### §4.2 — Three Mechanism Acquisition Strategies 🟡

Therapies can be classified by HOW they acquire their mechanism profile:

1. **Depth Strategy** (B01, B04, B11): Penetrate or press DEEPER to access mechanisms located in deeper tissue layers. More mechanisms per stimulation site, fewer sites needed. B01 = deepest access via needle bypass.

2. **Breadth Strategy** (B03, B13, B15): Stimulate MORE tissue area at moderate depth. Fewer mechanisms per site, but cumulative activation across large body surface. B15 = broadest coverage (all 9 Track A systems).

3. **Specificity Strategy** (B08, B10, B07): Exploit a UNIQUE physical property (thermal, velocity, negative pressure) to access mechanisms unavailable to standard mechanical stimulation. Narrower profile but distinctive.

B16 Yoga uniquely combines all three strategies: depth (Yin yoga = deep fascial), breadth (Vinyasa = whole body), and specificity (pranayama = unique ANS access). This may explain its Adaptive Spectrum property — it is a family of strategies, not a single strategy. 🟡

### §4.3 — The Confidence Gradient 🟢

Mechanism confidence follows a spatial pattern:

**Paradox:** The MOST clinically relevant mechanisms (L5-L6: sustained systemic and behavioral change) have the LOWEST confidence levels. The BEST-understood mechanisms (L1-L2: local tissue and spinal reflexes) are often the least clinically relevant (transient, not sustained). This explains the persistent gap between mechanism science quality and clinical trial quality noted in B03 §9.5. 🟡

### §4.4 — The Non-Specific Floor 🟡

Every therapy in Track B — even those with the weakest specific mechanisms — produces SOME measurable physiological effect. B14 craniosacral (CONTRADICTED premises) still reduces HR, BP, anxiety, and cortisol. B06 reflexology (UNSUPPORTED zone theory) still improves anxiety and sleep.

This defines a **Non-Specific Floor**: the baseline effect produced by ANY structured therapeutic encounter involving touch, time, attention, and narrative. This floor includes:
- Relaxation response (A02)
- Cortisol reduction (modest, A06)
- Evaluative Gates activation (Human-Predictive-Drive)
- Touch-as-care (social/emotional, not mechanistic)

**Estimated magnitude:** Based on B06/B14 data vs active controls, the non-specific floor ≈ SMD 0.2-0.4 for subjective outcomes (anxiety, sleep, well-being) and ≈ 0.0-0.2 for objective outcomes (function, range of motion, biomarkers). Therapy-SPECIFIC effects must rise ABOVE this floor to demonstrate mechanism-dependent action. 🟡

## §5 — PREDICTIONS & TESTABLE HYPOTHESES

Each prediction is 🔴 Hypothesis unless noted.

### P1 — Non-Specific Floor Is Measurable and Consistent

**Prediction:** The non-specific floor (§4.4) produces SMD 0.25 ± 0.10 for anxiety outcomes across any structured touch-based therapeutic encounter of ≥30 minutes, regardless of technique or theory used. This should be consistent across B06, B13, B14, and a novel "structured random touching" control.

**Confirm:** Structured random touching (same duration, same setting, same practitioner warmth, NO technique) shows equivalent outcomes to B06/B14 for anxiety/sleep.
**Refute:** B06/B14 significantly outperform structured random touching, suggesting even their weak specific mechanisms contribute measurably.

### P2 — Scaffolding Dismantling Reduces Effectiveness

**Prediction:** Removing the theoretical scaffolding from B06 reflexology (telling patients "this is foot massage, zone maps have no anatomical basis") will REDUCE clinical outcomes by 0.2-0.4 SMD compared to standard B06 with full zone-map narrative, because dismantling removes Evaluative Gates enhancement.

**Confirm:** Honest-label B06 shows significantly lower patient-reported outcomes than traditional-label B06 with identical physical technique.
**Refute:** Honest-label B06 shows equivalent outcomes, suggesting narrative/scaffolding contribution is smaller than predicted (or foot massage alone is sufficient regardless of framing).

### P3 — Mechanism-Specific Effects Map to Mechanism-Specific Conditions

**Prediction:** Therapies with unique mechanisms should show DIFFERENTIAL efficacy for conditions matching those mechanisms:
- B10 (proprioceptive reset) > others for balance/proprioception disorders
- B07/B09 (HO-1) > others for inflammatory conditions responsive to CO/biliverdin
- B08 (HSP) > others for conditions responsive to cytoprotection
- B01-EA (PROKR2) > others for conditions requiring specific autonomic modulation

**Confirm:** Head-to-head trials show >0.3 SMD advantage for the mechanism-matched therapy in the predicted condition, but NOT for conditions outside the mechanism match.
**Refute:** No differential efficacy by mechanism match — suggesting shared mechanisms dominate clinical outcomes for all conditions.

### P4 — The Shiatsu Natural Experiment

**Prediction:** Namikoshi Shiatsu (Western anatomy, no meridians) and Masunaga Zen Shiatsu (extended meridians, Kyo/Jitsu diagnosis) should show equivalent clinical outcomes for the SAME conditions, because both use the SAME physical stimulus (perpendicular pressure at similar points). If equivalent → meridian theory = scaffolding. If Masunaga is superior → either (a) extended meridian point selection identifies genuinely different anatomical targets, or (b) more elaborate theory = stronger scaffolding = stronger Evaluative Gates.

**Confirm:** Head-to-head RCT shows no significant difference for chronic pain or well-being outcomes.
**Refute:** Masunaga significantly outperforms Namikoshi, requiring investigation of whether the difference is anatomical or psychological.

### P5 — Cognitive Component Isolation in EFT

**Prediction:** EFT's cognitive protocol alone (verbal exposure + acceptance phrase + SUDS monitoring, NO tapping) should produce ≥80% of full EFT's effect for anxiety and PTSD, because C03 analysis shows somatic contribution is near-zero (C01: light percussion = 0/9 Track A systems above MINIMAL).

**Confirm:** Cognitive-only EFT shows effect within 0.2 SMD of full EFT for anxiety and PTSD.
**Refute:** Full EFT shows >0.4 SMD advantage over cognitive-only, requiring reassessment of somatic contribution (trigeminal-vagal from facial tapping? attentional competition?).

### P6 — Depth-Mechanism Cascade Is Predictable

**Prediction:** Applying the SAME therapy (B03 massage) at four standardized pressure levels (10N, 30N, 50N, 80N) should produce a predictable cascade of biomarker changes matching the Depth-Mechanism Cascade (§3.6 Pattern 2):
- 10N: oxytocin↑, no endocannabinoid system change, minimal opioid
- 30N: oxytocin↑, gate control (EEG), vagal shift (HRV), no endocannabinoid system change
- 50N: AEA↑ (endocannabinoid system threshold crossed), gate control + descending modulation
- 80N: AEA↑↑, β-endorphin↑, cortisol biphasic, maximum mechanotransduction

**Confirm:** Biomarker cascade follows predicted pattern with ≤1 level deviation.
**Refute:** Biomarkers do not follow predicted pattern — individual variation, body region, or chronicity dominates over pressure magnitude.

### P7 — GABA Replication Would Reorder the Spectrum

**Prediction:** If Streeter's GABA hypothesis (B16 §5.2 L4) is replicated in a large independent sample (n≥60, active control matched for METs), B16 Yoga would move from rank 11 to rank 4-5 on the specificity spectrum (§3.5), because a confirmed yoga-specific neurochemical mechanism at 🟢 confidence would be among the strongest specific mechanisms in Track B.

**Confirm:** Independent replication shows yoga > metabolically-matched walking for thalamic GABA (MRS) with p<0.01 and ≥medium effect size.
**Refute:** Replication shows no difference or difference attributable to breathing component (shared with B15).

## §6 — LIMITATIONS & GAPS

### §6.1 — Methodological Limitations

1. **Frequency counting is coarse.** Scoring mechanisms as present/absent across therapies obscures magnitude differences. Gate control in B01 (Aδ depth) is mechanistically different from gate control in B06 (Aβ through thick foot sole). Both count as "present" but their clinical relevance differs enormously.

2. **Therapy ≠ mechanism.** B03 "massage" spans ultra-light MLD to deep trigger point work — treating it as one therapy obscures that different B03 techniques engage different mechanism profiles. The Pressure-Dependent Pathway Model (B03 §5.3) partially addresses this within B03, but the problem applies across therapies.

3. **Inferred mechanisms.** B13 has ZERO dedicated mechanism studies — all mechanisms are inferred from B02/B03. B14's alternative mechanisms are inferred from general physiology. These inferred profiles are less reliable than directly studied profiles.

4. **Confidence level heterogeneity.** "🟢 Research" for B01 PROKR2 (Liu 2021 Nature, animal model with human anatomical confirmation) is a DIFFERENT quality of evidence than "🟢 Research" for B03 gate control (theoretical framework from 1965 applied to clinical observation). The 3-tier system collapses important distinctions.

5. **Level unification is interpretive.** Mapping B07's "L1 Vascular" onto the unified "L1 Local Tissue" involves judgment calls. Different mapping choices would change the level comparison table. The mapping preserves the SPATIAL location of each mechanism but may obscure the SYSTEM-specific logic of the original models.

### §6.2 — Missing Data

1. **Cognitive/contextual fraction unknown** for most therapies. The most important gap: what percentage of clinical effects comes from specific vs. non-specific mechanisms? Only B16 has quantified one component (self-efficacy = 36% of disability effect).

2. **No head-to-head mechanism comparison** exists for any natural experiment pair (§4.5). B01 vs B11, B13 Namikoshi vs Masunaga, B07 vs B09 — all predicted to be informative, none tested.

3. **Endocannabinoid system threshold not measured.** The ~40-65N estimate (C01 §4.6) is derived from comparing light vs deep massage studies, not from a systematic force-AEA dose-response study.

4. **GABA hypothesis unreplicated.** Streeter's yoga-GABA finding is from one research group with small samples. Independent replication would significantly change B16's position on the specificity spectrum.

5. **Scaffolding contribution never isolated.** No study has compared a therapy WITH its scaffolding narrative vs the SAME therapy without it (honest-label design).

6. **B13/B14 mechanism research absent.** Two therapies with a combined ~3,000 lines of mechanism analysis rely entirely on inference from other therapies.

## §7 — OPEN QUESTIONS

Questions RAISED BY the cross-reference analysis that only become visible when comparing across therapies.

**Q1 (CRITICAL). What fraction of each therapy's clinical effect is mechanism-specific?** The non-specific floor (§4.4) produces measurable effects. The shared mechanism base (§3.2 Tier 1) is engaged by nearly all therapies. How much ADDITIONAL effect comes from therapy-specific mechanisms? This is the central question of body-based therapy research, and no therapy has a definitive answer. B12 comes closest (sham tapping ≈ real tapping → cognitive component dominant), but even this is contested.

**Q2. Is there a universal mechanism hierarchy?** The Mechanism Inequality Distribution (§4.1) suggests a power law. Is this an artifact of incomplete research (unique mechanisms are harder to discover) or a genuine structural feature of how the body responds to external stimulation?

**Q3. Can mechanism profiles predict clinical equivalence?** If two therapies share >80% of their mechanism profile (e.g., B02 and B13 — same stimulus, different deployment), should we predict clinical equivalence? The Pressure-Point Divergence Model (B02) and Whole-Body Deployment Model (B13) suggest deployment DOES matter — but no head-to-head data exists.

**Q4. Does scaffolding dismantling help or harm?** The Scaffolding Paradox (§3.4.4): removing false theories might harm patients (by reducing Evaluative Gates) but would improve scientific integrity. Is there an optimal balance? Open-label placebo research (Kaptchuk) suggests some scaffolding can be transparent and still effective.

**Q5. Why are unique mechanisms concentrated in so few therapies?** 8 therapies account for ALL 15 unique mechanisms; 8 therapies have NONE. Is this because some therapies genuinely access unique pathways, or because some therapies are simply better-studied? B13 might have unique mechanisms that no one has looked for.

**Q6. Do composite stimulus interactions produce emergence?** B15/B16 combine movement + breathing + attention. C01 Q4 asked whether these composites produce EMERGENT effects beyond component sum. The fall prevention superiority of B15 over matched exercise suggests possible emergence, but component decomposition studies are needed.

**Q7. What determines the scaffolding-to-mechanism ratio?** Therapies span from B14 (nearly 100% scaffolding) to B01-EA (mostly mechanism). What determines where a therapy falls? Historical accident (TCM had millennia to refine acupuncture)? Intrinsic physics (needle reaches deeper tissue = more mechanisms)? Research investment (more studies = more discovered mechanisms)?

**Q8. Can mechanism-based therapy design outperform traditional protocols?** If we KNOW the mechanism profile for each stimulus type (C01) and each therapy (C03), can we design NOVEL therapy protocols optimized for specific conditions — e.g., combining B08 thermal (TRPV1/HSP) + B01 needle (PROKR2/adenosine) + B16 pranayama (voluntary ANS) for a condition requiring anti-inflammatory + analgesic + autonomic retuning?
