### §1.2 — Historical Timeline

```
🟡🔴 TIMELINE — FROM VIETNAMESE TRADITION TO GLOBAL PRACTICE:

  PRE-HISTORY:
    → Vietnamese traditional medicine includes facial massage
      as part of holistic health practice (centuries-old)
    → TCM facial acupuncture points (ST2, GB14, Yintang, etc.)
      existed independently of Dien Chan
    → Physiognomy (reading health from facial features) =
      ancient tradition in multiple Asian cultures

  1980 — THE FOUNDING DISCOVERY:
    → Professor Bùi Quốc Châu (GS.TSKH Bùi Quốc Châu)
    → Vietnam, Ho Chi Minh City
    → Background: studied acupuncture but was NOT a traditional
      acupuncturist — approached from outside the establishment
    → KEY DISCOVERY: stimulating a point on the nose resolved
      a patient's back pain → noticed face-body correspondence
    → Began systematic mapping of facial points to body regions
    → Tested on thousands of patients in Vietnamese clinical
      settings (not published in peer-reviewed journals)
    🔴 Historical details from practitioner sources, not verified

  1984 — CODIFICATION:
    → 257 fixed points codified on the face (BQC point system)
    → Own numbering: BQC 0 through BQC 256
    → NOT identical to TCM facial acupoints
    → Points determined through clinical observation, not
      neuroanatomical mapping
    🔴 Point validation methodology not published

  1980s-1990s — DEVELOPMENT IN VIETNAM:
    → Widely practiced in Vietnam as complementary therapy
    → Taught at some Vietnamese traditional medicine institutions
    → Developed multireflex tool system (non-penetrating)
    → Name established: "Diện Chẩn – Điều Khiển Liệu Pháp"
      (Facial Diagnosis and Cybernetic Therapy)
    → "Cybernetic" refers to feedback-loop concept:
      stimulate face → body responds → adjust stimulation

  1990s-2000s — EUROPEAN SPREAD:
    → Patryck Aguilar Cassarà (student of Bùi Quốc Châu)
    → Brought Dien Chan to Europe, primarily Spain and France
    → Founded International School of Multireflexology Dien Chan
    → Published training materials (non-peer-reviewed)
    → Renamed method "Multireflexology – Dien Chan" for
      international market

  2000s — PARALLEL DEVELOPMENT (Sorensensistem):
    → Lone Sorensen (Denmark/Argentina)
    → Developed "Facial Reflex Therapy — Sorensensistem"
    → Inspired by Dien Chan BUT different system:
      → Incorporates South American tribal body maps
      → Emphasizes neuroanatomy and CNS connections
      → Different point system from BQC
    → Two main streams now coexist globally

  2010s-2020s — CURRENT STATUS:
    → Training schools in 20+ countries
    → Mobile apps developed (FACEASiT) for point location
    → International Academy of Dien Chan established
    → STILL no published RCTs or systematic reviews
    → Vietnamese academic publications remain inaccessible
      to Western databases
    🔴 Practitioner claims of growing adoption

  CONTRAST WITH ACUPUNCTURE RESEARCH TRAJECTORY:
    → Acupuncture: 1970s Nixon visit → NIH → massive research
    → Dien Chan: 1980s Vietnam → practitioner network → NO
      equivalent research catalyst
    → The research gap is partly GEOPOLITICAL:
      Vietnam War aftermath → limited international scientific
      exchange in 1980s-1990s → missed the "alternative medicine
      research wave" that benefited acupuncture and yoga
    🟡 Geopolitical context is documented history
```

```

    → Thinnest skin on body (especially periorbital: ~0.5mm)

    → Entire surface innervated by CN V (trigeminal) — the

    🟢 Facial skin anatomy well-established (dermatology)

### §2.2 — Technique Variants

```
🔴 FOUR MAIN STIMULATION TECHNIQUES:

  ① POINT STIMULATION (détection/stimulation):
    → Small probe (detector) pressed on specific BQC point
    → Sustained pressure: 3-10 seconds per point
    → Used for: diagnosis (finding sensitive points) + treatment
    → Similar to acupressure but with tool, not finger
    → Probe diameter: ~2-3mm tip

  ② ZONE ROLLING (roulage):
    → Cylindrical roller moved across facial reflex zones
    → Covers larger area than point stimulation
    → Used for: general zone activation, relaxation
    → Multiple roller sizes for different facial areas
    → Rolling speed affects yin/yang quality:
      → Slow rolling = yin (dispersing)
      → Fast rolling = yang (tonifying)
    🔴 Speed-effect distinction from practitioner teaching

  ③ RAKING (ratissage):
    → Multi-tipped instrument dragged across facial surface
    → Creates parallel lines of gentle stimulation
    → Used for: broad activation, lymphatic drainage claims
    → Covers widest area of any Dien Chan technique

  ④ TAPPING (tapotement):
    → Gentle percussion on facial zones using tool or fingertips
    → Rhythmic stimulation
    → Used for: energizing (yang effect), waking up reflexes
    → Lightest touch of all techniques
```

### §9.2 — Why the Gap Exists

  not necessarily lack of efficacy:

  ① GEOPOLITICAL ISOLATION:
    → Developed in Vietnam (1980) during post-war period
    → Limited international scientific exchange in 1980s-90s
    → Vietnamese research institutions had minimal Western
      collaboration during this period
    → Contrast: acupuncture benefited from Nixon's 1972
      China visit → NIH Consensus Conference (1997) →
      massive research funding pipeline
    → Dien Chan had NO equivalent catalyst event

  ② LINGUISTIC BARRIER:
    → Founder published primarily in Vietnamese
    → Vietnamese medical databases not indexed in PubMed
    → European adopters focused on training, not research
    → No English-language research tradition established

  ③ INSTITUTIONAL GAP:
    → No university adopted Dien Chan as research subject
    → No medical school teaches it
    → No research grants specifically for Dien Chan
    → Practitioner network without academic infrastructure

  ④ METHODOLOGICAL CHALLENGES:
    → No standardized treatment protocol to test in RCT
    → 257 points + 30 maps = no clear protocol for study
    → Sham/blinding: patient feels tool on face →
      difficult to create convincing sham
    → What to compare: Dien Chan vs facial massage?
      vs specific point vs non-specific stimulation?

  ⑤ ECONOMIC FACTORS:
    → No device company to fund research (cf. Cefaly for eTNS)
    → Low-cost tools → no profit-driven research incentive
    → Contrast: eTNS (Cefaly) invested in clinical trials
      to achieve FDA clearance → research exists BECAUSE
      a company needed regulatory approval

  🟡 Structural analysis is well-supported by comparison
     with other therapies' research trajectories

### §9.3 — eTNS as Indirect Mechanistic Validation

```
🟢🟡 THE eTNS PARALLEL — WHAT IT PROVES AND WHAT IT DOESN'T:

  eTNS (external trigeminal nerve stimulation) provides the

    ✓ Stimulation of V1 (forehead) → therapeutic effects

    ✗ That 257 specific points matter (vs any facial stim)
    ✗ That projection maps have anatomical reality
    ✗ That 5-15 min of manual pressure = 20-60 min of eTNS
    ✗ That Dien Chan's specific protocols are optimal

    eTNS validates the NERVE TERRITORY.

  🟡 Extrapolation from electrical to mechanical is reasonable

### §9.4 — What a Proper Dien Chan RCT Would Need

```
🟡 RESEARCH DESIGN RECOMMENDATION:

  For Dien Chan to be properly evaluated, a study would need:

  ① STANDARDIZED PROTOCOL:
    → Select 1 condition (suggestion: migraine or anxiety)
    → Select specific BQC points (5-10 per session)
    → Standardize tool, pressure, duration
    → Specify number of sessions and frequency

  ② APPROPRIATE COMPARATORS:
    → Active control: non-specific facial massage (same duration,
      same pressure, random facial points)
    → Sham control: light touch with tool on non-BQC points
    → Positive control: Yintang acupressure (existing evidence)
    → = Tests point specificity AND general facial stimulation

  ③ OUTCOME MEASURES:
    → Primary: validated symptom scale (VAS for pain, STAI for
      anxiety, HIT-6 for migraine)
    → Secondary: physiological (HRV, HR, BP — parasympathetic)
    → Tertiary: neuroimaging (fMRI if feasible — brainstem
      activation patterns)

  ④ ADEQUATE POWER:
    → N ≥ 30 per group for pilot study
    → N ≥ 100 per group for definitive RCT
    → With 3 arms → 90-300+ participants

  ⑤ KEY QUESTION TO ANSWER:
    Is Dien Chan at BQC points superior to non-specific
    facial stimulation at random points?
    If YES → point specificity validated
    If NO but both > control → trigeminal gateway mechanism
      confirmed but point system NOT validated
    If both = control → facial manual stimulation insufficient
      for therapeutic effect (contrast with eTNS electrical)

  🟡 Research design is standard methodology

## §10 — OPEN QUESTIONS

```
🟡🔴 EIGHT OPEN QUESTIONS — RANKED BY IMPORTANCE:

  Q1 — ORGAN SPECIFICITY (CRITICAL):
    Can different facial zones (V1/V2/V3) produce DIFFERENT
    organ-specific effects? Or does ALL facial stimulation →
    same general parasympathetic activation regardless of zone?
    → This determines whether Dien Chan's maps have any basis
    → eTNS only studies V1 → V2/V3 effects unknown
    → Would require comparison of V1-only vs V2-only vs V3-only
      facial stimulation with organ-specific outcome measures
    → 🔴 No data exists

  Q2 — POINT SPECIFICITY:
    Do the 257 BQC points correspond to any measurable tissue
    or neural difference? Or are they arbitrary locations?
    → Compare: acupoints have documented electrical impedance
      differences, neurovascular bundle concentration (A09)
    → BQC points have NEVER been tested for any of these
    → 🔴 No data exists

  Q3 — MECHANICAL vs ELECTRICAL EQUIVALENCE:
    Does manual pressure on V1 activate the SAME brainstem
    pathways as electrical V1 stimulation (eTNS)?
    → If yes → Dien Chan has eTNS-equivalent mechanism
    → If no → Dien Chan mechanism is fundamentally weaker
    → Could be tested with fMRI: compare Dien Chan tool
      pressure vs Cefaly eTNS at same forehead location
    → 🔴 No comparative study exists

  Q4 — DOSE-RESPONSE:
    What pressure, duration, and frequency of facial
    stimulation produces measurable parasympathetic response?
    → eTNS: calibrated (up to 16mA over 14 min)
    → Dien Chan: unstandardized manual pressure
    → Threshold for meaningful vagal activation via manual
      facial pressure is completely unknown
    → 🔴 No dose-response data for manual facial pressure

  Q5 — PROJECTION MAP VALIDATION:
    How many of the 30+ projection diagrams have ANY
    correspondence to known neuroanatomical pathways?
    → The "spine on nose" map (founding observation) is
      potentially testable: stimulate nose points →
      measure segmental spinal effects
    → Other maps (organs on cheeks, etc.) seem less plausible
    → 🔴 No systematic validation attempted

  Q6 — SELF-TREATMENT EFFECTIVENESS:
    Dien Chan emphasizes self-treatment. But does self-
    application produce the same effect as practitioner
    application? Self-treatment has:
    → Different pressure control
    → Different emotional context (no therapeutic relationship)
    → Possible proprioceptive interference (own hand on face)
    → 🔴 No data

  Q7 — COMPARISON WITH ESTABLISHED FACIAL THERAPIES:
    How does Dien Chan compare with:
    → Facial acupressure (Yintang point — 5 RCTs)
    → Cold Face Test (validated vagal activation)
    → Facial massage (beauty industry standard)
    → A direct comparison would clarify the value-add
      of Dien Chan's specific point system
    → 🔴 No comparative studies

  Q8 — VIETNAMESE RESEARCH ACCESS:
    Does published Dien Chan research exist in Vietnamese-
    language journals that are not indexed in Western databases?
    → Vietnamese traditional medicine journals may contain
      clinical observations or studies
    → A systematic search of Vietnamese databases could
