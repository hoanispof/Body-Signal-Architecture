## SCOPE ASSESSMENT RESULTS

### Evidence Landscape
```
REFLEXOLOGY-SPECIFIC EVIDENCE:
  Systematic reviews:            Ernst 2009 (18 RCTs, 949 pts — "not convincingly
                                   effective for any condition")
                                 Ernst 2011 update (added more trials — conclusion
                                   unchanged)
                                 Australian Gov 2024 review (Cochrane methods,
                                   endorsed Nov 2024)
  Meta-analyses (condition-specific):
    Depression/Anxiety/Sleep:    Wang 2020 (26 RCTs, 2,366 pts — positive vs
                                   non-active controls, meta-regression shows
                                   dose-response)
    Sleep disturbances:          Huang 2021 (Hedges' g = -1.37 — large effect
                                   vs controls)
    Vital signs (BP/HR/RR):     Jing 2022 (13 RCTs, 819 pts — SBP -4.62mmHg,
                                   HR -4.76 bpm, short-term only)
    Cancer symptom management:  Klaus 2024 (26 studies, 2,465 pts — superior to
                                   passive controls for pain/fatigue/QoL,
                                   moderate-to-low quality)
    Constipation:               2020 SR (positive but limited)
    Nausea/vomiting:            2025 SR (mixed)

  TOTAL RCTs in literature:     ~50-80+ (estimated from multiple SRs)
  = SUBSTANTIALLY more evidence than B05 Dien Chan (~0 RCTs)
  = LESS evidence than B01 Acupuncture (thousands of RCTs)

KEY EVIDENCE PROBLEM:
  Almost ALL positive results are vs non-active / passive controls.
  When compared to sham foot massage:
    → Descamps 2023 fMRI RCT (n=30): NO difference between
      reflexology and sham massage on brain connectivity
    → Ernst 2009: 6/9 placebo-controlled trials = negative
    → Hughes 2009 MS trial: both reflexology and sham improved
      symptoms equally
  = SPECIFICITY PROBLEM: reflexology MAY work, but NOT because
    of specific reflex zones — possibly because ANY systematic
    foot massage activates the same mechanisms.

MECHANISM RESEARCH:
  Descamps 2023 fMRI:           Brain connectivity changes in DMN,
                                sensorimotor network, pain network
                                — BUT same for reflexology AND sham
  Kennedy & Inglis 2002:        Foot sole mechanoreceptor mapping
                                (4 types, glabrous skin, balance)
  Manzanares:                   Proposed neurophysiological basis
                                (non-peer-reviewed, practitioner)
  NO Cochrane review on reflexology specifically
  Australian Gov 2024 = most authoritative recent assessment
```

### Central Question
```
THE FOOT AS BODY MAP — IS THERE A NEUROANATOMICAL BASIS?

  Reflexology's CORE CLAIM:
    → The foot sole contains a "map" of the entire body
    → Each zone/area on the foot "corresponds to" a specific organ
    → Stimulating a foot zone → therapeutic effect on that organ
    → Eunice Ingham (1938): "the reflexes on the feet are a mirror
      image of all the organs, glands and parts of the body"

  THE NEUROANATOMICAL CHALLENGE:
    → Foot sole is innervated by tibial nerve → L4-S3 spinal segments
    → L4-S3 viscerotomes = bladder (S2-S4), rectum (S2-S4),
      prostate/uterus (S2-S4), large intestine (partial, T10-L1)
    → MOST organs (heart T1-T5, lungs T3-T5, stomach T6-T9,
      liver T7-T9, kidney T10-L1) have NO segmental connection
      to L4-S3
    → = NO somatovisceral reflex pathway from foot to heart/lungs/
      stomach/liver via segmental convergence
    → = The "body on foot" map has NO neuroanatomical basis via
      the mechanism that DOES explain body acupoints (A07 §4.4)

  CONTRAST WITH DIEN CHAN (B05):
    → Face (CN V) → brainstem → vagal nucleus = PROVEN pathway
    → Trigeminal has DIRECT brainstem connections to autonomic centers
    → Foot spinal nerves (L4-S3) have NO such direct cranial pathway
    → B05 already noted: "Dien Chan has a STRONGER neuroanatomical
      case than foot reflexology"

  BUT: Foot stimulation CAN produce systemic effects via:
    ① Gate control (Aβ mechanoreceptors — well-established)
    ② General relaxation response (parasympathetic activation via
       pleasant tactile stimulation + therapeutic context)
    ③ Descending modulation (if pressure sufficient for Aδ/C fiber
       activation → PAG → endorphin)
    ④ Cortical body matrix effects (sensory input to foot region
       of S1 → integration with broader body representation)
    ⑤ Psychological/contextual (therapeutic ritual, practitioner
       attention, expectation)
    → ALL of these are NON-SPECIFIC mechanisms
    → = They explain why foot massage works
    → = They do NOT explain why SPECIFIC zones should matter
```

### Boundary Decisions
```
B06 vs B05 (Dien Chan):
  BOTH claim "body map" projected on one body surface
  B05 = face (trigeminal, brainstem connections)
  B06 = foot (spinal L4-S3, NO brainstem connections)
  B05 already compared in §7.2. B06 extends with FOOT perspective.
  KEY DIFFERENCE: B05 has a neuroanatomical case (trigeminal-vagal).
  B06 does NOT have an equivalent pathway.

B06 vs B02 (Acupressure):
  B02 includes some foot acupressure points (KI1 Yongquan, KI3, etc.)
  B06 uses a COMPLETELY DIFFERENT point system (reflex zones, not TCM)
  Different theoretical basis (organ projection vs meridian theory)
  B02's foot points ARE segmentally relevant (some match dermatome)
  B06's foot zones are NOT segmentally organized

B06 vs B03 (Massage-General):
  Foot massage = subset of general massage
  B06 claims SPECIFIC zone effects beyond general massage benefits
  B03's pressure-dependent pathway model applies to foot massage
  B06's added claim = zone specificity. Evidence says: probably no.

B06 vs B04 (Deep Tissue — not yet written):
  B04 = deep pressure on body. B06 = moderate pressure on foot.
  Limited overlap. B06 does not typically involve deep tissue work.
```

→ §1 Definition & Origin (~200 lines):

  §1.2: Historical timeline:
        Ancient: Saqqara tomb painting (2330 BCE) — debated
        1917: William Fitzgerald — Zone Therapy (10 zones,
              pressure analgesia, ENT surgeon)
        1919: Joe Shelby Riley — expanded Fitzgerald's work
        1930s-1938: Eunice Ingham — "mother of modern reflexology"
              Mapped reflex points to foot sole specifically
              Published "Stories The Feet Can Tell" (1938)
        1960s: Doreen Bayly brought to UK
        1980s-present: global spread, professional associations
        Parallel development: TCM foot massage (足底按摩),
        Thai foot massage, Ayurvedic pada abhyanga
  §1.3: Traditional explanations:
        Fitzgerald's zone theory (10 longitudinal zones)

        ① Thumb-walking (caterpillar walk — signature technique)

        Duration: 30-60 minutes typical session

        Frequency: weekly typical, varies by practitioner
        Sequence: usually systematic (heel→toe or zone-by-zone)

        Actual foot innervation (tibial → medial/lateral plantar)
