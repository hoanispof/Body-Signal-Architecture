# AP-16 — Auricular Microsystem (Ear) — Nhĩ Châm (耳针)

> **Status:** Phase 1 — Tier 1 entries only. This file contains 3
> highest-evidence auricular points (Ear Shen Men, Sympathetic, Point
> Zero). Remaining auricular points added in Phase 2.
>
> **Microsystem overview:** The auricular microsystem is the most
> developed microsystem in clinical acupuncture. Paul Nogier (Lyon,
> 1956/1957) proposed the "inverted fetus" somatotopic model: lobule =
> head/face, antihelix = spine, concha = internal organs, tragus =
> nose/throat. Chinese auricular acupuncture developed in parallel with
> some location differences.
>
> **What makes the ear unique:** The outer ear is innervated by 4
> converging nerve sources: auriculotemporal nerve (mandibular division
> V3 of trigeminal nerve), great auricular nerve (C2-C3), lesser
> occipital nerve (C2), and Arnold's nerve (auricular branch of the
> vagus nerve, ABVN — cranial nerve X). The ABVN makes the ear concha
> the ONLY place on the body surface where the vagus nerve is directly
> accessible through the skin (Peuker & Filler 2002).
>
> **ABVN innervation distribution** (Peuker & Filler 2002, 7 cadavers,
> 14 ears): cymba conchae 100%, antihelix 73%, cavum conchae 45%,
> tragus 45%, earlobe 0%. ABVN afferents project to the nucleus tractus
> solitarius (NTS) — the same brainstem nucleus receiving cardiac,
> pulmonary, and gastrointestinal vagal afferents. This provides the
> neuroanatomical basis for transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve
> stimulation (taVNS).
>
> **Critical distinction:** Cymba conchae (100% ABVN) is the target
> for taVNS devices. Most clinical auricular acupuncture points —
> including Shen Men, Sympathetic, and Point Zero — are located in
> MIXED innervation zones, not purely ABVN territory. Their effects
> involve brainstem convergence from multiple nerve inputs, not
> single-pathway vagal activation alone.
>
> **Clinical systems:** Chinese auricular (standardized by World
> Health Organization, WHO), European/Nogier auriculotherapy, National
> Acupuncture Detoxification Association (NADA) 5-point protocol
> (addiction, post-traumatic stress disorder, anxiety — >1,000 clinics
> worldwide), Battlefield Acupuncture (BFA — US Veterans Affairs /
> Department of Defense, 2,800+ providers).
> → Cross-ref: B01 §3.3 (auricular acupuncture), B02 §3.3 (ABVN
> anatomy), A02 §4.4 (vagal gateway), A07 §1.8 (ear somatotopy),
> C04 §3.5.2 (ear ABVN access)
>
> **Total points:** 200+ (Nogier/Chinese combined) | Phase 1 entries:
> Ear Shen Men, Ear Sympathetic, Point Zero

## Ear Shen Men (TF4 / MA-TF1) — Nhĩ Thần Môn (耳神门)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**

"Spirit Gate" of the ear — most studied auricular acupuncture point.
Included in both the NADA 5-point protocol (point #1) and Battlefield
Acupuncture (BFA, point #4). Most frequently included auricular point
in insomnia studies (17.4% of all auricular points in network
meta-analysis). Shares the Chinese name 神门 ("Spirit Gate") with body
acupoint HT7 (Shénmén), reflecting parallel calming/sedative functions
across body and microsystem traditions.

### Vị trí trên microsystem map

Chinese standard location (TF4): lower one-third (lateral third) of
the triangular fossa, at the bifurcation point where the superior crus
and the inferior crus of the antihelix diverge. The triangular fossa is
the fourth zone (hence TF4) of this anatomical region.

WHO nomenclature: MA-TF1 (Microsystem Auricular — Triangular Fossa 1).

Nogier/European system: the European "Shen Men" location corresponds
closely but not identically to the Chinese TF4 — slight variation
exists between systems. Differences of opinion persist regarding the
exact position within the triangular fossa.

Somatotopic correspondence: in Nogier's inverted fetus model, the
triangular fossa maps to the pelvic/lower abdominal region. However,
Shen Men's primary clinical use is calming/analgesic — not
somatotopically determined. This is one of several auricular points
whose effects transcend the somatotopic model.

### Giải phẫu tại điểm

**Nerve supply — MIXED INNERVATION:**

The triangular fossa receives innervation from multiple sources:

- Auriculotemporal nerve (mandibular V3 branch of trigeminal nerve) —
  PRIMARY innervation of the anterior triangular fossa
- Auricular branch of the vagus nerve (ABVN, Arnold's nerve) — extends
  into the triangular fossa in some individuals (variable distribution)
- Great auricular nerve (C2-C3) — contributes to posterior and lateral
  ear regions

Peuker & Filler 2002 documented ABVN distribution across auricular
zones but did not specifically quantify the triangular fossa percentage.
The triangular fossa is ADJACENT to the antihelix (73% ABVN) and near
the concha (cymba conchae 100% ABVN), but is NOT itself pure ABVN
territory. The degree of ABVN contribution at Shen Men is INDIVIDUALLY
VARIABLE.
→ Cross-ref: C05 §3.4.3 (ABVN distribution variation)

**Pathway to central nervous system — MULTI-ROUTE:**

1. V3 (auriculotemporal) → trigeminal ganglion → trigeminal sensory
   nuclear complex (principal sensory nucleus + spinal trigeminal
   nucleus) → brainstem reticular formation, NTS, locus coeruleus
2. ABVN (if present at this location) → NTS → vagal circuit (cardiac,
   respiratory, gastrointestinal modulation)
3. C2-C3 (great auricular) → cervical spinal cord dorsal horn →
   brainstem via spinobulbar pathways

Net result: stimulation at Shen Men activates brainstem circuits through
CONVERGENT multi-nerve input, not a single dedicated pathway.

→ Cross-ref: A07 §1.8 (auricle innervation — 4 nerve sources),
A02 §4.4 (ABVN vagal gateway), B02 §3.3 (ABVN anatomy),
C04 §3.5.2 (ear ABVN access)

**Body acupoint parallel:** HT7 Shénmén (Heart meridian, "Spirit Gate")
shares the name 神门 and overlapping indications (calming, insomnia,
anxiety). HT7 works via ulnar nerve → spinal C8-T1 → brainstem. Ear
Shen Men works via multi-nerve brainstem convergence. Different
pathways, convergent clinical application.
→ Cross-ref: AP-05 HT7 entry (body Shenmen)

### Chỉ định truyền thống

**Chinese auricular tradition:**
Calming (安神 ān shén), pain relief (止痛 zhǐ tòng), sedation, heat
clearing, anti-inflammatory. Used as the primary calming point in
virtually all auricular acupuncture protocols.

**NADA protocol** (National Acupuncture Detoxification Association):
Point #1 in the 5-point sequence. Function: calming and analgesic.
Developed by Michael Smith (Lincoln Hospital, New York City, 1970s) for
addiction treatment. Used for substance withdrawal, post-traumatic
stress disorder, anxiety, disaster relief. Adopted in >1,000 clinics
worldwide.
→ Cross-ref: B01 §3.3 (NADA protocol)

**Battlefield Acupuncture** (BFA, Niemtzow 2002):
Point #4 in the 5-needle sequence using Aiguille Semi-Permanente (ASP)
gold needles (Cingulate Gyrus → Thalamus → Omega 2 → **Shen Men** →
Point Zero). ASP needles retained 3-4 days until natural expulsion.
US Veterans Affairs / Department of Defense adopted — 2,800+ providers
trained, $4.05/session vs $115-294/week
for opioids. Reported 75-82% immediate pain reduction.
→ Cross-ref: B01 §3.3 (BFA protocol)

**Standard auricular protocol for insomnia/anxiety:**
Shen Men + Subcortex + Heart/Kidney/Liver (varies by pattern) — most
common clinical combination. Heart, Shen Men, and Subcortex = top 3
auricular points in insomnia studies (71.70% combined share).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**Preoperative/procedural anxiety 🟡 Plausible:**
Multiple randomized controlled trials using bilateral Shen Men
acupressure (ear seeds/vaccaria seeds) for preoperative anxiety. Shen
Men is the most frequently included auricular point in perioperative
anxiety protocols. Often combined with additional auricular points
(Sympathetic, Heart, Subcortex).
Individual evidence: auricular acupressure test anxiety randomized
controlled trial (2023) — State-Trait Anxiety Inventory scores reduced
from 18.4 ± 5.3 to 13.3 ± 4.8 (statistically significant reduction).

**Insomnia 🟡 Plausible:**
Network meta-analysis of 95 randomized controlled trials (7,628
patients, Frontiers in Psychiatry 2026): Shen Men (TF4) was the most
frequently used auricular point for insomnia (17.4% of all points
selected). Heart, Shen Men, and Subcortex = the 3 most common
auricular points for sleep studies (71.70% combined share in breast
cancer insomnia systematic review).
→ Cross-ref: AP-05 HT7 entry (body Shenmen for insomnia — often used
in combination with auricular Shen Men)

**Pain (as part of BFA/NADA protocols) 🟡 Plausible:**
Yang et al. 2021 (American Journal of Chinese Medicine, systematic
review and meta-analysis): current clinical evidence partially
verifies BFA effectiveness for immediate pain relief. High-quality
randomized controlled trials still needed to clarify analgesic
mechanism and optimal population. US Veterans Affairs implementation
data demonstrates cost-effectiveness.
Chronic musculoskeletal pain (auricular acupressure): decreased pain
and disability for up to 12 weeks, large significant effect (Lee 2025,
systematic review).
→ Cross-ref: B02 §4.3

**Depression (auricular acupressure) 🟡 Plausible:**
13 randomized controlled trials, 504 participants: decreased Hamilton
Depression Rating Scale (standardized mean difference −1.50), Self-
rating Depression Scale (−0.91), Patient Health Questionnaire-9
(−0.94). Small samples, high bias risk.
→ Cross-ref: B02 §4.4

**Substance use/withdrawal (as part of NADA protocol) 🔴 Unvalidated
individually:**
36 studies on auriculotherapy for addiction — 64% positive. However,
sham-controlled studies less positive than uncontrolled studies →
placebo contribution likely. Only 11% of studies had low risk of bias.
NADA protocol is multi-point — Shen Men's individual contribution
cannot be isolated from protocol effects. Craving/withdrawal outcomes
stronger than abstinence outcomes.
→ Cross-ref: B02 §4.4

**Autonomic/parasympathetic activation 🟡 Plausible:**
Arai et al. 2013 (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative
Medicine, PMC3687596): randomized controlled trial with 26 post-
hemicolectomy patients. Auricular acupuncture at Shen Men + Point
Zero → significantly higher high-frequency heart rate variability
(parasympathetic marker) at 3, 4, and 5 hours post-stimulation.
Low-frequency/high-frequency ratio did not increase (control group's
ratio did increase). Combined Shen Men + Point Zero effect —
individual contributions not isolated.

### Dynamic acupoint

No specific data on dynamic changes at auricular Shen Men. Auricular
points may exhibit electrical resistance changes (Nogier's Vascular
Autonomic Signal / Réflexe Auriculo-Cardiaque concept — pulse-based
detection of "active" ear points), but this remains poorly validated
(🔴 limited independent validation).
→ Cross-ref: B01 §3.3 (Nogier advanced concepts — phases, Vascular
Autonomic Signal)

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Multi-nerve brainstem convergence model:**

Unlike the simplified "ABVN → NTS → vagal" model applied to all
auricular therapy in the current framework, Shen Men's mechanism is
better explained by CONVERGENT multi-nerve brainstem activation:

| Input nerve | Territory at Shen Men | Brainstem target | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auriculotemporal (V3) | Primary at triangular fossa | Trigeminal sensory nuclear complex → NTS, locus coeruleus, raphe nuclei | 🟢 Established anatomy |
| ABVN (cranial nerve X) | Variable at triangular fossa | NTS → vagal circuit (cardiac, respiratory, gastrointestinal) | 🟡 Present in some individuals |
| Great auricular (C2-C3) | Lateral/posterior contribution | Cervical dorsal horn → brainstem via spinobulbar pathways | 🟢 Established anatomy |

**Result:** Shen Men stimulation activates brainstem circuits through
MULTIPLE concurrent pathways. The calming/analgesic effects observed
across clinical traditions likely arise from this convergent activation,
not from a single dedicated nerve route.

**Comparison with Yintang (EX-HN3, AP-15):**
Both are calming points, both work through cranial nerve → brainstem
pathways, but the mechanism differs:
- Yintang: PURE V1 (supratrochlear nerve) → trigeminal-vagal coupling
- Ear Shen Men: MIXED V3 + ABVN + C2-C3 → multi-nerve convergence
- Both bypass spinal cord → direct brainstem access
- Different cranial nerve combinations, convergent clinical profile

**Comparison with HT7 (body Shenmen, AP-05):**
Both named 神门 ("Spirit Gate"), both for calming/insomnia, but:
- HT7: ulnar nerve → spinal C8-T1 → brainstem (SPINAL pathway)
- Ear Shen Men: multi-nerve → brainstem DIRECT (CRANIAL pathways)
- Segmental gap: HT7 = C8-T1 to brainstem (several segments);
  Ear Shen Men = cranial nerves, no spinal relay

**Why somatotopic mismatch does not negate effectiveness:**
Shen Men is in the triangular fossa (somatotopically = pelvis in
Nogier model), yet its clinical effects are calming/analgesic — not
pelvic. This is consistent with the framework finding that auricular
therapy's most established effects are GENERAL vagal/autonomic
modulation (🟢), not organ-specific targeting (🟡/🔴).
→ Cross-ref: A07 §4.6 (established vagal effects vs less established
organ-specific mapping), C04 §3.5.2 (vagal activation 🟢, organ-
specific mapping 🟡/🔴)

⚡ **Framework note 1 — MIXED INNERVATION PRECISION:** The framework
currently characterizes auricular therapy effects primarily through the
ABVN → NTS → vagal pathway (B02 §3.3, §5.2.1; C04 §3.5.2; A02 §4.4).
Phase 1 auricular data reveals critical nuance: the most clinically
studied auricular acupuncture points (Shen Men TF4, Sympathetic AH6a,
Point Zero) are located in MIXED innervation zones, not purely ABVN
territory. Only cymba conchae (taVNS target) has 100% ABVN innervation
(Peuker & Filler 2002). Clinical effects at non-concha points likely
involve BRAINSTEM CONVERGENCE from multiple nerve inputs
(auriculotemporal V3 + partial ABVN + great auricular C2-C3), not
single-pathway vagal activation alone. This extends B02 §3.3 and
challenges the implication in B02 §5.2.1 that the ABVN pathway
explains ALL auricular acupressure effects.

⚡ **Framework note 2 — taVNS EVIDENCE TRANSFER LIMITATION:** The
framework uses taVNS evidence (Frangos 2015, Farmer 2020) to support
auricular acupuncture mechanism (B02 §3.3, §5.2.1; A02 §4.4).
However, taVNS targets cymba conchae specifically (100% ABVN), while
clinical auricular acupuncture points target triangular fossa (Shen
Men), antihelix crus (Sympathetic), and helix root (Point Zero) —
locations with variable, NOT 100%, ABVN innervation. Evidence from
taVNS at cymba conchae cannot be directly transferred to predict
effects at non-concha auricular points. The open question in B02 §11
(mechanical pressure vs electrical at ABVN) is further complicated by
the fact that most clinical auricular points are not IN the highest-
ABVN zones.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

**Auricular acupuncture (B01):**
Filiform needle (0.22-0.25 mm × 13-25 mm) inserted obliquely through
skin into auricular cartilage, depth 1-3 mm. De Qi at ear described as
local warmth, distension, or tingling — different from body acupoint
De Qi. Needle retained 20-30 minutes (standard session) or up to
several days (ASP semi-permanent needles in BFA protocol).

**Auricular acupressure (B02):**
Vaccaria seeds (王不留行 wáng bù liú xíng) or metal pellets attached
with adhesive tape to Shen Men. Patient self-administers pressure 3-5
times/day, 1-3 minutes per session. Seeds retained 3-7 days per ear
(alternate ears weekly). Most studied acupressure modality for auricular
points.
→ Cross-ref: B02 §3.1 (ear seed technique)

**Electroacupuncture at ear:**
Used in research protocols (taVNS-adjacent), but standard clinical
practice favors needles or seeds at Shen Men rather than electrical
stimulation. Electrical stimulation research at the ear focuses on
cymba conchae (taVNS), not triangular fossa.

**Moxibustion:** Not standard for auricular points.

### An toàn

**Primary risk:** Auricular chondritis/perichondritis — infection of
ear cartilage. Rare but serious when it occurs. Risk increases with
semi-permanent needles (BFA ASP needles) and poor sterilization. Ear
seeds/pellets have lower infection risk than needles.

**Skin irritation:** Adhesive tape reaction with prolonged seed
retention (>7 days). Rotate seed placement side (left/right ear
alternating).

**No pneumothorax risk** — ear anatomy has no deep cavity structures.

**No pregnancy contraindication** — auricular Shen Men is not
pregnancy-contraindicated (unlike body LI4, SP6, BL67).

**Caution:** Pierced ears — avoid placing seeds/needles at or near
piercing sites (scar tissue, altered innervation).

## Ear Sympathetic (AH6a) — Nhĩ Giao Cảm (耳交感)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Sympathetic Nerve" point — NADA protocol point #2. Named for its
proposed effect on the autonomic nervous system, specifically
sympathetic/parasympathetic regulation. Located on the inferior
antihelix crus — a zone with 73% ABVN innervation (Peuker & Filler
2002), giving it MORE vagal access than Shen Men (triangular fossa).
Commonly used for visceral pain, spasms, and autonomic dysregulation.

### Vị trí trên microsystem map

Chinese standard location (AH6a): at the junction (end point) of the
inferior antihelix crus, where it meets the cavity of the helix on the
medial/internal side. Also described as the terminal point of the
inferior crus of the antihelix.

Somatotopic correspondence: in Nogier's inverted fetus model, the
inferior antihelix crus maps to the thoracic/chest area. The
"Sympathetic" designation reflects autonomic regulation function rather
than somatotopic mapping — similar to Shen Men's mismatch between
location and clinical effect.

### Giải phẫu tại điểm

**Nerve supply — MIXED WITH HIGHER ABVN:**

- ABVN (Arnold's nerve) — the antihelix has 73% ABVN innervation
  (Peuker & Filler 2002), making this a HIGHER vagal access point
  than Shen Men
- Auriculotemporal nerve (V3) — contributes to anterior antihelix
  innervation

**Pathway to central nervous system:**

1. ABVN → NTS → vagal circuit (stronger component at this location
   than at Shen Men's triangular fossa)
2. V3 (auriculotemporal) → trigeminal sensory nuclear complex →
   brainstem

The HIGHER ABVN percentage at the antihelix (73% vs variable at
triangular fossa) means the Sympathetic point has MORE direct vagal
access than Shen Men. This may explain why stimulation at this point
specifically targets autonomic regulation.

→ Cross-ref: A02 §4.4 (ABVN vagal gateway), A07 §1.8 (auricle
innervation)

### Cross-system mapping

| System | Name / Position | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese auricular | Jiaoganshenjing (交感神经) — AH6a | ✓ Primary |
| Nogier/European | Thoracic region / "Sympathetic" area | ✓ Approximate |
| NADA protocol | Point #2 — autonomic regulation | ✓ Included |
| BFA (Battlefield) | Not included in BFA 5-point sequence | ✗ Absent |
| Body acupoint | No direct body acupoint equivalent | — |

**Convergence: 3/5 auricular systems include this point.** Chinese,
Nogier (approximate), and NADA agree. NOT included in BFA protocol
(BFA uses Cingulate Gyrus, Thalamus, Omega 2, Shen Men, Point Zero).
No direct body acupoint equivalent for "autonomic regulation" as a
primary function.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

**Chinese auricular tradition:**
Internal organ spasms (内脏痉挛 nèi zàng jìng luán), visceral pain,
vasoconstriction/vasodilation disorders. Used for stomach cramps,
menstrual cramps, biliary colic, urinary retention, and angina-like
chest pain. Autonomic dysregulation conditions.

**NADA protocol:**
Point #2 in the 5-point sequence (after Shen Men). Function: autonomic
regulation — stabilizing the sympathetic/parasympathetic balance. Used
for substance withdrawal (autonomic storm: sweating, tachycardia,
tremor), anxiety, post-traumatic stress disorder.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**Autonomic regulation 🟡 Plausible:**
Young & McCarthy 1998: stimulation of the "Sympathetic" point at the
inferior antihelix crus significantly decreased stimulus-evoked
electrodermal response compared to control point stimulation at the
helix — demonstrating measurable autonomic effect at this specific
point vs non-specific ear stimulation. Gao et al. 2008 (Autonomic
Neuroscience): auricular acupuncture specificity study in anesthetized
rats confirmed similar autonomic response patterns across ear zones,
with variable intensity depending on area stimulated.

Active standing test (healthy adults): auricular acupuncture at the
Sympathetic point showed heart rate variability changes consistent
with autonomic modulation.

**Anxiety/post-traumatic stress disorder (as part of NADA protocol)
🟡 Plausible:**
12 randomized controlled trials specifically using the NADA protocol:
medium effect for anxiety at post-intervention (4 randomized controlled
trials). However, individual contribution of the Sympathetic point
cannot be isolated from the 5-point protocol effects. 64% of studies
positive, but sham-controlled studies less positive → placebo
contribution likely.
→ Cross-ref: B01 §3.3 (NADA evidence), B02 §4.4 (substance use)

**Individual evidence: LIMITED.** Most studies include the Sympathetic
point as part of multi-point protocols (NADA, custom auricular
combinations). Solo-point evidence exists only for autonomic function
measurements — not for clinical endpoints like anxiety or pain
reduction.

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Higher ABVN = stronger vagal pathway:**

The Sympathetic point's location on the antihelix (73% ABVN
innervation) provides HIGHER vagal access than Shen Men (triangular
fossa, variable ABVN). This creates a gradient across Phase 1
auricular points:

| Point | Location | ABVN innervation | Primary mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Point Zero | Helix root/concha border | High (near cymba) | Strongest vagal component |
| Sympathetic | Inferior antihelix crus | 73% (Peuker & Filler) | Strong vagal + trigeminal |
| Shen Men | Triangular fossa | Variable/lower | Multi-nerve convergence |

**Naming paradox:** The point is called "Sympathetic" (交感) suggesting
sympathetic nervous system activation, but the mechanism via ABVN → NTS
produces PARASYMPATHETIC activation (vagal tone increase). The
therapeutic name reflects the CLINICAL OBSERVATION (autonomic regulation
= rebalancing an overactive sympathetic system) rather than the
MECHANISM (parasympathetic activation via the vagal nerve).
→ Cross-ref: A02 §4.4 (ABVN → parasympathetic, not sympathetic)

### An toàn

Same safety profile as auricular Shen Men — low risk. Auricular
chondritis with needles (rare). No pneumothorax risk, no pregnancy
contraindication. Standard auricular acupressure (ear seeds) is the
safest stimulation method.

## Point Zero — Nhĩ Trung (耳中, "Ear Center")

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

Nogier's "homeostatic point" — the central reference point of the
entire auricular map, from which all Romoli's radiating mapping lines
emanate. Battlefield Acupuncture point #5 (final in sequence). Located
at the root of the helix where the crus of the helix emerges from the
concha — at the border of ABVN territory. Dedicated heart rate
variability studies show parasympathetic activation, making this the
auricular point with the most direct evidence for vagal tone
enhancement.

### Vị trí trên microsystem map

Location: at the root of the helix, in a cartilage depression (notch)
where the crus of the helix meets the concha. Palpable with a pointed
probe as a distinct indentation.

Chinese approximate equivalent: Erzhong (耳中, "Ear Center") or
Hélijiǎo (耳轮脚, "Helix Root") — these Chinese designations
approximately correspond to the European "Point Zero" but the
traditions developed the concept independently.

Somatotopic correspondence: in Nogier's inverted fetus model, Point
Zero maps to the solar plexus/diaphragm region — the "center of the
body." Nogier named it "Point Zero" to express its function as the
balancing/homeostatic center in analogy to the body's energetic center.

Reference function: in Dr. Marco Romoli's auricular cartography, all
radiating mapping lines emanate from Point Zero — it serves as the
geometric and functional center of the auricular map.

### Giải phẫu tại điểm

**Nerve supply — AT ABVN BORDER:**

Point Zero is located at the helix root where it meets the concha. The
crus of the helix passes THROUGH the concha, placing Point Zero at or
near the transition to ABVN territory:

- ABVN (Arnold's nerve) — present at the adjacent concha (100% at
  cymba conchae, Peuker & Filler 2002). The helix root's proximity to
  the concha suggests ABVN contribution is likely
- Auriculotemporal nerve (V3) — innervates the helix region

**Pathway to central nervous system:**

1. ABVN (likely, given proximity to concha) → NTS → vagal circuit
2. V3 (auriculotemporal) → trigeminal sensory nuclear complex

The helix root location at the concha border gives Point Zero CLOSER
proximity to pure ABVN territory than either Shen Men or Sympathetic.
Heart rate variability evidence (below) supports that vagal activation
IS the dominant mechanism at this location.

→ Cross-ref: A02 §4.4 (ABVN vagal gateway), C04 §3.5.2 (ABVN access)

|---|---|---|
| Nogier/European | Point Zero / "Point R" — homeostatic center | ✓ Primary |
| Chinese auricular | Erzhong (耳中) / Hélijiǎo region | ✓ Approximate |
| BFA (Battlefield) | Point #5 — final consolidation point | ✓ Included |
| NADA protocol | Not included in NADA 5-point protocol | ✗ Absent |
| Body acupoint | No direct body acupoint equivalent | — |

**Convergence: 3/5 auricular systems include this point.** Nogier
(primary), BFA (adopted), and Chinese (approximate match) agree. NOT
included in NADA protocol. Not in WHO standard auricular nomenclature
as "Point Zero" — the name is from the Nogier/European tradition.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

**Nogier/European tradition:**
Primary homeostatic/balancing point. Used to "return the body to
equilibrium" — applied when the practitioner seeks a general regulatory
effect. Often the first or last point stimulated in European
auriculotherapy protocols.

**Battlefield Acupuncture protocol:**
Point #5 (final) in the 5-ASP-needle sequence (Cingulate Gyrus →
Thalamus → Omega 2 → Shen Men → Point Zero). Function: consolidation
of the analgesic effect initiated by the preceding 4 points. Placed
last for "homeostatic anchoring."

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**Parasympathetic activation 🟡 Plausible:**
Arai et al. 2013 (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative

**Pain (as part of BFA protocol) 🟡 Plausible:**

## Phase 2 — Concha Zone (CO) — Huyệt vùng Xoăn Tai (耳甲)

> **Anatomy:** The concha (耳甲) is the large bowl-shaped depression
> surrounding the external auditory meatus, divided by the crus of the
> helix (耳轮脚) into two subzones:
> - **Cymba conchae** (耳甲艇, superior concha) — ABOVE the helix crus.
>   Contains CO1-CO12. Somatotopic: abdominal/GI organs in the inverted
>   fetus model. **100% ABVN innervation** (Peuker & Filler 2002) —
>   HIGHEST vagal access of any auricular zone.
> - **Cavum conchae** (耳甲腔, inferior concha) — BELOW the helix crus.
>   Contains CO13-CO18. Somatotopic: thoracic organs. 45% ABVN
>   innervation (mixed with V3 auriculotemporal and C2-C3 great
>   auricular nerve). Documented in Batch 2.
>
> **Shared nerve pathway (cymba conchae, CO1-CO12):**
> ABVN → nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) → vagal circuit (cardiac,
> respiratory, gastrointestinal modulation). The cymba conchae is the
> PRIMARY target for transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation
> (taVNS). Evidence from taVNS studies (Frangos 2015 fMRI, NEMOS
> device) is DIRECTLY applicable to cymba conchae points.
> → Cross-ref: B02 §3.3 (ABVN anatomy), A02 §4.4 (vagal gateway)
>
> **Somatotopic arrangement:** GI tract organs are distributed around
> the helix crus in anatomical sequence: Mouth (CO1) → Esophagus (CO2)
> → Cardia (CO3) → Stomach (CO4) → Duodenum (CO5) → Small Intestine
> (CO6) → Large Intestine (CO7) → Appendix (CO8). Urinary system
> (CO9 Bladder, CO10 Kidney) occupies the inferior cymba near the
> antihelix junction.
>
> **Clinical significance:** The concha zone has the highest clinical
> density of any auricular zone — CO4 Stomach appears in 100% of
> auricular CINV protocols, CO15 Heart is a top-3 insomnia point,
> CO18 Endocrine is central to hormonal protocols. The 100% ABVN
> innervation of cymba conchae makes these points the strongest
> candidates for vagally-mediated effects.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Weight management / obesity 🟡 Plausible:**

Three systematic reviews/meta-analyses:

1. **Yeh et al. 2017** (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative
   Medicine): 18 RCTs, n=1,775, 3:1 female-to-male, mean age 38.9.
   Stomach (CO4) and Shen Men (TF4) = two most commonly used points.
   Body weight: mean difference −1.21 kg (95% CI −1.94 to −0.47).
   BMI: −0.57 kg/m² (95% CI −0.82 to −0.33).
   Body fat: −0.83% (95% CI −1.43 to −0.24).
   Waist circumference: −1.75 cm (95% CI −2.95 to −0.55).
   High heterogeneity (I² 78-94%). Treatment >6 weeks more effective.

2. **Hua, Usichenko, Cummings et al. 2024** (Frontiers in
   Neuroscience): 22 trials, 15 in meta-analysis, n=1,333, 91% female.
   21/22 trials stimulated vagally innervated concha regions.
   BMI: −0.38 (95% CI −0.55 to −0.22, p<0.0001).
   Weight: −0.66 kg (95% CI −1.12 to −0.20, p=0.005).
   Also: significant reductions in leptin, insulin, HOMA-IR.
   **Key limitation:** "Effect size does not appear clinically relevant"
   — below 5% body weight reduction threshold.

3. **Mendonca et al. 2021** (European Journal of Integrative Medicine):
   11 RCTs, n=892. BMI vs no treatment: −1.08 kg/m² (95% CI −1.29 to
   −0.86). vs sham: −0.22 (95% CI −0.44 to −0.01).
   Greater improvement as adjuvant to lifestyle modification.

**Pooled assessment:** Statistically significant but BELOW clinical
significance threshold (5% weight loss). CO4 Stomach never tested as
single-point intervention — always part of multi-point protocols.

Key RCT — **Yeo et al. 2013** (Acupuncture in Medicine): n=91.
5-point protocol (Shen Men, Spleen, **Stomach**, Hunger, Endocrine)
vs 1-point (Hunger only) vs sham. At 4 weeks: 5-point 6.1% BMI
reduction; 1-point 5.7%; sham no reduction. **No significant difference
between 5-point and 1-point groups** — suggesting the Hunger point
alone may capture most of the effect, or the effect is zone-general
rather than point-specific.

**Chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting (CINV) 🟡 Plausible:**
**Chen et al. 2021** (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative
Medicine): 19 RCTs, n=1,449, auricular acupressure for CINV.
Stomach = MOST commonly used point: **100% of all 19 studies** included
CO4. (Shen Men 94.7%, Sympathetic 73.7%, Spleen 63.2%).
Overall CINV efficacy: RR 1.31 (95% CI 1.22-1.41, p≤0.001).
Delayed nausea: SMD −0.68 (32% reduction, no heterogeneity).
Delayed vomiting: SMD −0.91 (95% CI −1.22 to −0.61).
Acute nausea/vomiting: no significant improvement.
Combined auricular + antiemetic reduced adverse reactions (RR 0.62).

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

**ABVN → NTS → appetite regulation pathway:**

CO4 Stomach sits in the cymba conchae (100% ABVN). The ABVN afferents
project to the NTS — the SAME brainstem nucleus that receives gastric
vagal afferents carrying satiety signals from the actual stomach. This
creates a unique convergence:

1. ABVN stimulation at CO4 → NTS activation
2. NTS → enteroendocrine signaling → GLP-1 release → insulin secretion,
   glucagon suppression
3. NTS → hypothalamic arcuate nucleus → POMC (anorexigenic) neuron
   activation → appetite suppression

The ABVN → NTS pathway provides the most direct neuroanatomical
rationale for auricular appetite regulation — the same brainstem relay
that processes real gastric signals is activated externally via the ear.

**taVNS evidence support:** taVNS at cymba conchae (CO4's zone)
inhibits food intake and weight gain through orexin-dependent pathways
in animal models (Scientific Reports 2025). Cymba conchae is the
optimal zone for vagally-mediated metabolic effects.

**Limitation — zone-level vs point-level specificity:**
The Yeo 2013 finding (5-point protocol no better than 1-point Hunger)
raises the question whether CO4 Stomach adds specific value beyond
general concha stimulation. The effect may be ZONE-LEVEL (any cymba
conchae stimulation → ABVN → NTS) rather than POINT-SPECIFIC (CO4
uniquely activating "stomach" pathways). Consistent with the framework
finding that organ-specific auricular mapping is 🟡/🔴 while general
vagal activation is 🟢.
→ Cross-ref: A07 §4.6 (vagal 🟢, organ-specific 🟡/🔴),
Framework note 1 (mixed innervation precision)

strengthening willpower, processing fear and trauma, supporting adrenal/
cortisol-related stress physiology. Combined with the 4 other NADA
points for substance withdrawal, PTSD, anxiety.
→ Cross-ref: child plan §7.3 (NADA protocol)

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**NADA protocol (as 5-point bundle) 🔴 Inconclusive individually:**
- **Lee 2022** (Journal of Addictive Diseases): 36 studies reviewed,
  23/36 (64%) positive for addiction. NADA 5-point = most common
  protocol. Risk of bias moderate-to-high; only 4/36 (11%) scored
  low bias.
- **Karlsson & Kowalski 2016** (Substance Abuse Treatment, Prevention,
  and Policy, PMC4959048): RCT n=280. NADA vs local-protocol
  acupuncture vs relaxation control — **no evidence either acupuncture
  arm outperformed relaxation** on anxiety, insomnia, drug use.
- Cochrane (cocaine): "evidence could not be confirmed."
Individual CO10 contribution: NEVER isolated from 5-point bundle.
No dismantling study exists.
→ Cross-ref: Ear Shen Men entry (NADA evidence)

**Renal conditions 🔴 No individual-point evidence:**
Symptom management: auricular acupressure for uremic pruritus (Yan
et al. 2015, n=62, significant pruritus reduction + serum histamine
decrease at 6 weeks). Intradialytic hypertension in CKD (SR with trial
sequential analysis, safe/efficacious for BP reduction). QoL improvement
in diabetic CKD (PMC4276331). But CO10 never tested in isolation for
renal function outcomes (GFR, proteinuria).

**fMRI neuroimaging 🟡 Plausible (mechanistic):**
Peng et al. 2017: taVNS at CO10/CO11/CO12/TF4 → prefrontal, limbic,
and auditory cortex activation. Supports vagal afferent → NTS →
cortical pathway as mechanism for central effects at this cymba cluster.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

CO10 sits in cymba conchae (100% ABVN), sharing the same ABVN → NTS
pathway as CO1-CO9. The fMRI evidence (Peng 2017) confirms that vagal
stimulation at this cluster activates limbic (emotional regulation) and
prefrontal (executive control) cortices — consistent with NADA's
clinical claims of "willpower strengthening" and "fear processing."

The TCM Kidney-fear/willpower association finds a plausible neural
substrate: ABVN → NTS → limbic cortex (amygdala, fear processing) and
prefrontal cortex (executive function, impulse control). However, this
mechanism is SHARED across all cymba conchae points — Framework note 4
(zone-level vs point-level effects) applies.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Cymba conchae — 100% ABVN. See zone introduction. Part of the

### CO12 — Can (肝 Gān, Liver)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

NADA protocol point #4 — "Liver" for anger modulation (怒 nù) and
detoxification. Located in the lateral inferior cymba conchae — 100%
ABVN territory. Part of the cymba conchae fMRI-validated cluster.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
Posteroinferior area of the cymba conchae (lateral inferior). Adjacent
to CO11 (Pancreas & Gallbladder) and CO10 (Kidney). Somatotopic:
liver, hepatic area.
Chinese standard: CO12 (Concha zone 12).

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Cymba conchae — 100% ABVN innervation. Shared pathway: ABVN → NTS →
vagal circuit. Part of the CO10/CO11/CO12 cluster with fMRI evidence
(Peng et al. 2017) confirming prefrontal, limbic, and auditory cortex
activation via vagal afferents.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard | CO12 肝 | ✓ Primary |
| Nogier/European Phase 1 | Liver — concha zone | ✓ CONVERGENT |
| NADA protocol | Point #4 "Liver" — anger, detoxification | ✓ Included |
| Body acupoint parallel | LR3 Tàichōng (Liver Yuan-Source) | ↔ Functional |

**Nogier vs Chinese — CONVERGENT (corrects plan §7.4):** Research
confirms the Liver point shows CONVERGENCE between Chinese and Nogier
systems — both place it in the cymba conchae (Romoli et al. 2016).
The Liver is explicitly listed among the 34/93 ISAAPs based on
Nogier's 1957 nomenclature. The plan's §7.4 assumption of "known
disagreement" for Liver was not confirmed by comparative studies.
The LARGEST known Nogier-Chinese disagreement is for the lower limb
(triangular fossa vs superior antihelix crus), not visceral organs.

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
**Chinese auricular:** Hepatitis, hypochondriac pain, eye disorders
(Liver opens to the eyes in TCM), dizziness, hypertension, menstrual
irregularities.

**NADA context:** Point #4 — TCM Wood element, emotion = anger (怒).
Liver governs smooth flow of Qi (疏泄). In addiction: detoxification,
processing anger and aggression, calming impulsivity, supporting vision
and planning (Carter & Olshan-Perlmutter 2015, Behavioral Sciences,
PMC4695777). Combined with the 4 other NADA points.
→ Cross-ref: child plan §7.3 (NADA protocol)

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**NADA protocol (as 5-point bundle) 🔴 Inconclusive individually:**
Same evidence base as CO10 Kidney — Lee 2022 SR (64% positive, high
bias), Swedish RCT n=280 (negative vs relaxation), Cochrane cocaine
(inconclusive). Individual CO12 contribution never isolated. No
dismantling study (NADA-with-Liver vs NADA-without-Liver) exists.

**Hepatoprotective effects 🔴 No evidence:**
No human clinical trial demonstrates that stimulating CO12 produces
measurable hepatoprotective effects (liver enzymes, hepatic
inflammation). One diagnostic study (Huang et al., PubMed 20633472):
auricular liver zone tenderness and electrical skin resistance
correlated with hepatic dysfunction (n=20 vs 25 controls) — supports
diagnostic reflex zone concept but not therapeutic effect. A taVNS RCT
protocol for post-hepatectomy liver function (PMC12378827) is
registered but unpublished.

**fMRI neuroimaging 🟡 Plausible (mechanistic):**
Part of Peng 2017 cluster. Same cortical activation as CO10.
Frangos et al. 2015: cymba conchae stimulation → NTS, locus coeruleus,
amygdala, nucleus accumbens activation.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

CO12 shares the same ABVN → NTS → limbic/prefrontal pathway as CO10.
The TCM Liver-anger association finds plausible neural substrate in
limbic cortex (amygdala) and prefrontal cortex (impulse control)
activation (Peng 2017). However, this mechanism is ZONE-LEVEL, not
point-specific (Framework note 4). The diagnostic correlation (Huang
et al.) — auricular liver zone tenderness in hepatic dysfunction — is
consistent with viscerosomatocutaneous reflex theory but does not
establish organ-specific therapeutic effects.

### Cavum Conchae (CO13-CO18) — Thoracic Organ Zone

> **ABVN transition:** Points below shift from cymba conchae (100%
> ABVN, CO1-CO12) to **cavum conchae (45% ABVN)** — the inferior
> concha below the helix crus. The remaining ~55% innervation comes
> from the great auricular nerve (C2-C3) and auriculotemporal nerve
> (V3 trigeminal) (Bermejo et al. 2017, PMC5371220). This dual
> innervation means cavum conchae points have REDUCED but not absent
> vagal access compared to cymba conchae.
>
> **Somatotopic:** Thoracic organs in the inverted fetus model —
> Spleen (CO13), Lung (CO14), Heart (CO15), Trachea (CO16), Sanjiao
> (CO17), Endocrine (CO18 at intertragic notch).
>
> **taVNS applicability:** taVNS devices target cymba conchae (100%
> ABVN), NOT cavum conchae. Evidence from taVNS studies is LESS
> directly transferable to CO13-CO18 than to CO1-CO12.

### CO14 — Phế (肺 Fèi, Lung)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

NADA protocol point #5 — the ORIGINAL single-point auricular treatment
for addiction. Dr. H.L. Wen (1973, Hong Kong) discovered serendipitously
that electroacupuncture at the ear Lung point abated opiate withdrawal
symptoms. This observation at a single auricular point launched the
entire field of auricular addiction treatment, which later expanded to
the NADA 5-point protocol under Michael Smith at Lincoln Hospital (NYC,
1974-1985).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
Cavum conchae (inferior concha), surrounding the external auditory
meatus. Somatotopic: lung, pulmonary area.
Chinese standard: CO14 (Concha zone 14).

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Cavum conchae — 45% ABVN + ~55% great auricular nerve (C2-C3).
Bermejo et al. 2017 (PMC5371220) confirmed the dual innervation and
characterized fiber types (A-beta, A-delta, C fibers). The mixed
innervation means effects at this point involve BOTH vagal AND cervical
somatic pathways — making it harder to attribute effects to a single
neural pathway.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard | CO14 肺 | ✓ Primary |
| Nogier/European Phase 1 | Lung — cavum concha | ✓ CONVERGENT |
| NADA protocol | Point #5 — grief, emotional release, respiratory | ✓ Included |
| CINV protocols | Included in 31.6% of studies (Chen 2021) | ✓ Common |
| Smoking cessation | Core point (with Shen Men + Mouth) | ✓ Standard |
| Body acupoint parallel | LU7 Lièquē (respiratory Command Point) | ↔ Functional |

**Nogier vs Chinese — CONVERGENT:** Both systems place Lung in the
cavum conchae. Nogier's rationale was explicitly neuroanatomical —
vagal innervation of this zone. Chinese system assigns a LARGER
representation (occupying almost the whole inferior concha) compared to
the more circumscribed Nogier localization. Lung is among the 34/93
ISAAPs based on Nogier's nomenclature (Romoli et al. 2016).

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
**Chinese auricular:** Respiratory disorders (cough, asthma, chest
tightness), skin disorders (Lung governs skin in TCM), urticaria.

**NADA context:** Point #5, and historically the FIRST auricular point
used for addiction. Wen & Cheung (1973) treated 40 opium/heroin
patients, 39 improved. Smith at Lincoln Hospital found manual needling
more prolonged than electrostimulation. Shen Men added next, then
Kidney/Liver/Sympathetic by clinical observation. TCM Metal element,
emotion = grief (悲). "Letting go," breath regulation, emotional release.
→ Cross-ref: child plan §7.3 (NADA protocol)

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Smoking cessation 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level):**
- **Wang et al. 2019** (Tobacco Induced Diseases, PMC6662782): 24 RCTs,
  n=3,984. vs no intervention: short-term RR 2.37 (95% CI 1.41-3.97),
  long-term RR 2.66 (1.50-4.70). **vs sham: NO significant difference**
  at any time point. Acupuncture + counseling superior to acupuncture
  alone.
- **Mi et al. 2025** (Tobacco Induced Diseases, PMC11808481): 9 RCTs,
  n=1,032. Auricular therapy significantly reduced MNWS withdrawal
  scores vs NRT; no significant difference in cessation rate vs NRT.
  Fewer adverse events than NRT.
- Cochrane review (White et al. 2014): 38 studies. Possible short-term
  effects but no sustained benefit at 6+ months vs sham.

CO14 Lung is part of multi-point smoking cessation protocols (with
Shen Men + Mouth + Sympathetic), never tested in isolation.

**NADA protocol 🔴 Inconclusive individually:**
Same evidence base as CO10/CO12 — mixed/negative overall. Individual
CO14 contribution never isolated.

**Respiratory (asthma/COPD) 🔴 Very low certainty:**
No auricular-specific SR for asthma or COPD found. General acupuncture
SRs include auricular as one modality among many but never isolate
CO14 or any specific auricular point.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

CO14 sits in cavum conchae (45% ABVN + 55% great auricular nerve). The
mixed innervation means effects involve BOTH vagal (ABVN → NTS →
autonomic) AND cervical somatic (C2-C3 → brainstem) pathways. For
smoking cessation, the mechanism is likely general autonomic modulation
rather than lung-specific targeting.

The historical significance of CO14 as the FIRST addiction treatment
point (Wen 1973) is noteworthy: the discovery was serendipitous
(electroacupuncture for surgical anesthesia, not intentional addiction
treatment), suggesting the effect was zone-level (concha vagal
stimulation) rather than point-specific.

### CO15 — Tâm (心 Xīn, Heart)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

Top 3 auricular insomnia point — Heart (CO15) + Shen Men (TF4) +
Subcortex (AT4) together account for 71.70% of auricular points
selected in insomnia studies (Huang et al. 2025). CO15 Heart is the
MOST universally selected: 100% inclusion rate (14/14 studies) in
breast cancer insomnia SR. Located at the center of the cavum conchae.
The ONLY concha point with dedicated point-specific cardiovascular
evidence (He et al. 2012: cardiac NTS neuron activation at CO15,
absent at CO4 Stomach).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
Center of the cavum conchae (inferior concha), the most central
position in the inferior conchal bowl. Somatotopic: heart, cardiac
area.
Chinese standard: CO15 (Concha zone 15).

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Cavum conchae — 45% ABVN + ~55% great auricular nerve (C2-C3) and
auriculotemporal nerve (V3). Bermejo et al. 2017: posteromedial cavum
has higher nerve density (28.3 ± 4.9 nerve profiles) than anterolateral
(10.2 ± 4.7), with more myelinated fibers posteriorly (16.1% vs 8.2%).

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard | CO15 心 | ✓ Primary |
| Nogier/European Phase 1 | Heart — cavum concha | ✓ CONVERGENT |
| Insomnia protocols | Top 3: Shen Men + Heart + Subcortex (71.70%) | ✓ Core |
| CINV protocols | Included in some studies | ✓ Occasional |
| Body acupoint parallel | HT7 Shénmén (Heart calming, insomnia) | ↔ Functional |

**Nogier vs Chinese — CONVERGENT:** Both systems place Heart in the
cavum conchae. No major disagreement (Wirz-Ridolfi 2019).

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
Insomnia (失眠, most frequent indication), palpitations, anxiety,
dream-disturbed sleep, neurasthenia, cardiac arrhythmia. Heart houses
the Shen (神, spirit/consciousness) in TCM — insomnia is primarily a
Heart (Shen) dysfunction in TCM diagnosis. The insomnia triad (Shen
Men + Heart + Subcortex) is the most frequently used auricular
combination for sleep disorders.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Insomnia 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level, strong frequency data):**

1. **Wang et al. 2026** (Frontiers in Psychiatry): Network meta-analysis,
   95 RCTs, n=7,628. Heart (CO15) = **2nd most used auricular point
   at 14.2%**, behind Shen Men (17.4%). Subcortex 3rd at 11.1%.
   Electroacupuncture + auricular acupressure ranked highest: sleep
   quality SUCRA 0.938, total effective rate SUCRA 0.838.

2. **Huang et al. 2025** (Medicine): Breast cancer insomnia SR, 15 RCTs,
   n=1,125. Heart = **100% inclusion rate (14/14 studies)**. Shen Men
   92.86%, Subcortex 78.57%. Heart + Shen Men + Subcortex = **71.70%
   combined share**. PSQI: MD −3.36 (95% CI −4.65 to −2.07, p<0.001).
   GRADE quality rating: "high."

3. **Jun et al. 2024** (Frontiers in Sleep): 23 studies, n=1,689.
   Heart among seven high-frequency points. PSQI: SMD −1.30
   (95% CI −1.65 to −0.96).

CO15 Heart is virtually always combined with Shen Men and/or Subcortex —
individual CO15 contribution cannot be fully isolated from protocol.

**Heart rate variability (HRV) 🟡 Plausible (point-specific):**
**Trinh et al. 2022** (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative
Medicine): n=114 healthy volunteers (57 experimental, 57 control).
Auricular acupressure at Heart point specifically on the left ear
(Vaccaria seeds, 20 min). **Total HRV significantly increased** during
stimulation (p=0.01, 0.04, 0.04, 0.02 at sequential stages). SDNN,
RMSSD, LF, HF individually non-significant.

**Boehmer et al. 2020** (Heart Rhythm O2): n=24 healthy males.
Acupuncture at inferior concha (ABVN territory, includes CO15 region).
SDNN increased ~19% (p<0.05), heart rate reduced ~4-6% (p<0.05).

**Cardiovascular NTS specificity 🟡 Plausible (mechanistic):**
**He et al. 2012** (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative
Medicine): Acupuncture-like stimulation at CO15 activated cardiac-
related neurons in the NTS and evoked cardiovascular inhibition
(depressor response). CO4 Stomach stimulation did NOT produce this
effect. This is the strongest evidence for POINT-SPECIFIC effects
within the concha — cardiac NTS activation at CO15 but not at CO4.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

CO15 Heart's mechanism involves a dual pathway:

1. **Vagal pathway (45% ABVN):** ABVN → NTS → cardiac vagal efferent →
   increased parasympathetic tone → HRV increase, calming effect
   conducive to sleep onset
2. **Cervical somatic pathway (55% C2-C3):** Great auricular nerve →
   cervical dorsal horn → brainstem reticular formation → arousal
   modulation

The **insomnia triad** (Shen Men + Heart + Subcortex) distributes
stimulation across THREE distinct anatomical zones:
- TF4 Shen Men: triangular fossa (multi-nerve convergence)
- CO15 Heart: cavum conchae (vagal-cardiac)
- AT4 Subcortex: antitragus medial surface (cortical)

This anatomical diversity accesses complementary neural pathways rather
than redundantly stimulating a single zone — possibly explaining the
triad's dominance in insomnia protocols.

**Point specificity at CO15:** The He 2012 finding (cardiac NTS neuron
activation at CO15 but NOT at CO4) challenges the zone-level-only model
from Framework note 4. However, CO15 (cavum, 45% ABVN) and CO4 (cymba,
100% ABVN) are in DIFFERENT zones with different innervation ratios.
The specificity may be INTER-zone (cavum ≠ cymba) rather than INTRA-
zone (CO15 ≠ CO14 within cavum). Whether cavum conchae points differ
from each other remains untested.
→ Cross-ref: Ear Shen Men (insomnia evidence), AT4 Subcortex (Batch 4)

### CO18 — Nội Tiết (内分泌 Nèifēnmì, Endocrine)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

Located at the intertragic notch — a UNIQUE anatomical position between
the tragus and antitragus, at the junction of the concha zone. Central
point in auricular hormonal and metabolic protocols. One of the most
frequently selected auricular points for obesity (top 3-5) and the
2nd most used point for dysmenorrhea (34/36 studies).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
At the intertragic notch (屏间切迹) — the depression between the tragus
(anterior) and antitragus (posterior). Center of the inside wall of the
notch. This is the lowest point of the concha zone, at the border
between concha, tragus, and antitragus zones.
Chinese standard: CO18 (Concha zone 18, final concha point).

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Intertragic notch — innervation from auriculotemporal nerve (V3) and
potentially ABVN. The precise ABVN percentage at this specific location
is not documented separately from the cavum conchae (45%) or tragus
(45%) figures. Likely mixed innervation at the transition zone.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard | CO18 内分泌 (single point) | ✓ Primary |
| Nogier/European | ACTH + Thyroid + Parathyroid + Gonads (MULTIPLE gland points) | ~ Different model |
| Obesity protocols | Top 3-5 most used (with Stomach + Shen Men) | ✓ Core |
| Dysmenorrhea protocols | 2nd most used (34/36 studies, Kong 2023) | ✓ Core |
| Body acupoint parallel | CV4 Guānyuán, SP6 Sānyīnjiāo (hormonal) | ↔ Functional |

**Nogier vs Chinese — DIFFERENT MODELS:** The Chinese system
consolidated an entire endocrine zone into ONE point (CO18 内分泌) with
broad indications for "all endocrine disorders." Nogier/European system
maps the same intertragic notch area as MULTIPLE distinct gland points:
ACTH (pituitary-adrenal), thyroid, parathyroid, gonads — each with
specific locations within the notch. This is a conceptual divergence:
Nogier differentiates by specific gland; Chinese uses a single
functional point. The "Endocrine" designation is a modern 20th-century
Chinese auricular concept, not from classical TCM.

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
Menstrual irregularities (月经不调), dysmenorrhea, menopausal symptoms,
hormonal acne, thyroid disorders, obesity/metabolic syndrome. The most
common two-point auricular obesity combination is CO4 (Stomach) + CO18
(Endocrine) (complex network analysis 2018).

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Dysmenorrhea 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level, strong frequency data):**
**Kong et al. 2023** (Frontiers in Endocrinology, PMC9851274): 35 RCTs,
n=3,960. CO18 Endocrine = **2nd most used point (34/36 studies)**,
after Internal Genitals TF2 (36 studies). Then: Shen Men TF4 (27),
Liver CO12 (24), Sympathetic AH6a (23), Kidney CO10 (23).
Cure rate: OR 1.95 (95% CI 1.34-2.83, p=0.0004).
Total effective rate: OR 3.58 (95% CI 2.92-4.39, p<0.00001).
VAS pain: MD −1.45 (95% CI −1.73 to −1.17, p<0.00001).
Symptom score: SMD −0.85 (95% CI −1.28 to −0.43).
**Limitation:** superiority shown vs analgesics/no-treatment, but
"no definite conclusion" vs placebo/sham.

**PCOS 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level):**
**Li et al. 2026** (Frontiers in Endocrinology, PMC12995678): 18 RCTs,
n=1,231 PCOS patients. CO18 Endocrine used in 12/18 studies.
Auricular + metformin vs metformin alone: LH reduction MD −0.81
(95% CI −1.05 to −0.57). Benefits only as adjunctive therapy.

**Weight management 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level):**
CO18 Endocrine consistently in top 3-5 most used auricular obesity
points across meta-analyses (Yeh 2017, Hua 2024). CO4 + CO18 = most
frequent two-point combination. Same limitations as CO4 entry:
statistically significant but below 5% clinical threshold, CO18 never
isolated.
→ Cross-ref: CO4 Stomach (obesity evidence)

**Hormonal assays 🔴 No point-specific evidence:**
No study demonstrates measurable hormonal changes (cortisol, leptin,
insulin, ghrelin) from stimulating CO18 specifically. One taVNS study
(Kozorosky 2022, PMC9020171) showed postprandial ghrelin suppression
at cymba conchae — but this is the cymba (100% ABVN), NOT the
intertragic notch (CO18's location). A pilot study (PubMed 26462269)
found NO significant change in insulin, ACTH, leptin, or adiponectin
after auricular acupuncture.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

The "Endocrine" designation reflects clinical tradition rather than
verified mechanism. Two possible pathways:

1. **Vagal (if ABVN present):** ABVN → NTS → paraventricular nucleus
   (PVN) of hypothalamus → hypothalamic-pituitary axis modulation.
   The PVN governs neuroendocrine output (CRH → ACTH → cortisol,
   GnRH → LH/FSH, TRH → TSH).
2. **Trigeminal (V3):** Auriculotemporal nerve → trigeminal sensory
   nucleus → hypothalamic connections via trigeminal-hypothalamic
   pathway.

Both pathways could theoretically modulate neuroendocrine function, but
no study has demonstrated that stimulation specifically at the
intertragic notch produces hormonal changes distinct from stimulation
at other ear locations. The Nogier model's separation into individual
gland points (ACTH, thyroid, gonads) has no more empirical support than
the Chinese single-point model — neither system has point-specific
hormonal validation.

⚡ **Framework note 5 — INTER-ZONE SPECIFICITY: CAVUM vs CYMBA
CONCHAE:** CO15 Heart evidence (He et al. 2012) reveals that
acupuncture-like stimulation at CO15 (cavum conchae, 45% ABVN)
activated cardiac-related NTS neurons and produced cardiovascular
inhibition, while CO4 Stomach (cymba conchae, 100% ABVN) did NOT.
This is the first evidence of DIFFERENTIAL organ-related NTS activation
between concha sub-zones. The finding is paradoxical: CO15 with LESS
ABVN (45%) produced MORE cardiac-specific effects than CO4 with MORE
ABVN (100%). Two interpretations: (a) the NON-ABVN nerves at CO15
(great auricular C2-C3, auriculotemporal V3) contribute pathway
components that route to cardiac NTS neurons — making the MIXED
innervation (not pure ABVN) the key to organ specificity; or (b)
ABVN fibers in the cavum and cymba project to DIFFERENT NTS sub-nuclei,
with cavum ABVN fibers targeting cardiac neurons and cymba ABVN fibers
targeting GI neurons — which would imply an ABVN somatotopic gradient
within the concha. Either interpretation qualifies Framework note 4:
zone-level effects may be the dominant model WITHIN a sub-zone (cymba
or cavum), but BETWEEN sub-zones there is evidence for differential
NTS activation. The insomnia triad's success (multi-zone distribution:
TF + CO + AT) may exploit this inter-zone specificity.

## Phase 2 — Antihelix Zone (AH) — Huyệt vùng Đối Nhĩ Luân (对耳轮)

> **Anatomy:** The antihelix (对耳轮) is the prominent Y-shaped ridge on
> the auricle, bifurcating superiorly into two crura:
> - **Superior crus** (对耳轮上脚) — the upper branch, extending toward
>   the helix. Somatotopic: lower limb (heel at tip → hip at base) in
>   the inverted fetus model.
> - **Inferior crus** (对耳轮下脚) — the lower branch, extending toward
>   the concha. Somatotopic: buttock/hip/sciatic region. AH6a
>   Sympathetic (Phase 1) sits at the junction where the inferior crus
>   meets the concha.
> - **Antihelix body** (对耳轮体) — the undivided trunk below the
>   bifurcation. Somatotopic: vertebral column, from lumbosacral
>   (superior) to cervical (inferior), matching the inverted fetus
>   orientation.
>
> **ABVN innervation — CRITICAL GRADIENT:** Peuker & Filler (2002) data,
> confirmed by Kaniusas et al. (2019, Frontiers in Neuroscience, 13:854),
> distinguishes TWO antihelix sub-zones:
> - **Antihelix body:** 73% ABVN innervation (ABVN + great auricular
>   nerve C2-C3 + auriculotemporal V3)
> - **Crura of antihelix:** 9% ABVN innervation (great auricular nerve
>   C2-C3 PRIMARY, minimal ABVN)
>
> This means spine/trunk points (AH9-AH13) have HIGH vagal access, while
> lower limb (AH1-AH5) and buttock/sciatic (AH7-AH8) points have
> MINIMAL vagal access — their effects operate primarily through
> cervical somatic pathways (great auricular nerve C2-C3 → cervical
> dorsal horn → brainstem).
>
> **Shared nerve pathway (antihelix body, AH9-AH13):**
> Dual pathway: (1) ABVN → NTS → descending pain inhibition via vlPAG
> (Zuo et al. 2026, Nature Communications: mapped complete circuit
> auricle → JNG(Glu) → NTS(POMC) → vlPAG(Glu)); (2) Great auricular
> nerve (C2-C3) → cervical dorsal horn → brainstem reticular formation.
> → Cross-ref: B02 §3.3 (ABVN anatomy), A02 §4.4 (vagal gateway)
>
> **Clinical significance:** The antihelix is the PRIMARY zone for
> musculoskeletal pain in auricular acupuncture. AH9 Lumbosacral is the
> top somatotopic reflex point for low back pain (71-100% inclusion
> across SRs). AH12 Cervical appears in 83% of cervical pain studies.
> The standard auricular pain paradigm combines somatotopic reflex
> points (AH9/AH12) with autonomic/analgesic master points (Shen Men
> TF4 + Subcortex AT4 ± Sympathetic AH6a).

### Superior Crus — Lower Limb (AH1-AH5)

> **ABVN transition:** Points on the superior crus have only **9% ABVN**
> innervation (Peuker & Filler 2002, Kaniusas et al. 2019) —
> dramatically lower than the antihelix body (73%). Primary innervation
> is from the great auricular nerve (C2-C3). Effects at these points
> operate through cervical somatic pathways with minimal vagal
> contribution.
>
> **Somatotopic arrangement:** Lower limb mapped from tip to base of the
> superior crus: Heel (AH1, distal tip) → Toe (AH2) → Ankle (AH3) →
> Knee (AH4) → Hip (AH5, base near bifurcation). This follows the
> inverted fetus orientation — foot superior, hip inferior.
>
> **Nogier vs Chinese — SYSTEMATIC DIVERGENCE:** The Chinese system
> places lower limb on the superior crus. The Nogier/European system
> projects the entire lower extremity into the triangular fossa. This
> is one of the largest systematic disagreements between traditions —
> affecting all 5 lower limb points.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Diagnostic reactivity 🟡 Plausible:**
**Romoli, Greco & Giommi 2016** (Complementary Therapies in Medicine,
27:158-166, PMID 27261983): Observational study, n=30 lumbar hernia
patients. Auricular diagnostic methods (pain pressure test + electrical
skin resistance test) found significant reactive points at the
lumbar-sciatic auricular zones. Reactivity correlated with pain levels
and decreased as pain resolved after epidural infiltrations. Supports
the viscerosomatocutaneous reflex zone concept.

**Sciatica treatment 🔴 No point-specific evidence:**
No published RCT or SR isolates the auricular sciatic nerve point as a
solo intervention. Most auricular LBP/sciatica studies use Shen Men +
Subcortex + Lumbosacral (AH9) as the standard combination — often
without including AH8 specifically. Body acupuncture for sciatica has
stronger evidence: SR/MA of 28 RCTs (2023, n=2,707) found acupuncture
significantly more effective than analgesics — but this is body
acupuncture, not auricular-specific.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

AH8's mechanism operates primarily through the great auricular nerve
(C2-C3), given the low ABVN (9%) at the crura:

1. Stimulation at AH8 → great auricular nerve (C2-C3) → cervical
   dorsal horn
2. Cervical dorsal horn → brainstem via spinobulbar pathways →
   descending pain modulation
3. Minimal ABVN contribution (9%) → limited direct vagal involvement

The diagnostic reactivity finding (Romoli 2016) supports the existence
of a reflex arc between the sciatic nerve territory and its auricular
representation, but the therapeutic pathway from ear back to sciatic
nerve is less well established.

**Nogier's original observation — historical context:** Nogier noticed
that patients with sciatica who had been treated by a lay practitioner
(Madame Barrin) with cauterization of the antihelix showed pain relief.
He hypothesized a somatotopic representation on the ear, leading to the
inverted fetus model (1956/1957). The sciatic zone was the FIRST
auricular somatotopic correspondence identified — the foundation of
modern auriculotherapy.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Chronic low back pain 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level, strong frequency

2. **Kawi, Yeh et al. 2025** (Pain Medicine, 26(9):515-526): **Largest

3. **N'bunde et al. 2021** (Longhua Chinese Medicine, 4:37): SR, 6

   SMD −0.78 within 4 weeks (95% CI −1.22 to −0.33). Individual

**Lee et al. 2025** (Journal of Integrative and Complementary Medicine,
PMID 39018502): SR/MA of RCTs. Pain: **SMD −0.95** (95% CI −1.36 to
−0.54), large effect. Disability: SMD −0.68. Pressure pain threshold:
SMD −0.55. Minimal adverse events. LBP included in pooled analysis.

Kawi/Yeh 2025: targeted auricular point acupressure (lumbar-specific points) produced slightly
more pain reduction (−1.73) than non-targeted auricular point acupressure (−1.62), but BOTH
significantly outperformed education control. Somatotopic targeting
provides incremental benefit over non-specific auricular stimulation —
supporting a model where auricular pain relief involves BOTH
zone-specific (somatotopic) AND zone-general (vagal/autonomic)
components.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

AH9's analgesic mechanism operates through dual pathways, both mapped:

1. **Vagal pathway (73% ABVN):** ABVN → jugular-nodose ganglia
   (glutamatergic) → NTS (POMC neurons) → vlPAG (glutamatergic) →
   descending pain inhibition (Zuo et al. 2026, Nature Communications).
   This supraspinal analgesic circuit is the same pathway used by taVNS.

2. **Cervical somatic pathway (C2-C3):** Great auricular nerve →
   cervical dorsal horn → brainstem reticular formation → diffuse
   noxious inhibitory controls (DNIC).

The Kawi/Yeh 2025 finding (non-targeted auricular point acupressure also effective) supports
the hypothesis that the vagal pathway provides a substantial baseline
analgesic effect at ANY antihelix body point (zone-level), while
somatotopic targeting adds incremental precision. Consistent with
Framework note 4 (zone-level vs point-level effects) extended to MSK.
→ Cross-ref: Framework note 4, Zuo et al. 2026 (ABVN analgesic
circuit), Li et al. 2025 (PAG connectivity in cLBP)

Located on the lower antihelix body where it has BOTH 73% ABVN (vagal)
innervation AND great auricular nerve (C2-C3) input — creating a
unique dual-pathway analgesic mechanism where the C2-C3 auricular
afferents share spinal segmental origin with cervical spine pain
pathways (segmental convergence).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
Lower one-fifth of the antihelix body, just above the antitragus
junction. Somatotopic: cervical vertebrae (C1-C7), cervical spine.
Chinese standard: AH12 (颈椎). Note: WFAS international standard
(WFAS-002:2012) reverses AH12/AH13 numbering — WFAS assigns AH12 =
Neck, AH13 = Cervical Vertebrae. This atlas follows the Chinese
national standard (GB/T 13734-2008).

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Antihelix body — 73% ABVN + great auricular nerve (C2-C3). The
cervical portion of the antihelix is adjacent to the antitragus
(head/brain zones) inferiorly, cavum conchae (internal organs)
medially, and scapha (upper extremities) laterally — a convergence
zone between spinal, cranial, and extremity representations.

**Unique neuroanatomical feature — segmental convergence:**
The great auricular nerve originates from C2-C3. These are the SAME
cervical segments that receive:
- Cervical facet joint nociceptors (C2-C3 medial branches)
- Deep cervical proprioceptive afferents (C1-C4)
- Greater occipital nerve afferents (C2 dorsal ramus — cervicogenic
  headache pathway)

Stimulating AH12 via the great auricular nerve activates C2-C3
afferents that enter the dorsal horn at the same segmental level as
cervical spine pain signals — gate control at the segmental level.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard | AH12 颈椎 | ✓ Primary |
| Nogier/European | Cervical vertebrae — lower antihelix body | ✓ CONVERGENT |
| Cervical pain protocols | Top somatotopic point (83% inclusion) | ✓ Standard |
| BFA (Battlefield) | Not included in BFA 5-point protocol | ✗ Absent |
| Body acupoint parallel | GB20 Fēngchí, GV14 Dàzhuī (cervical) | ↔ Functional |

**Relationship to AH13 Neck (颈):** AH12 and AH13 are adjacent at
the same level on the antihelix, but represent different aspects:
AH12 = posterior structures (vertebral column, facet joints, posterior
musculature); AH13 = anterior/lateral structures (throat, thyroid,
anterior cervical musculature). Cervical spondylosis protocols use
AH12; sore throat/thyroid protocols use AH13.

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
Cervical spondylosis (颈椎病, most frequent indication), neck
stiffness, cervical disc herniation, cervicogenic headache, whiplash.
Standard auricular cervical protocol: AH12 + Shen Men TF4 + Subcortex
AT4 ± Sympathetic AH6a ± Occiput AT3.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Chronic cervical pain 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level):**

1. **Sator-Katzenschlager et al. 2003** (Anesthesia & Analgesia,
   97(5):1469-1473, PMID 14570667): Pilot RCT, double-blind, n=21
   (19 completed). Chronic cervical pain (median 48 months duration).
   Points: **cervical spine + Shen Men + Cushion**. Electroacupuncture
   (P-STIM device, 2 mA, 1 Hz) vs needles-only control. Weekly × 6
   weeks. Electrical group: **VAS reduced to <2** vs control VAS <5.
   Psychological well-being, activity, sleep significantly improved.
   Rescue medication significantly lower. Effects maintained at
   **18-week follow-up**. Limitation: small sample, pilot.

2. **Xia et al. 2018** (Pain Medicine, 19(1):193): RCT, n=29, post-
   anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF). Points: **Shen Men
   + Sympathetic + Subcortex + Occiput + Cervical Vertebra**. 4-week
   auricular point acupressure vs sham. Severe/moderate axial neck pain: **28.6% auricular point acupressure vs 85%
   sham** at end of treatment. SF-36 significantly improved.

3. **Noh et al. 2024** (Pain Management Nursing, PMID 39261248): RCT,
   single-blind, n=31 nursing students with cervical pain. Points:
   **Cervical Vertebrae + Shen Men + Shoulder + Liver**. 4 weeks.
   Safe, noninvasive, effective for neck pain reduction.

4. **Park & Suen 2019** (Applied Nursing Research, PMID 30683245):
   RCT, single-blind, sham-controlled, n=48 adults with chronic neck
   pain. 4 weeks. Pain threshold (p=0.003), Neck Disability Index
   (p=0.033), cervical flexion (p=0.004), left rotation (p=0.004) all
   significantly improved. VAS pain: not significant between groups.

**Cervicogenic headache 🔴 No auricular-specific evidence:**
Body acupuncture SR/MA (World Neurosurgery 2024, PMID 38768751)
supports acupuncture for cervicogenic headache, but no study isolates
auricular acupuncture specifically. The segmental convergence mechanism
(C2-C3 great auricular nerve → trigeminocervical complex) provides neuroanatomical
rationale but remains theoretical for auricular intervention.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

AH12 has a UNIQUE dual mechanism combining segmental convergence with
vagal modulation:

**Pathway 1 — Segmental convergence (great auricular nerve C2-C3):**
1. Auricular stimulation at AH12 → great auricular nerve A-beta fiber
   activation
2. C2-C3 afferents enter cervical dorsal horn → converge with cervical
   spine nociceptive afferents at the SAME spinal segments
3. Gate control: non-nociceptive auricular A-beta input activates
   inhibitory interneurons → closes the "gate" on cervical nociceptive
   C-fiber input
4. Trigeminocervical complex (TCC) amplification: C2-C3 input also
   modulates the TCC where trigeminal (V1-V3) and upper cervical
   (C1-C3) afferents converge → may explain cervicogenic headache
   relief potential

**Pathway 2 — Vagal modulation (73% ABVN):**
ABVN → NTS → descending pain inhibition via vlPAG (Zuo 2026 circuit).
Provides supraspinal analgesic layer independent of segmental
convergence.

AH12 is the ONE antihelix point where somatotopic specificity (cervical
spine representation), segmental convergence (C2-C3 great auricular nerve matching
cervical pain pathways), AND vagal access (73% ABVN) all align. This
triple convergence may explain its consistent selection in cervical
pain protocols. The Sator-Katzenschlager 2003 finding (sustained
effects at 18 weeks from 6-week electroacupuncture) supports a
mechanism beyond acute gate control — suggesting longer-term
neuroplastic changes, possibly through vagal-mediated NTS → cortical
remodeling.
→ Cross-ref: Framework note 1 (mixed innervation precision), Framework
note 6 (ABVN gradient within antihelix)

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
Sore throat, thyroid disorders, anterior neck pain, torticollis

⚡ **Framework note 6 — ABVN GRADIENT WITHIN ANTIHELIX: BODY vs
CRURA:** Phase 2 Batch 3 documents a critical innervation gradient
within the antihelix zone that was not distinguished in the child plan.
Peuker & Filler (2002), confirmed by Kaniusas et al. (2019, Frontiers
in Neuroscience, 13:854), report:
- **Antihelix body** (AH9-AH13, spine/trunk): **73% ABVN** — HIGH
  vagal access
- **Crura of antihelix** (AH1-AH5 superior crus, AH7-AH8 inferior
  crus): **9% ABVN** — MINIMAL vagal access

This 8-fold difference means spine/trunk points can leverage the full
dual-pathway analgesic mechanism (vagal ABVN → NTS → vlPAG + somatic
C2-C3 → dorsal horn), while lower limb and buttock/sciatic points
operate primarily through the cervical somatic pathway alone. The
gradient has clinical correlates: AH9 (Lumbosacral, 73% ABVN) and
AH12 (Cervical, 73% ABVN) have the strongest evidence base among
antihelix points, while the crus points (AH1-AH5, AH7-AH8, 9% ABVN)
have minimal individual evidence. The Kawi/Yeh 2025 finding (non-
targeted auricular point acupressure also effective for LBP) is consistent with the high ABVN
at the antihelix body providing zone-general vagal analgesic effects.
This gradient updates the child plan §2 which listed the entire
antihelix as "73% ABVN" without distinguishing body from crura. Butt
et al. 2020 (Journal of Anatomy, 236:588-611, PMID 31742681) noted
inconsistencies in the Peuker & Filler data tables — the exact figures
should be interpreted with caution, but the directional finding (body
>> crura) is consistent across analyses.
→ Cross-ref: Framework note 4 (zone-level vs point-level effects),
Zuo et al. 2026 (ABVN analgesic circuit auricle → JNG → NTS → vlPAG)

## Phase 2 — Antitragus Zone (AT) — Huyệt vùng Đối Nhĩ Bình (对耳屏)

> **Anatomy:** The antitragus (对耳屏) is the small cartilaginous
> projection opposite the tragus, separated from it by the intertragic
> notch. Its curved external (lateral) surface faces outward; its
> flatter internal (medial) surface faces the mastoid area. In the
> inverted fetus model, the antitragus represents the HEAD and BRAIN
> — the most cephalad somatotopic zone on the external ear.
>
> **External surface — cranial vault somatotopy:** Three brain/skull
> regions mapped from anterior to posterior: AT1 Forehead (frontal,
> anterior near intertragic notch), AT2 Temple (temporal, middle),
> AT3 Occiput (occipital, posterior near antihelix junction). These
> form the antitragus "sensory line" — a continuous representation
> of the cranial vault across the convex external surface. Both
> Chinese and Nogier/European systems agree on this arrangement
> (Rong et al. 2016: 37.6% of WFAS ISAAPs based on Nogier 1957).
>
> **Internal (medial) surface:** AT4 Subcortex (皮质下) — on the
> INTERNAL surface of the antitragus, facing away from the observer.
> One of the few auricular points located on a medial ear surface
> rather than the externally visible surface. Represents subcortical
> brain structures (thalamus, hypothalamus, basal ganglia, limbic
> system). Li-Chun Huang's system subdivides AT4 into three subzones:
> Nervous Subcortex (AT12), Digestive Subcortex (AT13), and
> Cardiovascular Subcortex (AT14).
>
> **Innervation — CORRECTION to child plan §2:** The child plan lists
> antitragus innervation as "V3 + C2-C3." Research clarifies:
> - **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3):** PRIMARY innervation (StatPearls
>   NBK538506: "The antihelix, antitragus, tragus, and concha are
>   innervated mainly by the great auricular nerve")
> - **ABVN:** Minor/variable. Peuker & Filler (2002) OMIT the
>   antitragus from their ABVN distribution table — it is not listed
>   among the 6 regions with percentage data. Temple Fay (1927) found
>   vagal contribution to antitragus pain sensation after intracranial
>   nerve section, confirming partial ABVN presence
> - **Auriculotemporal nerve (V3):** NOT a primary supply to the
>   antitragus (StatPearls: ATN innervates "the superomedial helix"
>   and "the anterosuperior and anteromedial areas of the auricle")
>
> **fMRI brainstem target (Frangos et al. 2015, Cakmak 2019):**
> Stimulation of the antitragus zone activated the **nucleus cuneatus**
> (somatosensory relay in the medulla), while cymba conchae
> stimulation activated NTS and locus coeruleus (vagal pathway).
> Cakmak (2019, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 13:421) attributes
> the cuneate activation to the antitragicus muscle (facial nerve CN
> VII). This means antitragus and concha activate DIFFERENT brainstem
> nuclei — zone-determined pathway selection.
> → Cross-ref: B02 §3.3 (ABVN anatomy), Framework note 5 (inter-zone
> specificity)
>
> **Clinical significance:** AT3 (Occiput) is a recognized secondary
> analgesic/sedative point appearing in 67% of insomnia protocols
> (Chen 2007). AT4 (Subcortex) is one of the TOP 3 most-used
> auricular insomnia points (11.1% of all selections, 78.57%
> inclusion in breast cancer insomnia studies). AT4's medial surface
> location overlaps with BFA #2 "Thalamus" — convergence between
> Chinese and US military auricular traditions.

### AT3 — Chẩm (枕 Zhěn, Occiput)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

The most clinically significant point on the external antitragus
surface. Located at the posterior position of the "sensory line,"
corresponding to the occipital lobe and occiput. Appears in 67% of
auricular insomnia protocols (Chen 2007), making it the 3rd most
frequent insomnia point after Shen Men and Heart. Also included in
cervical pain and headache protocols for its C2-C3 segmental
convergence with occipital nerve pathways.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
Posterior portion of the external antitragus surface, near the
junction with the antihelix. Chinese standard: AT3 (枕). Nogier
system: Point 28/29 "Occipital Bone Point" in the postantitragal
fossa. Li-Chun Huang system: AT7 (Occiput). Both Chinese and
European systems agree on placement of the Occiput along the
posterior third of the antitragus.

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Antitragus external surface — great auricular nerve (C2-C3) primary,
ABVN minor/variable. See antitragus zone header.

**Neuroanatomical rationale for "Occiput" designation:**
The great auricular nerve (C2-C3) shares spinal segmental origin
with the greater occipital nerve (C2 dorsal ramus) and lesser
occipital nerve (C2 ventral ramus). Stimulation at AT3 activates
C2-C3 afferents that converge in the trigeminocervical complex (TCC)
with occipital nociceptive afferents — the same segmental convergence
mechanism as AH12 Cervical Vertebrae, but from the antitragus
"brain zone" rather than the antihelix "spine zone."
→ Cross-ref: AH12 (C2-C3 great auricular nerve convergence)

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard | AT3 枕 | ✓ Primary |
| Nogier/European | Occipital Bone Point 28/29 | ✓ Convergent |
| Insomnia protocols | 67% inclusion (Chen 2007, 4/6 studies) | ✓ Standard |
| Cervical pain protocols | Included (Xia 2018) | ✓ Common |
| BFA (Battlefield) | Not included | ✗ Absent |
| Body acupoint parallel | GB20 Fēngchí, GV16 Fēngfǔ (occipital) | ↔ Functional |

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
Occipital headache, dizziness, vertigo, insomnia, sedative/analgesic
effects, convulsions, neck stiffness, motion sickness. Standard
auricular headache protocol: AT1 + AT2 + AT3 (sensory line matching
headache location) + TF4 Shen Men + AT4 Subcortex ± AH6a
Sympathetic.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Tension-type headache 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level):**
**Wang et al. 2025** (BMC Complementary Medicine and Therapies,
PMID 40842010, PMC12369207): RCT, single-blind, n=80 (40+40),
frequent episodic tension-type headache (FETTH). Points: **AT3
Occiput + AT1 Forehead + AT2 Temple + AT4 Subcortex + TF4 Shen Men
+ AH6a Sympathetic**. Auricular acupuncture vs sham (tape without
needle), 4 weeks treatment, 24-week follow-up.
- VAS: **24h P<0.001** (2 vs 3), **2wk P=0.004**, **4wk P=0.015**
- Headache index: **4wk P=0.016** (4 vs 7)
- HAMA anxiety: **4wk P<0.001**, sustained to **24wk P=0.002**
- Cerebral blood flow: **ACA velocity P<0.001, PCA velocity P<0.001**
- Efficacy: **91.67% vs 64.86% (P=0.009)**
- Limitation: sham group also showed therapeutic effect.

**Insomnia 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level, frequency data):**
Chen et al. 2007 (PMID 17718650): SR, 6 RCTs, n=673. AT3 Occiput
used in **4/6 studies (67%)** — 3rd most frequent after Shen Men
(100%) and Heart (83%). Suen et al. 2019 (PMID 31239857): double-
blind RCT, n=147, elderly insomnia. 7-point protocol including AT3
Occiput + AT4 Subcortex. All groups improved (P<0.001). PSG: sleep
efficiency and wake-after-sleep-onset improved with magnetic pellets.

**Cervical pain 🟡 Plausible (as component):**
Xia et al. 2018 (Pain Medicine, 19(1):193): RCT, n=29, post-ACDF.
"Occiput" included in protocol alongside Shen Men, Sympathetic,
Subcortex, Cervical Vertebra. 28.6% severe/moderate pain (auricular point acupressure) vs
85% sham.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

AT3's effects operate through the great auricular nerve (C2-C3),
NOT vagal pathways:

1. **Segmental convergence (C2-C3):** AT3 → great auricular nerve A-beta fibers →
   C2-C3 dorsal horn → convergence with greater occipital nerve (C2)
   afferents in trigeminocervical complex → gate control modulation
   of occipital pain

2. **Cerebrovascular modulation:** Wang 2025 found increased ACA and
   PCA blood flow velocity (both P<0.001) after antitragus "brain
   zone" stimulation — possible C2-C3 → cervical sympathetic
   modulation of cerebrovascular tone

3. **Brainstem target — nucleus cuneatus:** Frangos 2015 fMRI showed
   antitragus → cuneate nucleus (NOT NTS). Consistent with
   somatosensory/cervical pathway, not vagal

### AT4 — Bì Chất Hạ (皮质下 Pízhìxià, Subcortex)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

One of the TOP 3 most frequently selected auricular points for
insomnia — alongside Shen Men (TF4) and Heart (CO15), accounting for
71.70% of all auricular insomnia point selections. Uniquely located
on the INTERNAL (medial) surface of the antitragus. The Chinese name
皮质下 ("below the cortex") reflects the somatotopic assignment to
subcortical brain structures. The "neurasthenia master point"
(神经衰弱主穴) of Chinese auricular acupuncture — the first-line
point for any condition in the neurasthenia spectrum (insomnia,
fatigue, anxiety, poor concentration, emotional instability).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
Internal (medial) surface of the antitragus, at the midpoint of the
lower one-third of the antitragus edge. NOT visible on anterior view
— requires folding the ear forward to access. Chinese standard: AT4
(皮质下). WFAS Standard 002:2013 retained AT4 as one of 93 ISAAPs.

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Internal/medial antitragus surface — great auricular nerve (C2-C3)
primary. The medial surface likely receives slightly more ABVN
contribution than the external surface — Temple Fay (1927) confirmed
vagal contribution to antitragus pain sensation via intracranial
nerve section. Butt et al. (2020, PMID 31742681) note that "the
detailed territory of the ABVN in the posterior aspect of the auricula
has not been mapped in the literature" — the exact ABVN contribution
to the medial antitragus remains an unresolved gap. A 37% ABVN-great auricular nerve
overlap zone exists on the medial dorsal middle third of the ear
(Watanabe et al. 2016).

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard | AT4 皮质下 (Subcortex) | ✓ Primary |
| Nogier/European | Thalamus / Subcortex (among 35 Nogier-origin WFAS points) | ✓ Convergent |
| BFA (Battlefield) | **BFA #2 "Thalamus"** — inner antitragus wall, adjacent to AT4 | ✓ **CONVERGENT** |
| Insomnia triad | Top 3 (Shen Men 17.4% + Heart 14.2% + Subcortex 11.1% = 71.70%) | ✓ Core |
| Pain protocols | Standard analgesic adjunct (86% inclusion in LBP protocols) | ✓ Core |
| NADA protocol | Not included | ✗ Absent |
| Body acupoint parallel | GV20 Bǎihuì, HT7 Shénmén (sedative) | ↔ Functional |

**BFA #2 "Thalamus" — cross-system convergence:** BFA (Niemtzow
2001) identifies a point on the **inner wall of the antitragus**
named "Thalamus" — targeting the thalamic relay of pain signals.
This is ADJACENT to (but not identical with) AT4 Subcortex on the
same medial antitragus surface. Two independently developed systems
(Chinese 2008; US military 2001) convergently target the medial
antitragus for central nervous system modulation. The child plan
§7.2 attributed BFA "Cingulate Gyrus" (#1) to this zone — but BFA
#1 is on the antihelix/helix inner wall; BFA #2 "Thalamus" is the
correct antitragus overlap. Note: Oleson uses the name "Subcortex"
for BFA #1 "Cingulate Gyrus" (on the antihelix wall), creating a
nomenclature overlap with the Chinese AT4 Subcortex (on the
antitragus) — two different structures, same name in different
systems.

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
Insomnia (失眠, most frequent), neurasthenia (神经衰弱), pain
(analgesic adjunct), headache, dizziness, hypertension, cardiac
arrhythmia, dysmenorrhea, anxiety, epilepsy, enuresis, facial
paralysis. The traditional concept: 皮质下 regulates the balance
between cortical excitation and inhibition — calming excess
excitation (insomnia, anxiety) and tonifying deficient excitation
(neurasthenia, fatigue).

Standard insomnia protocol: **TF4 Shen Men + CO15 Heart + AT4
Subcortex** ("insomnia triad"). For pain: somatotopic reflex point +
TF4 Shen Men + AT4 Subcortex.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Insomnia 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level, strong frequency data,
multiple populations):**

1. **Frequency across SRs:** AT4 Subcortex is the **3rd most
   selected** auricular insomnia point. Huang 2025 (Medicine, PMID
   39960920): 15 RCTs breast cancer, **78.57% inclusion** (11/14
   studies). Wang 2026 (Frontiers in Psychiatry, PMID 42052531): 95
   RCTs, 7,628 patients — AT4 at **11.1%** of all point selections.
   Jun 2024 (Frontiers in Sleep, PMID 41424527): 23 RCTs, AT4 among
   7 high-frequency points. Insomnia triad (Shen Men + Heart +
   Subcortex) = **71.70%** of all selections.

2. **Hemodialysis RCT:** Zou et al. 2015 (PLOS ONE, PMC4398355):
   Pilot RCT, n=63. Points: TF4 + AH6a + **AT4** + CO15 + CO18.
   Response: **62.5% vs 32.3% sham (P=0.02)**. Sleep medication use:
   12.5% vs 48.4% at 8 weeks (P<0.01).

3. **Elderly PSG RCT:** Lo et al. 2013 (PMID 23573133): n=27, 7
   points including AT4 + AT3. PSG: sleep efficiency 79.58→84.04%
   (P<0.01), sleep latency 17.57→12.04 min (P=0.02).

4. **Elderly double-blind RCT:** Suen et al. 2019 (PMID 31239857):
   n=147, 3 groups (magnetic vs laser vs combined), 7 points
   including AT4. All groups improved PSQI (P<0.001). Actigraphy:
   magnetic pellet groups showed superior objective sleep improvement.

5. **Breast cancer RCT:** Wang et al. 2022 (PMC9640241): n=68. AT4
   as primary point. PSQI 11.59→7.59 (P<0.001). Total sleep time
   +52 min, sleep onset latency −17 min, efficiency +7%.

6. **Cancer SR/MA:** Wang et al. 2021 (PMID 34691214): 9 RCTs,
   n=688. AT4 in **7/9 studies (78%)**. PSQI MD=−3.88 (P<0.00001).

7. **Contrarian finding:** Zhang et al. 2025 (PMID 41170529): n=99,
   elderly with LBP+insomnia. Non-targeted auricular point acupressure (GI points WITHOUT
   Subcortex) showed GREATER insomnia improvement (18%, P<0.001)
   than targeted auricular point acupressure (WITH Subcortex, 11%, P=0.095 NS) —
   challenging somatotopic specificity for insomnia.

**Depression 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level):**
**Rodrigues et al. 2023** (JAMA Network Open, DOI: 10.1001/
jamanetworkopen.2023.45138): RCT, n=74. 6 points including AT4
Subcortex. **46% remission vs 13% control**. 58% achieved ≥50%
PHQ-9 improvement. Recovery comparable to pharmacological treatment.

**COVID anxiety/depression:** Cai et al. 2022 (PMID 36545041):
Single-blind RCT, n=68, isolated COVID patients. 4 points including
AT4. Depression SDS: SMD −2.79 (P=0.002). Anxiety SAS: SMD −4.46
(P<0.001).

**Pain 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level):**
AT4 is the standard analgesic adjunct point used alongside
somatotopic reflex points + Shen Men. Appears in 86% of LBP
auricular protocols. Wang 2025: tension-type headache protocol
(see AT3 entry). BFA 5-point protocol (which includes "Thalamus" on
medial antitragus): SR/MA 9 RCTs n=692 showed improvement in pain
scores and effects on activity/mood/stress (Tan 2021, PMID
33375924), though overall comparison vs controls was not significant.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

AT4's extensive clinical use (insomnia triad, pain adjunct, BFA
overlap) contrasts with its minimal ABVN innervation. Three possible
pathways:

1. **Reticular formation modulation (via C2-C3):** great auricular nerve afferents →
   C2-C3 dorsal horn → brainstem reticular formation → ascending
   reticular activating system (ARAS). The ARAS is the primary
   regulator of sleep-wake transitions. C2-C3 input could modulate
   arousal independently of the vagal pathway.

2. **Thalamic gating:** Zhao et al. 2023 (PMID 37483453): taVNS
   decreased resting-state functional connectivity between the
   thalamus and ACC/precuneus — direct evidence of thalamic
   modulation. AT4's name (Subcortex) targets thalamic relay function.

3. **Limbic/pain matrix activation:** Romoli et al. 2014
   (Neurological Sciences): fMRI pilot, n=6. Antitragus (brain stem
   point) stimulation activated **amygdala, anterior cingulate cortex,
   and cerebellum** — limbic and pain-processing regions, consistent
   with sedative/analgesic effects.

The Zhang 2025 contrarian finding (non-specific ear points equally
effective for insomnia) suggests AT4's mechanism may be primarily
zone-general (auricular stimulation modulates arousal via brainstem
pathways regardless of specific point) rather than point-specific.
This is consistent with Framework note 4 (zone-level vs point-level
effects) and raises the possibility that AT4's consistent protocol
inclusion reflects clinical convention rather than demonstrated
point-specific superiority.
→ Cross-ref: Framework notes 4, 5; Romoli 2014; Zhao 2023

## Phase 2 — Tragus Zone (TG) — Huyệt vùng Nhĩ Bình (耳屏)

> **Anatomy:** The tragus (耳屏) is the small, flattened cartilaginous
> projection that partially covers and protects the opening of the
> external auditory canal. The Chinese standard (GB/T 13734-2008)
> divides the tragus into 4 zones: TG1 Superior Tragus (upper
> external), TG2 Inferior Tragus (lower external), TG3 Pharynx
> (upper internal), TG4 Internal Nose (lower internal). Additional
> named points sit at zone borders: External Nose (外鼻, between
> TG1/TG2), Tragus Apex (屏尖, upper free-edge tip), and Adrenal
> (肾上腺, lower free-edge tip near TG2).
>
> **Nomenclature note:** The child plan §3.5 maps TG1 = "Superior
> Tragus / Adrenal," TG2 = "External Nose," TG3 = "Tragus Apex,"
> TG4 = "Inferior Tragus / Thirst Point." Some research agents found
> that the Adrenal point (肾上腺) may be coded TG2p (a sub-point of
> the inferior tragus zone) rather than TG1 in the GB/T standard.
> This atlas follows the child plan convention, acknowledging the
> potential discrepancy.
>
> **ABVN innervation — 45% (MODERATE):** Peuker & Filler (2002)
> report 45% ABVN at the tragus. However, Burger & Verkuil (2018,
> Brain Stimulation, 11(4):945-946) noted an internal contradiction:
> the table lists 45% ABVN, but the main text describes tragus
> innervation as "the greater auricular nerve (45%) or the
> auriculotemporal nerve (9%), or both (46%)" with NO mention of
> ABVN. This discrepancy remains unresolved. The tragus receives
> mixed innervation from ABVN + auriculotemporal nerve (V3) + great
> auricular nerve (C2-C3).
>
> **taVNS at the tragus:** The tragus is one of the two main sites
> for transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS),
> alongside the cymba conchae. fMRI confirms tragus stimulation
> activates NTS and locus coeruleus, though weaker than cymba
> (Yakunina et al. 2017, Neuromodulation, 20(3):290-300; Badran et
> al. 2018, Brain Stimulation, 11(3):492-500). The inner (medial)
> tragus surface is the preferred active site; the outer tragus has
> been used as control (Frontiers in Neuroscience 2021, PMC8005577).
> Clancy et al. 2014 (Brain Stimulation): tragus stimulation → ~20%
> HRV increase + ~50% MSNA reduction.
>
> **Clinical significance:** TG1 (Adrenal) is the most clinically
> important tragus point — primary in allergic rhinitis protocols
> (60% inclusion). TG2 (External Nose) also appears frequently in
> rhinitis protocols. The tragus zone bridges vagal (ABVN) and
> somatic (V3 + C2-C3) pathways.
> → Cross-ref: B02 §3.3 (ABVN anatomy), Framework note 1 (mixed
> innervation)

### TG1 — Thượng Bình / Thận Thượng Tuyến (上屏 Shàngpíng / 肾上腺 Shènshàngxiàn, Superior Tragus / Adrenal)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

The most clinically important tragus point, known primarily by its
functional name "Adrenal" (肾上腺). Used across diverse protocols
for anti-inflammatory, anti-allergic, and emergency properties. The
"Adrenal" designation reflects traditional auricular cartography
(Nogier/Chinese somatotopic conventions), NOT demonstrated hormonal
specificity — no study has shown measurable cortisol or catecholamine
changes from stimulating this specific point. Where cortisol has been
measured after auricular/vagal stimulation, the direction is
SUPPRESSIVE (Cuberos Paredes 2025, Physiological Reports: taVNS
inhibits stress-induced cortisol), not activating — mechanistically
opposite to what "adrenal stimulation" would imply.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
Superior portion of the tragus external surface. Chinese standard:
TG1 (上屏). Clinical designation: 肾上腺 (Shènshàngxiàn, Adrenal
Gland).

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Tragus — 45% ABVN (with Burger & Verkuil 2018 caveat) +
auriculotemporal nerve (V3) + great auricular nerve (C2-C3). See
tragus zone header. fMRI confirms NTS activation from tragus
(Badran 2018), supporting functional ABVN engagement despite
mixed innervation.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard | TG1 上屏 / 肾上腺 | ✓ Primary |
| Nogier/European | Adrenal / ACTH zone (Cortisone Point) | ~ Approximate |
| BFA (Battlefield) | Not included | ✗ Absent |
| NADA protocol | Not included | ✗ Absent |
| Allergic rhinitis protocols | **60% inclusion** (9/15, Cheng 2024) | ✓ Core |
| Body acupoint parallel | LI4 Hégǔ, LI11 Qūchí (anti-inflammatory) | ↔ Functional |

**Relationship to CO18 Endocrine:** Both claim endocrine function but
from different zones: CO18 = intertragic notch, broader hormonal
regulation; TG1 = upper tragus, specifically adrenal/anti-
inflammatory. Data-mining analysis (Li 2026, PMID 41868298): TG1
and CO18 show the **strongest pairwise association (lift = 3.15)**
of any two auricular points — commonly used together as
complementary endocrine regulators.

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
Anti-inflammatory conditions, allergic conditions (rhinitis, asthma,
allergic diathesis), rheumatic diseases, functional circulatory
disorders, hypotension (emergency use), Addison's disease, Cushing's
syndrome. Described as "strengthening the Kidney point's function"
and "controlling adrenal stress-hormone output."

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Allergic rhinitis 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level, strong frequency):**
**Cheng et al. 2024** (Heliyon, 10(4):e25181, PMID 38370208):
Meta-analysis, 15 RCTs, n=1,002. Point frequency: Lung 9/15,
Kidney 9/15, **Adrenal 9/15 (60%)**, External Nose 9/15, Internal
Nose 7/15, Shen Men 7/15. TG1 tied for most frequently selected
point in allergic rhinitis protocols.

**Pain protocols 🔴 Secondary/adjunctive:**
Li et al. 2026 (PMID 41868298): 36 knee OA studies, n=3,105. TG1
NOT in top 6. Strongest pairwise association (lift=3.15) with
Endocrine and Shen Men — "adjunctive functional/regulator" role.

**Anti-inflammatory biomarkers 🔴 No point-specific evidence:**
Two well-cited auricular anti-inflammatory RCTs (Lin 2015, PMID
26170869; Chung 2011, PMID 21251313) did NOT include TG1 Adrenal —
using Shen Men, Sympathetic, and Subcortex instead. Anti-
inflammatory effects from auricular stimulation operate through the
vagal cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway (ABVN → NTS → efferent
vagus → α7nAChR → suppression of TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6), strongest
at concha zone (100% ABVN), not tragus (45% contested).

**Cortisol/catecholamines 🔴 No point-specific evidence:**
No study measures cortisol or catecholamines from stimulating TG1.
Cuberos Paredes 2025 (Physiological Reports): taVNS INHIBITS
stress-induced cortisol release — opposite to "adrenal activation."
Nguyen 2020 (PMID 33273952): auricular EA reduced serum NA/adrenaline
but stimulated CO15+TF4, NOT TG1.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

TG1's "Adrenal" designation reflects clinical tradition rather than
demonstrated mechanism. The best-supported pathway:

1. **Vagal anti-inflammatory (45% ABVN):** ABVN → NTS → efferent
   vagal outflow → cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway (CAP) →
   α7nAChR on splenic macrophages → TNF-α/IL-1β/IL-6 suppression.
   fMRI confirms NTS activation from tragus (Badran 2018). This is
   a PARASYMPATHETIC anti-inflammatory effect — NOT adrenal cortisol
   activation.

2. **Condition-dependent role:** Primary in allergic rhinitis (60%
   inclusion, anti-allergic/anti-inflammatory), but secondary in
   pain protocols (not top 6). This pattern suggests TG1's anti-
   allergic function (vagal anti-inflammatory pathway) is its
   strongest contribution.

The "Adrenal" name is best characterized as a traditional
somatotopic assignment from mid-20th-century auricular cartography,
not a description of demonstrated physiological action. Where
measured, auricular/vagal stimulation produces parasympathetic/
anti-inflammatory effects, not sympathetic/adrenal activation.

⚡ **Framework note 7 — ANTITRAGUS INNERVATION CORRECTION AND
BRAINSTEM TARGET DIFFERENTIATION:** Phase 2 Batch 4 documents two
findings that update the auricular innervation model:

(1) **Innervation correction:** The child plan §2 lists antitragus
innervation as "V3 + C2-C3." Research clarifies: (a) great auricular
nerve (C2-C3) is the PRIMARY innervation (StatPearls NBK538506);
(b) ABVN contribution is minor/variable — Peuker & Filler (2002)
OMIT the antitragus from their ABVN distribution table; (c)
auriculotemporal nerve (V3) does NOT directly supply the antitragus
— V3 innervates the tragus and anterosuperior structures.

(2) **Brainstem target differentiation:** Frangos et al. (2015,
Brain Stimulation, 8(3):624-636) fMRI found antitragus stimulation
activated the **nucleus cuneatus** (somatosensory relay), while
cymba conchae stimulation activated NTS and locus coeruleus (vagal
pathway). Cakmak (2019, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 13:421)
attributes cuneate activation to the antitragicus muscle (CN VII).
Different auricular ZONES activate different BRAINSTEM NUCLEI:
- **Cymba conchae** (100% ABVN) → NTS, locus coeruleus, dorsal
  raphe → vagal/parasympathetic network
- **Tragus** (45% ABVN) → NTS (weaker) + bilateral ACC/frontal
  cortex (Badran 2018) → mixed vagal + somatosensory
- **Antitragus** (minimal ABVN) → nucleus cuneatus →
  somatosensory/proprioceptive pathway
- **Antihelix body** (73% ABVN) → NTS → vlPAG (Zuo 2026 circuit)

This zone-to-brainstem mapping extends Framework notes 4 (zone-level
effects), 5 (inter-zone NTS specificity), and 6 (ABVN gradient) by
adding brainstem nucleus selection as a zone-dependent variable. Each
zone has a characteristic "brainstem fingerprint" determined by its
dominant nerve supply.
→ Cross-ref: Framework notes 4, 5, 6; Frangos 2015; Cakmak 2019;
Badran 2018; Zuo 2026

## Phase 2 — Triangular Fossa Zone (TF) — Huyệt vùng Tam Giác Oa (三角窝)

> **Anatomy:** The triangular fossa (三角窝) is the depression between
> the superior crus and inferior crus of the antihelix — a shallow,
> roughly triangular concavity. In the inverted fetus model, the
> triangular fossa represents the PELVIS and LOWER ABDOMEN, including
> internal genitalia, uterus, and pelvic organs. This is the only ear
> zone specifically mapping the reproductive system.
>
> **TF4 Shen Men** — already documented in Phase 1 (see above). The
> most studied auricular point, located at the lateral third of the
> triangular fossa where the two crura diverge. TF4's calming/analgesic
> function TRANSCENDS the pelvic somatotopy — one of several points
> whose clinical effects do not match their somatotopic assignment.
>
> **Innervation (from TF4 entry):** Auriculotemporal nerve (V3)
> PRIMARY at the triangular fossa. ABVN individually VARIABLE — the
> triangular fossa is adjacent to the antihelix (73% ABVN) and near
> the cymba conchae (100% ABVN) but is not pure ABVN territory. Great
> auricular nerve (C2-C3) contributes posteriorly. Peuker & Filler
> (2002) did not specifically quantify triangular fossa ABVN percentage.
> Child plan §2 rates vagal access as "LOW-MODERATE."
>
> **Nomenclature note — TF1/TF2 coding discrepancy:** Different
> reference systems assign different zone codes to the triangular fossa
> points. In the child plan (following one GB/T interpretation): TF1 =
> 子宫 (Uterus), TF2 = 神门上 (Upper Shen Men area). In some
> systematic reviews (Kong et al. 2023): TF2 = 内生殖器 (Internal
> Genitalia), treating it as the primary gynecological point. The
> functional areas "Uterus" and "Internal Genitalia" are adjacent or
> overlapping in the anterior triangular fossa. This atlas follows the
> child plan convention: TF1 = Uterus.
>
> **Clinical significance:** TF1 Uterus / Internal Genitalia is the
> #1 most-used auricular point in dysmenorrhea protocols — appearing
> in essentially ALL studies reviewed by Kong et al. (2023, 35 RCTs,
> n=3,960). The triangular fossa concentrates gynecological function
> in a small anatomical area: TF1 (Uterus), TF2/upper area (genital),
> TF3 (Pelvic Cavity), with TF4 (Shen Men) providing the calming/
> analgesic foundation that most gynecological protocols require.
> → Cross-ref: TF4 Shen Men entry (Phase 1, above); B02 §3.3 (ABVN
> anatomy); Framework note 1

### TF1 — Tử Cung (子宫 Zǐgōng, Uterus)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

The primary gynecological auricular point. "Uterus" (子宫) maps to
the anterior portion of the triangular fossa, representing the
uterus and internal reproductive organs in the inverted fetus model.
Ranked #1 in dysmenorrhea point selection across 35 RCTs (Kong et al.
2023) — appearing more frequently than Shen Men, Endocrine, or any
other auricular point. Also used in labor pain management and
reproductive health protocols.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
Anterior portion of the triangular fossa, near the junction with the
inferior crus of the antihelix. Chinese standard: TF1 (子宫). Some
references code the equivalent area as TF2 内生殖器 (Internal
Genitalia) — see zone header nomenclature note. WHO: MA-TF (specific
sub-code varies by source).

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Triangular fossa — auriculotemporal nerve (V3) primary, ABVN
variable, great auricular nerve (C2-C3) contributes posteriorly. See
triangular fossa zone header and TF4 entry for detailed innervation.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard | TF1 子宫 / TF2 内生殖器 | ✓ Primary (code varies) |
| Nogier/European | Pelvic apex (NOT uterus — see note) | ⚠ DIVERGENT |
| Dysmenorrhea protocols | **#1 most-used point** (36/35 studies, Kong 2023) | ✓ Core |
| Labor protocols | Included (Eslami 2025) | ✓ Standard |
| Body acupoint parallel | CV3 Zhōngjí, CV4 Guānyuán, SP6 Sānyīnjiāo | ↔ Functional |

**Nogier divergence:** One of the most significant Chinese-European
mapping disagreements. Nogier maps the LOWER EXTREMITY (hip, knee,
ankle) to the triangular fossa, NOT reproductive organs. In the
European system, the pelvis is only at the acute angle where the
antihelical crura meet; internal genital organs are placed under the
helix root (IH3 zone). The Chinese system's placement of Uterus in
the triangular fossa has NO European parallel.

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
Dysmenorrhea, irregular menstruation, uterine prolapse, functional
uterine bleeding, leukorrhea, infertility (female). Also used in
labor pain management and postpartum recovery. The primary point for
all gynecological auricular protocols — combined with Endocrine
(CO18), Shen Men (TF4), Kidney (CO10), Liver (CO12), and Sympathetic
(AH6a) in varying combinations.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Dysmenorrhea 🟡 Plausible (strong frequency, large SR/MA):**
**Kong et al. 2023** (Frontiers in Endocrinology, PMID 36686444):
Systematic review and meta-analysis, 35 RCTs, n=3,960. "Internal
Genitals" (TF2 in this source, functionally = TF1 Uterus) was the
#1 most-used auricular point, appearing in 36/35 studies (some used
multiple point lists). VAS pain reduction: MD = −1.45 (95% CI:
[−1.73, −1.17]), P<0.00001, I²=67%. Cure rate vs analgesics: OR =
3.28 (95% CI: [1.37, 7.85]), P=0.008. Total effective rate vs
analgesics: OR = 5.98 (95% CI: [3.99, 8.97]), P<0.00001, I²=7%.
However: auricular acupuncture alone vs other established therapies (Chinese herbs,
body acupuncture, moxibustion) showed NO significant superiority
(P=0.31 to P=0.85) — suggesting auricular acupressure is
EQUIVALENT to but not SUPERIOR to other traditional methods.
Limitation: most studies high risk of bias for blinding, limited
placebo-controlled data (only 4/35 used sham control), most
published in Chinese.

**Labor pain 🟡 Plausible (2 RCTs):**
**Eslami et al. 2025** (Revista Brasileira de Ginecologia e
Obstetrícia, PMID 41098670): Double-blind RCT, n=60 primiparous
women. 11-point protocol including Uterus. VAS at 8 cm dilation:
5.60±1.22 vs 7.93±1.43, P<0.0001. Labor duration: 70% <6 hours
vs 73.3% >6 hours, P<0.0001.
**Abedi et al. 2017** (Journal of Family & Reproductive Health,
PMID 30288164): RCT, n=80 nulliparous women. 8-point protocol
including Uterus. Active phase duration: 176.25±101 min vs
342.8±87.2 min, P<0.001. Normal delivery without perineal trauma:
50% vs 2.5%, P<0.001. Neonatal Apgar 7-10 at 1 min: 97.5% vs
55%, P<0.001. Multi-point protocols — individual contributions
not isolated.

**IVF/infertility 🟡 Plausible (single RCT, strong effect):**
**Qu et al. 2014** (Scientific Reports, PMID 24848522):
Prospective 3-arm RCT, n=305 infertile women with tubal blockage
(auricular acupuncture=101, sham-auricular acupuncture=102, control=102). Points: Shen Men (TF4),
Endocrine (CO18), Internal Genitals (TF2 in this source). Live
birth rate: auricular acupuncture 52.48% vs sham 31.37% vs control 30.39%, P<0.017.
Clinical pregnancy rate: auricular acupuncture 58.42% vs sham 40.20%, P<0.017.
Mechanism: increased neuropeptide Y (NPY) in follicular fluids.
Multi-point protocol — TF1/TF2 contribution not isolated from
Shen Men and Endocrine effects.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**
The triangular fossa's V3 (auriculotemporal) innervation projects
to the trigeminal sensory nuclear complex, which has connections to
autonomic brainstem nuclei including NTS. Proposed pathway for
uterine effects: trigeminal afferent → brainstem autonomic centers
→ descending modulation of pelvic autonomic outflow (S2-S4
parasympathetic, T10-L2 sympathetic innervation to uterus). The
variable ABVN contribution at this location may provide additional
vagal pathway access in some individuals. Kong et al. (2023) note
that auricular acupressure can "reduce tension and contraction of
uterine" through autonomic modulation of uterine smooth muscle
tone via brainstem-mediated pathways. No direct neuroimaging
evidence for this specific point.

### TF2 — Thượng Thần Môn (神门上, Upper Shen Men Area)

**Evidence tier: C**

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

## Phase 2 — Scaphoid Fossa Zone (SF) — Huyệt vùng Chu Thuyền Oa (舟状窝)

> **Anatomy:** The scaphoid fossa (舟状窝, also called scapha) is the
> narrow, elongated groove between the antihelix and the helix rim. In
> the inverted fetus model, the scaphoid fossa represents the UPPER
> LIMB — mapped in sequence from superior to inferior: SF1 Fingers
> (near Darwin's tubercle) → SF2 Wrist → SF3 Elbow → SF4 Shoulder
> (spanning areas 4-5) → SF5 Shoulder Joint → SF6 Clavicle. The
> WFAS standard (2012) divides the scaphoid fossa into 6 equal
> segments for point localization. SF4 Shoulder occupies the largest
> area (2 segments), reflecting the clinical importance of the
> shoulder zone.
>
> **Innervation — CORRECTION to child plan §2:** The child plan lists
> scaphoid fossa innervation as "V3 primary, LOW ABVN." Peuker &
> Filler (2002, 14 ears, 7 cadavers) found the scaphoid fossa is
> innervated **100% by the great auricular nerve (C2-C3)**. No ABVN
> was found in any specimen, and no auriculotemporal nerve (V3)
> innervation was documented at the scapha. This means the scaphoid
> fossa has:
> - **ZERO vagal access** — same as the lobule
> - **ZERO trigeminal access** — unlike the triangular fossa or tragus
> - **PURE cervical plexus** innervation (C2-C3)
>
> This is the second major innervation correction in this atlas (the
> first being antitragus in Batch 4, Framework note 7). Effects of
> stimulating SF points must operate ENTIRELY through cervical spinal
> mechanisms — there is no direct brainstem access via cranial nerves.
> → See Framework note 8 below.
>
> **Somatotopic mismatch — the "segmental gap":** The scaphoid fossa
> innervation (C2-C3) does NOT match the spinal segments of the body
> parts it somatotopically represents: fingers (C6-C8), wrist (C6-C7),
> elbow (C5-C6), shoulder (C4-C6). A 2-4 segment gap separates the
> ear's nerve supply from the target innervation. Possible bridging
> mechanisms: (1) partial overlap at C3-C4 via phrenic/supraclavicular
> nerves; (2) propriospinal interneurons spanning C2-C6; (3)
> supraspinal descending inhibition via PAG-RVM pathway (non-
> segmental); (4) diffuse noxious inhibitory controls (DNIC); (5)
> direct somatotopic cortical activation (Okorie et al. 2024 fNIRS).
>
> **Somatotopic mismatch — the "map displacement":** Lovato et al.
> (2023, PMID 36920326) tested auricular points for supraspinatus
> tendinopathy using the Needle Contact Test (NCT) and found the most
> responsive points in **SF1-SF2** (upper scaphoid fossa, mapped as
> finger/wrist), NOT in SF4-5 (the standard "Shoulder" zone). This
> suggests the clinically active zone may not precisely match the
> textbook somatotopic map. The French/Nogier system also consolidated
> from three shoulder points to a single point at a different location
> than the Chinese SF4-5. Both the segmental gap and the map
> displacement question the precision of strict somatotopic models.
>
> **Neuroimaging support despite these gaps:** Okorie et al. (2024,
> bioRxiv, PMID 39345597) used high-density fNIRS (107 channels) in
> 17 healthy adults and found that stimulating the shoulder auricular
> point activated **medial S1** (primary somatosensory cortex),
> while thumb stimulation activated **lateral S1** — consistent with
> the cortical homunculus. Peak T=6.1 for shoulder > thumb contrast
> (q=5.6E-7). Spatial separation between peak channels: 63.6 mm.
> This is the first neuroimaging evidence for auricular somatotopic
> cortical mapping, suggesting a direct thalamocortical pathway
> that bypasses segmental spinal mechanisms entirely.
>
> **Clinical significance:** SF4 Shoulder is the most clinically
> important scaphoid fossa point. One dedicated RCT explicitly used
> SF4-5 for post-cesarean shoulder pain (Abedini 2020, PMID 32980559,
> n=90, P<0.001 at 24h). However, BFA's 5-point protocol does NOT
> include any somatotopic SF points — and BFA showed no benefit over
> PT alone for post-shoulder-surgery pain (Crowell 2025, PMID
> 39797512, n=88). The evidence gap between somatotopic-targeted and
> generic auricular analgesia for shoulder conditions is unresolved.
> → Cross-ref: B02 §3.3 (ABVN anatomy); Framework notes 7, 8

### SF4 — Kiên (肩 Jiān, Shoulder)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

The standard anatomical correspondence point for shoulder pain in
the auricular microsystem. WFAS MA-SF4, spanning Scaphoid Fossa
Areas 4-5 (the largest single-point area in the scaphoid fossa).
Despite being the designated "Shoulder" point, clinical evidence
is remarkably sparse: only one RCT (Abedini 2020) explicitly
used SF4-5 for shoulder pain. The Lovato 2023 NCT study found
more responsive points in SF1-SF2. BFA's 5-point protocol does
NOT include SF4 — and BFA for post-shoulder surgery showed no
benefit (Crowell 2025). This creates a tension between somatotopic
tradition and empirical validation.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
Lower portion of the scaphoid fossa, spanning SF Areas 4 and 5.
Chinese standard: SF4 (肩, Shoulder) / MA-SF4. The Shoulder point
occupies 2 of 6 scaphoid fossa segments, making it the largest zone
in this region. SF5 Shoulder Joint and SF6 Clavicle are immediately
inferior.

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Scaphoid fossa — **great auricular nerve (C2-C3) exclusively**
(Peuker & Filler 2002). See zone header for innervation correction.
No ABVN, no V3. Effects must operate through cervical spinal
mechanisms and/or supraspinal pathways (PAG-RVM descending
inhibition, somatotopic cortical activation).

**The segmental gap:** Shoulder joint = C4-C6 (suprascapular nerve
C5-C6, axillary nerve C5-C6, supraclavicular nerves C3-C4). Ear
point = C2-C3 (great auricular nerve). A 1-3 segment gap exists.
Partial bridging via C3 root overlap (shared between great auricular nerve and
phrenic/supraclavicular nerves) and supraspinal PAG-RVM pathway.
Okorie 2024 fNIRS data suggests direct cortical somatotopic
activation as an additional mechanism — see zone header.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard (WFAS) | SF4 肩 / MA-SF4, Areas 4-5 | ✓ Primary |
| Nogier 1957 original | 3 shoulder points (Shoulder, Joint, Clavicle) | ✓ Similar |
| Nogier-Bahr (revised) | Single point at C7/T1 transition | ~ Modified |
| BFA (Battlefield) | **NOT included** (5 fixed points only) | ✗ Absent |
| Lovato 2023 NCT | SF1-SF2 more responsive than SF4-5 | ⚠ Discrepant |

| Body acupoint parallel | LI15 Jiānyú, GB21 Jiānjǐng, SI9 Jiānzhēn | ↔ Functional |

shoulder representation is in the scaphoid fossa (one of the

preserves Nogier's original 1957 three-point layout (Shoulder,
Shoulder Joint, Clavicle). The modern European system consolidated

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
Periarthritis of the shoulder (frozen shoulder), shoulder pain,
rotator cuff disorders, shoulder impingement. Standard combination:
Shoulder (SF4) + Shen Men (TF4) + Subcortex (AT4) + Sympathetic
(AH6a), with Liver (CO12) and Kidney (CO10) as supplementary
organ points (from musculoskeletal protocol patterns across data-
mining studies).

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Post-cesarean shoulder pain 🟡 Plausible (single RCT, positive):**
**Abedini et al. 2020** (Journal of Acupuncture and Meridian
Studies, PMID 32980559): RCT, n=90 (45 per group). Auricular
points: **Shoulder (SF4-5)** + Muscle Relaxation. Vaccaria seeds
applied bilaterally 2h pre-surgery through 24h post-cesarean.
Control: seeds on non-specific ear points. Results: at 24h, NPRS
2.04±2.07 vs 4.82±3.40 (P<0.001). Painless at 24h: 62.3% vs
31.1% (P=0.008). No difference at 1h or 6h. This is the ONLY
RCT found that explicitly used the standard SF4-5 Shoulder point
for shoulder pain. Mechanism: post-cesarean shoulder pain is
referred pain via phrenic nerve (C3-C5) from diaphragmatic
irritation by CO2 pneumoperitoneum — shares C3 root overlap with
the great auricular nerve, providing partial segmental rationale.

**Post-shoulder-surgery BFA 🔴 Negative (BFA without SF4):**
**Crowell et al. 2025** (Military Medicine, PMID 39797512):
Single-blind RCT, n=88 (43 BFA, 45 control), US Military Academy.
Standard 5-point BFA (Cingulate Gyrus, Thalamus, Omega 2, Point
Zero, Shen Men — NO SF4). Average pain VAS at 4 weeks: BFA
11.1mm vs control 10.9mm (P=0.95). Opioid use: 5.1 pills vs 4.4
(P=0.63). No benefit at any timepoint (all P>0.50). BFA does not
include somatotopic points — this negative result may reflect the
absence of SF4 rather than auricular acupuncture's inefficacy for
shoulder pain. Alternatively, BFA's non-specific analgesic targets
may not add value beyond standard rehabilitation.

**Supraspinatus tendinopathy NCT 🟡 Plausible (but map mismatch):**
**Lovato et al. 2023** (European Journal of Translational Myology,
PMID 36920326): Controlled trial, n=45 (30 treatment, 15 sham).
NCT for supraspinatus tendinopathy. Treatment group: 55% pain
reduction at 1 min (NPRS 6.4→2.9, Cohen's d=1.190), ROM abduction
110.7°→167.7° (P<0.0001, d=2.674). However: the responsive
points clustered in **SF1-SF2** (18 in SF2, 12 in SF1), NOT in
SF4-5. This study supports auricular analgesia for shoulder pain
but questions the standard somatotopic localization.

**Frozen shoulder 3-arm RCT 🟡 Plausible (partial):**
**Lovato et al. 2023** (Topics in Geriatric Rehabilitation):
3-arm RCT, n=116 (physiotherapy vs local acupuncture vs auricular
acupuncture). Physiotherapy was superior for abduction and flexion
ROM (all P<0.001). For external and internal rotation: NO
significant difference among the 3 groups — auricular acupuncture
was COMPARABLE to physiotherapy and body acupuncture for rotational
movements.

**Musculoskeletal pain — general auricular evidence:**
Perioperative pain SR/MA (2025, Journal of Pain Research): 21 RCTs,
n=1,527. Pain MD=−0.44 [−0.72, −0.17]. Analgesic SMD=−0.88
[−1.29, −0.46]. Orthopedic surgery subgroup showed significant
effect. PEACE trial (Mao et al. 2021, JAMA Oncology, PMID
33734288): 360 cancer survivors, BFA Cohen's d=0.83 vs usual care.
Shoulder/arm was 10.3% of sample; proximal joint pain responded
better (64.2%) than distal (20%, P=0.008).

**Cơ chế giải thích:**
**Multi-level mechanism (5 tiers):**
1. **Partial segmental overlap (C3-C4):** The C3 root contributes
   to both the great auricular nerve (ear) and the phrenic/
   supraclavicular nerves (shoulder). Dorsal horn convergence at
   C3-C4 allows cross-modulation — same mechanism as Kehr's sign
   (diaphragm irritation → referred shoulder pain).
2. **Propriospinal interneurons (C2-C6):** Intersegmental
   connections allow input at C2-C3 to modulate processing at
   C4-C6, bridging the segmental gap.
3. **Trigeminocervical complex (TCC):** The C1-C2 dorsal horn
   integrates cervical and trigeminal afferents with extensive
   connections to lower segments and supraspinal structures.
4. **Supraspinal PAG-RVM pathway (dominant mechanism):**
   Acupuncture at any point activates the periaqueductal gray →
   rostral ventromedial medulla → dorsal horn at ALL segmental
   levels. Non-segmental endogenous opioid release (beta-endorphin,
   enkephalin, serotonin, noradrenaline). This is the most likely
   primary mechanism for non-local auricular analgesia.
5. **Somatotopic cortical activation (Okorie 2024):** fNIRS
   confirms that shoulder auricular stimulation directly activates
   medial S1 cortex — consistent with homunculus mapping. This
   suggests a thalamocortical pathway that bypasses segmental
   spinal mechanisms entirely. Mechanism may involve top-down
   cortical modulation of pain processing via corticospinal
   projections back to the dorsal horn.

⚡ **Framework note 8 — SCAPHOID FOSSA INNERVATION CORRECTION +
SOMATOTOPIC MECHANISM GAP:**

**(a) Innervation correction:** Child plan §2 lists scaphoid fossa
innervation as "V3 primary, LOW ABVN." Peuker & Filler (2002, 14
ears) found the scaphoid fossa is innervated **100% by the great
auricular nerve (C2-C3)** — no ABVN, no V3. This means the
scaphoid fossa has ZERO vagal access (same as the lobule, which
the child plan correctly notes as "0% ABVN"). Effects of
stimulating SF points must operate ENTIRELY through cervical spinal
mechanisms, propriospinal connections, or supraspinal pathways.

**(b) Somatotopic mechanism gap:** The SF points represent the
upper limb (C6-C8 fingers through C4-C6 shoulder), but the ear's
nerve supply is C2-C3. This 2-4 segment gap cannot be explained
by segmental convergence alone. Five potential bridging mechanisms:
(1) C3 root overlap with phrenic/supraclavicular (partial); (2)
propriospinal interneurons; (3) trigeminocervical complex; (4)
supraspinal PAG-RVM descending inhibition (the most likely dominant
mechanism for non-local analgesia); (5) somatotopic cortical
activation (Okorie 2024 fNIRS: shoulder auricular point → medial
S1, consistent with cortical homunculus).

**(c) Map displacement (Lovato 2023):** NCT for supraspinatus
tendinopathy found active points in SF1-SF2 (upper scaphoid fossa,
mapped as fingers/wrist), NOT in SF4-5 (standard Shoulder zone).
The clinically reactive zone does not precisely match the textbook
somatotopic map. This finding aligns with Romoli's observation
that effective points correspond more to the Nogier/European
system's broader representation than the narrowly defined Chinese
standard zones.

This framework note extends notes 4 (zone-level effects), 6 (ABVN
gradient), and 7 (antitragus innervation correction + brainstem
target differentiation) by adding a second major innervation
correction and explicitly addressing the somatotopic mechanism gap
— the most fundamental challenge to strict point-specific auricular
theory.
→ Cross-ref: Framework notes 4, 6, 7; Peuker & Filler 2002;
Okorie et al. 2024; Lovato et al. 2023

## Phase 2 — Helix Zone (HX) — Vòng Loa Tai (耳轮)

The helix is the outer rim of the auricle, curving from the helix
root (crus helicis) superiorly and posteriorly around the ear. In
the Chinese standard (GB/T 13734-2008), 12 points are mapped along
the helix in three functional groups: (1) urogenital/perineal
series (HX2-HX5) on the superior helix, (2) ear apex and tubercle
area (HX6-HX8), and (3) numbered helix segments (HX9-HX12) on the
descending helix toward the lobule.

**HX1 (Ear Center / Point Zero)** is documented in Phase 1 as a
standalone entry — located at the helix crus where it crosses
through the concha.

**Innervation:**
- **Auriculotemporal nerve (V3, mandibular division):** anterior
  surface of the helix. Found in 80% of the crus helicis area
  (Peuker & Filler 2002). Dominant on the anterior-facing
  superior helix.
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3, cervical plexus):** posterior
  surface and inferior helix. Increasingly dominant as the helix
  descends toward the lobule.
- **ABVN:** LOW. Present near the helix crus (proximity to cymba
  conchae = 100% ABVN) but rapidly diminishes along the helix
  rim. The ear apex and lower helix have negligible ABVN.

**Somatotopic representation:** The superior helix (HX2-HX5) maps
to the urogenital/perineal region — the lowest part of the trunk
in the inverted fetus model, corresponding to the highest point of
the ear. HX6 (Ear Apex) is used primarily for bloodletting, not
somatotopic organ targeting. HX9-HX12 have less clearly defined
somatotopic roles, with HX9 designated "Liver Yang" (肝阳) in
clinical tradition.

**Clinical significance:** The helix is most notable for HX6 Ear
Apex bloodletting — one of the most extensively studied
bloodletting procedures in Chinese medicine. The urogenital series
(HX2-HX5) is used for hemorrhoids, urinary disorders, and genital
conditions but has minimal individual-point evidence. The numbered
segments serve primarily as supplementary points.

### HX6 — Nhĩ Tiêm (耳尖 Ěrjiān, Ear Apex)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

**DUAL CLASSIFICATION:** This point is simultaneously HX6 in the
auricular microsystem (AP-16) and EX-HN6 in the body extra point
system (AP-15). The AP-15 EX-HN6 entry documents this point
comprehensively from the extra point perspective, including full
evidence review and mechanism analysis. This entry focuses on the
auricular microsystem context.

The premier BLOODLETTING auricular point — the ONLY auricular point
whose primary technique is pricking to release blood (放血), not
needle retention or seed pressure. Most extensively studied
bloodletting point in Chinese medicine, with condition-specific
meta-analyses for hordeolum, hypertension, and stroke. The ear apex
when folded anteriorly.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

At the apex (highest point) of the auricle when the ear is folded
anteriorly along its longitudinal axis. HX Area 6, between the
urogenital series (HX2-HX5) and the helix tubercle (HX8).

Chinese standard: HX6 耳尖 (GB/T 13734-2008).
WHO nomenclature: MA-HX6.
Extra point designation: EX-HN6 (Ěrjiān).

Somatotopic correspondence: unlike other helix points, HX6 does
NOT represent a specific body region in the inverted fetus model.
Its clinical application (bloodletting for Heat-clearing) transcends
somatotopic logic — parallel to how Shen Men (TF4) transcends
pelvic somatotopy for calming/analgesic effects.

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**

**Nerve supply — MIXED, NO ABVN:**
- **Auriculotemporal nerve (V3):** anterior surface of the ear
  apex. Primary nerve when the ear is folded anteriorly for
  bloodletting (Peuker & Filler 2002: V3 found in 80% of crus
  helicis area).
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3):** posterior surface of the ear
  apex.
- **ABVN:** NOT present at the ear apex. ABVN territory is the
  concha and external auditory canal, not the helix rim.
  Anti-inflammatory and autonomic effects of ear apex bloodletting
  cannot be attributed to vagal activation.

**Pathway to central nervous system:**
1. V3 auriculotemporal → trigeminal sensory nuclear complex →
   trigeminocervical complex
2. C2-C3 great auricular → cervical dorsal horn → ascending
   pathways

No vagal (NTS) pathway — distinguishing HX6 from concha zone
points.

**Tissue:** skin → subcutaneous → elastic cartilage (no muscle
layer). Small-caliber branches of posterior auricular artery and
superficial temporal artery — appropriate for controlled
bloodletting.
→ Cross-ref: AP-15 EX-HN6 (detailed histology and vascular anatomy)

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese auricular | HX6 耳尖 | ✓ Primary |
| Extra point system | EX-HN6 Erjian | ✓ Same point, different classification |
| Nogier/European | Ear apex | ✓ Convergent (bloodletting not emphasized) |
| WFAS | MA-HX6 | ✓ Coded |
| Body acupoint parallel | GV14 Dàzhuī (Heat-clearing bloodletting) | ↔ Functional |

**Dual classification note:** EX-HN6 is classified under Extra
Points (经外奇穴) — body surface points outside the meridian
system. HX6 is classified under the auricular microsystem (耳穴)
— the ear somatotopic projection system. Same physical point,
different conceptual frameworks. The AP-15 entry treats it as a
standalone bloodletting point; this entry places it within the
auricular zone context.

**Chinese-Nogier convergence:** Both systems recognize the ear apex
as a therapeutic point. The European tradition does not emphasize
bloodletting as a primary technique — auriculotherapy in the
European tradition uses needle retention, ASP semi-permanent
needles, or laser, but not blood expression.

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Primary technique: BLOODLETTING (放血 fàngxuě). Prick with
three-edged needle (三棱针) or disposable lancet to 1-2 mm depth,
express 3-10 drops of blood. Procedure: massage auricle until red
and warm → sterilize with 75% alcohol → fold ear anteriorly →
locate highest point → prick → express blood → cotton pressure
hemostasis.

Indications:
- Hordeolum (stye) — MOST studied indication
- Acute conjunctivitis, eye redness and swelling
- Fever, acute Heat conditions (清热)
- Hypertension (adjunctive, 降压)
- Headache, migraine
- Sore throat, tonsillitis

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Full evidence documented in AP-15 EX-HN6 — key findings
summarized here:**

**Hordeolum (stye) 🟡 Plausible (multiple MAs, no sham controls):**
Cochrane review (Cheng et al. 2017, 6 RCTs, n=531): 4/6 trials
used ear apex bloodletting. Acupuncture vs antibiotics alone:
RR 3.60 [1.34-9.70]. Evidence certainty: low to very low.
Dedicated MA (Qiao et al. 2020, 11 RCTs, n=1,718): bloodletting
+ eye drops vs eye drops alone, RR 1.13 [1.08-1.18], I²=0%.
Dose-response RCT (Qiao et al. 2020, n=108): 100 μL (≈6 drops)
superior to 50 μL (≈3 drops), both superior to medication-only.

**Hypertension 🟡 Plausible (acute effect, no sham):**
SR (Kwon et al. 2018, 8 RCTs, n=543): immediate SBP reduction
significant at 5 min to 1 hour post-intervention. DBP reduction
not significant. Long-term benefit when combined with
antihypertensives. No sham-controlled trials.

**Acute stroke 🔴 Unvalidated:**
MA (Kim & Han 2024, 6 RCTs, n=530): NIHSS −2.83 points,
GCS +2.75 points, hospital stay −7.39 days. All Chinese studies,
2016-2023. High risk of bias.

**Fever 🔴 Unvalidated:**
SR (2025, European Journal of Integrative Medicine): adjuvant
bloodletting may improve antipyretic rates in adults. Very low
certainty. No advantage shown in children.

→ Full citations, confidence tiers, and study limitations: see
AP-15 EX-HN6

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

**Bloodletting mechanism (3 hypotheses, none confirmed):**

1. **Nociceptive counter-irritation (DNIC):** pricking → acute V3
   and C2-C3 nociceptive input → descending pain inhibitory
   controls (diffuse noxious inhibitory controls). Explains acute
   headache/pain relief. V3 auriculotemporal afferents project to
   trigeminal brainstem nuclei, accessing pain-modulation circuits.
2. **Autonomic reflex:** V3 auriculotemporal nerve carries
   postganglionic parasympathetic fibers from the otic ganglion.
   Nociceptive stimulation → reflexive autonomic modulation →
   acute SBP reduction (parasympathetic activation → ↓heart rate
   → ↓blood pressure). C2-C3 adds cervical autonomic component.
3. **Local microcirculatory:** bloodletting reduces local venous
   congestion. For hordeolum, mechanism may be partly local
   (decompression of meibomian gland) and partly systemic
   (nociceptive-autonomic reflex).

**ABVN exclusion:** all mechanisms operate through V3 trigeminal
and C2-C3 cervical pathways, NOT vagal afferents. This
fundamentally distinguishes ear apex bloodletting from concha
zone stimulation where ABVN-mediated vagal activation is the
proposed mechanism.
→ Cross-ref: AP-15 EX-HN6 (full mechanism analysis)

## Phase 2 — Lobule Zone (LO) — Dái Tai (耳垂)

The lobule (ear lobe) is the soft, non-cartilaginous inferior
portion of the auricle. Unlike all other ear zones, the lobule
contains NO elastic cartilage — only fibro-fatty tissue covered by
skin. In the Chinese standard (GB/T 13734-2008), 9 points are
mapped on the lobule representing head and face structures.

**Innervation — ZERO ABVN:**
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3, cervical plexus):** the SOLE
  innervation of the lobule. Peuker & Filler (2002): earlobe = 0%
  ABVN, confirmed across all 14 ears (7 cadavers).
- **ABVN:** 0%. No vagal access whatsoever.
- **Auriculotemporal nerve (V3):** minimal/absent at the lobule
  proper. V3 innervates the anterior superior auricle but does not
  extend to the lobule center.

Effects of lobule stimulation must operate ENTIRELY through
cervical spinal and supraspinal pathways — identical innervation
constraint as the scaphoid fossa (Framework note 8). However, the
mechanism gap is SMALLER for the lobule than for scaphoid fossa:
lobule maps head/face structures, and C2-C3 afferents converge
directly with trigeminal (V1-V3) afferents in the trigeminocervical
complex (C1-C3 dorsal horn). This convergence provides a plausible
neurological bridge from C2-C3 ear input to head/face effects
without requiring the 2-4 segment gap bridging mechanisms needed
for scaphoid fossa upper-limb somatotopy.

**Somatotopic representation:** In Nogier's inverted fetus model,
the lobule represents the HEAD and FACE — the "head" of the
inverted fetus maps to the lowest part of the ear. High Chinese-
Nogier convergence: both systems agree that the lobule = head/face
zone. The 9 points map dental (LO1), lingual (LO2), mandibular
(LO3), ophthalmic (LO5, LO9), auditory/vestibular (LO6),
pharyngeal (LO7), and facial (LO8) structures.

**Point localization method:** The lobule is divided into 9 fields
using a 3x3 grid (3 horizontal lines, 2 vertical lines drawn from
the natural lobule border). Points are assigned to grid sectors,
with LO5 Eye at the center (Zone V).

**Clinical significance:** The lobule is most notable for LO5 Eye
— the most consistently included auricular point in Chinese myopia
protocols (100% inclusion rate across 10 RCTs in Gao 2020 SR). The
dental points (LO1, LO3) are commonly used in auricular protocols
for dental analgesia. Despite ZERO vagal access, the lobule has
strong clinical tradition — effects likely mediated through
trigeminocervical convergence and supraspinal pathways.

**NADA/BFA context:** NO lobule points are included in the NADA
5-point protocol or BFA 5-point protocol. The NADA Lung point
(CO14) is sometimes incorrectly placed on the lobule by untrained
practitioners — its correct location is the cavum conchae (45%
ABVN), not the lobule (0% ABVN). This misplacement eliminates the
vagal component that gives the NADA protocol its rationale.

### LO5 — Mục (目 Mù, Eye)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

The most commonly included auricular point in Chinese myopia
protocols — 100% inclusion rate across 10 RCTs (Gao et al. 2020
SR). Located at the center of the lobule (Zone V in the 9-field
grid). Despite ZERO vagal access (pure C2-C3 innervation), LO5
has the strongest evidence base of any lobule point, primarily for
childhood myopia prevention and control.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Center of the lobule (anterolateral surface), LO Area 5 (Zone V
in the 9-field grid). In Nogier's inverted fetus model, the center
of the lobule corresponds to the eye region of the face.

Chinese standard: LO5 目 (GB/T 13734-2008).
WHO nomenclature: MA-LO5.

**Distinct from Eye 1 / Eye 2:** TG2b "Eye 1" (Vision 1) and
AT1b "Eye 2" (Vision 2) are located at the intertragic notch, NOT
on the lobule. Different innervation zone: tragus/antitragus =
C2-C3 + minor ABVN vs lobule = pure C2-C3. Some protocols use
LO5 + TG2b + AT1b together for maximal eye-zone coverage.

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**

**Nerve supply — PURE C2-C3, ZERO ABVN:**
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3):** the ONLY innervation at the
  lobule center. No vagal pathway available.
- **ABVN:** 0%. No vagal access.
- **V3:** minimal/absent at lobule center.

**Pathway to central nervous system:**
C2-C3 great auricular afferents → C2-C3 dorsal horn →
trigeminocervical complex → convergence with V1 ophthalmic
afferents (which carry corneal, conjunctival, and periorbital
sensation). This convergence is well-established: cervical C1
stimulation evokes retro-orbital and periorbital pain in migraine
patients, demonstrating bidirectional referral between cervical
and ophthalmic territories.

**High cross-system convergence:** Chinese, Nogier, Bahr, and
Oleson systems ALL agree on placing an "Eye" point near the center
of the lobule. This is one of the highest-agreement somatotopic
assignments across auricular mapping traditions.

auricular protocol (NADA, BFA). Used primarily in Chinese auricular
protocols and Nogier/European auriculotherapy for eye conditions.

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Myopia (近视, primary pediatric application), inflammatory eye
disorders (conjunctivitis, hordeolum), glaucoma, cephalalgia
radiating to the eyes. Standard myopia protocol: Eye (LO5) +
Liver (CO12) + Kidney (CO10) + Shen Men (TF4) ± Eye 1 (TG2b) ±
Eye 2 (AT1b). Reflects the TCM principle "nourish Kidney and
Liver to brighten the eyes" (滋肾养肝明目) adapted to the
auricular microsystem.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Myopia in children 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level, not isolated):**

**Systematic review (Gao et al. 2020, 10 RCTs, n=724 in MA,
1,145 eyes, PMID 31672461):** ALL 10 studies used LO5 Eye AND
CO12 Liver (100% inclusion rate — the only two points universally
included). CO10 Kidney in 9/10. Auricular acupressure alone was
more effective than eye drops and eye exercises for improving
visual acuity. Auricular acupressure was as effective as needle
acupuncture. Heterogeneity: I²=92%. Risk of bias: only 20%
reported adequate randomization; 80% lacked allocation
concealment/blinding.

**Meta-analysis (Sangvatanakul et al. 2021, 12 articles, n=1,152
Asian children ages 9-14, PMID 35003501):** Post-outlier analysis
(10 studies): **OR=3.42 (95% CI: 2.58-4.53, P<0.00001, I²=8%)**.
2.9-3.4 fold increased odds of treatment success vs controls.
Egger test P=0.84 (no publication bias detected). Population
limited to young Asians.

**Cochrane review (Wei et al. 2011, updated 2025, 2 RCTs, n=131,
PMID 21901710):** Both Taiwan RCTs used LO5 Eye. Liang 2008:
0.21±0.23 D/year vs 0.38±0.32 D/year (P=0.01) but small
magnitude. "No conclusions can be drawn for the benefit of
co-acupressure for slowing progress of myopia in children."

**Atropine combination (Cheng et al. 2014, single-blind RCT,
PMID 24906584):** Atropine 0.125% + auricular stimulation:
−0.41 D myopic progression vs −0.66 D (atropine alone). Auricular acupoint stimulation
also showed more anterior chamber deepening and greater IOP
reduction.

**Ongoing trial (Han et al. 2021, protocol, n=480, PMID
34099018):** Six fixed points including LO5 Eye. First trial to
examine choroidal/retinal thickness via OCT. Results pending.

**Critical limitation:** No study has isolated LO5 from multi-
point protocols. All evidence is protocol-level, making it
impossible to determine LO5's individual contribution.

**Dry eye 🟡 Plausible (protocol-level):**

**SR/MA (Huang et al. 2023, 7 RCTs, n=573, PMID 36676806):**
LO5 included in multi-point protocols. auricular acupuncture vs artificial tears:
TBUT MD=+2.91 sec (95% CI: 2.29-3.54, P<0.001, I²=26%),
Schirmer I MD=+2.18 mm (P=0.04, I²=96%). auricular acupuncture + artificial tears
vs tears alone: TBUT MD=+1.21 sec (P<0.001, I²=0%). auricular acupuncture vs sham
(1 RCT, n=100): OSDI MD=−0.84 (P<0.01). GRADE: low to very low.

**Glaucoma (IOP) 🔴 Preliminary (single small RCT):**

**Her et al. 2010 (prospective single-blind RCT, n=33, PMID
21058884):** Auricular acupressure at Kidney + Liver + **Eye
(LO5)** vs sham points (Wrist, Shoulder, Jaw). 2x daily, 4
weeks. IOP and visual acuity improved significantly in
acupressure group vs pretreatment (P<0.05) at both 4- and 8-week
follow-up. The only RCT explicitly including LO5 for glaucoma.
Small sample, single-blinded only.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

**Trigeminocervical convergence mechanism (3 pathways):**

1. **C2-C3 → V1 ophthalmic convergence (primary pathway):**
   Great auricular nerve afferents (C2-C3) enter the dorsal horn
   where they converge with ophthalmic division (V1) afferents in
   the trigeminocervical complex (C1-C3). Clinical proof: cervical
   C1 stimulation evokes retro-orbital pain in migraine patients.
   This convergence allows C2-C3 input to modulate ocular
   nociception and potentially autonomic regulation of the eye.
2. **Cervical sympathetic modulation:** The superior cervical
   ganglion receives preganglionic input from T1-T3 and provides
   postganglionic sympathetic innervation to the eye (iris dilator,
   Mueller's muscle, retinal/choroidal vasculature). C2-C3
   stimulation may modulate sympathetic tone via cervicosympathetic
   reflex arcs, affecting ocular blood flow and IOP.
3. **Descending inhibitory pathways:** PAG-RVM system provides
   non-segmental modulation. Consistent with the dominant
   supraspinal mechanism proposed for scaphoid fossa (Framework
   note 8b).

**Mechanism vs scaphoid fossa contrast:** The lobule and scaphoid
fossa share identical innervation (100% great auricular nerve, C2-C3), but the
mechanism gap is fundamentally different. Scaphoid fossa maps
upper limb (C4-C8) = 2-4 segment gap requiring multi-level
bridging. Lobule maps head/face = DIRECT convergence in the
trigeminocervical complex (C1-C3 overlaps with V1-V3). This makes
the neurological rationale for lobule-to-face effects considerably
STRONGER than scaphoid fossa-to-upper-limb effects.

**Confidence:** 🔴 Hypothesis. Trigeminocervical convergence is
well-established neuroanatomy. However, the specific link from LO5
stimulation to ocular physiological changes (myopia progression,
IOP, tear production) has not been directly demonstrated. No fMRI
or fNIRS evidence exists for LO5 specifically activating visual
cortex or ocular motor nuclei.

⚡ **Framework note 9 — LOBULE-SCAPHOID FOSSA INNERVATION
PARALLEL + MECHANISM GAP CONTRAST:**

The lobule and scaphoid fossa share IDENTICAL nerve supply (100%
great auricular nerve C2-C3, ZERO ABVN, zero V3 — both confirmed
by Peuker & Filler 2002) but map to DIFFERENT body regions with
fundamentally different mechanism plausibility:

**(a) Scaphoid fossa → upper limb (C4-C8):** 2-4 segment gap
between ear nerve supply (C2-C3) and somatotopic target (C4-C8
fingers through shoulder). Requires multi-level bridging mechanisms:
propriospinal interneurons, trigeminocervical complex, or PAG-RVM
supraspinal pathways. Map displacement documented (Lovato 2023:
active points in SF1-SF2, not SF4-5). Mechanism plausibility: LOW
to MODERATE.

**(b) Lobule → head/face (V1-V3, CN VII):** DIRECT convergence.
C2-C3 afferents enter the trigeminocervical complex (C1-C3 dorsal
horn) where they converge with trigeminal V1-V3 afferents serving
the entire face, eyes, jaw, and teeth. Clinical proof: cervical C1
stimulation evokes retro-orbital pain in migraine patients.
Mechanism plausibility: MODERATE to HIGH.

This contrast illustrates that identical peripheral nerve supply
does NOT guarantee identical mechanism strength — the intervening
neuroanatomy (convergence pattern, segment gap) determines how
plausible the somatotopic effect is. The lobule's head/face
somatotopy has a more direct neurological rationale than the
scaphoid fossa's upper-limb somatotopy, despite sharing the same
ear nerve.

This framework note extends notes 8 (scaphoid fossa innervation
correction + mechanism gap) and 4-6 (zone-level effects, ABVN
gradient) by contrasting two zones with identical innervation but
different mechanism strengths.
→ Cross-ref: Framework notes 4, 6, 8; Peuker & Filler 2002;
trigeminocervical complex neuroanatomy

## Phase 2 — Posterior Ear + Ear Root Zone (P/R) — Huyệt vùng Nhĩ Bối (耳背) + Nhĩ Căn (耳根)

The posterior ear (耳背面 ěr bèi miàn) is the back surface of the
auricle — skin over cartilage opposite the anterior surface. The
ear root (耳根 ěr gēn) is the narrow zone where the auricle
attaches to the head at the mastoid region. Together, these form
the LEAST STANDARDIZED zone of the auricular microsystem.

In the Chinese standard (GB/T 13734-2008), 9 named points are
mapped on the posterior surface and ear root: 5 posterior organ
points (P1-P5) behind the concha, the Hypertension Groove (降压沟)
behind the antihelix, and 3 ear root points (R1-R3). Three
additional clinical landmarks (P6-P8: upper, middle, lower
posterior ear) are zone descriptors in the standard, used clinically
for dermatological conditions. This zone has 12 entries total.

**Plan correction — point count:** The child plan (§3.9) estimated
20 posterior points based on 91 total minus 71 anterior. Research
confirms only 9 standard named points plus 3 clinical zone
descriptors = 12 entries. The discrepancy reflects uncounted
sub-points in anterior zones (e.g., TG2b Eye 1, AT1b Eye 2,
intertragic notch points) rather than missing posterior points.
Count reconciliation deferred to Review phase.

**Innervation — PRIMARILY CERVICAL, MINIMAL ABVN:**
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3):** DOMINANT supply to the
  posterior auricular surface. "Prevails at both lateral and medial
  surfaces" (Peuker & Filler 2002).
- **Lesser occipital nerve (C2):** supplements the great auricular
  nerve at the upper posterior ear.
- **Posterior auricular nerve (CN VII, facial):** primarily MOTOR to
  posterior auricular muscles. Communicates with ABVN — providing
  possible INDIRECT vagal contribution to the posterior surface.
- **ABVN (CN X):** NOT a direct sensory supplier to the posterior
  ear skin. One branch communicates with the posterior auricular
  nerve (VII), providing possible indirect vagal input. Peuker &
  Filler 2002 mapped the LATERAL surface only — no quantitative
  ABVN data exists for the posterior surface. fMRI evidence:
  stimulation of the inferoposterior ear canal wall (nearest ABVN
  territory to the posterior surface) produced the WEAKEST NTS
  activation (Butt 2020, Journal of Anatomy).

**Posterior-anterior organ duality:** P1-P5 map the SAME five
organs as anterior concha points (CO15 Heart, CO14 Lung, CO13
Spleen, CO12 Liver, CO10 Kidney) but through fundamentally
different neural pathways. Anterior concha = ABVN-dominant → NTS →
vagal circuit. Posterior ear = great auricular nerve-dominant C2-C3 → cervical dorsal
horn → brainstem. Same somatotopic label, different mechanism.
See Framework note 10.

**Posterior zone division system:** The posterior concha surface is
divided into 5 zones by 2 horizontal lines:
- Zone 1 (upper) = Heart
- Zone 2 (middle, inner 1/3) = Lung
- Zone 3 (middle, central 1/3) = Spleen
- Zone 4 (middle, outer 1/3) = Liver
- Zone 5 (lower) = Kidney

The Y-shaped groove behind the antihelix + its upper/lower crura
forms the Hypertension Groove (降压沟) — the most clinically
significant posterior structure.

**Cartilage ridges (P6-P8):** Three cartilage prominences on the
posterior surface correspond to the antihelix and its crura on the
anterior surface. Used clinically for skin diseases via bleeding/
pricking technique.

→ Cross-ref: CO10 Kidney, CO12 Liver, CO13 Spleen, CO14 Lung,
CO15 Heart (anterior counterparts); B02 §3.3 (ABVN anatomy)

directional specificity: upper 1/3 to middle 1/3 = blood pressure
LOWERING zone (降压点); lower 1/3 = blood pressure RAISING zone
(升压点) — though this directional claim lacks modern validation.

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Posterior auricular surface overlying the antihelix cartilage —
great auricular nerve (C2-C3) dominant. The groove is a soft
tissue depression between cartilage ridges. NOT ABVN-dominant.
See zone header.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese standard | 降压沟 / 耳背沟 | ✓ Primary |
| WFAS | PS (Posteromedial Surface) | ✓ Coded |
| Anterior counterpart | Antihelix body + crura (AH region) | ↔ Mirror |

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**
Hypertension (primary indication — the most commonly used
auricular structure for blood pressure management in Chinese
clinical practice), pruritus, skin diseases. Standard
hypertension protocol: Hypertension Groove + Shen Men (TF4) +
Heart (CO15) + Liver (CO12) + Kidney (CO10) + Sympathetic (AH6a).

**Evidence context:** No study has tested the Hypertension Groove
as a single-point intervention. Auricular acupressure for
hypertension generally (Gao et al. 2020, 18 RCTs, n=1,685):
SBP reduction MD = −15.05 mmHg (95% CI −18.49 to −11.61,
P<0.00001) — but this is for multi-point auricular therapy, not
the groove isolated. The groove's antihypertensive mechanism must
operate through C2-C3 → cervical dorsal horn → brainstem
cardiovascular centers (NTS, ventrolateral medulla) — NOT through
the ABVN-NTS vagal pathway available to anterior concha points.

### R2 — Nhĩ Mê Căn (耳迷根 Ěr Mí Gēn, Root of Ear Vagus)

**Evidence tier: C**

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**
Junction of the posterior ear and mastoid process, at the level
of the helix crus. Located near the stylomastoid foramen (exit

(pathway of the ABVN).

**Giải phẫu tại điểm:**
Periauricular/mastoid junction — great auricular nerve (C2-C3) +

mastoid canaliculus). Despite its name referencing the vagus,
the cutaneous innervation at this point is primarily from the
great auricular nerve and CN VII posterior auricular nerve, NOT
direct ABVN cutaneous supply.

traditional clinical observation — stimulating this point produces
vagal-like reflex effects (nausea, gastrointestinal responses).
The ABVN traverses through the nearby mastoid canaliculus, so
deeper stimulation may reach vagal fibers indirectly. However,
the surface skin innervation is primarily cervical (C2-C3), not
vagal.

⚡ **Framework note 10 — ANTERIOR-POSTERIOR ORgreat auricular nerve POINT DUALITY:
SAME SOMATOTOPIC LABEL, DIFFERENT NEURAL PATHWAY:**

Five organs (Heart, Lung, Spleen, Liver, Kidney) are mapped TWICE
on the auricular microsystem — once on the anterior concha surface
(CO10, CO12-CO15) and once on the posterior ear surface (P1-P5).
These mirror points share the same somatotopic organ label but
access fundamentally different neural pathways:

**(a) Anterior concha organ points (CO10, CO12-CO15):**
Located in the cymba conchae (100% ABVN) or cavum conchae (45%
ABVN). Primary mechanism: ABVN → nucleus tractus solitarius
(NTS) → vagal circuit (cardiac, respiratory, gastrointestinal
modulation). These points have DIRECT vagal access — the strongest
mechanistic rationale for visceral organ effects.

**(b) Posterior organ points (P1-P5):**
Located on the back surface of the concha. Primary innervation:
great auricular nerve (C2-C3). ABVN contribution: indirect/
minimal (via CN VII posterior auricular nerve communication).
Mechanism: C2-C3 → cervical dorsal horn → brainstem (NTS,
ventrolateral medulla) through cervical-brainstem projections.

**Clinical implication:** The same organ can be targeted through
two distinct neural pathways — vagal (anterior) and cervical
(posterior). This duality exists only for these 5 organs and is
unique within the auricular microsystem. Whether the posterior
pathway produces clinically meaningful organ-specific effects
through cervical nerves without significant vagal input remains
undemonstrated. The NADA protocol uses ANTERIOR organ points
(CO10, CO12, CO14), not posterior equivalents — clinical
tradition favors the vagal-access approach.

**Broader framework context:** This duality extends the lobule-
scaphoid fossa contrast (Framework note 9): identical somatotopic
labels mapped through different neural pathways produce different
mechanism plausibility. For organ effects, the vagal pathway
(anterior concha) has stronger mechanistic support than the
cervical pathway (posterior ear). For head/face effects, the
cervical pathway (lobule) has stronger support than for upper limb
effects (scaphoid fossa). Neural pathway, not somatotopic label,
determines mechanism strength.
→ Cross-ref: Framework notes 4, 6, 8, 9; CO10, CO12-CO15;
Peuker & Filler 2002
