# AP-02 — Large Intestine Meridian (LI) — Hand Yangming (手阳明大肠经)

> **Status:** Phase 2 COMPLETE. 20/20 entries (2 Tier A, 10 Tier B,
> 8 Tier C). 30 architectural observations. 3 ⚡ framework notes.
> First complete Yang channel in the Atlas.
>
> **Meridian overview:** The Large Intestine channel (Hand Yangming) runs
> from the index fingertip (LI1) → radial side of hand → dorsal first web
> space (LI4) → radial forearm → lateral elbow (LI11) → lateral upper arm
> → shoulder (LI15) → neck → crosses midline to opposite side of face →
> ends beside contralateral nostril (LI20). 20 points total. Paired with
> Lung (Hand Taiyin). Peak time in Chinese clock: 05:00-07:00.

## LI4 — Hợp Cốc (合谷, Hé Gǔ)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**
The single point used in the first acupuncture anesthesia surgery (1958
tonsillectomy). Yuan-Source point of the Large Intestine. One half of the
"Four Gates" (四关, Si Guan) with LR3. NIH 1997 Consensus Statement cited
dental pain as having "clear evidence of efficacy." Command Point of face
and mouth (面口合谷收). One of the Ma Dan-yang Twelve Heavenly Star Points.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the dorsum of the hand, between
the 1st and 2nd metacarpal bones, approximately at the midpoint of the
2nd metacarpal bone on the radial side.

Anatomical landmarks: in the belly of the first dorsal interosseous muscle,
between the first and second metacarpal bones. The point is found by
pressing the thumb against the index finger — LI4 is at the highest
point of the muscular bulge, approximately level with the midpoint of the
second metacarpal's radial border.

Needle depth range: 12-20 mm perpendicular insertion. Oblique insertion
toward PC8 (Laogong) in classical technique.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply (Casey et al. 2022, Anatomical Record — 25 cadaveric hands):
- Superficial branch of radial nerve (18.9% of structures observed at LI4)
  — primary sensory innervation of dorsal hand skin at this location
- Deep branch of ulnar nerve (4.0%) — motor innervation of first dorsal
  interosseous muscle, found in the bulk of the muscle
- Spinal segments: C6-C7 (sensory, superficial radial nerve); C8-T1
  (motor, deep ulnar nerve)

Fascial plane: within the first dorsal interosseous muscle (52.4% of
structures, Casey 2022), between the first and second metacarpal bones.
Intermuscular connective tissue between the first dorsal interosseous and
adductor pollicis (deeper layer).
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: tributaries of dorsal venous plexus (22.3%, superficial);
arterial branches in first interosseous space (10.2%, deep) — first
dorsal metacarpal artery from radial artery. Umemoto et al. (2019) showed
LI4 is close to vascular branch from superficial branch of radial nerve
(mean distance 4.3 ± 4.3 mm).
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → dorsal subcutaneous tissue
- First dorsal interosseous muscle (primary)
- Adductor pollicis (deeper, oblique head)

Distant vascular effect: Kuo et al. (2015) demonstrated needling LI4
increases blood flow at DISTANT point LI11 on same forearm — both local
AND distal vascular effects.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Electrical properties: Kelvin probe measurement (Gow et al. 2012) showed
>60 mV maximum at LI4 (2 mm diameter) in 2/3 subjects. No maximum found
at PC6 in same study. Intriguing but tiny sample, inconsistent across
points. Methodological concerns per Ahn & Martinsen 2007.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S2.4

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **No.** LI4 falls between LI3 (Shu-Stream/Wood) and LI5
(Jing-River/Fire) in the channel sequence but is NOT one of the Five Shu
points of the Large Intestine meridian. The Five Shu of LI are: LI1
(Jing-Well/Metal), LI2 (Ying-Spring/Water), LI3 (Shu-Stream/Wood), LI5
(Jing-River/Fire), LI11 (He-Sea/Earth).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Yuan-Source: **Yes.** Yuan-Source point (原穴) of the Large Intestine
channel. One of 12 Yuan-Source points (one per regular meridian). Located
near the wrist — points of access to Yuan (Original) Qi. Used
diagnostically (tenderness = organ dysfunction) and therapeutically
(tonify constitutional energy). Combined with Luo-Connecting point of
paired channel in Host-Guest technique (主客配穴法): LI as host → LI4
(Yuan) + LU7 (Luo of paired Lung).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Luo-Connecting: No (LI6 Pianli is Luo of Large Intestine)

Xi-Cleft: No (LI7 Wenliu is Xi-Cleft of Large Intestine)

Back-Shu: No (BL25 Dachangshu is Back-Shu of Large Intestine)

Front-Mu: No (ST25 Tianshu is Front-Mu of Large Intestine)

Eight Confluent: No

Hui-Meeting: No

Special designations:
- **Command Point of Face and Mouth** (面口合谷收) — from the Song of the
  Four Command Points (四总穴歌, Zhenjiu Dacheng): "Face and mouth —
  Hegu controls." The four commands: LI4 (face/mouth), LU7 (head/neck),
  ST36 (belly/abdomen), BL40 (low back). This is the most famous classical
  prescription formula in acupuncture.
- **Ma Dan-yang Twelve Heavenly Star Points** (天星十二穴) — one of 12
  empirically validated points from the Song dynasty. Indications listed:
  headache, facial swelling, toothache, epistaxis, sore throat, lockjaw,
  deafness.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2

### Cross-tradition mapping

**TCM acupoint:** LI4 Hegu (合谷). Yuan-Source of Large Intestine.
Command Point of face/mouth. Primary function: releases the exterior,
expels wind, activates the channel, alleviates pain (especially face,
head, teeth), regulates sweating, clears heat. One of the most prescribed
acupoints across all TCM traditions.

**Trigger point:** First dorsal interosseous trigger point (Dorsher 2008
correspondence mapping). Referred pain pattern: radial side of index
finger and dorsal first web space. The 93.3% acupoint-trigger point
overlap (Dorsher 2008) includes LI4 as a clear correspondent. Note:
trigger point referred pain covers LOCAL hand/finger pain, not the distal
face/headache effects — the cross-tradition convergence is for location
identification, not for all clinical applications.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**Ashi correspondence:** Very commonly tender on palpation in the general
population and especially in headache/facial pain patients. One of the
most easily identified Ashi-reactive points. The first dorsal interosseous
is a common site of palpation tenderness.

**Marma point:** **Kshipra** (क्षिप्र) — Marma point located between the
thumb and index finger (and correspondingly between the big toe and second
toe on the foot). One of the 107 Marma points in the Ayurvedic system
(Sushruta Samhita). Classified as Snayu (tendon/ligament) Marma in
classical sources (Sushruta Samhita); some modern references list hand
Kshipra as Sira (vascular) — classification varies by text. The foot
Kshipra (at LR3) is uniformly classified as Snayu.
→ Cross-ref: AP-12 LR3 (foot Kshipra, Snayu — consistent classification)
Anatomical correspondence with LI4 is within 1-2 cm.

**Head zone:** No direct visceral-dermatome correspondence. C6-C7
dermatomes are above the range of the thoracolumbar visceral Head zones
(T1-L2 for most organs per Beissner 2011). However, C6-T1 afferents
pass through the stellate ganglion region, providing indirect sympathetic
pathway connection to upper body autonomic regulation.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.5.6

**Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Back Arm Line (SBAL) per Myers — this
fascial line runs from deltoid → lateral intermuscular septum → lateral
epicondyle → forearm extensors → finger extensors. LI4 at the dorsal
first web space is at the distal termination of this line. The Large
Intestine channel trajectory closely parallels the SBAL path along the
lateral/radial arm. Dorsher 2009 confirmed significant acupoint-Anatomy
Trains correspondence.

**Neurogenic spot:** Insufficient data for hand points. Kim 2017 primarily
studied trunk/back neurogenic inflammatory spots correlating with organ
disease states. No specific prediction for hand acupoints.

**Cupping point:** Not applicable. Hand surface too small for standard
cupping. No traditional cupping protocol at LI4.

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Marma, Anatomy
Trains). High convergence confirms strong anatomical significance at this
location across independent traditions.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:

Neijing Lingshu (Chapter 1, Ben Shu — 本输): identified as Yuan-Source
point of the Large Intestine channel. The Yuan-Source concept establishes
LI4 as a fundamental access point to the channel's Original Qi.

Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing (针灸甲乙经, Huangfu Mi, 259 CE): toothache, facial
swelling, headache, eye pain, sore throat, malaria and alternating
fever-chills, lockjaw (trismus).

Nanjing (难经, Classic of Difficulties): elaborates Yuan-Source theory —
Yuan points access the source between the kidneys (ming men), making them
fundamental for constitutional treatment.

Zhenjiu Dacheng (针灸大成, Yang Jizhou, 1601): contains the Four Command
Points formula (四总穴歌): 面口合谷收 — "face and mouth, Hegu controls."
Specific indications: toothache, lockjaw, wind-cold headache, excessive
sweating, absence of sweating, nasal congestion, epistaxis.

Ma Dan-yang Heavenly Star Points (Song dynasty): headache, facial
swelling, toothache, epistaxis, sore throat, lockjaw, deafness,
alternating fever-chills.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, Manual of Acupuncture):
- Releases the exterior, expels wind (common cold, fever, headache)
- Regulates sweating (both excessive and absent sweating)
- Activates the channel, alleviates pain (face, head, teeth — primary)
- Restores the Yang, rescues from collapse (emergency use)
- Regulates the face, eyes, nose, mouth, ears
- Induces labor (traditional — see modern evidence §Bằng chứng)
- Intestinal disorders (constipation, diarrhea — Large Intestine organ)
- Amenorrhea, delayed menses

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật: consistent across ALL classical sources as
the primary distal point for face/mouth/dental conditions. The "Four
Command Points" formula has been validated through continuous clinical use
for over 400 years. LI4 is one of the 3-4 most frequently prescribed
acupoints in modern clinical practice worldwide (alongside ST36, PC6,
SP6). The Four Gates combination (LI4 + LR3) is the most commonly used
point pair for pain, stress, and Liver Qi Stagnation.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2, B02 S4

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Dental pain / postoperative oral analgesia — 🟢 Confirmed**

NIH 1997 Consensus Statement (JAMA 1998, 12-member panel, 25 experts):
placed postoperative dental pain in highest evidence tier — "clear evidence
that needle acupuncture is efficacious." Two primary studies cited both used
LI4 as principal point: Sung et al. 1977 and Lao et al. 1995.

Mueller et al. 2023 (Japanese Dental Science Review — SR/MA, 23
publications, 21 RCTs): verum vs sham postoperative: SMD **-0.77** (95%
CI: -1.52 to -0.03) — statistically significant favoring acupuncture, but
high heterogeneity (I²=83%). No included study achieved overall low
risk-of-bias.

Key individual RCTs:
- Sung 1977 (n=40, 4-arm): LI4 as sole point. LI4 alone > placebo
  (p<0.05); in first 30 minutes, LI4 provided SUPERIOR relief vs codeine
  (p<0.01) — faster onset but shorter duration than codeine.
- Lao 1995 (n=19, RCT): pain-free duration 181 min vs 71 min placebo
  (p≤0.046).
- Lao 1999 (n=39, double-blind RCT): pain-free 173 vs 94 min (p=0.01);
  analgesic consumption reduced (1.1 vs 1.65 tablets, p=0.05).
- Jalali 2015 (n=40, triple-blind RCT): LI4 as sole point. Inferior
  alveolar nerve block success: **60% vs 20%** (p<0.05) — LI4 tripled
  nerve block success in irreversible pulpitis.

Confidence: 🟢 — NIH consensus-level endorsement + SR with positive
pooled result + multiple RCTs with LI4 as sole or primary point.
Limitation: no dedicated Cochrane review for dental pain; all MAs show
high heterogeneity; modest sample sizes.

**2. Headache — tension-type and migraine — 🟡 Plausible**

Cochrane TTH prevention (Linde et al. 2016, CD007587 — 12 RCTs, 2,349
participants): acupuncture vs usual care: 48% vs 17% responders in larger
trial (RR 2.5). Acupuncture vs sham: 51% vs 43% (RR 1.3, 95% CI
1.09-1.5) — 11 additional responders per 100 patients over sham. Moderate
quality evidence.

Cochrane migraine prevention (Linde et al. 2016, CD001218 — 22 RCTs,
4,985 participants): acupuncture vs usual care: NNT=4; 41% vs 17%
achieved ≥50% headache reduction (RR 2.40). Acupuncture vs sham: NNT=10-11
(50% vs 41%, RR 1.23). Acupuncture ≈ prophylactic drugs (flunarizine,
topiramate, valproate, metoprolol) with 71% fewer adverse events (RR 0.29).

Supplementary MA (Xiang et al. 2023, 30 RCTs, 2,742 TTH patients):
long-term pain intensity vs sham SMD -0.26 (CI -0.44 to -0.07) — HIGH
certainty evidence.

LI4 is consistently among the top 2-4 most frequently selected acupoints
in ALL headache/migraine protocols across clinical trials.

fMRI evidence (Kong et al. 2015): LI4 stimulation in Bell's palsy patients
activated brain regions "broadly overlapping and adjacent" with facial
point ST6 — including face area of primary motor cortex (BA4). This is
direct neuroimaging support for LI4-orofacial functional connection.

Confidence: 🟡 — Cochrane reviews positive for acupuncture protocols that
INCLUDE LI4, but no RCT has isolated LI4 alone for headache. Individual
contribution of LI4 vs other protocol points remains unquantified.
Mechanism plausible (supraspinal descending modulation of trigeminal
nucleus caudalis). Specific sham-controlled effect is small (NNT 10-11
for migraine).

**3. Labor pain — 🟡 Plausible**

Cochrane 2020 (Smith et al., CD009232 — 28 trials, 3,960 women):
acupuncture vs sham for pain intensity: MD **-4.42** VAS (95% CI: -12.94
to 4.09) — NOT significant (low certainty). Acupuncture vs usual care:
SMD -1.31 (significant but very low certainty, I²=93%). Reduced
pharmacological analgesia use: RR **0.75** (95% CI: 0.63-0.89) — moderate
certainty, 25% reduction. Satisfaction: RR 2.38 (moderate certainty).

LI4 acupressure specifically (Najafi et al. 2018, SR/MA — 6 LI4 trials,
n=552): pain reduction vs touch SMD **-0.94** (95% CI: -1.36 to -0.53) —
significant. Active phase shortened: SMD -0.91.

Pattern: significant vs no treatment/touch; NOT significant vs sham →
substantial context/placebo component. But reduced drug use (RR 0.75)
and increased satisfaction are clinically meaningful.

Confidence: 🟡 — clinically useful (reduces analgesic need, improves
satisfaction) but specific needle-point effect not confirmed by sham
comparison. Likely operates through multiple mechanisms (analgesic context,
endorphin release, descending modulation) rather than point-specific
pathway alone.

**4. Labor induction — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Traditional claim that LI4 stimulates uterine contractions and can induce
labor is NOT confirmed by modern evidence.

Chen et al. 2025 (SR/MA, 10 RCTs, 1,432 women ≥37 weeks): onset of
spontaneous labor RR **1.02** (95% CI: 0.93-1.13, p=0.66) — NO effect.
Cesarean rate, epidural use, vaginal delivery rate: all non-significant.

Modlock et al. 2010 (double-blind RCT, Park-Sham needles, 125 women at
41+6 weeks): delivery within 24 hours: 12% vs 14%, **p=0.79** — NO
effect. Excellent blinding (66-70% reported "no idea" of allocation).

Cochrane 2017 (Smith et al., 22 trials, 3,456 women): no clear benefit
for cesarean reduction or labor onset.

Paradoxical animal finding: Pak et al. 2000 (pregnant rats) — LI4
acupuncture actually **SUPPRESSED** oxytocin-induced uterine contractions
(p<0.05), opposite of traditional claim. Tocolytic rather than oxytocic.

Confidence: 🔴 — traditional indication directly contradicted by modern
evidence. Multiple well-designed trials show no labor induction effect.

**5. Analgesic (general, perioperative) — 🟡 Plausible**

Historical: Aug 30, 1958 — first surgery under acupuncture anesthesia
alone. Tonsillectomy at Shanghai First People's Hospital (Dr. Zhuang
Xinliang, Dr. Yin Huizhu) using bilateral LI4 as sole anesthetic. By 1971:
~400,000 surgeries reportedly performed under acupuncture anesthesia.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S2.2

Modern perioperative evidence: EA at LI4 reduces anesthetic drug dosage by
10-50% (combined with conventional anesthesia). LI4+PC6 combination
reduces intraoperative heart rate, blood pressure, and postoperative
adverse reactions. Standard perioperative protocol: LI4, PC6, ST36, GB34.

Beta-endorphin release: Surbakti et al. 2017 — EA at LI4 increases plasma
beta-endorphin levels. Mechanism follows Han Jisheng's frequency-dependent
model: 2 Hz → enkephalin/beta-endorphin (mu/delta receptors); 100 Hz →
dynorphin (kappa receptors); mixed 2/100 Hz → maximum analgesic synergy.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

fMRI (Hui et al. 2000, 2010 — Harvard/MGH): LI4 produced the "most
prominent response" of any acupoint studied. Extensive limbic deactivation
(amygdala, hippocampus, anterior cingulate, hypothalamus) + somatosensory
activation. DeQi-dependent: when DeQi sensation occurred, full
deactivation pattern; when sharp pain occurred, pattern reversed. LI4
modulates the Limbic-Paralimbic-Neocortical Network (LPNN).

TRPV1 pathway: EA at LI4 reduces inflammatory pain via brain TRPV1
signaling in prefrontal cortex, hypothalamus, and periaqueductal gray
(PAG) (PMC6769885).

Confidence: 🟡 — strong mechanistic evidence (endorphin, fMRI, TRPV1/PAG)
+ historical validation. But no SR for LI4 as sole general analgesic point;
perioperative evidence from multi-point protocols.

**6. Autonomic regulation (sympathetic inhibition, blood pressure) — 🟡
Plausible**

A02 S4.3: LI4 → sympathetic inhibition → ↓ blood pressure. Specific
acupoint → specific autonomic pathway pattern (distinct from ST36 vagal,
PC6 cardiac vagal, ear concha direct vagal).

Some individual studies support antihypertensive effect through sympathetic
modulation, but no dedicated SR for LI4 and blood pressure specifically.

Confidence: 🟡 — autonomic pathway identified (A02) but large-scale
clinical evidence for blood pressure specifically is limited.

**Sham comparison note:** Across all indications, LI4 shows a consistent
pattern: large effects vs no-treatment controls, modest-to-null effects vs
sham acupuncture. This is consistent with the 3-layer sham resolution
(AP-00 §3.3): real therapeutic effects exist but arise from multiple
simultaneous mechanisms — some point-specific (endorphin release, specific
neural pathway activation), some location-independent (gate control,
relaxation, therapeutic context). The dental pain and perioperative evidence
show the clearest point-specific signal.

**Negative evidence:** Traditional labor induction claim (LI4 "forbidden in
pregnancy" due to ability to stimulate contractions) is directly
contradicted by multiple RCTs and one animal study showing the opposite
effect (tocolytic). This is recorded per AP-00 §3.2 rule 4: negative
findings are data, not failures.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization: clinically observed increased tenderness at
LI4 in headache and facial pain patients. Consistent with the dynamic
acupoint model (Kim 2017, AP-00 §1.2) but specific published data for LI4
sensitization thresholds is limited compared to trunk points.

Electrical distinguishability: Kelvin probe found >60 mV maximum at LI4
in 2/3 subjects (Gow et al. 2012), but sample too small and finding
inconsistent across acupoints (no maximum at PC6 in same study). Status:
intriguing, not confirmed.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon), A09 S2.4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at LI4:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): superficial radial nerve (C6-C7
  sensory), deep ulnar nerve (C8-T1 motor). Afferent activation of both
  A-beta (touch/pressure) and A-delta (De Qi sensation) fibers.
- A02 (Autonomic Nervous System): sympathetic inhibition → ↓BP. Specific
  acupoint → specific autonomic pathway.
- A04 (Vascular-Lymphatic): local axon reflex → vasodilation at point +
  distant vascular effect at LI11 (Kuo 2015).
- A06 (Chemical Signaling): endorphin/enkephalin release (Han JS model).
  Beta-endorphin increase in plasma (Surbakti 2017). TRPV1 signaling in
  PAG/hypothalamus/PFC.
- A07 (Pain-Nociception): gate control at spinal level (segmental); DNIC /
  conditioned pain modulation (heterosegmental); descending inhibition from
  PAG → RVM → trigeminal nucleus caudalis (supraspinal).

**Central mechanism question — WHY does a hand point (C6-T1) treat face/
mouth conditions (trigeminal = CN V)?**

This is the fundamental non-segmental question for LI4, analogous to PC6's
"why does forearm C8-T1 affect stomach T6-T9?" and ST36's "why does leg
L4-L5 affect stomach T6-T9?"

**Resolution — supraspinal descending modulation (NOT segmental convergence):**

LI4 (C6-T1) and the trigeminal territory share NO spinal segment. The
trigeminocervical complex (TCC) extends only to C1-C3 (Bartsch & Goadsby
2003). The gap between C3 and C6 means direct segmental convergence cannot
explain LI4's facial effects.

The mechanism operates through a supraspinal loop:

```
Step 1: Needling LI4 activates A-beta/A-delta fibers of radial nerve
        (C6-C7)
Step 2: Afferents ascend via spinothalamic and spinoreticular tracts
        to brainstem
Step 3: Signals activate the PAG (periaqueductal gray) and RVM
        (rostroventromedial medulla)
Step 4: PAG/RVM triggers:
        a) Endogenous opioid release (enkephalin, beta-endorphin)
        b) Serotonergic descending inhibition (raphe nuclei → 5-HT)
        c) Noradrenergic descending inhibition (locus coeruleus → NA)
Step 5: Descending inhibitory fibers project via dorsolateral funiculus
        NOT only to spinal dorsal horn BUT ALSO to trigeminal nucleus
        caudalis (TNC)
Step 6: TNC inhibition → reduced processing of facial/dental/headache
        pain signals

Key evidence:
- DLF lesioning abolishes EA analgesia (Lv et al. 2019)
  = descending pathway is necessary, not optional
- Naloxone blocks acupuncture analgesia (Pomeranz & Chiu 1976)
  = endorphin release is necessary component
- fMRI: LI4 activates face area of motor cortex BA4, overlapping
  with facial point ST6 activation (Kong et al. 2015)
  = cortical-level hand-face functional connection
- LI4 produces most prominent limbic deactivation of any acupoint
  (Hui et al. 2000, 2010) = affective pain dimension modulated
```

**Additional parallel mechanism — DNIC (Diffuse Noxious Inhibitory
Controls):** DeQi sensation at LI4 (carried by A-delta/C fibers) activates
subnucleus reticularis dorsalis (SRD) in caudal medulla → heterosegmental
inhibition throughout neuraxis, including trigeminal territory. Le Bars &
Willer 2002 proposed DNIC as primary mechanism of heterosegmental
acupuncture analgesia.

**Contrast with ST36 and PC6 non-segmental mechanisms:**
- ST36: PROKR2+ neurons → specific molecular vagal-adrenal pathway → NTS/DMV
- PC6: median nerve → NTS → GABA inhibition in DMV → vagal disinhibition
- LI4: radial nerve → ascending → PAG/RVM → descending modulation → TNC

Three Tier 1 points, three fundamentally different non-segmental pathways.
Common thread: all bypass segmental organization via supraspinal relay, but
through DIFFERENT relay stations and DIFFERENT neurotransmitter systems.

**Gap:** precise ascending pathway from C6-T1 to PAG (which specific relay
neurons, what role do propriospinal connections play?) is not fully mapped.
The functional connection is confirmed by fMRI and pharmacological
evidence, but the anatomical pathway intermediaries remain incomplete.

⚡ Framework note 1: LI4 represents the THIRD distinct non-segmental
mechanism pattern in the Atlas (after ST36 PROKR2-vagal and PC6
NTS-GABAergic). All three highest-evidence acupoints use DIFFERENT
supraspinal pathways for their primary clinical effects. Pattern: non-
segmental effects are the RULE, not the exception, for the most validated
acupoints. This challenges any single-mechanism model of acupoint action
and suggests the 30-40% non-segmental gap (A07 S8 Q1, C04 S3.5.6) is
resolved by MULTIPLE distinct pathways, not one universal mechanism.
Extends A01 S8 Q1, A07 S1, C04 S3.5.6.

⚡ Framework note 2: LI4 pregnancy contraindication is NOT evidence-based.
Carr 2015: >6,700 women received acupuncture at "forbidden points"
including LI4 during pregnancy — adverse event rates (miscarriage, preterm
birth, stillbirth) IDENTICAL to background population rates and untreated
controls. Chen 2025: no labor induction effect (RR 1.02). Pak 2000: LI4
actually SUPPRESSES uterine contractions (tocolytic). Cummings 2011: no
plausible physiological mechanism for harm. This is the strongest evidence
yet that traditional pregnancy contraindications are PRECAUTIONARY
PRINCIPLE, not evidence-based. Data contradicts traditional claim (AP-00
§1.1: "Never dismiss observations because mechanism is unknown" — but here
we have evidence directly opposing the observation). Challenges B01 S7.3
safety section which lists LI4 as "empirically-contraindicated" without
noting the contradicting evidence.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 12-20 mm perpendicular (standard). Oblique toward PC8
  for deeper stimulation in classical technique.
- De Qi: strong — typically produces distention, soreness, heaviness
  radiating toward index finger or up the forearm. DeQi quality
  correlates with fMRI activation pattern (Hui et al. 2010): DeQi
  sensation → limbic deactivation; sharp pain → reversed pattern.
- EA frequency mapping (Han Jisheng model):
  2 Hz → enkephalin + beta-endorphin (mu/delta receptors) — preferred
  for chronic pain, analgesic preconditioning
  100 Hz → dynorphin (kappa receptors)
  2/100 Hz alternating → all three opioid families, maximum analgesic
  synergy — preferred for perioperative use
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02):
- B02 Tier 1 point (most-studied in acupressure). Self-accessible:
  contralateral hand presses.
- Pressure: firm thumb pressure into the belly of the first dorsal
  interosseous, toward the second metacarpal bone. 30-60 seconds
  sustained pressure is typical protocol.
- Conditions: headache, facial pain, labor pain, general analgesia.
- LI4 acupressure labor pain evidence (Najafi 2018): SMD -0.94.
  Effective during active labor at 4-7 cm cervical dilation.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Cứu (B08):
- Direct or indirect moxa applicable. Not a primary moxa point (unlike
  ST36, BL67). Used with moxa for cold-type headache, common cold with
  wind-cold pattern.
→ Cross-ref: B08

Dry needling (B11):
- First dorsal interosseous trigger point: needle into the muscle belly
  between metacarpals. Local twitch response expected.
- Effects limited to local hand/thumb pain — does NOT reproduce the
  distant facial/headache effects that require activation of the
  supraspinal descending modulation system.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

So sánh: for dental/facial pain, acupuncture (with DeQi) > dry needling
(which addresses only local trigger point). For labor pain, acupressure
(LI4 + SP6 combined) has the most SR evidence. For perioperative analgesia,
EA at 2/100 Hz is the evidence-based technique.

### An toàn

Vascular risk: first dorsal metacarpal artery (branch of radial artery) in
first interosseous space. Avoid if patient is on therapeutic
anticoagulation. Apply pressure post-needling if bleeding occurs.

Neural risk: deep branch of ulnar nerve passes through the first dorsal
interosseous muscle — excessive depth or aggressive manipulation could
damage this motor nerve. Risk is LOW with standard 12-20 mm depth.
Superficial radial nerve branches are sensory — minimal risk.

Pregnancy: **traditionally contraindicated** (one of 5 "forbidden points"
in pregnancy — LI4, SP6, BL60, BL67, GB21). However, modern evidence does
NOT support this contraindication for healthy pregnancy (see §Bằng chứng,
item 4). Carr 2015: >6,700 women, no increased adverse events. Pak 2000:
tocolytic, not oxytocic. Clinical guideline status: still listed as
precautionary in most acupuncture safety guidelines (AACP 2012) despite
contradicting evidence. Practitioners should be aware of both the
traditional caution AND the evidence. LI4 acupressure during active labor
is intentional, beneficial, and supported by SR evidence (reduces pain,
shortens labor, reduces analgesic use).

Infection: standard clean needle technique. Single-use disposable needles.
Adverse events from acupuncture at LI4 are rare and minor across all
published trials.

## LI1 — Thương Dương (商阳, Shāng Yáng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Metal Sound Yang" (商阳) — 商 (Shāng) is the musical note associated
with Metal in the Five Element system, and 陽 (Yang) identifies this
as a Yang channel point. Jing-Well (**Metal**) point of the Large
Intestine channel — the **FIRST YANG Jing-Well** in the Atlas. All
previously documented Well points (SP1, LR1, HT9, PC9, LU11) are
Wood (the Yin assignment). Yang channels assign Metal to the Well
position, reversing the Yin sequence. Metal on a Metal channel =
**HORARY** point (self-resonance: point element coincides with channel
element). Part of the 12 Jing-Well emergency bloodletting set
(十二井穴放血). B12 identified sham contamination at LI1 — EFT
forefinger tapping inadvertently stimulates this point, confounding
sham-controlled studies.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the radial side of the distal
phalanx of the index finger, at the junction of lines drawn along the
radial border of the nail and the base of the nail, approximately
0.1 cun from the radial corner of the nail.

Anatomical landmarks: radial nail corner of the index finger. Mirror
pattern across documented channels: SP1 (medial nail corner, big toe),
LR1 (lateral nail corner, big toe), HT9 (radial nail corner, little
finger), PC9 (tip, middle finger), LU11 (radial nail corner, thumb).
LI1 is at the INDEX finger — the start of the LI channel trajectory.

Needle depth: 1–2 mm shallow puncture or prick to bleed. Standard
Jing-Well technique.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Dorsal digital nerve from superficial branch of radial nerve
  (C6-C7) — primary sensory innervation at dorsal/radial nail corner
- Proper palmar digital nerve from median nerve (C6-C7) — wraps
  around the radial side of the fingertip, contributing to sensory
  supply at the nail fold
- Spinal segments: C6-C7 (dual source: radial + median nerves)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: radial proper digital artery of the index finger (branch of
common palmar digital artery from superficial palmar arch). Rich
subungual capillary plexus at nail bed — anatomical basis for
bloodletting technique, paralleling SP1 and LR1.

Tissue: thin skin tightly adherent to underlying tissue, minimal
subcutaneous fat, no muscle belly. Periosteum of distal phalanx is
the floor.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Jing-Well (Metal)** — 1st of Five Shu on the Large
Intestine channel.

**FIRST YANG FIVE SHU in the Atlas.** Yang channels assign elements
in REVERSED order compared to Yin:
- **Yang:** Well/Metal → Spring/Water → Stream/Wood → River/Fire
  → Sea/Earth
- **Yin:** Well/Wood → Spring/Fire → Stream/Earth → River/Metal
  → Sea/Water

Both trace the Sheng (generating) cycle from different starting
points — Yang from Metal, Yin from Wood.

**Metal on Metal channel = HORARY** — the position where point
element and channel element coincide. Structurally equivalent to
LR1 (Well/Wood on Wood channel) in Yin channels.

**Horary occupies DIFFERENT Five Shu positions on paired channels:**
- LU (Yin Metal): horary = LU8 **River**/Metal (4th position)
- LI (Yang Metal): horary = LI1 **Well**/Metal (1st position)
Same element match, different anatomical location — from forearm
(LU8) to fingertip (LI1).

Yuan-Source: No (LI4)
Luo-Connecting: No (LI6)
Xi-Cleft: No (LI7)
Special: part of the 12 Jing-Well emergency bloodletting set
(十二井穴放血). Nan Jing Ch.68 — "Well points treat fullness below
the Heart" (井主心下满).

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI1 Shangyang (商阳). Jing-Well/Metal of Large
Intestine. Functions: clears heat, benefits the throat, restores
consciousness, revives from collapse. Emergency: pricking to bleed
for loss of consciousness, high fever, stroke. Channel-specific:
sore throat, toothache (Yangming territory).

**2. Trigger point:** No established trigger point at digit tip — no
skeletal muscle present. Consistent with SP1, LR1, HT9, PC9, LU11.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in index finger trauma, paronychia,
subungual hematoma. Not commonly tender in systemic pain conditions. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No Marma at fingertip. Nearest: Kshipra (web
space, ~3 cm proximal) = LI4 correspondence.

**5. Head zone:** C6-C7 dermatome. No visceral correspondence (below
T1-L2 range). Same non-segmental gap as LI4.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Back Arm Line (SBAL) terminates at
the finger extensors — LI1 at the extreme distal tip is beyond
meaningful myofascial station.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data for hand digit tips
(Kim 2017 studied trunk primarily).

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable (digit).

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi partial). Consistent with all
documented Jing-Well points: SP1 (2/8), LR1 (2/8), HT9 (2/8), PC9
(1/8), LU11 (2/8). Low convergence is a STRUCTURAL feature of
digit-tip points — absent muscle bulk eliminates trigger point
correspondence, cupping is physically impossible, Marma systems map
to larger tissue zones.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Lingshu Chapter 1 (Ben Shu): Jing-Well of the Large Intestine.
  "Where Qi emerges is called Well" (所出为井).
- Nan Jing Ch.68: "Well points treat fullness below the Heart"
  (井主心下满) — shared function across all 12 Jing-Well points.
- Jiayi Jing (Huangfu Mi, 259 CE): sore throat, deafness, toothache,
  jaw swelling, finger numbness, febrile disease with irritability.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: sore throat, toothache, epistaxis, lockjaw
  (trismus), febrile disease, loss of consciousness.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Clears heat, benefits throat: sore throat, tonsillitis, pharyngitis
- Restores consciousness: fainting, stroke, coma (Jing-Well emergency)
- Activates channel: toothache, jaw pain, finger numbness, hand
  swelling
- Additional: deafness/tinnitus, epistaxis, febrile disease

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- Part of the emergency 12 Jing-Well bloodletting protocol — pricking
  all 12 Well points for stroke/coma/seizure.
- LI1 + LU11: paired Well points from Interior-Exterior channels
  (LI-LU) for acute throat conditions.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Emergency consciousness restoration (12 Jing-Well) — 🔴
Unvalidated for LI1 individually**
Multi-center RCTs for 12 Jing-Well bloodletting in acute ischemic
stroke exist (Zhang et al.), but LI1's individual contribution within
the 12-point protocol cannot be isolated. The technique is studied as
a CLASS intervention.

**2. Sham contamination finding (EFT methodology) — not an efficacy
claim**
B12 documented that EFT sham conditions involving forefinger tapping
inadvertently stimulate LI1, confounding sham comparisons. The
"inactive" control may deliver real acupoint stimulation.
Methodological implication: any sham protocol involving index finger
contact should account for LI1 stimulation.
→ Cross-ref: B12

**3. Sore throat / pharyngitis — 🔴 Unvalidated**
Traditionally combined with LU11/LU10 for throat conditions, but
no study isolates LI1's contribution.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No specific sensitization data. Nail fold region is richly innervated
(high baseline receptor density in subungual tissue) but
pathology-dependent dynamic change at LI1 is unstudied.

### Cơ chế giải thích

A-delta/C fiber activation from nail fold pricking → rapid ascending
signal to brainstem via spinothalamic and spinoreticular tracts. The
Jing-Well emergency function (consciousness restoration) likely
operates through arousal via reticular activating system — nociceptive
alerting, not point-specific mechanism.

Non-segmental challenge: C6-C7 → throat/face effects require
supraspinal relay. Mechanism presumed similar to LI4 (ascending →
PAG/RVM → descending modulation → trigeminal nucleus caudalis) but
unvalidated at LI1.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Bloodletting: PRIMARY technique. Lancet prick → squeeze 3–10 drops
blood from nail fold capillaries. Emergency use: loss of
consciousness, high fever.

Acupuncture: 1–2 mm shallow perpendicular insertion. Avoid periosteal
contact. De Qi: mild tingling/pricking at fingertip.

Moxa: direct moxa (small cones) classically indicated at Well points
for cold-type conditions. Not the primary technique at LI1.

### An toàn

LOW risk. Finger tip — no major vessels or nerves at depth.

Precautions:
- Avoid periosteal contact (sharp pain)
- Nail fold proximity increases infection risk in paronychia
- Caution in diabetes/peripheral vascular disease (poor wound healing
  at extremities)
- Bloodletting: sterile lancet, adequate hemostasis post-procedure

## LI2 — Nhị Gian (二间, Èr Jiān)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Second Space" (二间) — the 2nd of three hand points in the gap (间)
series. Ying-Spring (**Water**) point of the Large Intestine channel.
In the Yang Five Shu sequence, Water occupies the Spring position (vs
Fire in Yin channels). Metal generates Water in the Sheng cycle
(金生水), making Water the child of Metal — therefore LI2 is the
**SEDATION** point of the LI channel (Nan Jing: "for excess, sedate
the child" 实则泻其子). Located at the radial side of the index
finger, distal to the 2nd metacarpophalangeal joint, at the
red-white skin junction (赤白肉际). The Ying-Spring position also
carries: "Spring points treat heat in the body" (荥主身热, Nan Jing
Ch.68) — dual logic of SEDATION (Five Element role) + HEAT CLEARING
(positional function).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the radial side of the index
finger, in the depression distal to the 2nd metacarpophalangeal
joint, at the junction of the red and white skin (赤白肉际).

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the radial condyle of the proximal
phalanx base at the 2nd MCP joint. The point is in the depression
just distal to the joint, on the radial aspect where thick palmar
(red/glabrous) skin meets thinner dorsal (white/hairy) skin. LI1 is
~2–3 cm distal (fingertip). LI3 is on the opposite side of the MCP
joint (proximal to metacarpal head).

Needle depth: 5–8 mm perpendicular or oblique. Limited by periosteum
of proximal phalanx.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Dorsal digital nerve from superficial branch of radial nerve
  (C6-C7) — sensory on dorsal/radial aspect
- Proper palmar digital nerve from median nerve (C6-C7) — sensory
  on palmar aspect
- At the red-white skin junction: dual nerve territory overlap
  (radial dorsal + median palmar), same transition pattern as
  LU10 thenar and SP1-SP3 medial foot
- Spinal segments: C6-C7
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: thin subcutaneous tissue over periosteum. At the
red-white skin junction — transition from glabrous palmar skin
(Meissner's + Pacinian corpuscles) to hairy dorsal skin (free nerve
endings). Distinct mechanoreceptor profile at this boundary.

Vascular: proper palmar digital artery (radial side). Small caliber.
Low vascular risk.

Muscle/tissue: no muscle belly at this level — the 1st dorsal
interosseous does not extend this far distally. Thin skin over joint
capsule, tendon sheaths, periosteum.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Ying-Spring (Water)** — 2nd of Five Shu on the Large
Intestine channel. First Yang Ying-Spring in the Atlas.

**Water on Metal channel = SEDATION** — Metal generates Water in the
Sheng cycle (金生水). Water is the CHILD of Metal. Nan Jing:
"for excess, sedate the child" (实则泻其子) = needle the child
element to drain excess from the parent.

**Comparison with Ying-Spring across documented channels:**
- LU10 (Yin Metal): Spring/**Fire** → Ke Controller (Fire controls
  Metal)
- LI2 (Yang Metal): Spring/**Water** → Sedation (Metal generates
  Water)
Same Five Shu POSITION on paired channels, DIFFERENT elements,
DIFFERENT Five Element roles.

Previously documented Ying-Spring points — ALL Yin = Fire:
- HT8, PC8 (Fire/Fire) = Horary
- LR2 (Fire/Wood) = Sedation
- SP2 (Fire/Earth) = Tonification
- LU10 (Fire/Metal) = Ke Controller
LI2 is the first Spring point at Water — a Yang-specific element.

Also: Nan Jing Ch.68 — "Spring points treat heat in the body"
(荥主身热). Position-based function, independent of element. LI2
carries dual logic: SEDATION (Five Element) + HEAT CLEARING
(positional).

Yuan-Source: No (LI4)
Luo-Connecting: No (LI6)
Xi-Cleft: No (LI7)

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI2 Erjian (二间). Ying-Spring/Water of Large
Intestine. Functions: clears Yangming Heat, benefits throat and teeth,
reduces swelling. Primary use: excess Yangming conditions — facial
swelling, toothache, epistaxis, sore throat.

**2. Trigger point:** No trigger point — no muscle belly at finger
base level. First dorsal interosseous (→ LI4) is 4–5 cm proximal.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in 2nd MCP joint arthritis

Also reactive in finger trauma. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No Marma at finger joint. Kshipra (web space)

**5. Head zone:** C6-C7 dermatome. No visceral correspondence.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SBAL distal terminus — not a meaningful

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data (hand/digit area).

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable (digit).

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi partial). Same digit-point
pattern as LI1.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Lingshu Chapter 1 (Ben Shu): Ying-Spring of the Large Intestine.
  "Where Qi glides is called Spring" (所溜为荥).
- Nan Jing Ch.68: "Spring points treat heat in the body" (荥主身热).
- Jiayi Jing: sore throat, epistaxis, toothache, jaw swelling,

- Zhenjiu Dacheng: toothache (primary), sore throat, epistaxis, eye
  pain, jaw stiffness.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Clears heat: sore throat, toothache, gum pain (Yangming territory)
- Reduces swelling: facial edema, jaw swelling
- Activates channel: finger stiffness, hand pain
- Additional: epistaxis, blurred vision, shoulder/arm pain

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- As SEDATION point: selected for LI channel excess (facial
  inflammation, constipation with heat). In practice, LI4 and LI11
  dominate — LI2 is among the least individually selected LI points
  in modern clinical practice.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review, no RCT for LI2 as an individual point. All
Yangming face/mouth protocols study LI4 or LI11 — LI2 is not
selected in modern clinical trials.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

**Sedation point evidence desert:** LI2 extends the pattern — NO

sedation function. Previously: LR2 (🔴), SP5 (🔴), LU5 (tested but
not for sedation). The "sedate the child" principle (实则泻其子) is
among the most taught treatment strategies in TCM education, yet zero
empirical validation exists at the point-specific level.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No sensitization data. The 2nd MCP joint region becomes tender in
rheumatoid arthritis (Ashi/joint pathology), not a channel-specific
phenomenon.

### Cơ chế giải thích

data specific to LI2.

pathways (as Five Element theory predicts) or produces identical
effects (suggesting the categorical system is organizational, not
physiological) is one of the largest unresolved questions in
acupuncture research.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 5–8 mm perpendicular or oblique. Joint capsule and
periosteum nearby — shallow. De Qi: mild tingling/distension.

Bloodletting: Ying-Spring bloodletting for acute Yangming Heat —
less common than Jing-Well technique but classically documented.

Moxa: generally NOT selected — LI2 is a heat-clearing/sedation

### An toàn

LOW risk. Finger joint level — no major structures at standard depth.

Precautions:
- Avoid periosteal contact and joint space penetration
- Caution in MCP joint arthritis (inflamed capsule)

## LI3 — Tam Gian (三间, Sān Jiān)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Third Space" (三间) — the 3rd in the gap series, completing the
three distal LI hand points before the web space (LI4). Shu-Stream
(**Wood**) point of the Large Intestine channel. In the Yang sequence,
Wood occupies the Stream position (vs Earth in Yin channels). Metal
controls Wood in the Ke cycle (金克木), making Wood the element that
Metal RESTRAINS — therefore LI3 is the **Ke-cycle subject** point, the
element over which the channel exerts controlling influence. Located
at the radial side of the hand, proximal to the head of the 2nd
metacarpal, at the red-white skin junction. Shu-Stream position
carries: "Stream points treat heaviness in the body and joint pain"
(输主体重节痛, Nan Jing Ch.68). For Yang channels specifically,
Nan Jing Ch.68 adds: "Stream points also treat intermittent diseases"
(时病) — a Yang-exclusive function not carried by Yin Shu-Stream
points.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the radial side of the 2nd
metacarpal bone, in the depression proximal to the head of the 2nd
metacarpal, at the junction of the red and white skin.

Anatomical landmarks: the 2nd MCP joint is the knuckle of the index
finger. LI3 is in the depression just proximal to the metacarpal
head, on the radial side. LI2 is distal (proximal phalanx base).
LI4 is ~2–3 cm proximal (web space, mid-2nd metacarpal). Three points
share the radial border of the 2nd ray: LI2 (phalanx) → LI3
(metacarpal head) → LI4 (metacarpal midshaft).

Needle depth: 8–15 mm perpendicular. Greater tissue depth than
LI1/LI2 — the metacarpal area has more subcutaneous tissue and the
beginning of the first dorsal interosseous muscle mass.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Dorsal digital nerve from superficial branch of radial nerve
  (C6-C7) — primary sensory innervation
- Proper palmar digital nerve from median nerve (C6-C7) — palmar
  contribution

  interosseous muscle, innervated by the deep branch of the ulnar
  nerve (C8-T1)
- Spinal segments: C6-C7 (sensory), potentially C8-T1 (motor at
  depth)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Transition zone from thin dorsal hand skin to the first dorsal
interosseous muscle bulk — beginning the tissue characteristics
prominent at LI4.

Vascular: 2nd dorsal metacarpal artery (from dorsal carpal arch,
branch of radial artery) runs in the interosseous space. Small
caliber — low risk.

- Skin (red-white junction) → dorsal subcutaneous tissue
- Edge of first dorsal interosseous muscle (lateral border)

"For Yang channels, Stream points also treat intermittent diseases"

Yuan-Source: No (LI4)
Luo-Connecting: No (LI6)
Xi-Cleft: No (LI7)

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

## LI5 — Dương Khê (阳溪, Yáng Xī)

### Vị trí giải phẫu

Overall: **MODERATE** — safe with standard perpendicular shallow

radial nerve. First LI point with elevated safety profile vs the

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review, no RCT for LI7 as an individual point. Acute

— LI7 is not included in any published protocol.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

**Yang Xi-Cleft evidence desert:** LI7 confirms the pattern — 6 of

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No specific sensitization data. Whether Yang Xi-Cleft points
sensitize during acute Yangming conditions (acute toothache, facial
inflammation) has not been studied.

Prediction from theory: if the Xi-Cleft hypothesis is correct
(Qi accumulates deeply during acute conditions), LI7 should show
measurable sensitization (lowered pressure pain threshold, increased
conductance) during acute LI-related pathology. SP8 showed
sensitization during dysmenorrhea (Chen 2015) — the only Xi-Cleft

→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Local effects (forearm/arm pain):** Segmentally explicable.

**Non-segmental effects (acute toothache, facial swelling):** Same

LI7.

**Yin-Yang Xi-Cleft mechanism hypothesis:** If Yin Xi-Cleft →
blood (via sympathetic → vascular control) and Yang Xi-Cleft →
inflammation (via sympathetic → immune modulation), the
differentiation may have an autonomic basis: Yin channels follow
arteries (vascular proximity → vascular effects), Yang channels
follow nerves in inter-muscular septa (neural proximity → neural/
inflammatory effects). Speculative (🔴) but anatomically grounded.

Gap: no mechanism data for any Xi-Cleft point explains WHY Qi
"accumulates" at this specific location. The Xi-Cleft concept
remains theoretical without physiological correlate.

## LI10 — Thủ Tam Lý (手三里, Shǒu Sān Lǐ)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Hand Three Li" — posterior-radial forearm, 2 cun distal to LI11.
The naming parallel with ST36 足三里 (Foot Three Li, Zusanli) is
deliberate: both are Yangming channel points, both are among the
most clinically important points on their respective channels, and
both are associated with general strengthening and GI regulation.
三里 (Three Li) carries historical weight — ST36 is arguably the
most famous acupoint in the canon. LI10 inherits the naming
convention as the upper-limb counterpart. Classical interpretation:
the point treats three regions (三 = three, 里 = village/region),
or restores strength to the arm — allowing one to "use the arm for
three li more." No special point category designation despite high
clinical importance. Posterior interosseous nerve territory (C7-C8),
with lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve superficially. The most
proximal of the forearm points before LI11 at the elbow.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterolateral aspect of
the forearm, on the line connecting LI5 (Yangxi) and LI11 (Quchi),
2 cun below the transverse cubital crease.

Anatomical landmarks: 2 cun distal to the lateral epicondyle, in
the muscular groove between extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL)
and extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB). Palpation: at the most
prominent part of the extensor muscle mass when the forearm is
pronated and the fist is clenched — the muscle belly fills out
distinctly at this level, providing a clear palpatory landmark
unlike the more distal LI8-LI9. 1 cun distal to LI9, 2 cun distal
to LI11.

Needle depth: 15–30 mm perpendicular. Greater muscle depth than
LI8-LI9 due to the thicker extensor mass proximally.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Superficial: lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve
  (musculocutaneous, C5-C6) — primary cutaneous innervation at
  the radial forearm. Posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve
  (C5-C8) on the posterior aspect.
- Deep: posterior interosseous nerve (deep branch of radial nerve,
  C7-C8) — runs between supinator heads at this level, supplying
  motor branches to the extensor compartment. The radial nerve
  trunk bifurcation (superficial + deep branches) occurs at the
  radial head, only 1-2 cun proximal — LI10 is close to this
  transition.
- Spinal segments: C5-C8
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular: radial recurrent artery — ascending branch from the
radial artery coursing proximally toward the lateral epicondyle.
More significant vascular supply than distal forearm points.
Muscular branches of the radial artery.

Fascia: robust inter-muscular septum between ECRL and ECRB. Deep
fascia well-developed at this proximal level — thicker and more
organized than at mid-forearm.
→ Cross-ref: A03, A04

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue → between ECRL and ECRB
→ supinator muscle → periosteum of radius.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No (closest: LI5 River/Fire distal, LI11 He-Sea/Earth
proximal).
Yuan-Source: No (LI4).
Luo-Connecting: No (LI6).
Xi-Cleft: No (LI7).

No special category designation. LI10's elevated clinical
importance derives from functional tradition and naming convention,
not from the formal TCM category system. Compare ST36: He-Sea,
Ma Dan-yang Star, Command Point, Lower He-Sea of Stomach —
multiple category designations backing the clinical tradition.
LI10 has none. The naming parallel creates a clinical expectation
without the theoretical infrastructure.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI10 Shousanli (手三里). Functions: regulates
Stomach and Intestines, activates the channel and alleviates pain,
clears channel obstruction. Primary indications: abdominal pain,
vomiting, diarrhea, toothache, swelling of cheek, arm-shoulder
pain, lateral epicondylitis, upper limb motor impairment,
hemiplegia. One of the most frequently selected forearm points in
modern TCM practice.

**2. Trigger point:** ECRL and ECRB trigger points at proximal
forearm — strongest TrP correspondence on the LI forearm segment.
ECRL TrP: referred pain to lateral epicondyle, dorsal wrist, and
dorsal web space (overlaps with LI4 referral zone). Dorsher: high
correspondence for lateral elbow pain. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Highly reactive in lateral
epicondylitis patients. LI10 tenderness is a recognized clinical
finding in tennis elbow — palpation 2 cun distal to the lateral
epicondyle is a classic examination point for extensor origin
pathology. Strong correspondence.

**4. Marma point:** Approaching Kurpara Marma (कूर्पर, elbow
joint). Kurpara is centered on the elbow proper (LI11 territory),
but its anatomical zone extends 1-2 cun distally. Weak-moderate
correspondence. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** C5-C7 dermatome. No direct large intestine
viscerotome overlap (T10-L1), but Yangming clinical tradition
claims GI effects from this non-segmental location.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SBAL — proximal extensor compartment.
Transition zone toward the Deep Back Arm Line (DBAL) as the
supinator muscle contributes to deeper rotational mechanics.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No published data specific
to LI10. However, the proximal extensor mass is a clinically
recognized zone of neural sensitization in lateral epicondylitis —
lowered pressure pain threshold documented in multiple studies,
though not framed as "neurogenic spots."

**8. Cupping point:** Applicable. Proximal forearm offers adequate
surface for standard cup. Used clinically for arm pain and
stiffness. 🟡

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi, Cupping/Marma partial).
Highest forearm convergence on LI — the named muscular landmark
attracts cross-tradition overlap. Consistent with the Atlas pattern:
convergence clusters at anatomical landmarks, not along homogeneous

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Lateral epicondylitis — 🔴 Unvalidated (LI10-specific)**
Systematic reviews of acupuncture for lateral epicondylitis
(Cochrane, updated analyses) find modest evidence for acupuncture
at the lateral elbow region, but protocols always use multi-point
combinations (LI10 + LI11 + LI4 ± local Ashi points). No study
isolates LI10's individual contribution.
Confidence: 🔴 — included in positive protocols but never tested
alone.

**2. Post-stroke upper limb motor recovery — 🔴 Unvalidated
(LI10-specific)**
Multiple RCTs and systematic reviews test acupuncture for
post-stroke motor recovery using standard point combinations
that include LI10 (typically LI4, LI10, LI11, LI15, TE5). Some
show modest benefit over control; others find no difference.
LI10's individual contribution cannot be isolated.
Confidence: 🔴 — combination-only evidence.

**3. GI conditions (abdominal pain, diarrhea) — 🔴 Unvalidated**
No controlled evidence for LI10's GI indications individually.
ST36 dominates the Yangming GI evidence base.
Confidence: 🔴

**LI10 × ST36 evidence asymmetry:** Despite sharing the Three Li
naming convention and the Yangming family, the evidence profiles
diverge completely. ST36 has extensive RCT data, a characterized
vagal-splanchnic mechanism (Liu S et al., Nature 2020, PROKR2+
neurons), and multiple special category designations. LI10 has
zero individual RCTs and no mechanism study. The naming parallel
creates an expectation of equivalence that the evidence does not
support.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No sensitization data specific to LI10. The proximal extensor
mass at LI10 is clinically known to be tender in lateral
epicondylitis — lowered pressure pain threshold in the extensor
muscle group is well-documented in tennis elbow research, though
not framed as "dynamic acupoint" sensitization.

Whether LI10 sensitizes during GI pathology (its Yangming
indication) is unknown. If the dynamic acupoint hypothesis
extends to Yang channels, LI10 should show measurable
sensitization during acute GI conditions. No study has tested
this.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Local effects (arm/elbow pain, lateral epicondylitis):**
Segmentally explicable. C5-C8 innervation at LI10 → gate control
for local MSK pain. Posterior interosseous nerve stimulation at
the extensor muscle mass → motor modulation + local circulatory
effects via axon reflex. Deep needling into the extensor muscle
belly produces local twitch responses — functionally equivalent
to dry needling at ECRL/ECRB trigger points (B11 correspondence).

**Non-segmental effects (GI, toothache, facial swelling):**
Same supraspinal pathway as all LI forearm points: C5-C8 →
ascending → brainstem → (1) trigeminal modulation for face/mouth
indications, (2) vagal/NTS relay for GI modulation. No mechanism
data specific to LI10.

**LI10 × ST36 mechanism comparison:**
- ST36: tibial nerve (L4-S3) → vagal relay via NTS → splanchnic
  anti-inflammatory pathway (Liu S et al., 2020, Nature). PROKR2+
  neurons in DRG identified as the specific afferent population.
- LI10: radial nerve territory (C5-C8) → supraspinal relay pathway
  not characterized. Different spinal entry (cervical vs lumbar),
  different afferent nerve (radial vs tibial).
- Prediction: if both work through vagal-splanchnic pathways,
  LI10 activates NTS via a different afferent route (cervical →
  brainstem vs lumbar → brainstem). Whether radial nerve afferents
  engage the same PROKR2+ pathway is unknown.

**Gap:** no mechanism study, no fMRI, no fiber-type data for LI10.
The "Hand Three Li = Foot Three Li" functional equivalence is
clinically traditional but mechanistically unverified.

## LI11 — Khúc Trì (曲池, Qū Chí)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**
The flagship point of the lateral elbow — one of the 4-5 most clinically
prescribed acupoints worldwide, alongside LI4, ST36, PC6, and SP6.
He-Sea/Earth point of the Large Intestine channel (Tonification point:
Earth is mother of Metal). Twelfth of Sun Si-miao's 13 Ghost Points
(鬼臣, Ghost Minister). One of the Ma Dan-yang Twelve Heavenly Star
Points. THE primary acupoint for dermatological conditions (urticaria,
eczema, pruritus) when combined with SP10 as the SP10+LI11 dermatology
pair. The most frequently selected acupoint in hypertension acupuncture
research — consistently the top choice across multiple systematic reviews.
With LI4, forms the channel's two-pillar structure: LI4 (distal/command,
face-mouth specialty) and LI11 (proximal/treatment, skin-fever specialty)
— both are Ma Dan-yang Stars. Kuo 2015 demonstrated that needling LI4
increases blood flow at LI11, establishing an intra-channel vascular
axis. Name 曲池 (Bent Pool) describes the depression at the elbow crease
when the arm is flexed — the "pool" formed at the bend.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): at the lateral end of the transverse
cubital crease, at the midpoint between LU5 (Chize) and the lateral
epicondyle of the humerus, when the elbow is fully flexed.

Anatomical landmarks: with the elbow flexed at 90°, locate the lateral
end of the transverse cubital crease. The point lies in the depression
between the lateral epicondyle of the humerus and the radial head, at
the origin of the extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL). Alternatively:
midpoint of the line connecting the lateral epicondyle and the radial
styloid process at the elbow crease.

Needle depth range: 15–25 mm perpendicular insertion. Oblique insertion
toward the medial epicondyle in classical technique for deeper
stimulation. The radial nerve trunk passes 1.3–3.3 cm below the skin
surface at this location.

Architectural note: LI11 is the TERMINAL point of the elbow cluster
(LI8→LI9→LI10→LI11) and the clinical importance apex of the LI
forearm. The forearm's 12-cun span from wrist (LI5) to elbow (LI11)
is organized as two clusters — wrist cluster (LI5-LI6-LI7, special
categories) and elbow cluster (LI8-LI9-LI10-LI11, clinical gradient)
— separated by a 3-cun mid-forearm gap. LI11 is the convergence point
where anatomical landmark (lateral epicondyle), functional importance,
and special category designations all peak.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Radial nerve trunk (C5-T1) — passes deep through the lateral
  intermuscular septum at this level, 1.3–3.3 cm below skin. The radial
  nerve at LI11 carries BOTH sensory and motor fibers before bifurcating
  into superficial (sensory) and deep (posterior interosseous, motor)
  branches just distal to this point at the supinator arcade. LI11 thus
  accesses the radial nerve BEFORE its division — the only LI forearm
  point with access to the undivided trunk.
- Lateral cutaneous nerve of forearm (musculocutaneous nerve, C5-C6) —
  superficial sensory branch providing cutaneous innervation of the
  lateral elbow.
- Posterior cutaneous nerve of forearm (radial nerve, C5-C8) — sensory
  branch in the superficial layer.
- Spinal segments: C5-T1 (broadest spinal access of any LI forearm
  point — extending one segment beyond the C5-C8 range of LI8-LI10)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**NEURAL ARCHITECTURE NOTE:** LI11 is positioned at the radial nerve's
PRE-BIFURCATION zone — proximal to the supinator arcade where the nerve
splits into superficial (sensory) and deep (motor) branches. This gives
LI11 access to the COMPLETE radial nerve fiber population: all sensory
AND all motor fibers destined for both branches. Points distal to this
bifurcation (LI5-LI10) access only ONE branch. The clinical importance
gradient (LI8→LI9→LI10→LI11) may reflect increasing nerve fiber
population accessible at each point level.

Fascial plane: at the junction of the lateral intermuscular septum and
the common extensor tendon origin. The lateral intermuscular septum
separates the anterior (brachialis, brachioradialis) and posterior
(triceps, anconeus) compartments. LI11 penetrates through this fascial
boundary.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular:
- Radial recurrent artery (branch of radial artery) — ascending artery
  at the lateral elbow contributing to the periarticular anastomotic
  network.
- Radial collateral artery (branch of deep brachial/profunda brachii
  artery) — descending from the upper arm.
- These vessels form part of the lateral periarticular anastomosis,
  making the lateral elbow a moderately vascular site.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle/tendon layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (lateral cutaneous nerve of forearm)
- Brachioradialis muscle (origin area)
- Extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL) — origin from lateral
  supracondylar ridge and lateral epicondyle
- Common extensor tendon origin (lateral epicondyle) — deep
- Supinator muscle (deeper still) — the posterior interosseous nerve
  passes through this muscle's proximal edge (supinator arcade/Frohse)
- Radial nerve trunk at depth

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Hypertension — 🟡 Plausible**

Systematic review (Frontiers Cardiovasc Med, 2023 — 12 RCTs, 1,466
elderly participants): acupuncture alone had comparable efficacy to
antihypertensive drugs for SBP reduction (MD: -4.85 mmHg, 95% CI:
-10.39 to -0.69). Acupuncture PLUS drugs significantly outperformed
drugs alone: SBP MD **-9.81** mmHg (95% CI: -13.56 to -6.06); DBP MD
**-7.04** mmHg (95% CI: -10.83 to -3.24). **LI11 was the most
frequently used acupoint, appearing in 9 of 12 studies** — more
frequent than ST36 (7), LI4 (8), SP6 (8), or GV20 (8). Quality: low
methodological quality (inadequate blinding, allocation concealment).

Additional SR (Eur J Integrative Med, 2022): acupuncture for grade 1
hypertension. LI11 again the most common acupoint.

2026 SR (Blood Pressure journal, RCTs 2015-2024): updated analysis of
acupuncture for primary hypertension. LI11 remains the most frequently
selected point.

Clinical observation (Litscher 2014, Evid Based Complement Alternat
Med): bilateral LI11 stimulation in hypertensive patients produced
immediate significant heart rate decrease — acute autonomic modulation.

Mechanism — preclinical: EA at LI11 in spontaneously hypertensive rats
significantly lowered BP, inhibited enhanced renal sympathetic nerve
activity, increased baroreflex sensitivity (BRS), and attenuated
cardiac hypertrophy. Mechanism: downregulation of p47phox mRNA and
protein in RVLM (rostral ventrolateral medulla) — reducing NADPH
oxidase-driven oxidative stress in the brainstem sympathetic control
center. BDNF upregulation in RVLM also observed. Critically, EA at
**LI15 (same channel, shoulder level) produced NO significant
antihypertensive effect** — demonstrating POINT SPECIFICITY.

Confidence: 🟡 — consistent SR data (LI11 as most-selected point in
positive protocols), characterized preclinical mechanism with point
specificity control. But LI11's individual clinical contribution never
isolated from multi-point protocols. Low quality of included RCTs.
→ Cross-ref: A02 (sympathetic inhibition), LI4 entry §Autonomic

**2. Chronic spontaneous urticaria — 🟡 Plausible**

Systematic review (2025, 22 RCTs, 1,867 patients): acupuncture
significantly improved overall response rate (RR **1.20**, 95% CI:
1.15-1.26), reduced recurrence (RR **0.33**, 95% CI: 0.20-0.53),
improved UAS7 score (MD -3.30, 95% CI: -5.26 to -1.34), and reduced
itch severity (SMD **-1.35**, 95% CI: -2.02 to -0.68). **LI11
appeared in 13-14 of 22 studies** — most frequently selected. Quality:
moderate-to-low (only 5 studies with proper blinding).

Preclinical mechanism 1 — IL-33/ST2 pathway: Wang et al. 2023 (Zhen
Ci Yan Jiu): EA at LI11 + SP10 (2 Hz/15 Hz, 1 mA, 20 min daily ×
7 days) in rat urticaria model reduced mast cell degranulation,
decreased IL-33/ST2 pathway activation, lowered histamine release.
n=8/group.
→ Cross-ref: SP10 entry §Bằng chứng (same study)

Preclinical mechanism 2 — Complement pathway: 2026 study (Frontiers
Immunol): EA at LI11 + SP10 in passive cutaneous anaphylaxis model
reduced wheal areas, scratching frequency, histamine, and IgE.
Suppressed **C3a/C5a-C3aR/C5aR complement axis** in skin tissue
(western blot, p<0.01). Serum IL-6, TNF-α, serotonin all significantly
reduced (p<0.0001). Multi-omics: 175 differentially expressed proteins
(proteomics), 447 differential metabolites (metabolomics) — primarily
lipid metabolism and arachidonic acid pathways. n=10/group, exploratory.

Antipruritic mechanism: cold stimulation at LI11 in mice (2014, BMC
Complement Med) showed effects on persistent itch and skin inflammation
via blockade of serotonin 5-HT2 and 5-HT7 receptors.

Confidence: 🟡 — positive SR with effect sizes (RR 1.20 efficacy, RR
0.33 recurrence) + two preclinical mechanism studies identifying
distinct molecular pathways (IL-33/ST2, complement C3a/C5a, serotonin).
SP10+LI11 is now the MOST mechanistically characterized point pair in
the Atlas. Clinical evidence always multi-point.

**3. Atopic dermatitis / eczema — 🟡 Plausible (combination)**

Zhang et al. 2024 (SR/MA, PMC11624827): acupuncture for atopic
dermatitis. SP10, LI11, ST36 = three most frequently used acupoints.
Concluded acupuncture "may be helpful for treating atopic dermatitis
with no indication of serious potential harm." Individual contribution
of LI11 not isolated.

Confidence: 🟡 — consistently included in positive protocols with
plausible mechanism (IL-33/ST2 and complement pathways relevant to
atopic dermatitis), but individual effect unknown.

**4. Lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow) — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Systematic review (Evid Based Complement Alternat Med, 2016 — 4 RCTs,
309 participants): poor methodological quality, GRADE very low to low.
"No firm conclusion can be drawn."

Data mining analysis (J Pain Research, 2025): LI11 was 2nd most
frequent point (14.8%, 29 prescriptions), LI11+LI10 most supported
pair. Core: Ashi + LI4 + LI11 + LI10 + TE5.

Confidence: 🔴 — despite being a universal clinical choice and
sharing its exact location with the common extensor TrP, SR evidence
too weak. Evidence-importance paradox: one of the most clinically
used combinations has the weakest evidence base.

**5. Post-stroke motor recovery — 🔴 Unvalidated (LI11-specific)**

Multiple RCTs test acupuncture for post-stroke motor recovery using
combinations that include LI11 (typically LI4, LI10, LI11, LI15,
TE5). Individual contribution not isolated.
Confidence: 🔴

**6. Fever / anti-inflammatory — 🔴 Unvalidated**

LI11's traditional primary function ("clears Heat") has no dedicated
controlled study.
Confidence: 🔴

**Sham comparison note:** LI11 shares the LI4 pattern: effects vs
usual-care but minimal sham-controlled data. Most hypertension and
urticaria SRs compare acupuncture + drugs vs drugs alone. The
preclinical point-specificity finding (LI11 effective, LI15 not) is
the strongest location-dependent evidence at this point.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No specific sensitization study for LI11. The lateral epicondylar
region is well-known to exhibit lowered pressure pain thresholds (PPT)
in lateral epicondylitis patients — the defining clinical sign of
tennis elbow. Whether this constitutes "dynamic acupoint sensitization"
(C04 S4) versus general tendinopathy sensitization is an open question.
If LI11 sensitizes during Large Intestine-associated conditions (GI
inflammation, skin conditions) rather than only local MSK pathology,
that would support the dynamic acupoint hypothesis for He-Sea points.
No study has tested this.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at LI11:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): radial nerve trunk (C5-T1) — the
  broadest spinal segment access of any LI forearm point. Pre-
  bifurcation position: full sensory + motor fiber population.
- A02 (Autonomic Nervous System): sympathetic modulation via RVLM
  p47phox downregulation and BRS enhancement (preclinical). The
  pathway at LI11 parallels LI4's sympathetic effects (A02 S4.3) but
  targets a DIFFERENT brainstem nucleus: LI11 → RVLM vs LI4 → PAG.
- A03 (Fascia-Connective Tissue): lateral intermuscular septum, SBAL
  station — fascial force transmission node.
- A04 (Vascular-Lymphatic): radial recurrent and radial collateral
  arteries. Kuo 2015: LI4 needling increases blood flow AT LI11,
  establishing this point as a RECEIVER of distant vascular effects.
- A06 (Chemical Signaling): IL-33/ST2 pathway modulation (Wang 2023,
  with SP10). Complement C3a/C5a suppression (2026, with SP10).
  5-HT2/5-HT7 receptor-mediated antipruritic effect.
- A07 (Pain-Nociception): gate control at C5-T1 for local MSK pain.
  Common extensor TrP = standard trigger point treatment site.

**Central mechanism question 1 — WHY does an elbow point (C5-T1)
lower systemic blood pressure?**

C5-T1 afferents enter the spinal cord at the cervical-thoracic
junction. T1 overlaps cardiac sympathetic outflow (T1-T4). But blood
pressure regulation depends on sympathetic outflow from the ENTIRE
intermediolateral column (IML, T1-L2).

Resolution — supraspinal sympathoinhibitory pathway (preclinical):

```
Step 1: EA at LI11 activates radial nerve afferents (C5-T1)
Step 2: Ascending signals reach RVLM (rostral ventrolateral medulla)
        — primary brainstem center controlling sympathetic outflow
Step 3: In RVLM: ↓ p47phox (NADPH oxidase subunit) → ↓ oxidative
        stress → ↓ sympathoexcitatory neuron activity
Step 4: ↑ BDNF in RVLM → neuroprotective effect
Step 5: ↑ Baroreflex sensitivity → improved cardiovascular reflex
Step 6: Net: ↓ sympathetic outflow → ↓ HR, ↓ BP, ↓ renal SNA

Point specificity: LI15 (same channel, shoulder) = NO effect
→ mechanism is NOT generic channel stimulation
```

**Central mechanism question 2 — WHY does an elbow point treat
systemic skin conditions?**

Same non-segmental problem as SP10 (L2-L4): whole-body dermatological
effects require systemic mechanisms. Preclinical evidence:
1. Immunomodulatory: IL-33/ST2 pathway + complement C3a/C5a
   suppression → ↓ mast cell degranulation, ↓ histamine
2. Serotonergic: 5-HT2/5-HT7 receptor blockade (antipruritic)

Both are supraspinal. The SP10+LI11 pair engages TWO spinal entry
zones (SP10: femoral L2-L4 + LI11: radial C5-T1), possibly providing
complementary supraspinal inputs for systemic immunomodulation.

**Four supraspinal pathways now documented in Atlas:**
- ST36 → NTS → vagal-splanchnic (PROKR2+, Nature 2020)
- PC6 → NTS → GABA → DMV (vagal)
- LI4 → PAG → descending modulation → TNC
- LI11 → RVLM → sympathoinhibition (↓ p47phox)

LI4 and LI11 share the SAME peripheral nerve (radial) but target
DIFFERENT brainstem nuclei. The peripheral nerve is necessary but not
sufficient — the POINT LOCATION on the nerve determines which central
pathway is engaged.

**Gap 1:** Ascending pathway from radial nerve (C5-T1) to RVLM is
anatomically plausible (spinoreticular projections) but specific relay
neurons not mapped.

**Gap 2:** How EA parameters at LI11 differentially activate RVLM-
mediated (sympathoinhibition) vs PAG-mediated (descending analgesia,
as at LI4) pathways. Both use radial nerve afferents. Possible:
fiber type recruitment (motor at LI11 vs sensory at LI4), EA
frequency differences, or separate central representations of
proximal vs distal limb afferents.

**Gap 3:** Ghost Point mechanism at LI11. No psychiatric-specific
mechanism identified. Sympathoinhibitory effects (↓ HR, ↓ BP) could
provide anxiolytic-like calming, but this is speculative.

⚡ Framework note 3: LI4 and LI11 share the same peripheral nerve
(radial, C5-T1) but activate DIFFERENT supraspinal brainstem nuclei:
LI4 → PAG (descending pain modulation), LI11 → RVLM
(sympathoinhibition via ↓ p47phox). Combined with ST36 → NTS
(vagal-adrenal, PROKR2+) and PC6 → NTS/DMV (GABAergic vagal), the
Atlas now documents FOUR distinct non-segmental pathways. The
LI4/LI11 divergence is the strongest evidence yet that POINT
LOCATION on a nerve, not merely nerve identity, determines which
central mechanism is engaged. This extends ⚡ note 1 (LI4 entry):
the "30-40% non-segmental gap" (C04 S3.5.6) is resolved by at least
four pathway-specific solutions — and even points on the SAME nerve
access different solutions depending on WHERE the nerve is stimulated.
Implication for C04 location specificity: specificity may operate at
finer grain than "nerve territory" — at the level of fiber populations
accessed at each anatomical location. Caveat: both LI4 and LI11
pathway evidence is preclinical; human confirmation needed. Extends
A01 S8 Q1, A07 S1, C04 S3.5.6, ⚡ note 1.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 15–25 mm perpendicular. Oblique toward medial
  epicondyle approaches radial nerve trunk (1.3–3.3 cm). Deeper
  insertion engages ECRL origin and approaches supinator.
- De Qi: strong — distension, heaviness, electrical sensation
  radiating down the forearm or up toward the shoulder. More robust
  than at forearm points due to higher nerve fiber density at the
  pre-bifurcation zone.
- EA: bilateral LI11 for hypertension (most common protocol). LI11 +
  LI10 electrode pair for lateral epicondylitis. LI11 + LI4
  longitudinal pair for channel conditions. LI11 + SP10 cross-limb
  pair for dermatology. 2 Hz for chronic conditions; 2/100 Hz for
  acute. Hypertension mechanism studies: 2–15 Hz, 1–2 mA.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02): self-accessible. Flex elbow at 90°, locate lateral
end of cubital crease with opposite thumb. Press firmly into the
depression between lateral epicondyle and radial head. 30–60 seconds
sustained. Used for self-care in fever, headache, and arm pain.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Cứu (B08): applicable for Cold-type elbow arthralgia, Wind-Cold
invasion, general immune support. For Heat conditions (fever, skin
eruptions), moxa CONTRAINDICATED in TCM theory ("don't add Heat to
Heat"). This modality restriction is consistent with LI11's primary
"clearing Heat" function.
→ Cross-ref: B08 S4.3

Dry needling (B11): common extensor origin TrP at lateral epicondyle
= PRIMARY dry needling target for lateral epicondylitis. One of the
most frequently needled locations in clinical dry needling. Local
twitch response in ECRL/ECRB expected. Addresses LOCAL tendinopathy
only — does NOT reproduce systemic effects (dermatological,
antihypertensive).
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

So sánh: for lateral epicondylitis, both acupuncture and dry needling
target the same structure. For hypertension and dermatological effects,
EA with De Qi is the technique with preclinical mechanism support —
acupressure and dry needling would not activate the radial nerve trunk
at the depth required for RVLM modulation.

### An toàn

Vascular risk: MODERATE. Radial recurrent and radial collateral
arteries form periarticular anastomotic network. Minor hematoma
possible. Apply pressure post-needling if bleeding. Avoid aggressive
deep needling in anticoagulated patients.

Neural risk: LOW-MODERATE. Radial nerve trunk at 1.3–3.3 cm depth —
standard insertion may approach but should not impinge with
perpendicular technique. Electrifying or shooting pain radiating down
the forearm indicates needle contact with radial/posterior interosseous
nerve — withdraw and redirect immediately. Highest risk with oblique
insertion toward medial epicondyle.

Pneumothorax: none (upper limb location).

Pregnancy: no specific contraindication. LI11 is NOT one of the
traditionally "forbidden" pregnancy points.

Overall: LOW-MODERATE complication risk. Standard precautions for an
elbow point with moderately deep neural structures.

## LI12 — Trửu Liêu (肘髎, Zhǒu Liáo)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Elbow Crevice" — on the lateral side of the upper arm, 1 cun
superolateral to LI11, on the lateral supracondylar ridge of the
humerus. No special point category. The 髎 (liáo) character
denotes a bony depression or crevice — the same naming element
appears at BL31-34 (八髎, Eight Liaos at sacral foramina) and
several facial points. At LI12, it references the palpable groove
on the supracondylar ridge between the brachioradialis origin and
the lateral epicondyle. LI12 is the first point ABOVE the lateral
epicondyle — the transition from the elbow landmark zone (LI11)
to the upper arm segment (LI13-LI14). Radial nerve territory at
the lateral intermuscular septum level.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral side of the
upper arm, with the elbow flexed, on the lateral supracondylar
ridge of the humerus, about 1 cun above the lateral epicondyle,
on the line connecting LI11 (Quchi) and LI15 (Jianyu).

Anatomical landmarks: flex the elbow — locate LI11 at the lateral
end of the cubital crease, then measure 1 cun superolaterally
along the lateral supracondylar ridge. The point lies in a shallow
depression between the origins of brachioradialis and ECRL from
the ridge. 1 cun above LI11, 2 cun below LI13.

Needle depth: 10–20 mm perpendicular. Thin tissue over the bony
ridge limits insertion depth.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Cutaneous: posterior cutaneous nerve of the forearm (radial
  nerve, C5-C8) wrapping around the lateral epicondylar region.
  Lower lateral cutaneous nerve of the arm (radial nerve) at
  the transition to the upper arm.
- Deep: radial nerve — at this level the nerve has emerged from
  the spiral groove and lies between brachioradialis and brachialis,
  anterior to the lateral intermuscular septum. 1 cun proximal to
  the bifurcation zone (at LI11 level).
- Spinal segments: C5-C8
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular: radial collateral artery (branch of profunda brachii)
descending with the radial nerve along the lateral intermuscular
septum. Radial recurrent artery ascending from below toward the
anastomotic network around the lateral epicondyle.

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue (thin over bony ridge)
→ origin of brachioradialis from the supracondylar ridge → deep:
brachialis muscle → periosteum of humerus.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
No special category. Standard channel point on the elbow-upper
arm transition.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI12 Zhouliao (肘髎). Functions: activates
the channel, dispels Wind, relaxes sinews and tendons. Primary
indications: elbow pain and contracture, arm numbness, upper limb
motor impairment.

**2. Trigger point:** Brachioradialis and ECRL origins at the
lateral supracondylar ridge. TrPs in these muscles refer pain to
the lateral epicondyle and dorsal hand — moderate Dorsher
correspondence for lateral elbow pain. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in lateral epicondylitis
patients — the supracondylar ridge is a secondary palpation site
proximal to the epicondyle. Less reactive than LI11 (epicondyle
proper) but tender in proximal extensor origin pathology.

**4. Marma point:** Kurpara Marma (कूर्पर, elbow joint). LI12
sits at the proximal margin of Kurpara's anatomical zone.
Weak-moderate correspondence. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** C5-C6 dermatome. No visceral correspondence.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SBAL — proximal origin of the forearm
extensor chain, at the supracondylar ridge where SBAL anchors
to bone before continuing toward the deltoid.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable — bony prominence with
insufficient soft tissue for cup adherence.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP partial, Ashi partial).
Consistent with other non-categorized elbow-region points.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: elbow pain and contracture, numbness of
  the arm, inability to extend and flex the elbow
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: arm pain radiating to the shoulder,
  cold-numbness of the upper limb

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Activates the channel: elbow and arm pain, motor impairment
  of the upper limb, contracture of the elbow joint
- Dispels Wind: numbness of the arm and hand

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: LI12 is among the LEAST frequently
selected LI points. LI11 covers virtually all elbow indications.
LI12 is selected supplementarily when pain extends proximally
along the supracondylar ridge, or in dense needling protocols
for lateral epicondylitis (LI10 + LI11 + LI12 in sequence along
the ridge).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for LI12 individually.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

LOW risk. Bony prominence with thin overlying tissue.

Precautions:
- Periosteal pain if needle contacts bone — redirect slightly
- Radial nerve lies anterior to the lateral intermuscular septum
  at this level — deeper than standard insertion depth but caution
  with aggressive perpendicular technique
- Radial collateral artery: minor hematoma risk
- No pneumothorax risk

## LI13 — Thủ Ngũ Lý (手五里, Shǒu Wǔ Lǐ)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Arm Five Li" — on the lateral side of the upper arm, 3 cun
above LI11, on the line connecting LI11 and LI15. The most
significant classical PROHIBITION point on the LI channel. Shares
the 五里 (Five Li) naming convention with LR10 (足五里, Foot Five
Li) on the medial thigh — both near major vessels, both
classically prohibited. The Ling Shu Chapter 60 (玉版, "Jade
Inscription") lists 五里 among the "Great Prohibitions" (大禁):
"气尽而死" (Qi exhausted, then death). The Jiayi Jing (282 CE)
provides the most anatomically descriptive prohibition in
classical sources: "五里在肘上三寸，大脉中，禁不可刺" — "Wuli is
3 cun above the elbow, IN THE GREAT VESSEL, forbidden to needle."
Modern anatomy confirms: radial nerve + radial collateral artery
(from profunda brachii) pass through the lateral intermuscular
septum at this exact level. LI13 is the PRIMARY prohibition
reference — LR10 receives its prohibition by name association.
→ Cross-ref: AP-12 LR10

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral side of the
upper arm, on the line connecting LI11 (Quchi) and LI15 (Jianyu),
3 cun above the transverse cubital crease.

Anatomical landmarks: 3 cun above the lateral epicondyle. At the
junction of the middle and distal thirds of the lateral upper arm.
2 cun above LI12, 4 cun below LI14. At this level, the lateral
intermuscular septum is palpable as a ridge separating the
anterior (brachialis) and posterior (triceps) compartments.

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular MAXIMUM (if needled at all).
Classical sources prohibit needling entirely. Modern sources that
permit it strongly advise very shallow insertion.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Cutaneous: lower lateral cutaneous nerve of the arm (radial
  nerve, C5-C6)
- Deep: **RADIAL NERVE** — this is a CRITICAL anatomical level.
  The radial nerve pierces the lateral intermuscular septum at
  approximately this level, transitioning from the posterior
  compartment (spiral groove of humerus) to the anterior
  compartment. The nerve is relatively SUPERFICIAL during this
  transition — neither protected by the deep position within the
  spiral groove nor by the thick muscle bellies of the anterior
  compartment.
- Spinal segments: C5-T1 (full radial nerve territory)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular: **RADIAL COLLATERAL ARTERY** (from profunda
brachii/deep brachial artery) — accompanies the radial nerve
through the lateral intermuscular septum. This is the "大脉"
(great vessel) referenced in the Jiayi Jing prohibition. The
profunda brachii is the largest branch of the brachial artery.
→ Cross-ref: A04

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue → lateral intermuscular
septum region — between **brachialis** (anterior) and **lateral
head of triceps** (posterior). The radial nerve and radial
collateral artery pass through this septum at or near this level.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
No standard category designation.

Special: **Classical needling prohibition (Great Prohibition,
大禁).**

Sources:
1. Ling Shu Chapter 60 (玉版): 五里 among the "Great
   Prohibitions" — "气尽而死" (Qi exhausted, then death).
2. Jiayi Jing (282 CE): among 17 forbidden acupuncture points —
   "五里在肘上三寸，大脉中，禁不可刺" (Wuli is 3 cun above the
   elbow, in the great vessel, forbidden to needle).

Modern status: the absolute prohibition is NOT strictly observed
in contemporary practice. When used, very shallow insertion
(10-15 mm) with extreme caution. Moxibustion sometimes preferred.
The prohibition has genuine anatomical basis — radial nerve and
profunda brachii artery branch are in the immediate vicinity.

LI13 is the PRIMARY 五里 prohibition. LR10 (足五里) receives its
prohibition by NAME ASSOCIATION — the Jiayi Jing explicitly
references "手五里" (Arm Five Li = LI13), while LR10 inherits
the safety coding through the shared 五里 naming convention.
→ Cross-ref: AP-12 LR10; B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI13 Shouwuli (手五里). Functions: regulates
Qi, activates the channel, resolves phlegm-nodules. Primary
indications: elbow and arm pain, scrofula (lymphadenopathy).
Classically prohibited.

**2. Trigger point:** No corresponding trigger point. The lateral
intermuscular septum zone is not a recognized TrP site in the
Dorsher mapping system.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Not typically palpated due to
classical prohibition and neurovascular sensitivity of the region.

**4. Marma point:** Between Kurpara Marma (elbow) and Ani Marma
(lateral arm). No precise Marma at the intermuscular septum level.

**5. Head zone:** C5-C6 dermatome. No direct visceral
correspondence.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral intermuscular septum = structural
boundary between SBAL (posterior arm) and Deep Front Arm Line
(DFAL, anterior arm). A fascial JUNCTION rather than a pathway
station.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable — neurovascular risk.

**Convergence score: 1/8** (TCM only). Lowest convergence on the
LI channel. The prohibition status appears to have SUPPRESSED
cross-tradition development: minimal clinical usage → minimal
cross-tradition data accumulation. Parallels LR10's low
convergence (2/8).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Despite the classical prohibition, traditional sources document
indications:

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: scrofula (lymphadenopathy), elbow and arm pain,
  pain in the upper arm preventing raising of the shoulder
- Ling Shu: primarily cited in the prohibition context, not for
  therapeutic indications

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Activates the channel: elbow and arm pain, upper limb motor
  impairment
- Resolves phlegm-nodules: scrofula (cervical lymphadenopathy)
- Dispels Wind-Damp: arm numbness, shoulder pain

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: one of the LEAST needled classical
acupoints on any channel. The prohibition has effectively removed
LI13 from routine clinical practice. When referenced, it is
typically in the context of classical prohibition discussion
rather than active clinical use. Scrofula (cervical
lymphadenopathy) is its most distinctive indication — shared with
LI14 (Binao), which has the same indication without the
prohibition.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for LI13. The classical prohibition
likely deterred research interest.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**HIGH risk — one of the highest-risk acupuncture points on the
upper limb.**

Classical prohibition: Ling Shu Ch.60, Jiayi Jing — Great
Prohibitions. The most explicitly prohibited upper limb acupoint
in classical literature.

Neurovascular risk:
- **Radial nerve** at the lateral intermuscular septum transition
  — the nerve is relatively superficial during this anatomical
  passage. Direct puncture causes wrist drop (motor) and sensory
  loss over the dorsal hand.
- **Radial collateral artery** (from profunda brachii) — the
  "great vessel" of the classical prohibition. Puncture causes
  deep hematoma, potential compartment syndrome.

Mandatory precautions (if used):
- VERY shallow insertion ONLY (10-15 mm maximum)
- NEVER needle perpendicular deep — radial nerve and artery
  are in the direct trajectory
- Moxibustion preferred when treatment at this level is needed
- Palpate for arterial pulsation before any insertion
- Many practitioners avoid this point entirely

Comparison with LR10 (足五里): both 五里 points carry HIGH
vascular risk. LI13's risk is neurovascular (radial nerve +
artery); LR10's risk is primarily vascular (femoral artery).
LI13 has the more explicit classical prohibition text.
→ Cross-ref: AP-12 LR10

## LI14 — Tý Nhu (臂臑, Bì Nào)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Upper Arm" (literal: 臂 = arm, 臑 = fleshy part of the upper
arm) — on the lateral side of the upper arm, at the inferior end
of the deltoid muscle, 7 cun above LI11. Meeting/intersection
point of Hand Yangming (LI) with Hand Shaoyang (TE) and Yang Wei
Mai. This is the first INTER-CHANNEL connection point on the LI
arm segment — all forearm points (LI1-LI13) belong exclusively
to the LI channel. The deltoid insertion marks the anatomical
transition from the arm proper to the shoulder region (LI15 at
the acromion is next). Traditional indications focus on arm-
shoulder pain and scrofula (cervical lymphadenopathy). The
scrofula indication is shared with LI13 — a proximal-arm
clustering of lymph-related indications that anatomically
corresponds to the deltopectoral and lateral axillary lymph node
groups.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral side of the
upper arm, at the inferior end (insertion) of the deltoid muscle,
on the line connecting LI11 (Quchi) and LI15 (Jianyu), 7 cun
above the transverse cubital crease.

Anatomical landmarks: the deltoid muscle's inferior attachment
(deltoid tuberosity) on the lateral aspect of the humeral shaft,
approximately at the junction of the proximal and middle thirds
of the arm. 7 cun above LI11, 4 cun above LI13. Palpation: ask
the patient to abduct the arm slightly against resistance — the
deltoid contracts and its inferior border becomes palpable.

Needle depth: 15–25 mm perpendicular or oblique.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Cutaneous: lateral cutaneous nerve of the arm (axillary nerve,
  C5-C6) — primary cutaneous branch at the lateral upper arm.
  Lower lateral cutaneous nerve of the arm (radial nerve, C5-C8)
  may contribute at this level.
- Deep: radial nerve in the spiral groove — at this level the
  nerve runs on the POSTERIOR aspect of the humerus, deep to the
  lateral head of the triceps. Significantly deeper than at LI13,
  and not in the direct trajectory of a lateral needle insertion.
- Axillary nerve: terminal motor branches supplying the inferior
  portion of the deltoid may be encountered at this level.
- Spinal segments: C5-C6 (cutaneous), C5-T1 (deep radial,
  not at risk from lateral approach)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular: deltoid branch of the profunda brachii artery (or
posterior circumflex humeral artery). Cephalic vein runs
superficially in the anterior-lateral groove, more anterior than
LI14 but nearby.
→ Cross-ref: A04

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue → inferior attachment
of **deltoid** muscle → **brachialis** muscle (anterior) and
**lateral head of triceps** (posterior). The humerus is palpable
beneath the muscles at this level (deltoid tuberosity).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

**Meeting/Intersection point:** LI14 is a meeting point of the
Hand Yangming (LI) channel with the Hand Shaoyang (TE) channel
and the Yang Wei Mai (Yang Linking Vessel). Sources vary — some
cite the Luo-connecting vessel of Yang Qiao Mai instead of Yang
Wei Mai (Deadman notes this variability). The intersection
reflects anatomical convergence: at the lateral upper arm, the LI
and TE channel trajectories approach each other before both
reaching the shoulder.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI14 Binao (臂臑). Functions: activates the
channel, benefits the eyes, resolves phlegm-nodules, dispels Wind.
Primary indications: arm and shoulder pain, scrofula, eye diseases,
upper limb motor impairment.

**2. Trigger point:** Deltoid (posterior fibers) and lateral
triceps TrPs at the mid-arm level. Deltoid posterior fiber TrP
refers pain locally to the deltoid region. Moderate Dorsher
correspondence. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in deltoid/shoulder pathology.
The deltoid insertion is a clear palpatory landmark that becomes
reactive in deltoid strain, adhesive capsulitis, and shoulder
impingement with deltoid compensation.

**4. Marma point:** **Ani Marma** (अणि, "pointed"). Located on
the lateral aspect of the arm where the deltoid meets the
brachialis — a relatively precise Marma-acupoint correspondence.
Ani is classified as a Snayu Marma (tendon/ligament) in Sushruta
Samhita. Moderate-strong correspondence. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: C04

**5. Head zone:** C5-C6 dermatome. No direct visceral
correspondence.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SBAL → Lateral Line transition at the
deltoid. The deltoid insertion anchors the Lateral Line's shoulder
component, connecting the lateral arm line to the neck/scalp via
the upper trapezius.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Applicable with a small cup on the lateral
deltoid muscle mass. Used clinically for arm and shoulder
stiffness. 🟡

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi, Marma). Highest
convergence on the LI upper arm segment (LI12: 3/8, LI13: 1/8).
The Ani Marma correspondence adds a tradition not previously
strong on the LI channel — Marma at LI14 is more anatomically
precise than at any other LI upper limb point.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: arm and shoulder pain, scrofula (cervical
  lymphadenopathy), eye diseases (redness and pain), neck
  stiffness
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: upper limb paralysis, inability to raise the
  arm, scrofula, throat swelling

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Activates the channel: shoulder and arm pain, upper limb motor
  impairment, inability to raise the arm
- Resolves phlegm-nodules: scrofula (cervical lymphadenopathy),
  neck masses
- Benefits the eyes: eye redness and pain, excessive tearing
- Dispels Wind: neck stiffness, headache

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: moderately used, primarily for shoulder-
arm pain where the pathology extends into the deltoid region. More
frequently selected than LI12-LI13 but less than LI15 (shoulder
proper). LI14 often included in protocols with LI15 for shoulder
joint conditions. The scrofula indication shared with LI13
creates a proximal-arm lymph node treatment zone — anatomically
corresponding to the deltopectoral and lateral axillary lymph
node groups.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for LI14 individually. Included in
multi-point protocols for shoulder conditions (frozen shoulder,
rotator cuff) but individual contribution not isolated.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

LOW-MODERATE risk. Deltoid muscle provides good tissue buffer.

Precautions:
- Radial nerve in the spiral groove on the posterior aspect of
  the humerus — NOT in the direct trajectory of a lateral
  insertion. Safe at standard depth from a lateral approach.
- Cephalic vein superficially in the anterior-lateral groove —
  minor hematoma risk
- Axillary nerve branches deep within the deltoid — avoid
  extremely deep insertion
- No pneumothorax risk (upper arm location)

> SAFETY SYSTEM.** LI13 is the PRIMARY reference for the 五里

> while LR10 inherits the safety coding through the shared 五里
> naming convention. The Jiayi Jing prohibition is the most
> anatomically descriptive in classical sources: it specifies
> LOCATION (3 cun above elbow), STRUCTURAL BASIS (大脉 = great
> vessel), and ABSOLUTE PROHIBITION (禁不可刺 = forbidden to
> needle). Modern anatomy confirms: radial nerve + radial
> collateral artery pass through the lateral intermuscular septum
> at this exact level. The Atlas now documents THREE distinct
> safety-coding strategies in classical acupuncture: (1) explicit
> textual prohibition (Ling Shu/Jiayi Jing — LI13 direct), (2)
> nomenclatural propagation (五里 naming convention extending
> prohibition from LI13 → LR10), (3) modality restriction
> (moxa-only: LR12 classical; moxa-prohibited: LU8). Each targets
> a different risk: strategies 1-2 encode vascular/neurovascular
> proximity; strategy 3 encodes thermal risk at specific tissue
> types.
>
> **Obs. 23 — CLINICAL GRADIENT COLLAPSE ABOVE LI11.** LI8 → LI9
> → LI10 → LI11 showed clear ascending clinical significance
> (C → C → B → A), correlating with increasing nerve fiber
> diversity toward the pre-bifurcation zone (Obs. 21). LI12 → LI13
> → LI14 immediately collapses to C → C → C despite following the
> same radial nerve PROXIMALLY. Above LI11, the radial nerve
> deepens into the spiral groove, becoming progressively LESS
> accessible to standard surface needling. Clinical importance
> tracks neural ACCESSIBILITY (how easily a needle can engage the
> nerve at a surface landmark), not neural PROXIMITY to the nerve's
> origin or hierarchical position along the trunk. The lateral
> epicondyle is the clinical apex because it combines neural
> density (pre-bifurcation trunk = all fiber types) with surface
> accessibility (subcutaneous position between landmark muscles).
> Generalization: the clinically important points on any channel
> cluster where nerves are simultaneously DENSE and ACCESSIBLE —
> not simply where they are anatomically close.
>
> **Obs. 24 — INTER-CHANNEL CONVERGENCE AT THE PROXIMAL LIMB.**
> LI14 is the first inter-channel meeting/intersection point on the
> LI arm segment — all forearm points (LI1-LI13) belong exclusively
> to the LI channel. The meeting point (LI + TE + Yang Wei Mai)
> coincides with the transition from the forearm to the upper arm,
> where the body's cross-sectional anatomy forces multiple channel
> trajectories to converge. The forearm has well-separated anterior
> (LU, PC, HT) and posterior (LI, TE, SI) compartments; the upper
> arm narrows and at the shoulder, all six hand channels must pass
> through the deltoid-axillary gateway. Inter-channel connections
> emerge where anatomical separation DECREASES — channels meet
> because the body converges, not because the channels seek each
> other. This is a structural explanation for why meeting/
> intersection points cluster at the proximal limb and trunk rather
> than at the distal extremities.

## LI15 — Kiên Ngung (肩髃, Jiān Yú)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Shoulder Bone" (肩 = shoulder, 髃 = bone prominence of the
shoulder) — at the anteroinferior aspect of the acromion, in
the depression between the anterior and middle fibers of the
deltoid when the arm is abducted to 90°. Meeting point of Hand
Yangming (LI) with Yang Qiao Mai (Yang Motility Vessel); some
classical texts cite Yang Wei Mai instead. THE primary shoulder
acupoint on the LI channel and one of the most frequently
needled shoulder points across all channels. Marks the
anatomical transition from the arm proper (LI14, deltoid
insertion) to the glenohumeral complex. The acromion provides
the skeletal landmark; the deltoid fibers provide the muscular
landmark; the subacromial space provides the deep target.
Most frequently included acupoint in systematic reviews of
acupuncture for frozen shoulder (Ben-Arie et al. 2020). The
shoulder is a critical multi-channel convergence zone — LI15
(anteroinferior), TE14 (posteroinferior), SI9-SI12 (posterior),
and GB21 (superior) ring the glenohumeral joint from different
channels. LI15 is the third consecutive meeting point on the
LI arm segment (LI14: LI+TE+Yang Wei Mai → LI15: LI+Yang Qiao
Mai), extending the inter-channel convergence pattern (Obs. 24)
into the shoulder proper.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): when the arm is abducted to
90°, in the anterior of the two depressions that appear at the
anteroinferior margin of the acromioclavicular joint. The
posterior depression is TE14 (Jianliao).

Anatomical landmarks: with shoulder abducted, LI15 is in the
depression between the anterior and middle fibers of the
deltoid, directly inferior to the anterolateral acromion. The
acromial end of the clavicle is palpable just superiorly.
Palpation: abduct the arm to 90° — two distinct depressions
appear below the acromion. LI15 is the ANTERIOR one; TE14 is
the POSTERIOR one. This paired landmark (LI15/TE14) provides
the clearest palpatory method.

Needle depth: 15–25 mm perpendicular or oblique inferiorly
toward the glenohumeral joint.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Cutaneous: supraclavicular nerve (C3-C4) and upper lateral
  cutaneous nerve of arm (axillary nerve, C5-C6)
- Deep: axillary nerve (C5-C6) — motor branches to the deltoid
  pass through the muscle from its deep surface. Suprascapular
  nerve (C5-C6) innervates the supraspinatus and the
  glenohumeral joint capsule deep to the point.
- Spinal segments: C3-C6 (broadest segment range on the LI
  shoulder segment)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular: acromial branch of the thoracoacromial artery. Deltoid
branch of the thoracoacromial trunk. Posterior circumflex humeral
artery deeper, in the quadrilateral space — typically posterior
to LI15's location.
→ Cross-ref: A04

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue → deltoid muscle
(between anterior and middle fibers) → subacromial bursa →
supraspinatus tendon → glenohumeral joint capsule.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

**Meeting/Intersection point:** LI15 is a meeting point of Hand
Yangming (LI) with the Yang Qiao Mai (Yang Motility Vessel).
Some classical texts cite Yang Wei Mai (Yang Linking Vessel)
instead (Deadman notes this variability). The intersection
reflects the convergence of multiple channel trajectories at the
shoulder: the LI channel ascending from the lateral arm meets
the Yang Qiao Mai's trajectory along the lateral body.

LI meeting points on the arm segment:
- LI14: LI + TE (Hand Shaoyang) + Yang Wei Mai
- LI15: LI + Yang Qiao Mai
- Three consecutive points with inter-channel connections
  (LI14→LI15 confirmed, LI16 variable)
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI15 Jianyu (肩髃). Functions: expels
Wind-Damp, benefits the sinews and joints, activates channel.
Primary indications: shoulder pain and stiffness, inability to
raise arm, adhesive capsulitis, frozen shoulder, hemiplegia
(upper limb), urticaria, goiter.

**2. Trigger point:** Deltoid (anterior fiber) TrP — referred
pain to anterolateral deltoid region. Supraspinatus TrP nearby
— referred pain to lateral shoulder and upper deltoid. Strong
Dorsher correspondence — one of the best TrP-acupoint matches
at the shoulder. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Highly reactive in shoulder
pathology — adhesive capsulitis, rotator cuff impingement,
frozen shoulder. One of the most common Ashi sites in the body.
The anteroinferior acromial depression is a universal site of
shoulder tenderness in clinical examination.

**4. Marma point:** **Amsa Marma** (अंस, "shoulder"). One of
the vital Marma points in Sushruta Samhita. Classified as Snayu
Marma (tendon/ligament). Located at the shoulder joint region.
Moderate correspondence — Amsa is broadly shoulder-centered;
LI15 is specifically anteroinferior acromion. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: C04

**5. Head zone:** C4-C5 dermatome. C4 cape distribution overlaps
with cervical spondylosis referral pattern. No direct visceral
correspondence.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line at the deltoid. The deltoid
connects laterally to the upper trapezius (Lateral Line
continuity) and anteriorly to the pectoralis major (Superficial
Front Arm Line). LI15 is at a myofascial node where multiple
lines converge at the shoulder. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data specific to LI15.

**8. Cupping point:** Applicable — the anterolateral shoulder is
a standard cupping site for shoulder pain and stiffness. Large
muscle mass supports cup adhesion. 🟡

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi, Marma, Cupping).
Highest convergence on the LI shoulder-neck segment. Consistent
with the universal recognition of the anterolateral shoulder
depression as a musculoskeletal treatment site across traditions.
Convergence score exceeds all upper arm points (LI14: 4/8) and
matches the wrist cluster peaks (LI5-LI7).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: shoulder pain, inability to raise the arm,
  Wind-Bi of shoulder and arm
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: hemiplegia (upper limb), goiter, scrofula,
  urticaria, skin wind conditions
- Ling Shu: shoulder pain, Wind disorders

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Activates the channel: shoulder pain and stiffness, frozen
  shoulder, inability to raise arm, adhesive capsulitis
- Benefits sinews and joints: periarthritis of shoulder, rotator
  cuff disorders
- Expels Wind: urticaria, rubella, skin wind conditions
- Motor impairment: hemiplegia (upper limb)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: THE most frequently selected shoulder
point in clinical practice. Used in virtually every shoulder
acupuncture protocol. Often combined with TE14 (Jianliao,
posteroinferior acromion) for comprehensive shoulder coverage —
LI15 + TE14 brackets the deltoid from anterior and posterior.
Also paired with SI9-SI12 for posterior shoulder, GB21 for
superior shoulder/trapezius. The urticaria indication connects
to LI11's dermatology role — the Yangming channel has a
distinctive Wind-Skin treatment axis (LI4+LI11+LI15 all share
urticaria/wind-skin indications).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Frozen shoulder / adhesive capsulitis — 🟡 Plausible**
Systematic review and meta-analysis (Ben-Arie et al. 2020,
Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine): 12 RCTs.
LI15 was the MOST FREQUENTLY included acupoint. Meta-analysis
showed significant VAS pain reduction, CMS shoulder function
improvement, and flexion ROM improvement vs controls. However,
GRADE assessment: "level of evidence was very low" due to high
risk of bias and heterogeneity. All trials used multi-point
protocols — LI15's individual contribution not isolable.
Confidence: 🟡

**2. Periarthritis of shoulder — 🟡 Plausible**
RCT (small): EA at LI15 vs oral Diclofenac Sodium for shoulder
periarthritis. Total effective rate: EA 93.3% vs Diclofenac
56.7%. Small sample, single center. Suggests benefit but
insufficient for confirmation.
Confidence: 🟡

**3. Post-stroke shoulder pain — 🔴 Unvalidated (LI15-specific)**
Systematic review (2019, Frontiers in Neurology): acupuncture
for post-stroke shoulder pain. LI15 included in standard point
combinations (LI15, TE14, SI9, LI4, TE5). Some positive results
for pain reduction and ROM, but LI15 not isolated.
Confidence: 🔴

**4. Urticaria/skin conditions — 🔴 Unvalidated**
No evidence for LI15 specifically for dermatological conditions.
LI11 + SP10 dominate this evidence base.
Confidence: 🔴

**Protocol inclusion paradox:** LI15 is the most frequently
INCLUDED shoulder acupoint in RCTs/SRs but has never been tested
in ISOLATION. It is simultaneously the most studied (by inclusion
frequency) and the least proven (by isolation standards) shoulder
acupoint in the acupuncture literature. See Obs. 27.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No sensitization data specific to LI15. However, the subacromial
region becomes markedly tender in shoulder pathology — adhesive
capsulitis, impingement, rotator cuff disorders all produce
increased sensitivity at the LI15 location. This is consistent
with dynamic acupoint behavior (disease-state sensitization) but
has not been measured using the specific dynamic acupoint
methodology (Chen Rixin heat-sensitive, Kim 2017).
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Local shoulder effects (pain, ROM, adhesive capsulitis):**
Segmentally explicable. Axillary nerve (C5-C6) + suprascapular
nerve (C5-C6) stimulation → segmental modulation of shoulder
pain via C5-C6 spinal segments. These same segments innervate
the glenohumeral joint capsule, rotator cuff, and subacromial
bursa. Needling into the subacromial region targets the deepest
source of shoulder pain directly.

EA mechanism: 2 Hz at the shoulder activates Aδ and C fibers →
spinal enkephalin release (segmental) + descending inhibition
from PAG. 100 Hz → local dynorphin release. LI15 EA provides
dual peripheral + central analgesia at the shoulder.

**Post-stroke motor recovery:** cortical reorganization via
peripheral stimulation of C5-C6 afferents is plausible but the
specific pathway from LI15 peripheral input to motor cortex
reorganization is not characterized.

**Distant effects (urticaria, skin):** not explained by local
anatomy (C5-C6). Presumably through the same Yangming supraspinal
pathway as other LI points, but the specific contribution of
shoulder-level afferents to anti-inflammatory effects is unknown.
LI4 (C6-C7) and LI11 (C5-T1) dominate both the evidence and
mechanism literature for these indications.

**Gap:** no mechanism study specific to LI15. The most frequently
used shoulder acupoint has never been studied mechanistically in
isolation — the same protocol-without-isolation pattern seen in
the clinical evidence.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 15–25 mm perpendicular or oblique inferiorly.
  Abduct the arm to open the deltoid depression. Some techniques
  direct the needle toward the glenohumeral joint space.
- De Qi: distension and soreness in the shoulder, often radiating
  into the deltoid or down the lateral arm. Deep needling may
  produce intra-articular sensation.
- EA: commonly used. LI15-TE14 electrode pair for shoulder pain
  (anterior-posterior deltoid bracketing). 2 Hz for chronic pain,
  2/100 Hz alternating for acute. LI15-LI11 longitudinal pairing
  for channel-based shoulder-elbow treatment.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02): accessible by contralateral hand. Press into
the depression below the acromion with the arm slightly abducted.
30-60 seconds sustained pressure. Self-accessible for shoulder
maintenance.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Cứu (B08): applicable — warming the shoulder for chronic
Wind-Cold-Damp Bi. Indirect moxa common. TDP lamp also used.
→ Cross-ref: B08 S4.3

Dry needling (B11): deltoid and supraspinatus TrPs at this
location are standard dry needling targets for myofascial
shoulder pain. Local twitch response in deltoid anterior fibers
expected. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

### An toàn

LOW-MODERATE risk. The shoulder provides good tissue depth
laterally.

Precautions:
- Subacromial bursa: deep perpendicular needling can enter the
  bursa — risk of septic bursitis with inadequate sterilization.
  Sterile technique essential.
- Glenohumeral joint space: very deep inferior-directed insertion
  may reach the joint capsule — risk of septic arthritis (rare
  but serious)
- Axillary nerve: passes through the quadrilateral space
  posteriorly — generally safe from a lateral approach
- No pneumothorax risk (lateral shoulder position)

## LI16 — Cự Cốt (巨骨, Jù Gǔ)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Great Bone" (巨 = great, 骨 = bone — referring to the
prominent acromioclavicular bony complex) — in the depression
between the acromial end of the clavicle and the spine of the
scapula. Some sources (Deadman) cite LI16 as a meeting point of
Hand Yangming (LI) with Yang Qiao Mai; others are less
consistent. Located at the acromioclavicular joint area — the
highest point on the LI channel's shoulder segment before the
channel descends to the neck. The supraspinous fossa beneath
provides depth for needle insertion but also introduces
PNEUMOTHORAX RISK — the first point on the LI channel with this
danger. Traditional indications focus on shoulder-arm pain and
motor impairment. The hemoptysis indication (blood-tinged
coughing) present in classical sources likely reflects the lung
apex's anatomical proximity rather than a channel-specific
therapeutic mechanism.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): in the depression between the
acromial end of the clavicle and the spine of the scapula.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the acromioclavicular joint, then
move posteriorly into the triangular depression between the
distal clavicle and the spine of the scapula. The supraspinatus
muscle fills the supraspinous fossa below. The trapezius (upper
fibers) is the most superficial muscle.

Needle depth: 5–15 mm perpendicular. CAUTION: deep perpendicular
or medially-directed insertion risks pneumothorax.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Cutaneous: supraclavicular nerve (C3-C4) — lateral and
  intermediate branches
- Deep: suprascapular nerve (C5-C6) — passes through the
  suprascapular notch (under the transverse scapular ligament)
  to innervate supraspinatus and the acromioclavicular joint
- Accessory nerve (CN XI): runs superficially in the posterior
  triangle, may cross near this level before entering trapezius
- Spinal segments: C3-C6
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular: suprascapular artery (passes over the transverse
scapular ligament). Transverse cervical artery (superficial
branch).
→ Cross-ref: A04

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue → trapezius (upper
fibers, thin at this level) → supraspinatus muscle →
supraspinous fossa. **Lung apex deep and medial.**

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

Meeting/Intersection point: some sources cite LI16 as a meeting
of Hand Yangming (LI) with Yang Qiao Mai. Sources are less
consistent for LI16 than for LI14 or LI15.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI16 Jugu (巨骨). Functions: activates the
channel, benefits the shoulder. Indications: shoulder and arm
pain, inability to raise arm, goiter, scrofula, hemoptysis.

**2. Trigger point:** Upper trapezius TrP zone at the
acromion/clavicle attachment — referred pain to posterolateral
neck and temporal region. Moderate correspondence. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Reactive in acromioclavicular joint
pathology, superior shoulder pain, and direct AC joint trauma.

**4. Marma point:** Amsa Marma zone extends to this region
(broadly shoulder) but Amsa's center is more inferior at the
deltoid/glenohumeral area (LI15 territory). Weak correspondence.

**5. Head zone:** C3-C4 dermatome, C4 cape distribution. No
direct visceral correspondence.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line → upper trapezius attachment
at the acromion/clavicle → connection to cervical region.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Difficult at this bony depression —
insufficient soft tissue for reliable cup adhesion. Not a
standard cupping site.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi). Standard for a
non-landmark bony-depression point. Drops from LI15's 5/8 —
the acromioclavicular junction is clinically recognized but
less universally treated than the deltoid depression.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: shoulder and arm pain, inability to raise
  arm, scrofula
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: goiter, hemoptysis, shoulder stiffness,
  upper limb weakness

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Activates channel: shoulder and arm pain, acromioclavicular
  joint disorders, motor impairment of upper limb
- Resolves phlegm-nodules: goiter, scrofula

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: less frequently selected than LI15 for
general shoulder conditions. Used specifically for
acromioclavicular joint pathology and superior shoulder pain.
The hemoptysis indication is clinically rare — likely
anatomically derived (lung apex proximity) rather than channel-
function derived.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for LI16 individually. Occasionally
included in multi-point shoulder protocols but not studied in
isolation.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**MODERATE-HIGH risk — pneumothorax.**

The lung apex lies deep and medial to LI16. This is the FIRST
point on the LI channel with pneumothorax risk — the entire arm
segment (LI1-LI15) is anatomically clear of the lung apex.
LI16's position over the supraspinous fossa introduces this risk
because the fossa provides a potential path to the pleural space.

Precautions:
- **Pneumothorax**: NEVER needle deep or medially directed.
  Maximum safe depth: 10-15 mm perpendicular, staying within
  the supraspinatus muscle mass. Oblique insertion toward the
  acromion (laterally) is safer.
- Suprascapular nerve in the suprascapular notch — excessive
  depth risks nerve injury (shoulder pain, supraspinatus/
  infraspinatus weakness)
- Suprascapular artery accompanies the nerve — hematoma risk
- In thin patients, the lung apex may be as little as 2-3 cm
  from the surface at this location

## LI17 — Thiên Đỉnh (天鼎, Tiān Dǐng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Heavenly Tripod" (天 = heaven/celestial, 鼎 = tripod/cauldron
— the 鼎 was a ceremonial bronze vessel in ancient China,
evoking stability and support for the head atop the neck) — on
the lateral side of the neck, at the posterior border of the
SCM, 1 cun below LI18 (Futu), at the level of the inferior
border of the thyroid cartilage. The 天 naming element places
this point in the neck's "celestial" region — the passage
between body and head — a naming convention shared with multiple
neck points (天窗 SI16, 天容 SI17, 天突 CV22, 天牖 TE16, 天柱
BL10). However, LI17 is NOT classified as a Window of Heaven
point despite the 天 element — demonstrating that the 天 naming
convention and the Window of Heaven functional group are
independent categorical dimensions. LI18 (扶突, no 天 element)
IS a Window of Heaven point while LI17 (天鼎, has 天 element) is
not. Traditional indications focus on throat disorders, voice
loss, and goiter — conditions related to the structures
immediately deep to the point (larynx, thyroid, pharynx).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral side of the
neck, 1 cun below LI18 (Futu), on the posterior border of the
sternocleidomastoid (SCM) muscle, at the level of the inferior
border of the thyroid cartilage.

Anatomical landmarks: locate the laryngeal prominence (Adam's
apple) — LI18 is at this level. LI17 is 1 cun (approximately
2 cm) inferior, on the posterior border of the SCM. Palpation:
with the head turned slightly to the opposite side, the SCM
becomes prominent and its posterior border palpable.

Needle depth: 5–10 mm perpendicular. CAUTION: shallow insertion
only — major vessels deep.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Cutaneous: transverse cervical nerve (C2-C3) and
  supraclavicular nerve (C3-C4) — branches of the cervical
  plexus emerging from the posterior triangle
- Deep: accessory nerve (CN XI) — crosses the posterior triangle
  from SCM to trapezius, may be at or near this level.
  Phrenic nerve (C3-C5) on the anterior surface of the anterior
  scalene, deep and medial.
- Ansa cervicalis (C1-C3): loop innervating the infrahyoid
  (strap) muscles, runs on the carotid sheath
- Spinal segments: C2-C4
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular:
- Superficial: external jugular vein — crosses the SCM
  superficially, may be visible and palpable. Avoid direct
  puncture.
- Deep: common carotid artery and internal jugular vein within
  the carotid sheath, deep to SCM. At this level (inferior
  border of thyroid cartilage), the common carotid has not yet
  bifurcated.
→ Cross-ref: A04

Tissue layers: skin → platysma → SCM (posterior border) →
anterior and middle scalene muscles deep. Carotid sheath (common
carotid artery, internal jugular vein, vagus nerve) deep to SCM.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
No special category designation. NOT a Window of Heaven point
despite the 天 naming element.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI17 Tianding (天鼎). Functions: benefits
the throat, resolves phlegm-nodules. Indications: sudden voice
loss (暴喑), hoarseness, sore throat, difficulty swallowing,
goiter, scrofula.

**2. Trigger point:** SCM (clavicular head) TrP — referred pain
to the sternal region, throat, and forehead. Moderate
correspondence for throat symptoms. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Reactive in acute torticollis, SCM
spasm, and neck stiffness. Tender when SCM is in spasm.

**4. Marma point:** Nila Marma (नील, "blue/sapphire") — on the
sides of the throat/neck, flanking the trachea. Classified as
Sira Marma (vascular) in Sushruta Samhita. Moderate
correspondence — Nila is centered more anteriorly on the throat,
while LI17 is at the posterior SCM border. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** C3-C4 dermatome. Within the cervical
viscerotome for thyroid and larynx — segmental correspondence
supports the throat/voice traditional indications.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line → SCM → connection to
mastoid process and temporal bone. The SCM is a key Lateral Line
component in the neck region.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — neck region, insufficient
muscle mass and too close to vascular structures for safe
cupping.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi, Marma). Moderate
convergence — the throat region is a universal treatment target
across traditions for voice and swallowing disorders.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: sudden voice loss (暴喑), throat swelling
  and pain, goiter, scrofula (cervical lymphadenopathy)
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: difficulty swallowing, hoarseness, sensation
  of something stuck in the throat (梅核气, plum-pit qi)

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Benefits the throat: sudden voice loss, hoarseness, sore
  throat, pharyngitis
- Resolves phlegm-nodules: goiter (thyroid enlargement), scrofula
- Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: moderately used for throat and voice
conditions. Often combined with LI18 (Window of Heaven, 1 cun
superior) and CV22 (天突, suprasternal notch) for comprehensive
throat treatment. LI17 + LI18 form a vertical pair on the
lateral neck that brackets the laryngeal prominence from below
and at level. The goiter indication has direct anatomical
basis — the thyroid gland is immediately deep and medial.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for LI17 individually.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**HIGH risk — major cervical vessels.**

Precautions:
- **External jugular vein**: superficial, may cross the needle
  path at the posterior border of SCM. Visible in thin
  patients — avoid direct puncture. Hematoma risk.
- **Carotid sheath** (common carotid artery + internal jugular
  vein + vagus nerve): deep to SCM. Deep insertion risks
  arterial puncture (hemorrhage, pseudoaneurysm) or vagal
  stimulation (bradycardia, syncope).
- **Accessory nerve (CN XI)**: in the posterior triangle at this
  level — injury causes weakness of trapezius (dropped shoulder)
  and sternocleidomastoid
- Shallow insertion ONLY (5-10 mm)
- Avoid in patients on anticoagulants
- Palpate for the external jugular vein before insertion —
  adjust needle position if the vein is visible

## LI18 — Phù Đột (扶突, Fú Tū)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Support the Prominence" (扶 = support/brace, 突 =
prominence/protrusion — referring to the laryngeal prominence,
Adam's apple) — on the lateral neck, level with the tip of the
laryngeal prominence, between the sternal and clavicular heads
of the SCM. A **Window of Heaven** (天牖穴) point — one of the
ORIGINAL 5 points listed in Ling Shu chapters 2 and 21, along
with ST9 (人迎), TE16 (天牖), BL10 (天柱), and LU3 (天府). The
later-expanded group includes ~10 points forming a "cervical
ring" around the neck at the body-head transition zone. Window
of Heaven points traditionally treat conditions where Qi fails
to ascend to the head — throat obstruction, voice loss, sensory
disturbances, disorientation. LI18 is the ONLY Window of Heaven
point on the LI channel, consistent with the channel's
trajectory crossing the throat en route to the contralateral
nostril. Positioned 1 cun above LI17 (Tianding), the two points
form a vertical pair on the lateral neck. SAFETY-CRITICAL — the
carotid sheath lies directly deep to the SCM at this level,
making LI18 one of the highest-risk acupoints on the LI channel
(alongside LI13).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral side of the
neck, level with the tip of the laryngeal prominence (Adam's
apple), between the sternal head and the clavicular head of the
sternocleidomastoid muscle.

Anatomical landmarks: find the laryngeal prominence (Adam's
apple). At this level, the SCM can be divided into its two
heads — the sternal head (medial, attaching to the sternum)
and clavicular head (lateral, attaching to the clavicle). LI18
is in the gap between these two heads. 1 cun above LI17
(Tianding). ST9 (Renying, carotid pulse) is at the level of the
carotid bifurcation, approximately 3 cun superior.

Needle depth: 5–10 mm perpendicular. EXTREME CAUTION — carotid
artery directly deep.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Cutaneous: transverse cervical nerve (C2-C3) — branch of the
  cervical plexus providing cutaneous sensation to the
  anterolateral neck
- Deep: ansa cervicalis (C1-C3) — branches to infrahyoid (strap)
  muscles. **Vagus nerve (CN X)** within the carotid sheath —
  the most significant deep neural structure at this location.
- Superior laryngeal nerve (vagal branch): external branch on
  the surface of the inferior constrictor muscle, deep and
  medial. Internal branch pierces the thyrohyoid membrane.
- Sympathetic chain: posterior to the carotid sheath, against
  the prevertebral fascia
- Spinal segments: C2-C3
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular:
- **Common carotid artery** — within the carotid sheath, directly
  deep to SCM. At the laryngeal prominence level, the common
  carotid has not yet bifurcated (bifurcation at approximately
  C3-C4 vertebral level, near the upper thyroid cartilage border)
- **Internal jugular vein** — within the carotid sheath, lateral
  to the carotid artery
- Superior thyroid artery — branch of the external carotid (or
  common carotid at this pre-bifurcation level)
→ Cross-ref: A04

Tissue layers: skin → platysma → between sternal and clavicular
heads of SCM → carotid sheath (common carotid artery, internal
jugular vein, vagus nerve).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

**Window of Heaven (天牖穴)**

Classical source: Ling Shu (灵枢) chapters 2 and 21. LI18 is one
of the ORIGINAL 5 Window of Heaven points in the Ling Shu Ch.21
listing: ST9 (人迎), **LI18 (扶突)**, TE16 (天牖), BL10 (天柱),
LU3 (天府). The expanded group of ~10 points — including SI16,
SI17, CV22, GV16, and PC1 — is a later compilation.

The ~10 Window of Heaven points:
- LU3 Tianfu (天府) — medial arm [ONLY limb point]
- **LI18 Futu (扶突) — anterolateral neck** [this point]
- ST9 Renying (人迎) — anterolateral neck, carotid pulse
- SI16 Tianchuang (天窗) — lateral neck
- SI17 Tianrong (天容) — below angle of mandible
- BL10 Tianzhu (天柱) — posterior neck
- TE16 Tianyou (天牖) — posterolateral neck
- CV22 Tiantu (天突) — suprasternal notch
- GV16 Fengfu (风府) — suboccipital
- PC1 Tianchi (天池) — lateral chest (some traditions)
→ Cross-ref: LU3 entry (Window of Heaven detailed documentation,
AP-01 LU)

Window of Heaven traditional indications: loss of consciousness,
disorientation, throat obstruction, goiter, sensory disturbances
(vision, hearing), epistaxis.

Anatomical pattern of the group: 9 of 10 points form a ring
around the neck at the body-head transition zone — the critical
anatomical bottleneck where ALL ascending/descending pathways
(carotid arteries, vertebral arteries, jugular veins, vagus
nerves, sympathetic chains, esophagus, trachea, spinal cord)
pass through the narrowest cross-section. LU3 is the sole
exception, sitting on the medial arm — explained by the Lung
channel's internal pathway rather than anatomical proximity.

LI18 within the cervical ring: anterolateral position. Together
with ST9 (also anterolateral, at the carotid pulse), LI18
flanks the carotid-jugular-vagal complex from the anterior
aspect. Compare: SI16/SI17 (lateral neck), BL10 (posterior),
TE16 (posterolateral), GV16 (posterior midline), CV22 (anterior
midline). The ring distributes Window of Heaven points around the full
circumference of the neck, providing therapeutic access from
every angle. See Obs. 26.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI18 Futu (扶突). Functions: benefits the
throat, descends Lung Qi, resolves phlegm. Indications: cough,
wheezing, sore throat, loss of voice, difficulty swallowing,
goiter, hiccup.

**2. Trigger point:** SCM TrPs between the sternal and
clavicular heads — referred pain to the throat, sternal region,
and periorbital area. Moderate correspondence. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Reactive in SCM spasm, torticollis,
and throat pain. Tender when palpated in patients with
voice/throat disorders.

**4. Marma point:** Nila Marma (नील, "blue/sapphire") — flanking
the throat. LI18's position between the SCM heads corresponds
more closely to Nila Marma than LI17 (which is more posterior).
Nila is classified as Sira Marma (vascular) — consistent with
LI18's carotid sheath proximity. Moderate correspondence. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: C04

**5. Head zone:** C2-C3 dermatome. Cervical viscerotome for
larynx, pharynx, thyroid — strong segmental support for
throat/voice indications.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line → SCM component. The SCM
connects the sternum/clavicle to the mastoid process, bridging
the cervical spine. Between the SCM heads is a fascial boundary
within the Lateral Line.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — neck region, major
vessels immediately deep. No cupping at cervical vascular
structures.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi, Marma). Same as
LI17 — moderate convergence at the anterolateral neck. The
throat region has universal cross-tradition recognition for
voice and swallowing treatment.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Ling Shu (Window of Heaven context, Ch. 2 and 21): included
  in a group of points for conditions where Qi fails to ascend
  to the head — disorientation, throat obstruction, sensory
  disturbances
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: cough, wheezing, sore throat, loss of
  voice (暴喑), difficulty swallowing, goiter
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: hiccup, sudden inability to speak, scrofula,
  throat swelling and pain

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Benefits the throat: sore throat, loss of voice, hoarseness,
  pharyngitis, laryngitis
- Descends Lung Qi: cough, wheezing, dyspnea
- Resolves phlegm-nodules: goiter (thyroid enlargement), scrofula
- Difficulty swallowing, globus sensation (梅核气)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: used for throat and voice conditions,
often combined with CV22 (天突) and LI17 (天鼎). The Window of
Heaven designation gives LI18 a broader theoretical scope than
LI17 — treating not just local throat symptoms but also
head/brain conditions where ascending flow is blocked. However,
in modern practice LI18 is primarily selected for throat/voice
conditions — the broader Window of Heaven indications (disorientation,
sensory disturbances) are more commonly treated via other Window of Heaven
points (GV16, BL10, ST9) or non-Window of Heaven points. The Window of
Heaven group as a whole lacks systematic clinical evaluation.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for LI18 individually. The Window
of Heaven group as a whole has not been systematically evaluated
in controlled studies.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

The evidence gap at LI18 is architecturally notable: a Window
of Heaven point with Ling Shu Ch.21 designation and rich
classical tradition, yet zero modern evidence. This mirrors the
pattern at LU3 (Window of Heaven, rich classical tradition, zero
evidence) and extends to the entire Window of Heaven group — none of the ~10
points has been tested specifically FOR its Window of Heaven-designated
indications (disorientation, ascending-Qi obstruction, sensory
disturbances).
→ Cross-ref: LU3 (AP-01)

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No sensitization data for LI18. The lateral neck between the
SCM heads may become tender in patients with throat/laryngeal
conditions, but this has not been studied using the dynamic
acupoint methodology (Chen Rixin, Kim 2017).
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Throat/voice effects (cough, voice loss, dysphagia):**
Segmentally plausible. LI18 accesses C2-C3 spinal segments,
which overlap with the cervical viscerotome for the larynx and
pharynx. The superior laryngeal nerve (vagal branch) runs deep
and medial — needle stimulation at LI18 may modulate laryngeal/
pharyngeal function via local neural effects on this branch.

**Cervical vagus proximity:** the vagus nerve within the carotid
sheath is the most significant deep structure. Stimulation near
(but not directly of) the vagus at this level could influence
vagal tone. Cervical vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) is an
established medical treatment (for epilepsy, depression) that
works via afferent vagal projections to the NTS/brainstem.
LI18's proximity to the cervical vagus raises the possibility
that traditional Window of Heaven effects partially operate through vagal
afferent stimulation — a non-invasive, naturally lower-intensity
analog of VNS. This hypothesis connects LI18 to the same
NTS/vagal pathway documented for PC6 and ST36, but via CERVICAL
vagal access rather than peripheral nerve → brainstem relay.
→ Cross-ref: A02 S2.3 (vagal pathways), PC6 entry (AP-09)

**Goiter indication:** the thyroid gland is immediately deep and
medial. Local neural and vascular effects at the thyroid's blood
supply (superior thyroid artery) are plausible but unconfirmed.

**Window of Heaven (head/brain effects):** the cervical ring
hypothesis (Obs. 26) suggests that Window of Heaven points modulate ascending
flow at the body's anatomical bottleneck. For LI18, the relevant
ascending pathways are: (1) carotid artery → cerebral blood
supply, (2) vagus nerve → NTS/brainstem, (3) cervical
sympathetic chain → sympathetic innervation of cerebral
vasculature. Modulating any of these could influence head/brain
function — but this is entirely theoretical for acupuncture at
LI18.

**Gap:** no mechanism study specific to LI18. The cervical vagus
proximity is the most interesting mechanistic hypothesis but
completely untested for acupuncture at this point. The entire
Window of Heaven group lacks mechanism investigation.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 5–10 mm perpendicular. SHALLOW ONLY. Identify
  the SCM heads by asking the patient to turn the head — the
  gap between sternal and clavicular heads becomes palpable.
- De Qi: local distension and soreness in the neck, may radiate
  toward the throat or ear.
- EA: RARELY used at this location due to carotid proximity.
  Manual needle manipulation preferred. If EA is used, low
  intensity only — electrical stimulation near the carotid sinus
  can cause reflex bradycardia.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02): gentle pressure at the lateral neck between
SCM heads. Patient supine with head supported. 10-30 seconds —
shorter duration than limb points due to vascular proximity.
Avoid sustained heavy pressure (carotid sinus stimulation risk
if pressure transmits medially to the carotid bifurcation area).
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Cứu (B08): indirect moxa applicable for chronic throat
conditions (Wind-Cold pattern). Direct moxa contraindicated
(thin neck skin, burn risk). Moxa stick at moderate distance.
→ Cross-ref: B08 S4.3

Dry needling (B11): SCM TrPs between the heads are dry needling
targets, but the location requires extreme caution due to
vascular risk — only experienced practitioners. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

### An toàn

**HIGH risk — one of the highest-risk points on the LI channel
(alongside LI13).**

The carotid sheath is directly deep to the SCM at this level.
LI18 sits between the sternal and clavicular heads — the
thinnest part of the SCM coverage over the carotid sheath.

Precautions:
- **Common carotid artery**: directly deep. Puncture risks
  hemorrhage in the neck compartment, pseudoaneurysm, or
  arterial dissection.
- **Internal jugular vein**: lateral to the carotid in the
  sheath. Puncture causes hematoma, potential air embolism.
- **Vagus nerve**: within the carotid sheath. Direct stimulation
  causes bradycardia, possible syncope. In elderly patients,
  carotid sinus hypersensitivity may cause severe bradycardia.
- **Superior laryngeal nerve**: deep and medial — injury causes
  voice changes, loss of protective cough reflex
- Shallow insertion ONLY (5-10 mm maximum)
- NEVER needle deep or medially directed
- Palpate for the carotid pulse before insertion — position
  needle POSTERIOR to the carotid pulse
- Contraindicated in patients with carotid artery disease,
  carotid sinus hypersensitivity, or on anticoagulants
- Many practitioners prefer moxibustion or acupressure at this
  location to avoid needling risk

Comparison with LI17 (1 cun below): both are HIGH risk, but
LI18 is higher because it is BETWEEN the SCM heads (thinner
muscle coverage) while LI17 is at the posterior BORDER of SCM
(more muscle bulk separating needle from carotid). LI18's
position directly over the carotid sheath with minimal SCM
protection makes it the more dangerous of the two.
→ Cross-ref: LI13 (HIGH risk — radial nerve/artery)

> **Batch 6 Architectural Observations (LI15, LI16, LI17, LI18 — 2 Tier B + 2 Tier C)**
>
> **Obs. 25 — SHOULDER-NECK TRANSITION: MULTI-RISK EMERGENCE.**
> LI15-LI18 spans only 4 points (shoulder to mid-neck) but
> introduces THREE new risk types not seen on the LI arm segment
> (LI1-LI14): (1) pneumothorax at LI16 (lung apex deep to
> supraspinous fossa — first pneumothorax risk on the LI channel),
> (2) major cervical vessel exposure at LI17-LI18 (external
> jugular superficially, carotid-jugular-vagal complex deep to
> SCM), (3) cranial nerve vulnerability at LI17 (accessory nerve
> CN XI in posterior triangle) and LI18 (vagus nerve CN X in
> carotid sheath). The entire forearm-arm segment (LI1-LI14) had a
> single neurovascular risk system: the radial nerve and its
> accompanying arteries. The shoulder-neck transition introduces
> multi-system risks because the anatomical architecture
> CONVERGES: the lung apex rises superiorly, the great vessels
> ascend through the neck, and cranial nerves traverse the
> posterior triangle — all in a compact region. The risk profile
> transforms from single-nerve to multi-system in the span of 4
> points. Safety coding shifts accordingly: forearm risks are
> addressable by needle DEPTH control, while neck risks require
> POSITIONAL awareness (palpating for vessels, identifying nerve
> paths, choosing between SCM borders vs. between SCM heads).
>
> **Obs. 26 — WINDOW OF HEAVEN: CERVICAL RING ARCHITECTURE.** 9
> of 10 Window of Heaven points form a ring around the neck at
> approximately the laryngeal prominence level: LI18
> (anterolateral), ST9 (anterolateral, carotid), SI16 (lateral),
> SI17 (submandibular), BL10 (posterior), TE16 (posterolateral),
> CV22 (anterior midline), GV16 (posterior midline), PC1 (lateral
> chest/cervicothoracic junction). Only LU3 (medial arm) breaks
> the pattern — explained by the Lung channel's internal pathway.
> The ring encircles the critical body-head transition zone where
> ALL ascending/descending pathways (carotid arteries, vertebral
> arteries, jugular veins, vagus nerves, sympathetic chains,
> esophagus, trachea, spinal cord) pass through the narrowest
> cross-section of the body. The Window of Heaven concept — treating conditions
> where Qi fails to ascend to the head — maps directly to this
> anatomical bottleneck. The clinical tradition identified the
> most VULNERABLE transit zone in the body and placed treatment
> points around its full circumference, providing access from
> every angle. This is the first FUNCTIONAL POINT GROUP in the
> Atlas with a coherent anatomical explanation: the cervical ring
> monitors and modulates ascending flow at the body's narrowest
> corridor. A second potential mechanism: the Window of Heaven ring provides
> access to the cervical vagus nerve from multiple angles (LI18
> and ST9 anterolateral, SI17 lateral) — a distributed
> non-invasive vagal modulation system that prefigured VNS by
> millennia. Cross-ref: LU3 Obs. 18 (only limb Window of Heaven point).
>
> **Obs. 27 — PROTOCOL INCLUSION WITHOUT ISOLATION: THE SHOULDER
> EVIDENCE PARADOX.** LI15 is the most frequently included acupoint
> in systematic reviews of acupuncture for frozen shoulder
> (Ben-Arie et al. 2020) and post-stroke shoulder pain, yet no
> study has isolated its individual contribution. This creates a
> paradox: LI15 is simultaneously the most STUDIED shoulder
> acupoint (by inclusion frequency) and the most UNPROVEN (by
> isolation standards). The same pattern recurs across the Atlas:
> LI10 for epicondylitis, LI4+LI11 in nearly all protocols, SP6
> in gynecological studies — multi-point protocols provide evidence
> for the COMBINATION but not for individual points. This is a
> systematic methodological gap in acupuncture research: the
> clinical tradition operates through point COMBINATIONS based on
> centuries of empirical pattern-recognition, while the evidence
> model (RCT) demands SINGLE-VARIABLE isolation. Points rated
> 🟡 Plausible in the Atlas are overwhelmingly points included in
> positive protocols but never tested alone. The distinction
> between "protocol evidence" (combination tested, individual
> contribution unknown) and "point evidence" (specific point
> isolated and tested) should be explicit in confidence
> assignments. Most of acupuncture's evidence base is protocol
> evidence.

## LI19 — Hòa Liêu (禾髎, Hé Liáo)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**
"Grain Crevice" (禾 = grain/cereal stalk, 髎 = bone
crevice/foramen) — on the upper lip, directly below the lateral
margin of the nostril, level with GV26 (Renzhong/Shuigou). The
name may reference the philtral columns (vertical ridges on the
upper lip resembling grain stalks) with the point in the
"crevice" beside them. LI19 is the penultimate point on the LI
channel, marking the channel's arrival on the face in trigeminal
nerve territory (V2, maxillary branch of CN V) — a dramatic
shift from the cervical plexus innervation at LI17-LI18.
Traditional indications center on nasal symptoms (obstruction,
epistaxis, rhinorrhea) and facial conditions (deviation of mouth
in facial paralysis). Clinically overshadowed by neighboring
LI20 (at the ala nasi), which captures the rhinitis evidence
base.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the upper lip, directly
below the lateral margin of the nostril, at the level of GV26
(Renzhong), approximately 0.5 cun lateral to the midline of the
philtrum.

Anatomical landmarks: locate the ala nasi (nostril wing) — LI19
is directly inferior, at the same horizontal level as the
philtrum midpoint. Palpation: in the nasolabial area, between
the nostril margin above and the vermilion border below.

Needle depth: 3–5 mm oblique or transverse, directed superiorly
toward the nostril base. Shallow insertion only — thin tissue.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Cutaneous: infraorbital nerve (V2, maxillary branch of
  trigeminal nerve CN V) — terminal branches providing sensation
  to the upper lip, lateral nasal area, and lower eyelid region.
  FIRST trigeminal territory point on the LI channel — all prior
  points (LI1-LI18) were innervated by spinal nerves (C2-C8).
- Motor: buccal branches of the facial nerve (CN VII) —
  innervating orbicularis oris, levator labii superioris,
  zygomaticus minor
- Spinal segments: N/A — cranial nerve territory (CN V2, CN VII)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular:
- Superior labial artery (branch of facial artery) — runs within
  the upper lip submucosa. Bleeding risk if punctured.
- Angular artery (terminal facial artery) ascending nearby toward
  the medial canthus
→ Cross-ref: A04

Tissue layers: skin → orbicularis oris muscle → submucosa of the
upper lip. Thin tissue layers — no deep compartments.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
No special category designation.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI19 Heliao (禾髎). Functions: opens the
nasal passages, expels Wind. Indications: nasal obstruction,
epistaxis, rhinorrhea, anosmia, deviation of mouth (facial
paralysis).

**2. Trigger point:** levator labii superioris / orbicularis
oris TrPs — referred pain and tenderness in the upper lip and
nasal area. Weak-moderate correspondence — facial TrPs are less
well-characterized than limb TrPs. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** some reactivity in facial paralysis
(Bell's palsy) — tenderness along the nasolabial fold when
facial muscles are in spasm or recovery.

**4. Marma point:** Phana Marma (फण, "nostril/snake hood") —
at the sides of the nostrils. Phana corresponds more precisely
to LI20 (at the ala nasi) than LI19 (below the nostril). Weak
correspondence. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: C04

**5. Head zone:** V2 (maxillary) dermatome. Within the
trigeminal viscerotome for nasal mucosa and upper dentition —
segmental support for nasal indications.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Front Line terminus at the
facial muscles. Minimal fascial significance at this level.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — face.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Ashi, Head zone). Low
convergence — LI19 sits in the shadow of LI20, which captures
the primary cross-tradition interest at the nostril.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: nasal obstruction (鼻塞), epistaxis
  (鼻衄), rhinorrhea
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: deviation of mouth (口喎, facial paralysis),
  lockjaw (口噤)

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Opens the nasal passages: nasal obstruction, rhinorrhea,
  epistaxis
- Expels Wind from the face: deviation of mouth (Bell's palsy),
  lockjaw

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: infrequently used as a primary point.
Most practitioners prefer LI20 (directly beside the nostril) for
nasal conditions and local facial points (ST4, ST6, LI4) for
facial paralysis. LI19 may be included as a secondary point in
nasal or facial protocols. The combination LI19 + LI20 provides
a vertical approach to the nasolabial region.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for LI19 individually.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**LOW risk.**

The upper lip region has no major deep structures.

Precautions:
- **Superior labial artery**: runs in the upper lip submucosa.
  Puncture causes moderate bleeding — apply firm pressure for
  2-3 minutes.
- Thin facial skin — bruising visible (cosmetic concern)
- Shallow insertion only (3-5 mm)
- Use fine gauge needles (0.20-0.25 mm) for comfort

## LI20 — Nghênh Hương (迎香, Yíng Xiāng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Welcome Fragrance" (迎 = welcome/greet, 香 = fragrance/aroma)
— in the nasolabial sulcus, at the level of the midpoint of the
lateral border of the ala nasi (nostril wing). The name directly
references the primary traditional function: restoring the sense
of smell — the point where one "welcomes fragrance" back. One of
the most descriptively named acupoints in the system. LI20 is
the TERMINAL POINT of the Large Intestine channel and the site
of the classical MIDLINE CROSSING (左之右，右之左, Ling Shu):
the channel from the ipsilateral arm traverses the face, crosses
the philtrum at GV26, and arrives at the CONTRALATERAL LI20,
then connects to ST1 (Chengqi) — establishing the Hand Yangming
→ Foot Yangming continuity. LI20 is the only point on the LI
face segment with modern evidence: multiple systematic reviews
and RCTs support acupuncture protocols including LI20 for
allergic rhinitis, with a proposed mechanism via the trigeminal
nerve-sphenopalatine ganglion-nasal mucosa pathway. Located in
trigeminal territory (V2, infraorbital nerve) — the same cranial
nerve that innervates the nasal mucosa internally, providing a
direct neural pathway from the surface stimulation point to the
internal treatment target.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): in the nasolabial sulcus
(nasolabial groove), at the level of the midpoint of the lateral
border of the ala nasi (nostril wing).

Anatomical landmarks: find the ala nasi — the wing of the nose.
At its midpoint laterally, drop into the nasolabial sulcus (the
fold between the cheek and the nose). LI20 is in this groove.
GV25 (Suliao) is at the nasal tip above. LI19 is inferior, at
the upper lip level. ST1 (Chengqi) — the next point in the Yang
Ming sequence — is above, at the infraorbital ridge below the
eye.

Needle depth: 5–10 mm oblique, directed medially and superiorly
along the nasolabial sulcus toward the nasal bone. Some
practitioners direct the needle upward along the lateral nasal
wall for maximal nasal effect.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Cutaneous: infraorbital nerve (V2, maxillary branch of CN V)
  — terminal branches emerging from the infraorbital foramen (at
  ST1/ST2 level), fanning downward to supply the lower eyelid,
  lateral nose, and upper lip. At LI20, these are the most
  distal terminal branches.
- **Deep significance:** the infraorbital nerve is a branch of
  the SAME maxillary division (V2) that gives off the
  nasopalatine and superior alveolar nerves supplying the nasal
  mucosa internally. LI20's cutaneous nerve shares its parent
  trunk with the nasal mucosal nerves — providing a direct
  neural pathway from the surface point to the internal target.
- Motor: buccal branches of the facial nerve (CN VII) — levator
  labii superioris alaeque nasi, nasalis (transverse part)
- **Sphenopalatine ganglion connection:** V2 gives off the
  pterygopalatine nerves that synapse in the sphenopalatine
  (pterygopalatine) ganglion — a major parasympathetic relay
  controlling nasal mucosal vasodilation, secretion, and
  inflammation. This ganglion is the key relay in LI20's
  proposed mechanism for rhinitis.
- Spinal segments: N/A — cranial nerve territory (CN V2, CN VII)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular:
- Lateral nasal artery (branch of facial artery) — along the
  ala nasi
- Angular artery — the terminal branch of the facial artery,
  ascending toward the medial canthus, passing near LI20 before
  anastomosing with branches of the ophthalmic artery
→ Cross-ref: A04

Tissue layers: skin → levator labii superioris alaeque nasi →
nasalis (transverse part) → periosteum of the maxilla (lateral
surface). The nasal cavity is medial — the lateral nasal wall
separates the needle from the nasal mucosa.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

**Channel terminal point (止穴):** LI20 is where the LI channel
ends on the face. The classical description (Ling Shu) specifies
a CONTRALATERAL crossing: "左之右，右之左" (left goes to right,
right goes to left) — the channel from the right arm arrives at
the LEFT nostril. From the contralateral LI20, qi flow continues
to ST1 (Chengqi, below the eye), establishing the Hand Yangming
(LI) → Foot Yangming (ST) transition. This is the only
contralateral crossing in the entire 12-channel cycle — all
other channel transitions connect ipsilaterally.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LI20 Yingxiang (迎香). Functions: opens
the nasal passages (通鼻窍), expels Wind, clears Heat. THE
primary acupoint for all nasal conditions in TCM — first-choice
point for rhinitis, sinusitis, anosmia, epistaxis. Also used for
facial paralysis and biliary ascariasis (蛔厥) — an empirical
tradition with specific mechanism support (vagal-mediated
biliary duct relaxation).

**2. Trigger point:** levator labii superioris alaeque nasi TrP
— referred sensation to the lateral nose and ala nasi. Moderate
correspondence. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** highly reactive in acute rhinitis,
sinusitis, and nasal obstruction. The nasolabial sulcus becomes
tender during nasal inflammation. Strong ashi response.

**4. Marma point:** Phana Marma (फण, "nostril/snake hood") —
at the sides of the nostrils. Classified as Snayu Marma
(ligament/tendon) in Sushruta Samhita. Strong correspondence
— both LI20 and Phana target the alar region for respiratory
and olfactory conditions. 🟢
→ Cross-ref: C04

**5. Head zone:** V2 (maxillary) dermatome. The nasal mucosa
receives sensory supply from V2 branches (nasopalatine, anterior
ethmoidal, superior alveolar) — LI20 is in the SAME dermatomal
territory as its internal treatment target, providing the
strongest segmental correspondence on the LI channel.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Front Line terminus at the
facial muscles. Minimal fascial significance.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** the nasolabial area may
exhibit neurogenic inflammatory changes during rhinitis — C-fiber
sensitization with SP, CGRP, VIP release. Consistent with the
proposed mechanism but not formally studied as a neurogenic spot.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — face.

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi, Marma, Head zone).
Highest convergence on the LI face segment. Matches LI15's
score (highest shoulder-neck). The nasal region is a universal
treatment target for respiratory and olfactory conditions.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Ling Shu: described in the LI channel trajectory. The name
  迎香 documents the olfactory function directly.
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: nasal obstruction (鼻塞), epistaxis
  (鼻衄), rhinorrhea (鼻涕), anosmia (不闻香臭), nasal polyps
  (鼻息肉)
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: deviation of mouth (口喎, facial paralysis),
  facial edema, biliary ascariasis (蛔厥)
- Bencao Gangmu (Li Shizhen): LI20 for restoring olfaction

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Opens the nasal passages: nasal obstruction, rhinorrhea
  (allergic and non-allergic), sinusitis, anosmia, nasal polyps
- Expels Wind from the face: facial paralysis (Bell's palsy),
  facial itching, trigeminal neuralgia
- Biliary ascariasis: empirical tradition with specific
  mechanism support (trigeminal → vagal reflex → biliary duct
  relaxation)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: THE most-used acupoint for nasal
conditions. Virtually every acupuncture protocol for rhinitis,
sinusitis, or anosmia includes bilateral LI20. Standard nasal
protocol: LI20 + EX-HN3 (Yintang) + GV23 (Shangxing) + LI4
(Hegu). Through-needling LI20 → EX-HN8 (Shangyingxiang/Bitong,
上迎香) is a common clinical technique for enhanced nasal effect,
activating V2 at LI20 insertion and V1 nasociliary branch at
the EX-HN8 endpoint (nasal bone–lateral cartilage junction).
EX-HN8 featured in 39-RCT MA (Yin 2020, n=3,433) for allergic
rhinitis as one of the most-used points.
→ Cross-ref: AP-15 EX-HN8
The LI4 + LI20 combination is one of the most
fundamental point pairs in acupuncture — distal (hand) + local
(nose) targeting, following the "yuan-distal + local"
prescription principle. The biliary ascariasis indication is
historically notable: one of the few traditional indications
with a specific modern mechanism explanation (needle stimulation
→ trigeminal afferents → brainstem → vagal efferents → biliary
duct relaxation → worm expulsion). This indication was validated
in Chinese hospitals in the mid-20th century but is now rarely
needed as antiparasitic drugs are more effective.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**Allergic rhinitis:**
- ACUSAR trial (Brinkhaus et al. 2013, Annals of Internal
  Medicine): large multicenter RCT (n=422), 3-arm (verum
  acupuncture vs sham acupuncture vs rescue medication alone).
  Protocol: bilateral LI20, LI4, ST36, ST2 + unilateral EX-HN3,
  GV23. Verum superior to sham and rescue medication for seasonal
  allergic rhinitis. TNSS (Total Nasal Symptom Score) and RQLQ
  (Rhinitis Quality of Life) significantly improved. NOTE: LI20
  was part of a 6-point protocol — individual contribution not
  isolated. 🟡
- Multiple systematic reviews (30+ RCTs, 4,400+ participants):
  acupuncture for allergic rhinitis consistently positive vs no
  treatment and sham. LI20 identified as the most frequently
  used acupoint across all rhinitis RCTs. Evidence quality:
  moderate to low (GRADE), significant heterogeneity.
- Warm needle acupuncture SR: LI20 in the top 3 most frequently
  applied points.

**Sinusitis/chronic rhinosinusitis:**
- RCTs combining acupuncture at LI20 with intranasal
  corticosteroid for chronic rhinosinusitis without polyps:
  significant improvement in SNOT-22 scores, particularly nasal
  obstruction and facial pressure. 🟡

**Mechanism evidence (strongest for any LI face point):**
- Trigeminal-sphenopalatine ganglion pathway: LI20 stimulation
  inhibits C-fiber afferent impulses via V2 → sphenopalatine
  ganglion → nasal mucosa. Downregulates neuropeptides (SP,
  CGRP, VIP) in the axon reflex, reducing neurogenic
  inflammatory response.
- Acupoint injection at LI20: downregulates histamine receptors
  H1 and H4 in nasal mucosa. Upregulates Foxp3 (regulatory
  T-cell marker), suppresses RORγt and serum IL-17 (Th17
  pathway suppression).
- IgE suppression: inhibits serum IgE increase.

**Anosmia:**
- Case reports for post-COVID anosmia include LI20. No
  controlled studies. 🔴

Overall evidence: **🟡 Plausible** for allergic rhinitis and
sinusitis. Strong protocol evidence (ACUSAR trial + multiple
SRs) with a well-characterized local mechanism (trigeminal-
sphenopalatine pathway). LI20's individual contribution has not
been isolated from multi-point protocols — the same "protocol
inclusion without isolation" pattern as LI15 (Obs. 27). However,
the mechanism evidence specifically favors LI20's contribution:
the trigeminal-sphenopalatine pathway is LOCAL to LI20's
anatomical position, making LI20 the most mechanistically
justified point in rhinitis protocols.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No formal sensitization data for LI20 using the dynamic acupoint
methodology (Chen Rixin, Kim 2017). Clinically, the nasolabial
area shows increased tenderness and reactivity during acute
rhinitis and sinusitis — consistent with disease-state
sensitization. The neurogenic inflammation model (SP, CGRP, VIP
release at the nasal mucosa) predicts lowered sensory thresholds
in the nasolabial sulcus during active nasal inflammation.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Nasal effects (rhinitis, sinusitis, nasal obstruction):**
LOCAL trigeminal pathway — the strongest mechanism for any LI
face point. LI20 stimulation activates infraorbital nerve (V2)
afferents that share the maxillary trunk with the nasopalatine
and superior alveolar nerves innervating the nasal mucosa. The
proposed pathway: needle at LI20 → V2 afferents → sphenopalatine
ganglion modulation → inhibition of parasympathetic outflow to
nasal mucosa → reduced vasodilation, secretion, and neurogenic
inflammation. Additionally, the axon reflex pathway: LI20
stimulation preemptively inhibits C-fiber afferent impulses,
reducing neuropeptide release (SP, CGRP, VIP) in the nasal
mucosa.

This mechanism is fundamentally DIFFERENT from the supraspinal
relay pathways documented for LI4 (→PAG descending modulation)
and LI11 (→RVLM sympathoinhibition). LI20 operates through a
LOCAL cranial nerve pathway — the stimulation point and the
treatment target share the same nerve trunk (V2). This represents
a different mechanism CLASS: local/segmental vs. distant/
supraspinal. The LI channel thus spans at least two mechanism
classes across its 20 points.
→ Cross-ref: LI4 (PAG pathway), LI11 (RVLM pathway), A02 S2.3

**Anosmia (olfactory restoration):**
The olfactory nerve (CN I) enters the nasal cavity through the
cribriform plate at the roof. LI20 is located laterally and
inferiorly — not near the olfactory epithelium. The mechanism for
olfactory restoration likely involves reducing mucosal edema and
inflammation that mechanically blocks odorant access to the
olfactory epithelium, rather than direct olfactory nerve
stimulation.

**Facial paralysis:**
CN VII motor branches (buccal, zygomatic) course near LI20.
Local stimulation may promote nerve recovery or reduce perineural
inflammation. Segmental mechanism.

**Biliary ascariasis (trigeminal-vagal reflex):**
The most unexpected traditional indication — a facial point for
biliary parasites. Proposed mechanism: LI20 stimulation →
trigeminal afferents (V2) → trigeminal nucleus in brainstem →
projections to nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) → vagal efferent
activation → hepatic vagal branch → biliary duct relaxation →
relief of biliary colic. This is a somato-visceral reflex via
the trigeminal-vagal connection in the brainstem. The trigeminal
nucleus has documented projections to the NTS — the same
brainstem relay used by PC6 (GABAergic) and ST36 (PROKR2+)
vagal pathways — but accessed via a CRANIAL nerve (V) rather
than peripheral vagal or spinal afferents. If confirmed, this
would represent a third access route to the NTS in the Atlas:
(1) peripheral vagal afferents (PC6, ST36), (2) spinal somatic
afferents (LI4→PAG), (3) trigeminal afferents (LI20→NTS).
→ Cross-ref: PC6 (NTS pathway, AP-09), ST36 (NTS/vagal pathway)

**Gap:** no fMRI or neuroimaging study for LI20 stimulation. The
trigeminal-sphenopalatine pathway is proposed from anatomical
reasoning and animal data, not confirmed by functional imaging
in humans. The biliary ascariasis trigeminal-vagal mechanism is
physiologically plausible (trigeminal-vagal reflexes are well-
established, e.g., diving reflex, oculocardiac reflex) but
unconfirmed for LI20 specifically.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 5–10 mm oblique, directed medially-superiorly
  along the nasolabial sulcus. The needle tip slides along the
  lateral nasal wall toward the nasal bone.
- De Qi: local distension and soreness in the nasolabial area.
  May produce lacrimation or nasal secretion — often considered
  a positive sign indicating activation of the nasal reflex.
- EA: applicable. Low frequency (2 Hz) between bilateral LI20
  is commonly used in allergic rhinitis protocols (ACUSAR trial
  used manual acupuncture, but other RCTs use EA).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02): press firmly into the nasolabial sulcus,
directing pressure medially against the maxilla. Self-acupressure
at LI20 is one of the most commonly taught self-care techniques
for nasal congestion. Circular pressure for 30-60 seconds per
side, repeated several times. Immediate effect often noticeable
(transient nasal opening via reflex vasomotor response).
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Cứu (B08): indirect moxa — moxa stick at moderate distance from
the face. Direct moxa contraindicated (facial burn risk). Used
for chronic rhinitis with Wind-Cold pattern (clear watery
discharge, cold sensitivity).
→ Cross-ref: B08 S4.3

Dry needling (B11): NOT a standard dry needling target. The
nasolabial area lacks well-defined myofascial trigger points
suitable for dry needling. Standard acupuncture technique
preferred.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

### An toàn

**LOW risk — one of the safest points on the LI channel.**

The nasolabial area has no major deep structures. Stark contrast
with the neck segment (LI17-LI18 = HIGH risk).

Precautions:
- **Angular artery**: terminal facial artery passes near LI20
  toward the medial canthus. Puncture causes moderate bleeding
  and potential periorbital hematoma if blood tracks superiorly.
  Apply pressure for 2-3 minutes.
- **Lateral nasal artery**: minor bleeding risk
- Thin facial skin — bruising visible (cosmetic concern)
- Do NOT insert deep enough to penetrate the lateral nasal wall
  into the nasal cavity (unlikely at standard depth)
- Use fine gauge needles (0.20-0.25 mm) for facial comfort
- Safe in pregnancy — no pregnancy contraindication for LI20
  (unlike LI4, which is classically contraindicated)
