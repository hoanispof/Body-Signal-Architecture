>
> **Vessel overview:** The Governing Vessel (Du Mai, 督脉) is one of
> the 8 Extraordinary Vessels and one of only 2 (with Ren Mai) that
> possess their own acupoints. Runs along the posterior midline: coccyx
> (GV1 Changqiang) → sacrum → lumbar spine → thoracic spine → cervical
> spine → occiput (GV16 Fengfu) → over cranium to vertex (GV20 Baihui)
> → forehead → nose → upper lip (GV26 Renzhong) → ends inside upper
> gum (GV28 Yinjiao). Internal branch enters brain. Called "Sea of all
> Yang Meridians" (阳脉之海) — all 6 Yang channels converge at GV20.
> Accessed via Eight Confluent pair: SI3 (Houxi) + BL62 (Shenmai).
> 28 points total. No paired channel (extraordinary vessel, not
> principal meridian). No Chinese clock assignment.
>

## Sacral–Lumbar Region (GV1–GV4)

Four points from coccyx tip (GV1) to L2 spinous process (GV4).
This region spans the sacral-lumbar transition of the Du Mai,
from the perineal starting point to the "Gate of Life" at the
level of the kidneys and adrenal glands. Nerve supply transitions
from the coccygeal plexus (S4–S5) at GV1 through the sacral
dorsal rami to the lumbar dorsal rami (L4 at GV3, L2 at GV4).
The corresponding BL Back-Shu points are: GV4↔BL23 (Kidney) at
L2, GV3↔BL25 (Large Intestine) at L4. GV2 at S4 lies below the
standard Back-Shu range.

### GV1 — Trường Cường (长强, Chángqiáng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Long Strong" (长 = long, 强 = strong). Between the coccyx tip
and the anus, on the posterior midline. The STARTING POINT of the
Du Mai and the **Luo-Connecting point of the Governing Vessel** —
part of the 15 Luo-Connecting system (12 regular meridian Luo +
GV + CV + Great Luo of Spleen). The GV Luo vessel ascends from
the coccyx alongside the spine, dispersing to the head — the
longest Luo pathway in the body (coccyx to vertex). Meeting point
of Du Mai and Ren Mai (some classical sources add KI). Primary
point for hemorrhoids, rectal prolapse, and perineal disorders.
Thread embedding at GV1 demonstrated p38 MAPK downregulation in
the lumbar dorsal horn, providing a molecular anti-nociceptive
mechanism.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): below the tip of the coccyx,
on the posterior midline, at the midpoint of the line connecting
the tip of the coccyx and the anus.

Anatomical landmarks: patient in prone or knee-chest position.
The point lies in the shallow depression between the coccyx tip
and the anus, over the anococcygeal ligament (raphe). The
anococcygeal ligament is a midline fibrous band connecting the
coccyx to the posterior border of the external anal sphincter.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.0 cun (~1.2–2.5 cm) oblique superiorly,
parallel to the coccyx/sacrum. Perpendicular insertion risks
rectal puncture. Direction: needle tip aimed superiorly toward
the sacral canal.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Anococcygeal nerve** (S4–S5, coccygeal plexus) — cutaneous
  innervation of the skin between coccyx and anus.
- **Inferior rectal nerve** — branch of pudendal nerve (S2–S4),
  carrying both sensory and motor fibers to the external anal
  sphincter and perianal skin.
- **Coccygeal nerve branches** (S4–S5 dorsal rami) — to the skin
  over the coccyx.
- Spinal segments: S2–S5 (dual territory — pudendal + coccygeal
  plexus).

Fascial plane: anococcygeal ligament (midline raphe between
coccyx and anus). Superficial: subcutaneous fat of the perineum.
Deep: coccygeus muscle (ischiococcygeus) and levator ani
(pubococcygeus, iliococcygeus) attach nearby.

Vascular: inferior rectal artery and vein (branches of internal
pudendal artery/vein). Rich perianal venous plexus — the internal
rectal venous plexus (hemorrhoidal plexus) drains to both portal
(superior rectal → inferior mesenteric) and systemic (inferior
rectal → internal pudendal → internal iliac) systems. This
portal-systemic anastomosis is the anatomical substrate for
hemorrhoid formation.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1.3, A04

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → anococcygeal ligament →
coccygeus muscle → levator ani (deep). External anal sphincter
borders the point anteriorly.

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No (Du Mai = extraordinary vessel). Yuan-Source: No.
Xi-Cleft: No. Back-Shu: No. Front-Mu: No. Eight Confluent: No.
Hui-Meeting: No.

**Luo-Connecting point of the Governing Vessel (督脉络穴).** Part
of the body's 15 Luo-Connecting system (12 regular + GV + CV +
Great Luo of Spleen). The GV Luo vessel separates at GV1 and
ascends alongside the spine, dispersing to the head. This is the
longest Luo pathway — from coccyx tip to vertex — spanning the
entire posterior midline.
→ Cross-ref: AP-02 (LI6), AP-04 (SP4 + SP21), AP-06 (SI7), B01
S3.1

**Meeting point (交会穴):** GV1 is the meeting point of the
Governing Vessel (Du Mai) with Ren Mai (Conception Vessel). Some
classical sources (Jiayi Jing) add the Kidney channel. The GV-CV
meeting at the perineum represents the lowest confluence of the
two midline extraordinary vessels.

#### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** GV1 (Changqiang 长强). Functions: resolves
Damp-Heat in the Lower Jiao, regulates Du Mai and Ren Mai, calms
the spirit (安神), opens and regulates the anus. The
Luo-Connecting function links GV (posterior) to CV (anterior) at
the perineal base. ✓

**2. Trigger point:** coccygeus (ischiococcygeus) muscle trigger
points refer pain to the coccyx, posterior perineum, and
sacrococcygeal junction. Levator ani trigger points (Travell &
Simons) refer pain to the perineum and rectum. GV1 overlies the
attachment zone of these deep pelvic floor muscles. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** coccygeal tenderness at the coccyx
tip is the defining diagnostic sign of coccydynia. Perineal
tenderness is common in hemorrhoids, anal fissure, and pudendal
neuralgia. GV1 location = spontaneous tender point in these
conditions. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Guda Marma (गुद, "Rectum") — located at the
anal/rectal region. Classified as Mamsa (muscle) Marma. Size: 4
anguli. Injury consequence: death (Sadyah Pranahara). Therapeutic:
regulates Apana Vayu (downward-moving vital force). Anatomically
close to GV1 (~1–2 cm). 🟡

**5. Head zone:** S4–S5 dermatome covers the perineum and perianal
skin. Viscerotome: S2–S4 segments innervate rectum, bladder, and
internal reproductive organs via pelvic splanchnic nerves
(parasympathetic). GV1's perineal location maps directly to the
sacral viscerotome for pelvic organs — the hemorrhoid/prolapse
indications are segmentally coherent. 🟡

**6. Anatomy Trains:** the Deep Front Line (DFL) includes the
pelvic floor as a transverse diaphragm — levator ani and
coccygeus muscles form the inferior boundary of the DFL. GV1 sits
at the posterior midline attachment of this pelvic diaphragm
(anococcygeal raphe → coccyx). The Superficial Back Line (SBL)
anchors at the sacrotuberous ligament, which attaches near the
coccyx. 🟡

**7. Neurogenic spot:** no data. Kim 2017 mapping did not include
the perineal/perianal region. ✗

**8. Cupping point:** not applicable. The perineal location
prevents cupping application. ✗

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Marma 🟡, Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡). High convergence for
a perineal point. The pelvic floor is a multi-tradition recognized
zone: TCM (GV1), Ayurveda (Guda), Western manual therapy (pelvic
floor trigger points), segmental neurology (S2–S4 viscerotome),
and fascial anatomy (DFL pelvic diaphragm).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Neijing (Ling Shu Ch.10 Jing Mai): Du Mai pathway begins at
  the perineum
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: hemorrhoids, bloody stool, rectal prolapse,
  constipation, perineal pain, epilepsy, mania
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: hemorrhoids (primary point), prolapse of
  rectum, diarrhea with blood, perineal eczema, childhood epilepsy

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Hemorrhoids, rectal prolapse, blood in stool
- Diarrhea, constipation
- Low back pain, coccygeal pain (coccydynia)
- Epilepsy, mania (Du Mai spirit-calming function)
- Enuresis, vaginitis

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- MOST-prescribed single point for hemorrhoids in TCM acupuncture
- Thread embedding (catgut implantation) at GV1 is a common
  clinical technique for chronic hemorrhoids
- Moxa at GV1 for rectal prolapse (raising Yang Qi)
- Combined with BL57 (Chengshan) for hemorrhoids — classical pair
- Combined with BL25 (Dachangshu) + BL32 (Ciliao) for anorectal
  disorders

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Post-hemorrhoidectomy pain — 🟡 Plausible**

SR (Li et al. 2022, Frontiers in Surgery) of acupuncture for
post-hemorrhoidectomy pain: 4 RCTs, N=275. Electroacupuncture VAS
scores significantly lower than sham at 6h, 24h post-surgery and
during first defecation (P<0.05), exceeding minimum clinically
important difference of 1 VAS point. GV1 was one of the most
frequently used points (2/4 studies). Overall conclusion: positive
signals but "not conclusive due to a very limited number of
studies."

Thread embedding at GV1: RCT showed favorable effect for
postoperative hemorrhoid pain, reducing analgesic use with fewer
adverse reactions vs indomethacin suppository (Zhong et al. 2012,
Zhongguo Zhen Jiu).

Molecular mechanism (animal): medicated catgut embedding at GV1
downregulates p38 MAPK expression in the dorsal horn of the
lumbar spinal cord in anus incisional pain rat model —
demonstrating a spinal-level anti-nociceptive pathway.

**2. Functional anorectal pain — 🟡 Plausible**

RCT protocol (2024, Trials): multicenter trial comparing
electroacupuncture (GV1 as primary point) vs biofeedback for
functional anorectal pain. Study registered and ongoing — no
results yet. GV1 identified as the empirically validated,
most-used acupoint for anorectal disorders.

**3. Hemorrhoids (conservative) — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Classical primary indication with strong historical documentation
across all major Chinese medical texts. No SR/MA specific to GV1
acupuncture as conservative treatment for hemorrhoids. Individual
small-scale studies exist but insufficient for confidence upgrade.

**4. Epilepsy / mental disorders — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Classical indication based on Du Mai spirit-calming function. No
modern controlled studies of GV1 specifically for neuropsychiatric
conditions. The epilepsy/mania indication likely reflects the
broader Du Mai connection (GV1 Luo → ascends to head) rather than
local perineal mechanism.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The perineal region (GV1 area) becomes sensitized in:
- Hemorrhoid flare: increased tenderness, lowered pressure pain
  threshold at coccyx/perianal area
- Coccydynia: chronic perineal sensitization with allodynia
- Pudendal neuralgia: neuropathic sensitization of S2–S4 territory

No specific published data on GV1 threshold changes comparable to
trunk/limb sensitization studies (Kim 2017).
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: sacral segmental mechanism.**
Inferior rectal nerve / anococcygeal nerve (S2–S5) → sacral
dorsal horn → sacral parasympathetic outflow (S2–S4, pelvic
splanchnic nerves).

This is a SEGMENTAL mechanism: the S2–S4 segments innervate the
rectum, anal canal, bladder, and internal reproductive organs.
Stimulation at GV1 activates the same spinal segments that control
anorectal function. The hemorrhoid/prolapse/anorectal pain
indications are segmentally coherent.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2 (sacral plexus), A07 S1 (somatovisceral
reflex)

p38 MAPK downregulation in the lumbar dorsal horn (animal model)
demonstrates spinal cord level anti-nociceptive processing,
consistent with gate control and segmental modulation mechanisms.

**Luo-Connecting architectural note:** GV1 Luo is the GV entry in
the 15 Luo system. The GV Luo pathway (coccyx → head along spine)
is the longest Luo trajectory. Anatomically, this corresponds to
the posterior midline fascial chain: anococcygeal ligament →
sacrococcygeal ligament → supraspinous ligament → nuchal ligament
→ epicranial fascia. This fascial continuum is also the
Superficial Back Line trajectory in Anatomy Trains.

**Gap:** the epilepsy/mania/spirit-calming function has NO
segmental basis (S4–S5 has no direct cortical pathway). TCM
explains this via the Luo vessel ascending to the head. Modern
mechanism: unknown. The coccygeal region has autonomic plexus
connections that could theoretically influence ascending
sympathetic pathways, but this is speculative.

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–1.0 cun oblique superior insertion, parallel to
the sacrum/coccyx. Perpendicular insertion risks rectal wall
puncture. DeQi: localized distention and heaviness in the
perineum; may radiate to the anus and lower sacrum. EA: 2 Hz, low
intensity; used in clinical trials for post-hemorrhoidectomy pain.

Thread embedding (catgut implantation): specialized technique
frequently used at GV1 for chronic hemorrhoids. Absorbable suture
material implanted at the point for sustained stimulation (days to
weeks). More common at GV1 than at most other acupoints.

Bấm huyệt (B02): self-acupressure difficult due to anatomical
location. Clinical finger pressure during palpation assessment.

Cứu (B08): indirect moxa (stick moxa) applied to the
coccyx/perineal region for rectal prolapse and Yang Qi sinking.
Direct moxa contraindicated due to proximity to anus and thin
skin.

Dry needling (B11): pelvic floor trigger point dry needling
targets the coccygeus and levator ani muscles via transrectal or
perineal approach. Related to but distinct from acupuncture at
GV1.

#### An toàn

**Rectal puncture risk:** deep perpendicular insertion directed
anteriorly can penetrate the posterior rectal wall. Always direct
the needle obliquely superiorly, parallel to the coccyx.

**Infection risk:** proximity to the anus. Strict sterile
technique essential. Thorough disinfection of the area before
needling. Single-use disposable needles.

**Vascular risk:** the hemorrhoidal venous plexus lies directly
beneath. In patients with hemorrhoids, the venous plexus may be
engorged — increased risk of bleeding.

**Pregnancy:** CAUTION in some sources due to perineal/sacral
location and Du Mai–Ren Mai connection. Not absolutely
contraindicated.

### GV2 — Yêu Du (腰俞, Yāoshū)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Lumbar Shu" (腰 = lumbar, 俞 = transport point). At the sacral
hiatus, in the depression between the two sacral cornua. NOT a
Back-Shu point despite the name "Shu" — here Shu means transport
point of the lumbar region. Landmark for epidural/caudal
anesthesia access. Hemorrhoids, irregular menses, lumbar
stiffness, epilepsy.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): at the sacral hiatus, on the
posterior midline.
Anatomical landmarks: the sacral hiatus is the opening at the
inferior end of the sacral canal, formed by failure of the S5
(and often S4) laminae to fuse. Located between the two sacral
cornua. Palpated as a depression approximately at S4 level.
Needle depth: 0.5–1.0 cun (~1.2–2.5 cm) oblique superiorly.
Deeper insertion enters the sacral canal (epidural space).

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: S4–S5 dorsal rami, branches of coccygeal nerve. Deep: the
sacral canal contains the filum terminale, cauda equina remnants,
and epidural fat.
Spinal segment: S4–S5.
Vascular: median sacral artery branches, sacral venous plexus.
Muscle: superficial fascia → sacrococcygeal ligament (covering the
sacral hiatus). No muscle belly at the midline sacral hiatus.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2

#### Phân loại huyệt

No special category designations.

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV2 (Yaoshu). Lumbar stiffness, hemorrhoids, irregular
menses, epilepsy. ✓
Trigger point: no specific trigger point at the sacral hiatus
midline. ✗
Ashi: sacral hiatus tenderness in coccydynia and sacral pain. 🟡
Marma: Trika Marma (त्रिक) at the sacral junction — nearby but
more superior (sacroiliac level). 🟡
Head zone: S4–S5 dermatome (perineal/gluteal). 🟡
Anatomy Trains: SBL sacral attachment via sacrotuberous ligament.
🟡
Neurogenic: no data. ✗
Cupping: not practical at sacral hiatus depression. ✗

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM ✓, Ashi 🟡, Marma 🟡, Head zone
🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: hemorrhoids, irregular menses, lumbar
stiffness, sacral pain, epilepsy. Secondary point in the sacral
region — less clinical emphasis than GV1 (below) or GV4 (above).

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic modern study specific to GV2. The sacral hiatus is
primarily studied as an access point for caudal epidural
anesthesia, not as an acupuncture point. 🔴 for all indications.

#### An toàn

**Epidural access:** deep needling through the sacral hiatus
enters the sacral canal (epidural space). The sacral canal
contains the dural sac terminus (typically ending at S2), cauda
equina remnants, and epidural venous plexus. The
sacrococcygeal ligament provides a palpable resistance — "pop" if
penetrated.

Standard depth (0.5–1.0 cun oblique) is safe.

### GV3 — Yêu Dương Quan (腰阳关, Yāoyángguān)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Lumbar Yang Gate" (腰 = lumbar, 阳关 = Yang Gate). Below the L4
spinous process, on the posterior midline. Named as the "gate" or
boundary between the upper body (Yang) and lower body (Yin) Qi
domains. Same spinal level as BL25 (Dachangshu, Back-Shu of
Large Intestine) 1.5 cun lateral. Lumbar pain, lower limb
weakness, irregular menses. The L4–L5 interspinous space is also
the standard access point for lumbar puncture (Tuffier's line at
iliac crest level).

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterior midline, in
the depression below the spinous process of the 4th lumbar
vertebra (L4).
Anatomical landmarks: L4 spinous process is typically level with
the intercristal line (iliac crest line / Tuffier's line). GV3
is in the interspinous space between L4 and L5.
Needle depth: 0.5–1.0 cun (~1.2–2.5 cm) perpendicular or oblique
superiorly.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: L4 dorsal ramus, medial branch — cutaneous and deep
innervation of the midline lumbar region.
Spinal segment: L4.
Vascular: dorsal branch of L4 lumbar artery.
Muscle: supraspinous ligament (surface) → interspinous ligament
(deep) → ligamentum flavum (deepest). No muscle belly at midline;
multifidus and erector spinae lie 1+ cm lateral.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03 S3.3

#### Phân loại huyệt

No special category designations. The name "Yang Gate" (阳关) is
a functional designation, not a formal point category.

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV3 (Yaoyangguan). Lumbar pain, lower extremity weakness,
irregular menses, seminal emission. ✓
Trigger point: L4–L5 interspinous ligament tender points refer
pain locally to the lower lumbar midline. 🟡
Ashi: L4–L5 midline tenderness is extremely common in low back
pain — the most common level for disc herniation. 🟡
Marma: no specific Marma at L4 midline. ✗
Head zone: L4 dermatome covers the anterior thigh and medial knee.
Limited viscerotome (below main visceral innervation range). 🟡
Anatomy Trains: SBL passes through erector spinae at this level.
Thoracolumbar fascia is a major fascial junction in the lumbar
region. 🟡
Neurogenic: no specific data for L4 midline. ✗
Cupping: lumbar cupping applicable at this region. 🟡

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡, Cupping 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: lumbar pain and stiffness, weakness and
numbness of lower limbs, irregular menses, seminal emission, knee
pain. The name "Yang Gate" reflects the classical concept of L4
as a transition boundary — below here, Du Mai enters the
sacral/perineal (Yin) territory.

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: combined with GB30 (Huantiao) and BL40
(Weizhong) for sciatica and lumbar-leg pain patterns.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic modern study specific to GV3. Included in general
lumbar acupuncture protocols but no individual point evidence.
🔴 for all indications.

#### An toàn

Low risk for standard depth. The L4–L5 interspinous space
provides access to the spinal canal — very deep perpendicular
insertion could theoretically reach the epidural space or dural
sac. Cauda equina (not spinal cord) is present at this level
(cord terminates at L1–L2). Standard acupuncture depth
(0.5–1.0 cun) is safe.

### GV4 — Mệnh Môn (命门, Mìngmén)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Gate of Life" or "Gate of Vitality" (命 = life/destiny, 门 =
gate). Below the L2 spinous process, on the posterior midline. THE
most important tonification point on the Du Mai. L2 is at the
exact anatomical level of the renal arteries, adrenal glands, and
the terminus of the spinal cord (conus medullaris). Same spinal
level as BL23 (Shenshu, Back-Shu of Kidney) 1.5 cun lateral —
the GV4 + BL23 combination is the most powerful lumbar protocol
for Kidney Yang tonification. Moxibustion is the PRIMARY
technique. EA at GV14 + GV4 showed neuroprotective effects in
spinal cord injury via Wnt/β-catenin pathway activation (Wang et
al. 2017).

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterior midline, in
the depression below the spinous process of the 2nd lumbar
vertebra (L2).

Anatomical landmarks: L2 spinous process, in the interspinous
space between L2 and L3. The L2 level is clinically significant:
- **Conus medullaris** — the spinal cord terminates at the L1–L2
  level. Below L2, the spinal canal contains cauda equina nerve
  roots, not spinal cord.
- **Renal arteries** — branch from the abdominal aorta at L1–L2,
  supplying both kidneys.
- **Adrenal glands** — sit atop the kidneys at approximately
  T12–L1, with sympathetic innervation from the lumbar splanchnic
  nerves at L1–L2.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.0 cun (~1.2–2.5 cm) perpendicular.
The needle passes through supraspinous ligament → interspinous
ligament. Standard clinical depth does not reach the ligamentum
flavum.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **L2 dorsal ramus, medial branch** — innervates the midline
  structures (supraspinous ligament, interspinous ligament,
  multifidus attachments) and overlying skin.
- **L1–L2 posterior rami** contribute to the superior cluneal
  nerves (cutaneous innervation of the buttock and upper sacral
  region).
- Spinal segment: L2 (with L1 contribution).
- Deep: the L2 spinal nerve exits through the L2–L3 intervertebral
  foramen — dorsal root ganglion at this level.

Fascial plane: supraspinous ligament (midline, connecting spinous
process tips) → interspinous ligament (between adjacent spinous
processes) → ligamentum flavum (deep, bordering the epidural
space). The thoracolumbar fascia (posterior layer) attaches to the
spinous processes — a major fascial junction.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03 S3.3 (thoracolumbar fascia)

Vascular: dorsal branch of L2 lumbar artery (from abdominal
aorta). Anterior to the L2 vertebral body: abdominal aorta,
inferior vena cava, renal arteries — separated by the full
vertebral body, not a safety concern for acupuncture.

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → thoracolumbar fascia
(posterior layer) → supraspinous ligament. Erector spinae
(longissimus, iliocostalis) and multifidus lie lateral to the
midline. No muscle belly directly at the midline spinous process.

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No (Du Mai = extraordinary vessel). Yuan-Source: No.
Luo-Connecting: No. Xi-Cleft: No. Back-Shu: No. Front-Mu: No.
Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No.

**Special designation: "Gate of Life" (命門).** Not a standard
point category but a unique functional title. In classical TCM,
Mingmen is both an acupoint AND a theoretical concept — the source
of "Original Yang" (元阳) and "Original Qi" (元气), the root of
Kidney Yang. The concept evolved across dynasties:
- Nanjing (Ch.36): "Mingmen is the place where the Shen resides
  and Yuan Qi is stored"
- Zhang Jingyue (Ming dynasty): Mingmen fire = the foundation of
  all Yang activity in the body

**Spinal level correspondence:** GV4 at L2 is at the same
vertebral level as BL23 (Shenshu, Back-Shu of Kidney). GV4 =
midline (supraspinous ligament), BL23 = 1.5 cun lateral (erector
spinae, L2 dorsal ramus lateral branch). Both access the L2
spinal segment.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL23

#### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** GV4 (Mingmen 命门). Functions: nourish
Kidney Yang (补肾阳), warm Ming Men Fire, strengthen the lower
back and knees, benefit Original Qi, regulate the uterus (暖宫).
The most important point for Kidney Yang deficiency syndromes. ✓

**2. Trigger point:** multifidus muscle trigger points at the L2
level refer pain to the lumbar midline and paravertebral region.
Interspinous ligament tenderness at L2 is documented. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** L2 midline tenderness is a common
finding in chronic low back pain, particularly in patients with
L2–L3 disc degeneration or facet joint syndrome. Palpation
tenderness at the L2 spinous process is used diagnostically. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** no specific Marma at the L2 posterior midline.
Kukundara Marma is at the sacral joints (more inferior). Katika
Taruna at the lateral hip. ✗

**5. Head zone:** L2 dermatome covers the anterior inguinal
region. L2 viscerotome: the L1–L2 sympathetic outflow provides
splanchnic innervation to the kidneys and adrenal glands. This is
a strong segmental correspondence: L2 stimulation → L2 sympathetic
efferents → renal/adrenal modulation — consistent with "Kidney
Yang" function. 🟡

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SBL passes through the erector spinae at
L2. The thoracolumbar fascia is a major fascial junction —
posterior, middle, and anterior layers converge at the lumbar
spinous processes and iliac crest. GV4 at L2 sits on the posterior
attachment of this three-layer fascial complex. 🟡

**7. Neurogenic spot:** no specific data for L2 midline in Kim
2017. ✗

**8. Cupping point:** lumbar cupping is practiced in TCM for low
back pain and kidney-related conditions. Not a specifically named
Sunnah cupping site, but lumbar region cupping is common in both
traditions. 🟡

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡, Cupping 🟡). High convergence
consistent with exceptional clinical importance. The L2
viscerotome-kidney correspondence is notably strong — TCM "Kidney
Yang" maps to L2 sympathetic territory.

#### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Nanjing (Ch.36): Mingmen as the residence of Shen and
  storehouse of Yuan Qi
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: lumbar pain and stiffness, inability to
  flex/extend, cold sensation in the lower back, enuresis, seminal

- Zhenjiu Dacheng: impotence, nocturnal emission, infertility
  (male and female), chronic diarrhea from Yang deficiency
  (五更泻 "cock-crow diarrhea"), frequent urination, leukorrhea,

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Lumbar pain and stiffness
- Impotence, seminal emission, infertility
- Irregular menstruation, dysmenorrhea, leukorrhea
- Chronic diarrhea
- Frequent urination, enuresis
- Tinnitus (Kidney deficiency pattern)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- **BL23 + GV4 = most powerful Kidney Yang tonification
  combination.** BL23 bilateral + GV4 midline = "three needles at
  the lumbar" (腰三针). Moxa is PRIMARY at all three points.
- **BL23 + GV4 + KI3 + KI7 = Kidney Yang protocol** — tonifies
  Kidney Yang from the Back-Shu (BL23), Du Mai source (GV4), and

- **GB26 + BL23 + GV4** for Belt Vessel (Dai Mai) disorders and

  stick. Needle alone is less commonly used for tonification.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL23, AP-08 KI3 + KI7, AP-11 GB26

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Spinal cord injury — neuroprotection (animal) — 🟡 Plausible**

EA at GV14 + GV4 (2 Hz, 1 mA, 20 min/day) in T10 spinal cord
injury rats (Wang et al. 2017, Neural Regeneration Research,
N=99): significantly improved hind limb locomotor function (BBB
scale, P<0.01 at days 7 and 14). Molecular: upregulated Wnt1,
Wnt3a, and β-catenin protein expression in injured spinal cord
tissue. A second study (Li et al. 2020) showed PI3K/AKT/mTOR
pathway modulation with the same GV14 + GV4 protocol.

The electrode placement brackets the T10 injury site (GV14 at C7
above, GV4 at L2 below), creating a bipolar electrical field
across the injured segment. This is a point-pair effect, not a
single-point mechanism.

**2. Kidney Yang deficiency (animal) — 🟡 Plausible**

Moxibustion at GV4 + CV4 (Guanyuan) improved kidney yang
deficiency symptoms in rat model (Journal of Traditional Chinese
Medicine, 2022). Laser moxibustion and traditional moxibustion
both effective. Acupoint temperature changes proposed as objective
indicator. Limited by animal model and single study.

**3. Low back pain — 🔴 Unvalidated**

GV4 is included in virtually all lumbar acupuncture protocols for
low back pain, but no SR/MA specifically isolates GV4's
contribution. Evidence for acupuncture for chronic low back pain
is generally positive (multiple SRs), but GV4-specific data is
absent.

**4. Reproductive function — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Strong classical tradition for impotence, infertility, and
menstrual disorders. Moxa at GV4 + CV4 is a standard TCM
fertility protocol. No GV4-specific modern evidence. General
evidence for acupuncture in IVF support is mixed.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The L2 lumbar midline area shows increased tenderness in chronic
low back pain, lumbar disc degeneration, and L2–L3 facet joint
dysfunction. Sensitization at GV4 likely follows the standard
pattern: dorsal ramus sensitization with central hyperexcitability.

The concept of GV4 "warmth deficit" in Kidney Yang deficiency —
patients with Yang deficiency often report subjective cold
sensation at the lumbar region, particularly at GV4 — is
consistent with decreased local circulation or altered sympathetic
vasomotor tone at the L2 level.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: L2 segmental somatovisceral mechanism.**

L2 dorsal ramus → L2 dorsal horn → L2 sympathetic lateral column
→ lumbar splanchnic nerves → abdominal sympathetic plexus.

L2 sympathetic outflow targets:
- **Renal arteries/kidneys** — sympathetic vasoconstriction/
  dilation regulates renal blood flow and renin secretion
- **Adrenal glands** — pre-ganglionic sympathetic fibers (greater
  and lesser splanchnic nerves) directly innervate the adrenal
  medulla (catecholamine release) and modulate adrenal cortical
  function (cortisol, DHEA, aldosterone)
- **Gonadal innervation** — superior hypogastric plexus receives
  L1–L2 sympathetic input, innervating uterus, ovaries, testes

TCM "Kidney Yang" maps to a functional cluster: adrenal function
(energy, warmth, stress response) + reproductive function
(fertility, libido) + fluid regulation (urination) + bone
metabolism (lumbar strength). This cluster is anatomically
coherent: all these functions receive sympathetic innervation from
the L1–L2 segments.

Moxa warming at GV4 → local tissue warming → L2 dorsal ramus
activation → L2 sympathetic facilitation → adrenal/renal/gonadal
modulation. This provides a segmental somatovisceral mechanism
consistent with "warming Kidney Yang."
→ Cross-ref: A02 S3 (sympathetic innervation), A07 S1
(somatovisceral reflex), AP-07 BL23

**GV4 vs BL23 — midline vs paravertebral at L2:**
Both points access the same L2 spinal segment. BL23 stimulates
erector spinae muscle (L2 dorsal ramus, lateral branch). GV4
stimulates supraspinous/interspinous ligament (L2 dorsal ramus,
medial branch). Same spinal segment → same sympathetic outflow.
The clinical pairing (BL23 + GV4) provides bilateral + midline
stimulation, maximizing afferent input to the L2 segment from
multiple tissue types.

**Spinal cord injury mechanism (GV14 + GV4):**
EA between GV14 (C7) and GV4 (L2) creates a 15-segment
electrical field spanning C7 → L2. The T10 injury site lies within
this field. Wnt/β-catenin pathway activation (neuroprotection,
axon regeneration) and PI3K/AKT/mTOR modulation (anti-apoptotic)
are the identified molecular mechanisms. This is an electrical
neuromodulation effect, not a classical somatovisceral reflex.

**Gap:** reproductive/fertility indications (impotence,
infertility) have a plausible segmental pathway (L2 → superior
hypogastric plexus → reproductive organs) but no modern mechanism
evidence specific to GV4 moxa.

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–1.0 cun perpendicular insertion between L2 and
L3 spinous processes. DeQi: local distention and heaviness in the
lumbar region, may radiate to the lower back. Warm-needle
technique (温针灸): needle with moxa cone on the handle, combining
needle stimulation with thermal input — PREFERRED technique at
GV4 for Kidney Yang tonification.

Cứu (B08): **PRIMARY technique at GV4.** Indirect moxa (stick
moxa, moxa box, salt-partition moxa) for tonification of Kidney
Yang. Direct moxa cones also used classically. GV4 is one of the
most commonly moxaed points in TCM — moxa at GV4 + BL23 is the
standard Kidney Yang warming protocol. Sustained warmth for 15–30
minutes.
→ Cross-ref: B08

Bấm huyệt (B02): self-accessible with fist or ball (bilateral
knuckle pressure at the lumbar midline). Common self-care for
lumbar pain. Therapeutic effect for tonification uncertain.

EA: 2 Hz or 2/15 Hz dense-sparse wave at GV4 for low back pain.
For spinal cord injury protocol (research): GV14–GV4 bipolar EA,
2 Hz, 1 mA, 20 min/day.

Dry needling (B11): multifidus/interspinous trigger point dry
needling at L2 — technique overlaps with GV4 acupuncture but
targets deep paraspinal muscle vs midline ligament.

#### An toàn

Low risk for standard depth acupuncture. The interspinous space
at L2–L3 is 3.5–5.0 cm deep to the epidural space in adults
(varies with body habitus). Standard acupuncture depth (1.2–2.5
cm) does not reach the epidural space.

**Spinal cord proximity:** L2 is at or near the conus medullaris
(spinal cord terminus). Deep insertion through the ligamentum
flavum could theoretically contact the conus or cauda equina.
The multiple ligamentous layers provide tactile resistance, and
standard depth is safe.

**Pregnancy:** CAUTION — lumbar and sacral points are listed as
cautionary during pregnancy in some sources, though not
universally contraindicated.

## Lower–Mid Thoracic Region (GV5–GV9)

Five points from L1 spinous process (GV5) to T7 spinous process
(GV9). This region traverses the thoracolumbar junction and the
lower-to-mid thoracic spine along the posterior midline. All five
points share the same anatomical substrate: supraspinous ligament
→ interspinous ligament → ligamentum flavum, with innervation from
the medial branch of the corresponding dorsal ramus at each level.
The thoracic spinous processes angle steeply inferiorly (especially
T4–T9), requiring oblique superior needle insertion rather than
the perpendicular approach used at lumbar levels. Each level
corresponds to a BL Back-Shu point 1.5 cun lateral: GV5↔BL22
(Triple Burner) at L1, GV6↔BL20 (Spleen) at T11, GV7↔BL19
(Gallbladder) at T10, GV8↔BL18 (Liver) at T9, GV9↔BL17
(Blood/Diaphragm) at T7.

### GV5 — Huyền Khu (悬枢, Xuánshū)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Suspended Pivot" (悬 = suspended, 枢 = pivot/hub). Below the L1
spinous process, on the posterior midline. L1 marks the
thoracolumbar junction — the transition from the mobile lumbar
spine to the more rigid thoracic spine. Same spinal level as BL22
(Sanjiaoshu, Back-Shu of Triple Burner) 1.5 cun lateral. Lumbar
stiffness, diarrhea, abdominal distention.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterior midline, in
the depression below the spinous process of the 1st lumbar
vertebra (L1).
Anatomical landmarks: L1 spinous process, interspinous space
between L1 and L2. L1 is typically level with the tip of the 12th
rib (lowest floating rib).
Needle depth: 0.5–1.0 cun (~1.2–2.5 cm) perpendicular or oblique
superiorly.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: L1 dorsal ramus, medial branch — innervates the
supraspinous ligament, interspinous ligament, and overlying skin.
Spinal segment: L1.
Vascular: dorsal branch of L1 lumbar artery.
Fascial: supraspinous ligament → interspinous ligament →
ligamentum flavum. The thoracolumbar fascia transitions from its
three-layer lumbar architecture to a single-layer thoracic
architecture at this level.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03 S3.3

#### Phân loại huyệt

No special category designations.

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV5 (Xuanshu). Lumbar stiffness, diarrhea, abdominal
distention, borborygmus. ✓
Trigger point: L1 multifidus and interspinous ligament tender
points refer pain locally to the thoracolumbar junction midline.
🟡
Ashi: L1 midline tenderness in thoracolumbar junction syndrome
and chronic low back pain extending superiorly. 🟡
Marma: no specific Marma at L1 posterior midline. ✗
Head zone: L1 dermatome covers the inguinal/suprapubic region. L1
viscerotome: T12–L1 sympathetic outflow contributes to the
inferior mesenteric ganglion → descending colon, sigmoid. Diarrhea
indication is segmentally consistent. 🟡
Anatomy Trains: SBL thoracolumbar fascia corridor at L1. 🟡
Neurogenic: no specific data for L1 midline. ✗
Cupping: applicable at the thoracolumbar region. 🟡

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡, Cupping 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: lumbar stiffness, diarrhea, abdominal
distention, borborygmus. Minor point in the lumbar–thoracic
transition. Combined with GV4 (below) and GV6 (above) in spinal
Du Mai protocols.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No SR/MA specific to GV5. Included in general spinal acupuncture
protocols for low back pain but no individual point evidence.
🔴 for all indications.

#### An toàn

Low risk. Standard depth safe. L1 is at or just below the conus
medullaris terminus — the same safety consideration as GV4 applies
(deep insertion could theoretically reach the epidural space, but
standard acupuncture depth is well within the safe zone).

### GV6 — Tích Trung (脊中, Jǐzhōng)

Anatomical landmarks: T11 spinous process. T11 is approximately
at the lower border of the rib cage. The interspinous space
between T11 and T12.

preferred.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

### GV9 — Chí Dương (至阳, Zhìyáng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Reaching Yang" or "Arriving at Yang" (至 = reaching/arriving at,
阳 = Yang). Below the T7 spinous process, on the posterior midline.
T7 marks the level of the diaphragm — the physical boundary
between the thoracic cavity (Upper Jiao, Yang) and the abdominal
cavity (Middle Jiao, Yin). The name suggests this is where
ascending Du Mai "reaches" the Yang territory. Same spinal level
as BL17 (Geshu, Hui-Meeting of Blood, Back-Shu of the Diaphragm)
1.5 cun lateral — the most functionally significant BL Back-Shu
in the blood system. Biliary disorders, jaundice, chest pain,
cardiac conditions. An RCT (Zhao et al. 2018, N=102) showed
acupuncture at GV9 combined with PPI significantly improved
GERD-related cough vs PPI alone.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterior midline, in
the depression below the spinous process of the 7th thoracic
vertebra (T7).

Anatomical landmarks: T7 spinous process, in the interspinous
space between T7 and T8. T7 is at the level of the inferior angle
of the scapula (classic surface anatomy landmark). This level
corresponds to the diaphragm — the musculotendinous partition
between thoracic and abdominal cavities.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.0 cun (~1.2–2.5 cm) oblique superiorly.
The mid-thoracic spinous processes (T4–T9) angle steeply
inferiorly, creating narrow interspinous spaces. Oblique superior
insertion is the standard technique.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **T7 dorsal ramus, medial branch** — innervates midline
  structures (supraspinous ligament, interspinous ligament) and
  overlying skin.
- T7 spinal nerve contributes to the **greater splanchnic nerve**
  (T5–T9/T12). The greater splanchnic nerve is the PRIMARY
  preganglionic sympathetic pathway to the celiac ganglion,
  innervating: liver, gallbladder, spleen, stomach, duodenum,
  pancreas.
- T7 intercostal nerve provides sensory innervation to the
  epigastric skin (anterior T7 dermatome at the xiphoid level).
- Spinal segment: T7.

Fascial plane: supraspinous ligament → interspinous ligament →
ligamentum flavum. At T7, the interspinous space is narrow due
to steeply angled spinous processes. The supraspinous ligament is
continuous from C7 (nuchal ligament) to the sacrum.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A02 S3 (sympathetic trunk), A03 S3.3

Vascular: dorsal branch of T7 posterior intercostal artery (from
thoracic aorta).

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue → trapezius (thin at
this level) → supraspinous ligament. The spinous processes of T7
project between the erector spinae muscles bilaterally. Rhomboid
major attaches to T2–T5 spinous processes (above GV9 level).

Five Shu: No (Du Mai = extraordinary vessel). Yuan-Source: No.
Luo-Connecting: No. Xi-Cleft: No. Back-Shu: No. Front-Mu: No.
Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No.

**Spinal level correspondence:** GV9 at T7 corresponds to BL17
(Geshu) 1.5 cun lateral. BL17 holds two significant designations:
- **Hui-Meeting of Blood (血会):** one of the 8 Hui-Meeting points
  (八会穴). The primary point for all Blood disorders in TCM.
- **Back-Shu of the Diaphragm (膈俞):** T7 = diaphragm level.

GV9 and BL17 share the T7 spinal segment but access different
tissue types: GV9 = midline supraspinous/interspinous ligament
(medial branch of dorsal ramus), BL17 = paravertebral erector
spinae muscle (lateral branch of dorsal ramus). Despite shared
segmental innervation, their traditional indication profiles
partially diverge: GV9 emphasizes biliary/hepatic/chest
conditions, while BL17 emphasizes blood disorders and
diaphragmatic conditions (hiccup, dysphagia).
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL17

**Diaphragmatic level significance:** T7 marks the anatomical
boundary between:
- **Above (thorax):** heart, lungs, pericardium — "Upper Jiao"
- **Below (abdomen):** liver, gallbladder, spleen, stomach,
  intestines — "Middle Jiao"

The diaphragm is both a respiratory muscle (motor: phrenic nerve
C3–C5) and a sensory structure (peripheral portion: T7–T12
intercostal nerves; central portion: phrenic nerve C3–C5).
Pathology at the diaphragmatic level can refer pain to the
shoulder (C3–C5 phrenic referral) and/or the epigastrium (T7
dermatomal referral).

#### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** GV9 (Zhiyang 至阳). Functions: benefits the
chest and diaphragm, clears Damp-Heat, regulates Qi mechanism at
the diaphragmatic level. Key point for jaundice (黄疸 — Damp-Heat
of Liver/Gallbladder), biliary disorders, fullness of the chest,
cardiac and epigastric pain. The name "Reaching Yang" positions
this point as the gateway to the Yang (thoracic) territory on the
Du Mai. ✓

**2. Trigger point:** multifidus and interspinous ligament trigger
points at T7 are documented in the mid-thoracic region. Paraspinal
trigger points at this level refer pain to the mid-back and
infrascapular area. The mid-thoracic spine (T4–T8) is a common
site for myofascial pain. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** T7 midline tenderness occurs in
thoracic back pain, posture-related mid-back pain, and as referred
tenderness from hepatobiliary pathology (viscerosomatic reflex).
Gallbladder-related referred pain is classically in the T7–T9
posterior zone. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** no specific Marma at T7 posterior midline.
Brihati Marma (बृहती) is located at the upper back/scapular
region, corresponding more to T2–T4 level, not T7. ✗

**5. Head zone:** T7 dermatome covers the epigastric/xiphoid
region (anterior trunk). T7 viscerotome: the greater splanchnic
nerve (T5–T9) → celiac ganglion → liver, gallbladder, spleen,
stomach, proximal duodenum. STRONG viscerotome-indication match:
GV9's traditional indications (jaundice, biliary disorders,
epigastric pain) map directly to the T7 viscerotome territory. 🟡

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SBL thoracic corridor — erector spinae and
supraspinous ligament at T7. The Deep Front Line (DFL) transverse
diaphragm is at this level. T7 marks where the SBL crosses the
DFL's thoracic diaphragm. 🟡

**7. Neurogenic spot:** no specific data for T7 midline in Kim
2017. ✗

**8. Cupping point:** cupping in the mid-thoracic/interscapular
region is common in both TCM and Hijama traditions. At the midline
spinous process, suction cups may not seal as effectively as on
the flatter paravertebral musculature. 🟡

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡, Cupping 🟡). The T7
viscerotome match (greater splanchnic nerve → hepatobiliary
territory) is the strongest correspondence — traditional
biliary/jaundice indications are segmentally coherent with the
anatomical innervation.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Gastroesophageal reflux-related cough — 🟡 Plausible**

SR/MA (Li et al. 2025, Frontiers in Medicine, 5 RCTs, N=390):
acupuncture-related therapies for GERD-related chronic cough.
Acupuncture reduced daytime cough scores (MD −0.41, 95% CI
[−0.75, −0.07]) and nighttime cough scores (MD −0.38, 95% CI
[−0.59, −0.17]). Quality of life improved (Leicester Cough
Questionnaire MD 2.29, 95% CI [1.99, 2.60]). No serious adverse
events. Dorsal GV points (GV6–GV12) were the most commonly used
acupoints in included studies.

Within this SR, Zhao et al. 2018 (Zhongguo Zhen Jiu, N=102)
specifically tested acupuncture at GV9 (Zhiyang) combined with
pantoprazole vs pantoprazole alone for GERD cough with damp-heat
pattern, once daily, 5 times/week for 8 weeks. Significant
improvements in both cough and accompanying symptoms vs PPI alone.

The T7 diaphragmatic mechanism is plausible: the diaphragm's crura
contribute to the lower esophageal sphincter barrier function, and
T7 sympathetic outflow (via greater splanchnic nerve) modulates
esophageal and gastric motility.

Evidence quality: moderate-to-low (SR assessment). GV9 specifically
studied in 1 RCT within the SR.

**2. Biliary disorders / jaundice — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Strong classical indication with consistent documentation across
major texts. GV9 is part of standard biliary acupuncture protocols
(with GB34 and Dannangxue). No SR/MA specifically isolates GV9's
contribution to biliary outcomes. Acupuncture for biliary colic
has general evidence (GB34 as primary point in most studies), but
GV9-specific controlled evidence is absent.

**3. Chest / cardiac pain — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Traditional indication based on T7 diaphragmatic and thoracic
significance. No modern controlled studies of GV9 specifically for
chest or cardiac pain.

**4. Thoracic back pain — 🔴 Unvalidated**

GV9 is included in general thoracic spine acupuncture protocols
but no individual point evidence. General evidence for acupuncture
for thoracic back pain is limited compared to lumbar back pain.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

T7 midline area may show increased tenderness in:
- Hepatobiliary pathology: viscerosomatic reflex at T7 can produce
  palpable tenderness at the spinous process. This is the midline
  equivalent of the T7 paravertebral tenderness seen with
  gallbladder disease.
- Thoracic back pain: postural-related mid-thoracic sensitization.
- GERD: diaphragmatic irritation may sensitize the T7 dermatome.

No specific published data on GV9 threshold changes under
pathological conditions. A study on skin temperature changes at
GV9 within 24 hours (Zhao et al., ResearchGate) suggests
circadian or physiological variation at this point, consistent
with the Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon concept.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: T7 segmental somatovisceral mechanism.**

T7 dorsal ramus → T7 dorsal horn → T7 sympathetic lateral column →
greater splanchnic nerve (T5–T9) → celiac ganglion →
hepatobiliary/gastric autonomic modulation.

The greater splanchnic nerve is the dominant preganglionic
sympathetic pathway to the celiac ganglion, providing autonomic
innervation to:
- **Liver:** hepatic sympathetic plexus → hepatocyte metabolism,
  bile production, hepatic blood flow
- **Gallbladder:** cystic plexus → gallbladder contraction/
  relaxation, biliary sphincter tone
- **Stomach:** gastric plexus → motility, acid secretion
- **Spleen:** splenic plexus → immune modulation, blood storage
- **Duodenum/Pancreas:** via secondary branches

The traditional indication profile (jaundice, biliary, epigastric
pain) maps precisely to the T7 viscerotome via the greater
splanchnic nerve pathway. This is the same segmental mechanism
documented for BL Back-Shu points, but accessing the T7 segment
from the midline (supraspinous/interspinous ligament) rather than
the paravertebral (erector spinae) approach.

**GV9 vs BL17 — midline vs paravertebral at T7:**
Both access the same T7 spinal segment via dorsal ramus branches.
BL17 stimulates erector spinae (lateral branch), GV9 stimulates
supraspinous ligament (medial branch). Same segment → same
sympathetic outflow → same visceral targets. However, the
traditional indication profiles partially diverge:
- **GV9:** biliary/jaundice/chest pain → hepatobiliary focus
- **BL17:** blood disorders/hiccup/dysphagia → blood system and
  diaphragmatic function

The indication divergence may reflect:
1. Different tissue types generating qualitatively different
   afferent signals (ligament vs muscle)
2. Historical accumulation guided by naming (BL17 "Geshu" =
   diaphragm → diaphragmatic indications; GV9 "Zhiyang" = Yang
   transition → Yang organ indications)
3. Category effects: BL17's Hui-Meeting of Blood designation
   channels clinical use toward blood disorders
→ Cross-ref: A02 S3 (sympathetic pathways), A07 S1
(somatovisceral reflex), AP-07 BL17

**GERD cough mechanism:** the diaphragm's crura form part of the
anti-reflux barrier at the gastroesophageal junction. T7 segmental
stimulation via GV9 → greater splanchnic modulation → possible
diaphragmatic crural tone adjustment → improved anti-reflux
barrier function. This is a plausible but unconfirmed segmental
pathway.

**Gap:** chest/cardiac pain indications extend beyond the T7
hepatobiliary viscerotome into cardiac territory (heart = T1–T5
sympathetic). T7 stimulation could theoretically affect cardiac
function via ascending sympathetic chain connections, but this is
a longer segmental pathway than the direct T1–T5 cardiac
mechanism. The chest pain indication may reflect
diaphragmatic/pericardial referral rather than direct cardiac
mechanism.

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–1.0 cun oblique superior insertion between T7 and
T8 spinous processes. Careful palpation needed — the mid-thoracic
spinous processes angle steeply and overlap. DeQi: local
distention and aching in the mid-back, may radiate along the
intercostal space laterally. EA: standard 2 Hz or 2/15 Hz
dense-sparse wave for visceral conditions.

Cứu (B08): indirect moxa (stick moxa) for jaundice/Damp-Heat
conditions. Not the dominant technique at GV9 (unlike GV4 where
moxa is primary). Warm-needle technique used in some clinical
protocols for biliary conditions.

Bấm huyệt (B02): accessible with partner assistance. Sustained
pressure at T7 spinous process for mid-back pain relief.
Self-application difficult due to location.

Dry needling (B11): multifidus trigger point dry needling at T7 —
functionally similar to acupuncture at GV9 but targets the
paravertebral muscle rather than the midline ligament.

#### An toàn

Low risk at standard depth. The spinal cord is present at T7
(cord extends to approximately L1–L2). Deep insertion through the
ligamentum flavum could theoretically contact the epidural space.
The distance from skin to epidural space at mid-thoracic levels
is 4.0–6.0 cm in adults — well beyond standard acupuncture depth
(1.2–2.5 cm).

Mid-thoracic levels have the narrowest interspinous spaces —
precise point location is important. The steeply angled T7 spinous
processes overlap significantly, providing an additional physical
barrier against inadvertent deep insertion.

**Pneumothorax risk:** minimal at the midline. Pneumothorax from
acupuncture typically occurs with paravertebral (BL line)
insertion directed laterally toward the intercostal space. Midline
insertion at the spinous process is directed away from the pleural
cavity.

## Upper Thoracic Region (GV10–GV13)

Four points from T6 spinous process (GV10) to T1 spinous process
(GV13). This region ascends through the upper thoracic spine to
the cervicothoracic junction. The upper thoracic segments carry
progressively more significant autonomic innervation: T5 = cardiac
sympathetic zone (BL15 Heart Shu), T3 = pulmonary sympathetic zone
(BL13 Lung Shu), T1 = stellate ganglion area (BL11 Hui-Meeting of
Bones). Two points carry "spirit" names: GV10 靈臺 (Spirit Tower)
and GV11 神道 (Spirit Path) — part of GV's concentration of
spirit-related nomenclature alongside GV24 神庭 (Spirit Court).
Corresponding BL Back-Shu points: GV10↔BL16 (GV Shu) at T6,
GV11↔BL15 (Heart) at T5, GV12↔BL13 (Lung) at T3, GV13↔BL11
(Bones) at T1.

Back-Shu of the Du Mai, meaning GV10 and BL16 at T6 are where the
GV meridian's own "organ energy" is regulated via the Back-Shu

→ Cross-ref: A01 S2

### GV12 — Thân Trụ (身柱, Shēnzhù)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Body Pillar" (身 = body, 柱 = pillar/column). Below the T3
spinous process, on the posterior midline. Same level as BL13
(Feishu, Back-Shu of Lung). T3 is within the pulmonary
sympathetic territory (T2–T4). Classical **PEDIATRIC MOXA point**
— particularly prominent in the Japanese acupuncture tradition
(小児の身柱灸, shōni no shinchū kyū), where moxibustion at GV12
is a standard preventive treatment for childhood diseases. Cough,
asthma, epilepsy (especially childhood), weak constitution.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

the depression below the spinous process of the 3rd thoracic
vertebra (T3).
Anatomical landmarks: T3 spinous process, approximately at the
level of the medial end of the scapular spine.
Needle depth: 0.5–1.0 cun oblique superiorly.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: T3 dorsal ramus, medial branch. Spinal segment: T3.
T3 sympathetic territory: T2–T4 = pulmonary sympathetic
innervation (bronchial smooth muscle, pulmonary vasculature). Also
contributes to upper cardiac sympathetic (T1–T5 overlap).
Vascular: dorsal branch of T3 posterior intercostal artery.
Fascial: supraspinous → interspinous ligament. Upper thoracic
spinous processes are more horizontal than mid-thoracic.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A02 S3

#### Phân loại huyệt

No standard category designations.

**Spinal level note:** GV12 at T3 = BL13 (Feishu, Back-Shu of
Lung). T3 is the core pulmonary sympathetic level. The cough/
asthma indications are segmentally coherent: T3 sympathetic
outflow modulates bronchial smooth muscle tone and airway caliber.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL13

single point for pediatric moxibustion in Japanese acupuncture
(Shōnishin tradition). 身柱灸 (Shinchū-kyū) = moxa at Body
Pillar, applied preventively to strengthen children's
constitution, improve immunity, and treat recurrent respiratory
conditions. Typically performed with small, direct rice-grain moxa

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV12 (Shenzhu). Cough, asthma, epilepsy (especially

Marma: no specific Marma at T3 posterior midline. Amsaphalaka
Marma (scapular region) is lateral, not midline. ✗

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡, Cupping 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: cough, asthma, epilepsy (childhood), fever,
back stiffness, boils/carbuncles. "Body Pillar" suggests the point
as a structural support of the body — consistent with its

### GV13 — Đào Đạo (陶道, Táodào)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Kiln Path" (陶 = kiln/pottery, 道 = path/way). Below the T1
spinous process, on the posterior midline. **Meeting point of GV
and BL (交会穴).** Same level as BL11 (Dashu 大杼, Hui-Meeting of
Bones) 1.5 cun lateral. T1 is the cervicothoracic junction — one
vertebra below GV14 (C7, meeting of all Yang). Classical
indications: fever, malaria, headache, back stiffness.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterior midline, in
the depression below the spinous process of the 1st thoracic
vertebra (T1).

(vertebra prominens). The C7–T1 junction is the cervicothoracic
transition. T1 is the first vertebra with a true rib articulation.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: T1 dorsal ramus, medial branch. Spinal segment: T1.
T1 spinal nerve contributes to the brachial plexus (lower trunk)
and the stellate ganglion (inferior cervical + T1 sympathetic
ganglia fusion). The stellate ganglion is the junction between
cervical and thoracic sympathetic chains.

supreme intercostal artery).
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A02 S3

## Cervical–Suboccipital Region (GV14–GV16)

Three points spanning the cervicothoracic junction (C7) to the
suboccipital region (external occipital protuberance). This is the
DENSEST special-designation zone on the Governing Vessel: GV14
(meeting of all Yang meridians), GV15 (dangerous speech point),
GV16 (triple designation — Window of Heaven, Ghost Point #6, Sea
of Marrow). Anatomically, the region transitions from the largest
spinous process in the spine (C7, vertebra prominens) through the
atlantoaxial junction (C1–C2) to the foramen magnum entry zone.
The stellate ganglion (C7–T1) underlies GV14, while the cisterna
magna (subarachnoid space posterior to the medulla oblongata)
creates the primary safety concern at GV15–GV16. Nerve supply
shifts from C7–T1 spinal nerves (GV14) to C1–C2 dorsal rami and
suboccipital nerve (GV15–GV16), with the greater occipital nerve
(C2) emerging lateral to GV16. The corresponding BL Back-Shu
system ends above this zone — GV14 at C7 is one level above BL11
(T1, Hui-Meeting of Bones).

### GV14 — Đại Chùy (大椎, Dàzhuī)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**

"Great Vertebra" (大 = great, 椎 = vertebra). Below the C7
spinous process (vertebra prominens), on the posterior midline.
THE HIGHEST-PROFILE GV POINT AFTER GV20. The **meeting point of
all 6 Yang meridians (LI, ST, SI, BL, TE, GB) plus GV** — 7
channels converge at this single point, making it the most
multi-channel meeting point in the 361-point system. Classical
designation "Sea of all Yang" (阳脉之海) centers on GV14.
Primary clinical uses: fever reduction, immunomodulation, cervical
conditions, common cold/flu (Wind-Cold/Wind-Heat invasion). GV14
is the most commonly cupped acupoint — both in TCM (for external
pathogen invasion) and in Islamic Sunnah tradition, where
Al-Kahil (الكاهل, "between the shoulders") corresponds
approximately to GV14 (independent tradition, not meridian-based).
A systematic review (Zheng et al. 2022, 18 RCTs, N=1,573) found
GV14 as a single acupoint effective for managing fever (MD
−0.39°C). Animal studies demonstrate immunomodulation via
macrophage polarization (M1→M2), cytokine modulation (IL-4↓,
IL-5↓, IL-13↓, TNF-α↓; IL-10↑, IFN-γ↑), and vagus–adrenal axis
activation.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterior midline, in
the depression below the spinous process of the 7th cervical
vertebra (C7).

Anatomical landmarks: C7 (vertebra prominens) has the LARGEST and
most palpable spinous process in the cervical spine — the surface
landmark "big vertebra" from which the point derives its name. To
locate: flex the neck forward; the most prominent spinous process
is C7 (T1 may be equally prominent in some individuals — confirm
by lateral neck rotation: C7 spinous process does not move with
rotation, while T1 may). The point lies in the interspinous space
between C7 and T1. Level approximately with the acromion and the
top of the shoulder.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.0 cun (~1.2–2.5 cm) oblique superiorly.
The C7 spinous process is long and nearly horizontal (unlike the
steeply angled mid-thoracic spinous processes), providing a
relatively accessible interspinous space. Perpendicular insertion
is also described.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **C7 and C8 dorsal rami, medial branches** — innervate the
  supraspinous ligament, interspinous ligament, and overlying skin
  at the cervicothoracic junction.
- **C8 spinal nerve** contributes to the lower trunk of the
  brachial plexus, innervating intrinsic hand muscles (ulnar
  nerve territory).
- **Stellate ganglion (cervicothoracic ganglion):** the MOST
  clinically significant nearby structure. Formed by fusion of the
  inferior cervical ganglion and the first thoracic ganglion
  (~80% of population), located anterior to the C7 transverse
  process and the neck of the first rib. Measures approximately
  2.5 × 1.0 × 0.5 cm. The stellate ganglion provides:
  - Sympathetic fibers to the head, neck, upper limbs, and heart
    (via inferior cardiac nerve)
  - Gray rami communicantes to C7, C8, T1 spinal nerves
  - Sympathetic innervation of the vertebral artery
- **Sympathetic innervation of immune organs:** all primary
  (thymus, bone marrow) and secondary (spleen, lymph nodes,
  mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue) immune organs receive
  substantial sympathetic innervation with norepinephrine as the
  main neurotransmitter. The stellate ganglion at C7–T1 is
  positioned at the junction between cervical and thoracic
  sympathetic chains, where sympathetic outflow to upper body
  immune structures originates or relays.
- Spinal segments: C7–T1 (cervicothoracic junction).

Fascial plane: nuchal ligament transitions to supraspinous
ligament at C7 — this is the anatomical boundary between cervical
(nuchal ligament, single midline sheet from external occipital
protuberance to C7) and thoracic (supraspinous ligament,
continuing from C7 to sacrum along spinous process tips). GV14
sits exactly at this transition point.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A02 S3 (sympathetic chain/stellate
ganglion), A03 S3.3 (nuchal ligament)

Vascular: dorsal branch of deep cervical artery (from
costocervical trunk). The vertebral artery runs in the transverse
foramina of C1–C6 (NOT C7 — C7 typically lacks a transverse
foramen for the vertebral artery). Therefore, the vertebral
artery is not at direct risk from midline needling at C7–T1.

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue → trapezius (upper
fibers, attaching to C7 spinous process and acromion) →
supraspinous ligament (transitioning from nuchal ligament).
The upper trapezius attachment at C7 is a common site for
myofascial trigger points. Rhomboid minor attaches to C7–T1
spinous processes. Splenius capitis and splenius cervicis
originate from C7–T4 spinous processes.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Fever / antipyretic effect — 🟡 Plausible**

SR (Zheng et al. 2022, Journal of Integrative Medicine, 18 RCTs,
N=1,573): evaluated all stimulation methods at GV14 as a single
acupoint for managing fever. Included bloodletting with cupping,
acupuncture, moxibustion, and guasha. 12 RCTs evaluating
temperature reduction showed MD −0.39°C (improved body
temperature vs control). Total effective rate of fever reduction
improved in patients >14 years old. Only 3 RCTs reported slight
adverse events. GRADE quality: most evidence rated low or very
low for individual RCTs.

The SR is notable for evaluating GV14 as a SINGLE ACUPOINT —
unusual in acupuncture research where multi-point protocols are
standard. This provides more specific evidence for GV14's
individual contribution than most acupuncture SRs.

A separate SR on bloodletting therapy for infectious fever
(2025) also included GV14 pricking-cupping protocols, supporting
the traditional bloodletting technique.

Evidence quality: moderate (SR evidence, but GRADE mostly low/very
low for individual RCTs). GV14 has MORE single-point evidence for
fever than any other acupoint.

**2. Immunomodulation — 🟡 Plausible**

SR (2025, systematic review of acupuncture's immunomodulatory
effects on macrophages in allergic disorders): GV14 was among the
most frequently studied acupoints. Findings:
- **Macrophage polarization:** electroacupuncture at GV14 promotes
  transition from pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype to
  anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype
- **Cytokine modulation:** IL-4↓, IL-5↓, IL-13↓ (Th2 cytokines),
  TNF-α↓, IL-1β↓ (pro-inflammatory); IL-10↑ (anti-inflammatory),
  IFN-γ↑ (Th1), TGF-β↑ (regulatory)
- **Treg enhancement:** acupuncture at GV14 enhanced regulatory
  T cells and balanced Th1/Th2 responses
- **NF-κB and p38 MAPK pathway modulation**

Conditions studied (animal models): allergic asthma, allergic
rhinitis, atopic dermatitis. Electroacupuncture at GV14 activated
the vagus–adrenal axis to inhibit systemic inflammation.

Evidence quality: moderate (systematic review of predominantly
animal studies). The M1→M2 macrophage polarization finding is the
most mechanistically specific.

**3. Leukopenia / WBC production — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Traditional claim: moxibustion at GV14 increases white blood cell
production. Clinical practice uses GV14 moxa for leukopenia (e.g.,
chemotherapy-induced). No controlled studies specifically isolating
GV14 moxa for WBC counts. The stellate ganglion → immune organ
sympathetic pathway provides a plausible anatomical basis, but
specific evidence is absent.

**4. Cervical spondylosis — 🔴 Unvalidated**

GV14 is included in standard multi-point protocols for cervical
spondylosis (with GB20, GB21, BL10, SI15). One study (N=52) of
cervical spondylosis of vertebral artery type treated with
acupuncture at GV14 reported 82.7% effective rate, with
improvement in vertebral-basilar artery blood supply. Single
uncontrolled study. No SR/MA specifically for GV14 in cervical
conditions.

**5. Night sweats — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Classical HT6+GV14 combination widely used. No controlled studies
specifically evaluating this combination.

**6. Allergic conditions (asthma, rhinitis, dermatitis) —
🔴 Unvalidated for human clinical evidence**

Animal studies show immunomodulation at GV14 in allergic models
(see immunomodulation section above). One RCT (N=70) tested GV14
pricking-bloodletting for chronic spontaneous urticaria. Human
clinical evidence for GV14 specifically in allergic conditions
remains limited to individual trials.

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: C7–T1 stellate ganglion → sympathetic
immunomodulation.**

C7–T1 dorsal rami → C7–T1 dorsal horn → C7–T1 sympathetic
lateral column → stellate ganglion → sympathetic innervation of
immune organs.

The stellate ganglion is the critical anatomical structure
underlying GV14's immunomodulatory effects:
- **Thymus:** receives sympathetic innervation originating from
  T1–T4 sympathetic chain, relaying through or near the stellate
  ganglion. The thymus (primary immune organ, T-cell maturation)
  is located in the anterior mediastinum at T1–T4 level — the
  spinal segments immediately adjacent to GV14.
- **Bone marrow:** sympathetic innervation modulates
  hematopoiesis (WBC production, lymphocyte release).
- **Spleen:** sympathetic innervation via greater splanchnic
  (T5–T9, below GV14 level) modulates immune function.
- **Lymph nodes:** cervical and mediastinal lymph nodes receive
  sympathetic fibers from the cervicothoracic sympathetic chain.

Stellate ganglion block (SGB) at C7 is used clinically for
immune modulation — SGB hinders sympathetic innervation of
immune organs, demonstrating that the C7–T1 sympathetic node is
a real immune regulatory junction. GV14 acupuncture/cupping at
C7 may modulate the same stellate ganglion territory through
somatosympathetic reflexes, providing the anatomical basis for
the traditional "strengthens Wei Qi" (defensive immunity)
function.

**Fever mechanism:** multiple pathways —
1. **Somatosympathetic:** C7–T1 segmental stimulation →
   sympathetic modulation → peripheral vasomotor adjustment →
   heat dissipation
2. **Vagus–adrenal axis:** electroacupuncture at GV14
   demonstrated activation of the vagus–adrenal anti-inflammatory
   pathway → adrenal catecholamine release → systemic
   anti-inflammatory effect
3. **Bloodletting + cupping:** pricking draws local blood →
   cupping creates negative pressure → mechanical removal of
   stagnant blood + local cytokine clearance → traditional
   "clearing Heat" cascade

**Meeting of all Yang — anatomical interpretation:**
The classical "meeting of all 6 Yang channels" at GV14 may reflect

- C5–C6: radial nerve (Large Intestine pathway territory)
- C5–C7: median nerve (crossing Yangming/Shaoyin territory)
- C7–T1: ulnar nerve (Small Intestine, Heart pathways)
- C1–C3: greater occipital nerve (Bladder pathway, posterior)
- Accessory nerve (cranial nerve XI): trapezius and
  sternocleidomastoid (Triple Burner, Gallbladder pathways)

GV14 at C7 sits at the CONVERGENCE of brachial plexus
contributions (C5–T1) that supply the upper limb nerves running
along the Hand Yang meridians (Large Intestine, Small Intestine,
Triple Burner). The Foot Yang meridians (Stomach, Bladder,
Gallbladder) have cervical connections through the accessory
nerve and upper cervical nerves. Whether this neural convergence
underlies the classical "meeting of all Yang" designation is
speculative but anatomically suggestive.

The Islamic hijama point Al-Kahil (الكاهل, "between the

#### An toàn

Moderate risk — primarily due to deep insertion concerns:

**Spinal cord proximity:** the spinal cord at C7 level is
approximately 3–4 cm from the skin surface (varies by body
habitus). Standard acupuncture depth (1.2–2.5 cm) is well within
the safety margin. However, the C7–T1 interspinous space does
provide potential access to the epidural space if a needle is
directed medially and deeply.

**Vertebral artery:** NOT at risk at midline GV14. The vertebral
artery enters the transverse foramina at C6 (not C7) and courses
through C6 to C1 before entering the skull. At C7 level, the
vertebral artery is anterior and lateral, protected by the
vertebral body and transverse process.

**Bloodletting + cupping safety:** the technique is generally safe
when performed by trained practitioners. Risks: excessive bleeding
(manage with direct pressure), cupping blisters (limit suction
duration), infection at incision/puncture site (standard aseptic
technique).

**Pneumothorax risk:** LOW at the midline. The lung apex rises
to ~2–3 cm above the medial third of the clavicle, lateral and
anterior to GV14. Midline needling directed into the interspinous
space is away from the lung apex. Risk increases only with lateral

### GV15 — Á Môn (哑门, Yǎmén)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Mute Gate" (哑 = mute/dumb, 门 = gate). Below the C1 spinous
process (posterior tubercle of the atlas), in the midline
depression at the nape, 0.5 cun above the posterior hairline.
Classical name reflects primary indication: sudden loss of voice,
speech disorders, aphasia. ⚠️ **DANGEROUS POINT:** needle directed
medially can penetrate the posterior atlantooccipital membrane →
cisterna magna → medulla oblongata. One of the TWO MOST DANGEROUS
points in the entire 361-point system (with GV16). Safety depth
from MRI study (Wang et al. 2015, N=69): dangerous depth (skin to
spinal dura) = 43.32 ± 7.87 mm; recommended safety depth (75%
of dangerous depth) = 32.48 ± 5.90 mm (males 37.91 mm, females
29.59 mm). Classical combination HT5 + GV15 for aphasia. Post-
stroke aphasia: one RCT (Gao et al. 2020, N=70) found 94.3%
effective rate for acupuncture at GV15 combined with speech
rehabilitation vs 80.0% for rehabilitation alone.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterior midline of
the neck, in the depression below the spinous process of the
1st cervical vertebra (C1, atlas).

Anatomical landmarks: the C1 (atlas) has a small posterior
tubercle instead of a true spinous process. GV15 is located in
the midline depression between the atlas posterior tubercle and
the external occipital protuberance, approximately 0.5 cun below
the posterior hairline. Palpated with the head slightly flexed.
The point lies in the posterior atlantooccipital interspace —
above C1 is the occiput (foramen magnum).

Needle depth range: **0.5–1.0 cun MAXIMUM** (~1.2–2.0 cm),
directed toward the MANDIBLE (anteroinferior direction), NOT
toward the foramen magnum. ⚠️ NEVER insert directed superiorly
or perpendicularly deep — this risks penetrating the posterior
atlantooccipital membrane and entering the cisterna magna.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Suboccipital nerve (C1 dorsal ramus)** — exclusively motor
  in most individuals (innervates rectus capitis posterior
  major/minor, obliquus capitis superior/inferior). No cutaneous
  branch.
- **Greater occipital nerve (C2 dorsal ramus, medial branch)** —
  the primary sensory nerve of the posterior scalp. Provides
  motor branches to semispinalis capitis.
- **Third occipital nerve (C3 dorsal ramus medial branch)** — may
  extend superiorly to contribute sensory innervation to this
  area.
- Spinal segment: C1–C2 (atlantoaxial level).

Fascial plane: posterior atlantooccipital membrane — a broad, thin
membrane spanning between the posterior arch of the atlas and the
posterior margin of the foramen magnum. This membrane is the
CRITICAL BOUNDARY separating the needle from the cisterna magna
(subarachnoid space containing cerebrospinal fluid, posterior to
the medulla oblongata and cerebellum).

Deep structures at risk:
- **Cisterna magna (cerebellomedullary cistern):** the largest
  subarachnoid cistern, located posterior to the medulla
  oblongata and inferior to the cerebellum. Contains CSF and the
  posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA).
- **Medulla oblongata:** contains vital cardiorespiratory centers
  (nucleus ambiguus, dorsal motor nucleus of vagus, respiratory
  centers). Needle contact = potential cardiorespiratory arrest.
- **Vertebral arteries:** course along the posterior arch of the
  atlas before entering the foramen magnum.

**Safety depth data (Wang et al. 2015, PMC4331983):**
MRI measurements in 69 subjects (24 male, 45 female):
- Dangerous depth (skin to spinal dura): 43.32 ± 7.87 mm
- Safety depth (75% of dangerous depth): 32.48 ± 5.90 mm overall
  - Males: 37.91 ± 5.54 mm
  - Females: 29.59 ± 3.64 mm
- Independent predictors: gender (β=0.474), BMI (β=0.249),
  head length DGO (β=0.272)
- R² = 0.571 (these three factors explain 57% of variation)
- Age showed no significant correlation
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2 (cranial nerve anatomy)

Vascular: deep cervical artery branches, vertebral artery
(nearby but lateral to midline).

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue → nuchal ligament (at
midline) → posterior atlantooccipital membrane (the danger
boundary). The suboccipital muscles (rectus capitis posterior
major, obliquus capitis inferior) are lateral to the midline at
this level.

**Myodural bridge:** at the C1–C2 level, the rectus capitis
posterior minor muscle has direct connective tissue attachments
to the spinal dura mater (Hack et al. 1995). This "myodural
bridge" provides a physical pathway from suboccipital musculature
to the intracranial dura — anatomically relevant for headache
pathophysiology and potentially for the mechanism of acupuncture
at GV15.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.3.4 (myodural bridge)

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Post-stroke aphasia — 🟡 Plausible**

with speech rehabilitation training vs speech rehabilitation alone
for post-stroke motor aphasia. Results: total effective rate 94.3%
(33/35) in acupuncture group vs 80.0% (28/35) in control group.
Speech function scores and daily life speech ability significantly
improved in both groups, with greater improvement in the
acupuncture group.

Additional evidence: a systematic review of neuroimaging
mechanisms in acupuncture for post-stroke aphasia (Zhuo et al.
2023, Behavioural Neurology) found GV15 among the most frequently
used acupoints. Acupuncture stimulation at GV15 and related
points may modulate cortical language areas, hemodynamics, and
brain metabolism.

Evidence quality: limited (single RCT, moderate quality). The
Jingou Diaoyu technique is specific to specialized practitioners
and not widely replicated.

**2. Traumatic brain injury / neuroprotection — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Animal study: acupuncture at GV15 + GV16 + GV20 + GV26 + LI4
reduced brain microglia activation in a traumatic brain injury
rat model (2020). Neuroprotective effects demonstrated but in a
multi-point animal protocol — cannot isolate GV15's specific
contribution.

**3. Occipital headache / neck stiffness — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Traditional indication with plausible anatomical basis (C1–C2
junction dysfunction, trigeminocervical convergence). No controlled
studies specific to GV15 for headache or neck conditions.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The C1–C2 junction area may show increased tenderness in:
- Cervicogenic headache: C1–C2 dysfunction is the primary cervical

  midline may reveal deep tenderness.

#### Cơ chế giải thích

**Primary pathway: C1–C2 brainstem-adjacent mechanism.**

Upper cervical afferents (C1–C3) → trigeminocervical nucleus →
brainstem connections.

The trigeminocervical nucleus is a convergence zone where upper
cervical (C1–C3) and trigeminal (V1) sensory inputs merge. This
convergence underlies cervicogenic headache referral and may
mediate GV15's effects on headache conditions.

**Speech mechanism:** GV15's aphasia/speech indications likely
involve proximity to the medulla oblongata, which contains:
- **Nucleus ambiguus:** motor neurons for cranial nerve IX
  (glossopharyngeal) and cranial nerve X (vagus), controlling
  pharyngeal and laryngeal muscles essential for speech production
- **Hypoglossal nucleus:** motor neurons for cranial nerve XII,
  controlling tongue movements

Deep acupuncture at GV15 (within safe limits) stimulates
suboccipital tissues whose afferent input enters the upper
cervical spinal cord, with potential segmental connections to
bulbar motor nuclei. The mechanism is topographic proximity
rather than segmental somatovisceral — the C1–C2 segments are
adjacent to the brainstem, not reflexly connected through long
segmental pathways.

**Myodural bridge pathway:** the rectus capitis posterior minor →
dura connection at C1–C2 (Hack et al. 1995) provides a
structural pathway by which suboccipital needle stimulation could
affect dural tension and potentially CSF dynamics at the foramen
magnum level.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, C04 S3.3.4

Châm (B01): ⚠️ **RESTRICTED TECHNIQUE.**

- Direction: toward the mandible (anteroinferior)
- NEVER perpendicular deep or directed superiorly
- Slow insertion, monitoring patient response continuously
- The Jingou Diaoyu ("Golden Hook Fishing") technique uses a

  manipulation — specialized training required
- DeQi: local heaviness at the nape, may radiate to the head.
  ⚠️ Any radiation to the limbs, sudden weakness, or respiratory

  strict depth limitations

Cứu (B08): moxa applicable (indirect stick moxa at the nape).
No safety concern for moxa (surface application, no needle depth
issue). Traditional moxa for chronic neck stiffness and headache.

Bấm huyệt (B02): pressure at the base of the skull midline —
safe and effective for occipital headache. The suboccipital

#### An toàn

⚠️ **HIGH RISK — ONE OF THE TWO MOST DANGEROUS POINTS (with
GV16) IN THE 361-POINT SYSTEM.**

**Primary danger:** penetration of the posterior atlantooccipital
membrane → cisterna magna → medulla oblongata contact.

**Consequences of excessive depth:**
- **Cerebrospinal fluid leak** (headache, meningism)
- **Medullary damage:** cardiorespiratory arrest (nucleus ambiguus
  and respiratory centers), cranial nerve palsy
- **Subarachnoid hemorrhage:** vertebral artery or PICA injury
  within the cisterna magna
- **Death:** historical case reports in Chinese literature
  document fatal outcomes from deep needling at GV15/GV16

**Safety protocol:**
1. Maximum depth 0.5–1.0 cun (12–20 mm), depending on body
   habitus (females: more conservative depth)
2. Direction: toward mandible (anteroinferior), NEVER superior
3. Slow insertion with continuous patient communication
4. Immediate withdrawal at ANY radicular symptoms (limb weakness,
   tingling, breathing difficulty)
5. MRI-derived safety depth (Wang et al. 2015): 32.48 ± 5.90 mm
   overall (males 37.91, females 29.59 mm) — clinical practice
   should stay well below this threshold
6. Contraindicated in patients with craniovertebral anomalies
   (Chiari malformation, basilar invagination), upper cervical
   instability (rheumatoid arthritis, Down syndrome), and
   anticoagulation

  acute stroke — GV16 (brainstem proximity) + GV26 (emergency
  resuscitation) + PC6 (cardiac/consciousness) + SP6 (Yin
  anchor).
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL10, AP-11 GB20, AP-09 PC6, AP-04 SP6

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Migraine / headache — 🔴 Unvalidated**

GV16 is included in acupuncture protocols for migraine and
cervicogenic headache, typically in combination with GB20 and
other points. Animal study: diluted bee venom injection at GV16
suppressed migraine-induced allodynia. No SR/MA specifically
evaluating GV16 for headache.

**2. Neuroprotection (traumatic brain injury, spinal cord
injury) — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Animal studies: electroacupuncture at GV16 combined with GV26
promoted neuroprotection and dorsal neuron function recovery
after acute spinal cord injury. GV15 + GV16 + GV20 + GV26 + LI4
reduced brain microglia activation in a TBI rat model (2020).
These are multi-point animal protocols; GV16's individual
contribution is not isolated.

**3. Psychiatric conditions / Ghost Point indication —
🔴 Unvalidated**

The Ghost Point #6 designation implies traditional use for acute
psychiatric disturbance. No modern controlled studies specifically
evaluating GV16 for psychiatric conditions. Ghost Point protocols
(using all 13 points in sequence) have not been subjected to
clinical trials.

**4. Common cold / Wind disorders — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Most extensively documented classical indication (277 ancient
articles). GV16+GB20 is the standard clinical combination for
Wind-Cold. No modern SR/MA specifically for GV16 in upper
respiratory infection.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The suboccipital midline is highly dynamic:
- **Headache/migraine:** the suboccipital region shows consistent
  tenderness and muscle hypertonicity during headache episodes.
  Allodynia (sensitization) may extend to the GV16 zone.
- **Wind-Cold invasion (common cold):** neck stiffness and
  occipital heaviness — the classical "Wind enters at Fengfu"
  symptom complex — may reflect genuine suboccipital muscle
  guarding and sensitization during upper respiratory infection.
- **Emotional stress:** the suboccipital muscles are highly
  responsive to stress via the reticulospinal tract — the
  "tension headache" zone. GV16 tenderness correlates with
  emotional state.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

#### Cơ chế giải thích

**Primary pathway: suboccipital–brainstem proximity mechanism.**

Upper cervical afferents (C1–C3) → trigeminocervical nucleus →
brainstem connections (medulla, pons, reticular formation).

GV16's mechanism is fundamentally different from the segmental
somatovisceral model that explains most spinal GV points
(GV3–GV14). Instead, GV16 operates through PROXIMITY to the
brainstem — the needling zone is within centimeters of the
medulla oblongata. This proximity creates:

1. **Trigeminocervical convergence:** upper cervical sensory input
   (C1–C3 dorsal rami) converges with trigeminal input (V1–V3) in
   the trigeminocervical nucleus. This convergence is the
   established neuroanatomical basis for cervicogenic headache and
   explains GV16's headache/migraine indications.

2. **Reticular formation access:** the brainstem reticular
   formation (consciousness regulation, autonomic control) is the
   deepest structure near GV16. Traditional "Wind" symptoms
   (dizziness, mania, loss of consciousness, epilepsy) are
   consistent with reticular formation involvement.

3. **Myodural bridge:** the rectus capitis posterior minor → dura
   connection at GV16 allows mechanical force from needle
   manipulation to potentially transmit to the spinal dura at the
   foramen magnum level. This could modulate dural tension and
   CSF dynamics — though the magnitude of clinically meaningful
   effect from a 0.25mm needle is uncertain.

4. **Vagal nucleus proximity:** the dorsal motor nucleus of the
   vagus (parasympathetic) is within the medulla oblongata at the
   GV16 level. Stimulation in this zone may produce
   parasympathetic effects (bradycardia, gastrointestinal
   activation) via proximity-mediated vagal modulation. This could
   contribute to GV16's calming/spirit-settling effects.

**Window of Heaven mechanism:** the cervical ring controls
vascular/neural traffic between body and head. GV16's position at
the posterior midline gives it unique access to the vertebral
artery territory and the posterior cranial fossa — the deepest,
least accessible part of the cranial cavity. While anterior Window
of Heaven points (ST9, LI18) modulate carotid/thyroid/vagal
function, GV16 modulates vertebrobasilar/brainstem/cerebellar
function.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, AP-07 BL10 (Window of Heaven mechanism)

**Ghost Point mechanism:** the "Ghost Pillow" designation
positions GV16 as a point where pathogenic spirits lodge at the
brainstem level. In modern terms, this may reflect
brainstem-mediated psychiatric symptoms (altered consciousness,
psychomotor agitation, autonomic dysregulation) — conditions where
brainstem dysfunction produces mental symptoms that were
historically attributed to spirit possession.

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): ⚠️ **RESTRICTED TECHNIQUE.**
- Maximum depth: 0.5–0.8 cun (12–16 mm)
- Direction: toward the tip of the nose (anteroinferior)
- NEVER deep perpendicular or directed superiorly
- Same safety restrictions as GV15 — see GV15 safety section
- DeQi: local heaviness and distention at the suboccipital region.
  ⚠️ Any limb symptoms, breathing changes, or sudden weakness =
  IMMEDIATE withdrawal
- Electroacupuncture: applicable with strict depth limitations.
  Low-frequency (2 Hz) electroacupuncture used in neuroprotection
  animal studies

Cứu (B08): some classical texts extend the GV17 (Naohu) moxa
prohibition to GV16. Modern practice: indirect moxa (stick moxa
at safe distance) is used clinically. Direct moxa is NOT
recommended due to the sensitivity of the suboccipital zone.
→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV6 (moxa prohibition), AP-10 TE23, AP-11 GB42

Bấm huyệt (B02): SAFE and commonly used. Thumb pressure in the
suboccipital midline depression — one of the most intuitive
acupressure locations. Effective for headache and neck tension.
Self-application: possible by placing thumbs at the base of the
skull and pressing medially.

Giác hơi (B07): NOT recommended — same restrictions as GV15. The
curved surface and proximity to dangerous structures preclude
cupping at GV16.

#### An toàn

⚠️ **HIGH RISK — ONE OF THE TWO MOST DANGEROUS POINTS (with
GV15) IN THE 361-POINT SYSTEM.**

Same danger as GV15: penetration of posterior atlantooccipital
membrane → cisterna magna → medulla/cerebellum contact. See GV15
safety section for detailed consequences and protocol.

GV16-specific considerations:
- GV16 is slightly superior and closer to the foramen magnum than
  GV15, potentially requiring even shallower maximum depth.
- The external occipital protuberance provides a bony landmark
  for orientation, but the membrane is DIRECTLY below the point
  (less distance to danger).
- Bone landmark safety study (2024, PMC11034117): the line
  between the upper posterior border of the mastoid process and
  the tip of the C2 spinous process provides an approximate depth
  marker — if the needle reaches this plane (~10 mm along the
  spinous process), it remains in the safe region.
- **Greater occipital nerve:** emerges lateral to GV16. Needle
  deviation laterally at depth risks greater occipital neuralgia.

**Contraindications:**
- Chiari malformation (cerebellar tonsils extend below foramen
  magnum — reduced safe depth)
- Basilar invagination
- Upper cervical instability (rheumatoid arthritis, Down syndrome)
- Anticoagulated patients (bleeding risk near vertebral arteries
  and PICA)
- Open foramen magnum procedures or recent posterior fossa surgery

## Cranial Vault Region (GV17–GV22)

Six points along the sagittal suture from the occipital bone (GV17)
across the parietal bones to the anterior cranium (GV22). GV20 at
the vertex is included in this region (Phase 1 entry, retained at
original heading level). The cranial vault points share a common
anatomical substrate: galea aponeurotica (epicranial aponeurosis)
overlying fused cranial sutures. No muscle bellies are present at
the midline vault — the occipitalis muscle lies posteriorly (near
GV17) and frontalis anteriorly (beyond GV22). All points use
subcutaneous oblique/transverse needle insertion (5–15 mm) within
the galea; the skull prevents deeper penetration. Nerve supply
transitions along this trajectory: greater occipital nerve (C2
dorsal ramus) at GV17 → C2/V1 overlap zone at GV18–GV19 → dual
C2+V1 at GV20 (vertex) → supraorbital nerve (V1 ophthalmic
division) at GV21–GV22. This C2→V1 gradient along the posterior-
to-anterior midline is the cranial continuation of the spinal
dorsal rami that innervate GV1–GV16. Three of six points in this
region are Sea of Marrow points (GV16 border, GV17, GV20) —
reflecting the Du Mai's traditional role accessing the brain.

### GV17 — Não Hộ (脑户, Nǎohù)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Brain's Door" (脑 = brain, 户 = door/gate). On the posterior
midline, 2.5 cun above the posterior hairline, at the upper border
of the external occipital protuberance. 1.5 cun directly above
GV16 (Fengfu). The name indicates this point's traditional role as
the gateway to the brain — it sits at the boundary between the
suboccipital danger zone (GV15–GV16) and the protected cranial
vault. One of three **Sea of Marrow** (髓海) points on Du Mai
(with GV16 and GV20). Epilepsy, dizziness, heaviness of the head,
visual disorders, neck stiffness. **Moxa prohibited** (classical).

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the head, 2.5 cun directly
above the midpoint of the posterior hairline, in the depression on
the upper border of the external occipital protuberance.
Anatomical landmarks: external occipital protuberance (inion) — the
prominent midline bony projection at the occiput. GV17 is at the
superior nuchal line, where the occipitalis muscle and the galea
aponeurotica attach. The point marks the transition from the
suboccipital region (nuchal muscles, foramen magnum proximity) to
the cranial vault (smooth occipital bone, galea).
Needle depth: 0.5–0.8 cun (~1.2–2.0 cm) transverse/oblique along
the scalp, directed superiorly. The occipital bone is thick at
this level — no risk of cranial penetration with standard needles.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: greater occipital nerve (GON) — C2 dorsal ramus, medial
branch. The GON emerges from between C1 and C2 vertebrae, ascends
through the semispinalis capitis, pierces the trapezius and
occipitalis near the superior nuchal line, then distributes over
the posterior scalp to the vertex. GV17 is in the proximal
distribution of the GON after its emergence through the
occipitalis.
Spinal segment: C2.
Vascular: occipital artery (branch of external carotid artery),
ascending after crossing the superior nuchal line.
Fascial: occipitalis muscle attachment → galea aponeurotica. The
occipitalis is the posterior muscular belly of the epicranius
(frontalis is the anterior belly). GV17 sits at or near the
junction of occipitalis fibers with the galea.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1 (cranial nerve anatomy), A03 S3.3 (galea)

#### Phân loại huyệt

**Sea of Marrow (髓海):** one of three Du Mai points accessing the
"Sea of Marrow" (brain) — with GV16 (Fengfu) and GV20 (Baihui).
The Ling Shu Chapter 33 identifies the Sea of Marrow with entry at
GV16 (below) and GV17 (above). Modern interpretation: GV17 sits
directly over the occipital lobes (visual cortex), providing the
closest scalp access to the posterior brain.

No other standard point category designations.

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV17 (Naohu). "Brain's Door" — gate point for the brain on
Du Mai. Epilepsy, dizziness, head heaviness, visual disturbance
(consistent with occipital cortex location), neck stiffness. ✓
Trigger point: occipitalis muscle trigger points at the superior
nuchal line refer pain to the posterior scalp and retroorbital area.
GV17 is at the occipitalis attachment zone. 🟡
Ashi: occipital tenderness is a common finding in tension-type
headache, cervicogenic headache, and greater occipital neuralgia.
The external occipital protuberance region is frequently tender in
these conditions. 🟡
Marma: no specific Marma at the external occipital protuberance.
Krikatika Marma is at the suboccipital zone (GV15–GV16 level). ✗
Head zone: C2 does not carry direct viscerotome projections. No
Head zone convergence. ✗
Anatomy Trains: Superficial Back Line terminates at the galea
aponeurotica via the occipitalis muscle. GV17 is at the
occipitalis-galea junction — the cranial termination of the SBL. 🟡
Neurogenic: no data for occipital scalp (Kim 2017 trunk only). ✗
Cupping: not practiced on the scalp/occiput. ✗

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Anatomy Trains 🟡). Lower than spinal GV points — the cranial
vault lacks tissue diversity (deep muscle, visceral innervation)
that gives spinal points higher convergence.

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing (Huangfu Mi): epilepsy, heaviness and pain of the head,
dizziness, facial swelling, visual disorders, neck stiffness.
Dacheng (Yang Jizhou): epilepsy, Wind-dizziness, face redness and
swelling. Sea of Marrow deficiency manifestations: dizziness,
tinnitus, blurred vision, fatigue.

**Moxa prohibition:** multiple classical texts prohibit moxa at
GV17. The Jiayi Jing explicitly states moxa is prohibited (禁灸).
This is consistent across most classical references. Possible
rationale: proximity to the occipital bone with minimal soft tissue
between scalp and periosteum — thermal injury risk to the
periosteum. Compare with GV6 moxa prohibition and TE23/GB42
prohibitions in AP-10/AP-11.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No SR/MA specific to GV17. Included in scalp acupuncture protocols
(Jiao's scalp acupuncture: occipital region lines for vision and
balance), but no individual point evidence.
🔴 for all indications.

The visual cortex location beneath GV17 is anatomically consistent
with classical visual disorder indications — though the needle does
not reach the cortex (separated by galea, periosteum, skull, and
meninges), the GON (C2) afferent pathway reaches the
trigeminocervical complex, which converges with V1 input (ophthalmic
nerve serves the eye/orbit).

#### An toàn

Low risk. The occipital bone is thick at the external occipital
protuberance — no risk of cranial penetration with standard needles.
Transverse insertion along the scalp is the standard technique.
Scalp hematoma risk from rich vascular supply (occipital artery) —
apply pressure post-needling.

**Moxa prohibited** (classical, see above).

### GV18 — Cường Gian (强间, Qiángjiān)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Rigid Space" or "Unyielding Space" (强 = strong/rigid, 间 =
space/between). On the posterior midline, 4 cun above the posterior
hairline (= 1.5 cun above GV17, 3 cun posterior to GV20). Located
on the posterior parietal bone along the sagittal suture. The name
may reflect the rigidity of the cranial vault at this location or
its traditional use for neck/head stiffness. Headache, dizziness,
mania, vomiting, insomnia. Nerve supply transitions from
C2-dominant to C2/V1 overlap at this level.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the head, 4 cun directly
above the midpoint of the posterior hairline.
Anatomical landmarks: posterior parietal bone, on the sagittal
suture. The lambdoid suture (junction of parietal and occipital
bones) crosses the midline approximately 1.5 cun below this point.
GV18 lies on the parietal bone, between the lambdoid suture and
the vertex.
Needle depth: 0.5–0.8 cun (~1.2–2.0 cm) transverse/oblique along
the scalp.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: transition zone between greater occipital nerve (C2,
posterior scalp) and supraorbital nerve (V1, anterior scalp). At 3
cun behind the vertex, C2 (GON) predominates but V1 terminal
branches begin to contribute. The exact boundary varies
individually.
Spinal segment: C2 (primary) with V1 overlap.
Vascular: occipital artery branches (posterior scalp territory of
external carotid). Parietal emissary veins may traverse the nearby
parietal bone.
Fascial: galea aponeurotica — the aponeurotic sheet between
occipitalis and frontalis. No muscle fibers at this location.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A03 S3.3

#### Phân loại huyệt

No special category designations.

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV18 (Qiangjian). Headache, dizziness, neck stiffness, mania,
vomiting, insomnia. ✓
Trigger point: no muscle belly at this location — galea
aponeurotica only. No myofascial trigger points. ✗
Ashi: parietal scalp tenderness can occur in tension-type headache
and migraine with parietal involvement. 🟡
Marma: no specific Marma at this location. Adhipati is at the
vertex (GV20). ✗
Head zone: no visceral Head zone (C2/V1 territory). ✗
Anatomy Trains: galea aponeurotica as SBL terminus — diffuse
distribution, not a specific termination point. 🟡
Neurogenic: no data for parietal scalp. ✗
Cupping: not practiced on the scalp. ✗

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM ✓, Ashi 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡).
The lowest convergence among GV points — reflects the anatomical
uniformity of the mid-cranial vault (aponeurotic tissue only, no
muscle, no visceral innervation, no cupping tradition).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: headache, dizziness, stiffness and pain of
the neck, mania (狂症), vomiting, insomnia. Minor cranial vault
point typically combined with GV20 and other scalp points rather
than used alone.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No SR/MA specific to GV18. No individual point evidence.
🔴 for all indications.

#### An toàn

Low risk. Parietal bone beneath the point is solid — no risk of
cranial penetration. Standard transverse/oblique scalp insertion.
Scalp hematoma is the primary risk (rich vascular supply).

### GV19 — Hậu Đỉnh (后顶, Hòudǐng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Behind the Vertex" (后 = behind/posterior, 顶 = vertex/top). On
the posterior midline, 1.5 cun posterior to GV20 (= 5.5 cun above
the posterior hairline). Located on the parietal bone along the
sagittal suture. The point name places it explicitly in relation
to GV20 (vertex) — the mirror counterpart of GV21 (Before the
Vertex) on the anterior side. Located in the C2/V1 overlap zone,
very close to the dual innervation documented at GV20. Headache
(especially vertex type), dizziness.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the head, 1.5 cun directly
posterior to GV20 (= 5.5 cun above the midpoint of the posterior
hairline).
Anatomical landmarks: parietal bone, on the sagittal suture, just
posterior to the vertex. In the vicinity of the parietal foramina
(paired openings in the parietal bone that transmit emissary veins
connecting scalp drainage to the superior sagittal sinus).
Needle depth: 0.5–0.8 cun (~1.2–2.0 cm) transverse/oblique along
the scalp.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: C2/V1 overlap zone. Both greater occipital nerve (C2) and
supraorbital nerve (V1) contribute innervation at this level. GV19
is 1.5 cun behind the vertex where GV20's documented dual C2+V1
convergence occurs — at the posterior edge of this convergence zone.
Spinal segment: C2 and V1 (dual).
Vascular: occipital artery and superficial temporal artery branches
overlap at the posterior parietal region. Parietal emissary veins
pass through the parietal foramina nearby.
Fascial: galea aponeurotica. No muscle fibers.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A03 S3.3

#### Phân loại huyệt

No special category designations.

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV19 (Houding). Vertex headache, dizziness, tinnitus, nasal
congestion. Paired with GV20 in vertex headache protocols. ✓
Trigger point: no muscle belly — galea aponeurotica. ✗
Ashi: vertex/posterior parietal tenderness in tension-type headache
and vertex migraine. 🟡
Marma: Adhipati Marma (GV20) is at the vertex; GV19 is at the
posterior boundary of Adhipati's zone but not a distinct Marma. ✗
Head zone: no visceral Head zone (C2/V1). ✗
Anatomy Trains: galea as SBL terminus, diffuse. 🟡
Neurogenic: no data. ✗
Cupping: not on scalp. ✗

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM ✓, Ashi 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: vertex headache, dizziness, tinnitus, neck
stiffness. Used with GV20 (Baihui) for vertex pain. Part of the
"five-needle" cranial technique (五针法): GV20 (center) + GV19 +
GV21 + bilateral BL7 in a cruciform pattern centered on the vertex.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No SR/MA specific to GV19. No individual point evidence.
🔴 for all indications.

#### An toàn

Low risk. Parietal bone is solid. Standard transverse/oblique scalp
insertion.

**Parietal emissary vein note:** the parietal foramina are in the
vicinity of GV19. These transmit emissary veins connecting scalp
venous drainage to the superior sagittal sinus. Deep perpendicular
needling directly into a parietal foramen could theoretically
transmit infection intracranially — standard oblique insertion
avoids this risk entirely.

### GV20 — Bách Hội (百会, Bǎi Huì)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the head, 5 cun directly above
the midpoint of the anterior hairline, approximately at the midpoint of
the line connecting the apexes of both ears.

Anatomical landmarks: at the vertex of the skull, on the sagittal
suture, approximately at the junction of the frontal and parietal bones.
In practice, found at the intersection of the midline and the
interaural line (connecting the ear apices over the cranium).

Needle depth range: 5-15 mm transverse/oblique insertion along the
scalp (subcutaneous, within the galea aponeurotica). Perpendicular
insertion is NOT standard — the skull prevents deep penetration beyond
the galea. Standard clinical technique: oblique posterior insertion
(向后平刺) 5-8 mm.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply: dual cranial nerve convergence zone.
- Supraorbital nerve, medial branch (CN V1 — ophthalmic division of
  trigeminal nerve). Innervates anterior scalp to vertex.
- Greater occipital nerve (C2 dorsal ramus, medial branch). Innervates
  posterior scalp to vertex.
- The two territories overlap at the vertex — GV20 sits at the boundary
  where trigeminal (V1) and cervical (C2) innervation converge.

Fascial plane: galea aponeurotica (epicranial aponeurosis) — a dense
fibrous sheet connecting the frontalis muscle (anterior) to the
occipitalis muscle (posterior). This aponeurosis is the superficial
layer of the scalp's layered architecture (skin → connective tissue →
aponeurosis → loose areolar tissue → pericranium).

Vascular: occipital artery branches (from external carotid). Parietal
emissary veins pass through the parietal foramina (located just lateral
to the sagittal suture near GV20) connecting scalp venous drainage to
the superior sagittal sinus (intracranial). This creates a direct
vascular communication between the extracranial and intracranial
compartments.

Muscle layer: no muscle belly at GV20 — the point lies within the
aponeurotic (tendinous) sheet between frontalis and occipitalis. The
closest muscle fibers are frontalis (~3 cm anterior) and occipitalis
(~5 cm posterior).

Cortical location beneath GV20: MRI study (Shen et al. 2011, eCAM)
found GV20 lies 22.7 ± 2.2 mm anterior to the central sulcus in
adults, placing it over the premotor/supplementary motor area of the
frontal lobe. The needle does NOT reach the cortex — separated by
galea, pericranium, skull, meninges.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1 (cranial nerve anatomy), A03 S3.3 (epicranial
fascia as SBL terminus)

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

GV20 is NOT a limb point — Five Shu classification does not apply to
trunk/head points on the Du Mai.

Meeting point (交会穴): GV20 is the meeting point of the Governing
Vessel (Du Mai) with the Bladder channel (Foot Taiyang), Gallbladder
channel (Foot Shaoyang), Triple Burner channel (Hand Shaoyang), and
the Liver channel (Foot Jueyin). The name "Hundred Meetings" (百会)
reflects this convergence of multiple channels at a single point. Some
classical sources extend this to ALL 6 Yang channels.

Sea of Marrow point (髓海): GV20 (along with GV16 Fengfu and GV17
Naohu) accesses the "Sea of Marrow" (brain). This is a functional
classification linking certain GV points to brain/cognitive function —
consistent with GV20's modern use for neurological/psychiatric
conditions.

Special designations:
- Command point for the head and vertex (traditional prescription logic)
- Key point for "raising Yang Qi" (升提阳气) — classical function for
  treating prolapse conditions
- NOT one of Sun Si-miao's 13 Ghost Points (confirmed: GV20 is absent
  from the ghost point list; GV16, GV23, and GV26 are ghost points but
  GV20 is not)
- NOT Ma Dan-yang Star Point

Eight Confluent access: Du Mai is accessed via SI3 (Houxi) + BL62
(Shenmai) as the Eight Confluent pair (八脉交会穴). GV20 is not itself
a confluent point but is the most clinically significant point on the
vessel that SI3+BL62 access.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2 (Eight Confluent Points table)

#### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** GV20 (Baihui, 百会). Meeting of all Yang
meridians. Traditional functions: raise Yang, extinguish Liver Wind
(熄肝风), calm the spirit (安神), benefit the brain and marrow (益脑髓).
Most important point on the Du Mai for neuropsychiatric indications.

**2. Trigger point:** No direct correspondence. The galea aponeurotica
at the vertex contains no muscle fibers and therefore no myofascial
trigger points. The nearest muscles with documented trigger points are
frontalis (referring pain to forehead) and occipitalis (referring to
posterior scalp), but neither trigger point maps to the vertex itself.
Dorsher's systematic mapping does not identify a trigger point
corresponding to GV20.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Vertex pressure/tenderness is a common
clinical finding in tension-type headache, migraine (particularly
vertex-type), and stress-related scalp tension. Vertex palpation is
used diagnostically in several manual therapy traditions. However, this
is a broad zone rather than a specific point.

**4. Marma point:** Adhipati (अधिपति, "Overlord/Supreme Ruler"). One of
the 3 Maha (great) Marmas in Ayurveda (along with Hridayam/heart and
Basti/bladder). Located at the crown/vertex. Classification: Sandhi
(joint) Marma — at the junction of cranial bones (sagittal suture).
Size: traditionally described as ½ anguli (finger-width).
Injury classification: Sadyah Pranahara — immediately lethal if
severely injured. This reflects the fontanelle vulnerability in
neonates and the proximity to the superior sagittal sinus in adults.
Traditional therapeutic function: enhances mental clarity, calms the
mind, connects to higher consciousness — closely paralleling GV20's
TCM function of "calming the spirit" and "benefiting the brain."

**5. Head zone:** GV20 lies at the convergence of the C2 dermatome
(posterior scalp) and V1 ophthalmic territory (anterior scalp). Neither
C2 nor V1 carry direct viscerotome projections to internal organs.
Therefore: NO Head zone convergence at GV20. The therapeutic effects
on internal organs (e.g., prolapse, autonomic regulation) cannot be
explained by segmental dermatome-viscerotome convergence — consistent
with a non-segmental, supraspinal mechanism.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Back Line (SBL) terminates at the
epicranial fascia (galea aponeurotica). The SBL runs: plantar fascia →
gastrocnemius → hamstrings → sacrotuberous ligament → erector spinae →
epicranial fascia. GV20 sits at the SBL's cranial terminus.
Additionally, the Superficial Front Line (SFL) reaches the scalp via
sternocleidomastoid (SCM) → scalp fascia. The vertex is where both longitudinal lines
terminate — a fascial convergence point. (Dorsher 2009: 8/9 Anatomy
Trains lines overlap with TCM meridian distributions.)
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.3 (Anatomy Trains)

**7. Neurogenic spot:** No data. Kim 2017's neurogenic inflammatory
spot mapping focused on trunk dermatomes (T1-L2). Scalp points were
not included in the study protocol.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable. Cupping on the scalp vertex is
not practiced in either TCM or Sunnah/Hijama traditions due to hair
and scalp anatomy.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, Ashi, Marma, Anatomy Trains).
Moderate convergence. Lower than limb points (LI4 5/8, LR3 5/8)
because the scalp vertex lacks muscle bellies for trigger points, has
no visceral Head zone projection, and is anatomically unsuitable for
cupping. The convergence that DOES exist is significant: Adhipati is
one of only 3 Maha Marmas, and the SBL/SFL terminus at the vertex
represents a unique fascial convergence of both anterior and posterior
body lines.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Depression — 🟡 Plausible**

GV20 is the most commonly selected acupoint in depression acupuncture
neuroimaging studies (Lin et al. 2025, Frontiers in Psychiatry — SR of
26 neuroimaging studies 2014-2024, N=1138). 17 of these studies used
the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD) as the outcome measure.

Acupuncture for depression (general, not GV20-specific): SR/MA (Tan
et al. 2024, Frontiers in Neuroscience, 43 studies) — acupuncture vs
sham: HAMD reduction -1.63 (95% CI: -2.49 to -0.76, P=0.0002,
I²=86%). GV20
was part of most included protocols but individual contribution cannot
be isolated.

fMRI evidence specific to GV20: electroacupuncture (EA) at GV20 in
drug-naïve first-episode major depressive disorder (MDD) modulated the
Default Mode Network (DMN) — reduced functional connectivity between
medial prefrontal cortex and posterior cingulate cortex / precuneus
(Deng et al. 2016, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience). A separate study
found altered amygdala resting-state functional connectivity following
EA at GV20 in first-episode drug-naïve MDD: changed connectivity
between amygdala and hippocampus, precuneus, precentral gyrus, and
angular gyrus (Wang et al. 2019, Brain Imaging and Behavior).

Synergy evidence: HT7 alone activated PKA/CREB pathway; SP6 alone
activated BDNF/TrkB pathway; combined HT7+SP6+GV20 produced
synergistic effects activating BOTH pathways with superior outcomes
(Qiao et al. 2023, Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine, rat model).
→ Cross-ref: AP-05 (HT7 synergy data)

Confidence: 🟡 — GV20 is the most-used point in depression protocols
and has point-specific fMRI evidence showing DMN and amygdala network
modulation. However: no sole-point GV20 trial for depression exists;
all clinical evidence uses multi-point protocols. The fMRI evidence
establishes neural specificity but not clinical efficacy in isolation.

**2. Anxiety — 🟡 Plausible**

EA at GV20 produces anxiolytic effects via oxytocin release from the
paraventricular nucleus (PVN) of the hypothalamus (Gong et al. 2026,
Chinese Medicine). Key findings:
- Pseudorabies virus retrograde tracing confirmed neural circuit from
  GV20 scalp → PVN oxytocin-synthesizing neurons
- EA at GV20 significantly increased PVN oxytocin neuron activation
  and elevated peripheral oxytocin concentrations
- Oxytocin receptor blockade COMPLETELY abrogated the anxiolytic
  effects — confirming oxytocin as the essential effector molecule
- Optimal parameters: intermittent 1.5 mA, dense-sparse wave

Clinical: HT7+GV20 combination for generalized anxiety disorder (GAD)
— 20 min/session × 10 days reduced anxiety in younger and middle-aged
patients without affecting cardiovascular parameters (Dhanushya Devi
et al. 2025, American Journal of Chinese Medicine).
→ Cross-ref: AP-05 (HT7+GV20 anxiety data)

Multi-point protocol with sham control: GV20+Yintang+HT7+SP6 vs sham
acupuncture in chronic insomnia patients (Liu et al. 2021, Annals of
Translational Medicine, RCT, N=60). Acupuncture group showed
significantly greater decrease in serum cortisol and increase in serum
serotonin (5-HT) vs sham. Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAMA) scores significantly improved.

Confidence: 🟡 — MOLECULAR mechanism identified (PVN oxytocin pathway
with receptor blockade confirmation), making this one of the
strongest mechanistic findings for any anxiolytic indication in the
Atlas. Clinical evidence exists but only as part of multi-point
protocols. No sole-point sham-controlled clinical trial for GV20 and
anxiety alone.

**3. Post-stroke cognitive impairment — 🟡 Plausible**

Animal evidence: SR/MA of GV20-based scalp acupuncture in experimental
ischemic stroke (Wang et al. 2014, Scientific Reports). 54 studies,
1816 animals. GV20-based acupuncture significantly improved infarct
volume (12 studies, P<0.01) and neurological function score (32
studies, P<0.01) vs middle cerebral artery occlusion controls.

Human evidence: multiple SRs support acupuncture for post-stroke
cognitive impairment (PSCI). Overview of SRs and MAs (Zhou et al.
2023, Neuropsychiatric Disease and Treatment).
GV20+GV24 (Shenting) = most commonly used combination. Assessment
via Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), Montreal Cognitive Assessment
(MoCA), Loewenstein Occupational Therapy Cognitive Assessment (LOTCA),
P300 latency. SR/MA (Luo et al. 2024, Brain and Behavior): acupuncture
for PSCI shows positive effects on cognitive function measures.

CRITICAL LIMITATION: B01 note and C02 record that 99.92% of stroke
acupuncture RCTs originate from China — extreme geographic bias.
This affects all post-stroke acupuncture evidence, not GV20
specifically.

Confidence: 🟡 — strong and consistent animal evidence specifically
for GV20. Human SRs positive but limited by geographic bias, variable
protocols, and inability to isolate GV20's individual contribution
within multi-point scalp acupuncture. Animal SR is notable because it
IS GV20-specific (54 studies using GV20-based protocols).

**4. Insomnia — 🟡 Plausible**

Chinese Medicine evidence-based guideline recommends HT7+GV20+SP6 as
a standard protocol for insomnia.

RCT (Liu et al. 2021, Annals of Translational Medicine, N=60):
GV20+Yintang+HT7+SP6 vs sham acupuncture, 3×/week for 4 weeks.
Acupuncture group showed significantly improved Pittsburgh Sleep Quality
Index (PSQI), sleep efficiency,
and sleep onset latency vs sham. Biochemical: serum cortisol↓, serum
5-HT↑ (both between-group significant).

EA at GV20 combined with HT7 and SP6 improved spatial learning and
memory deficits in insomnia rats — combined acupoints outperformed
single acupoints (Qiao L et al. 2023, Journal of Traditional Chinese
Medicine).

Confidence: 🟡 — part of guideline-recommended protocol with one
positive sham-controlled RCT (multi-point, N=60). GV20's individual
contribution to the insomnia protocol cannot be isolated. C02 note:
no therapy reaches 🟢 for sleep as primary outcome.

**5. Prolapse (uterine, rectal, gastric) — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Classical indication with strong historical documentation. GV20
moxibustion for prolapse is recorded in multiple ancient texts (Tai
Chan Xu Zhi for postpartum uterine prolapse; Zhenjiu Dacheng for
rectal prolapse). The mechanism framework is "raising sunken Yang Qi"
(升提阳气) — a traditional theoretical explanation, not a modern
mechanism.

Modern evidence: pilot trials combining acupuncture at GV20 with
pelvic floor muscle training show promise. No SR/MA exists for GV20
specifically and organ prolapse. Individual small-scale RCTs with
methodological limitations.

Confidence: 🔴 — strong classical consensus across multiple texts, but
no systematic review or rigorously controlled clinical trial. Modern
pilot trials are preliminary only.

**Sham comparison note:** GV20 shares the pattern seen across Phase 1
points: effects are more clearly demonstrated vs no-treatment controls
than vs sham acupuncture. The fMRI evidence (DMN modulation, amygdala
connectivity changes) provides the strongest point-specific signal.
The oxytocin finding (receptor blockade abrogating the effect) provides
molecular-level specificity evidence.

**Negative evidence note:** No published null results for GV20 as a
sole point were found in the literature search. This likely reflects
the absence of sole-point GV20 trials rather than selective
publication of only positive results. GV20 is almost always used
within multi-point protocols.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

Scalp vertex sensitivity is clinically observed in tension-type
headache, migraine, and stress/anxiety states. The concept of "Yang
rising to the vertex" in TCM corresponds to observed increased scalp
muscle tension and tenderness under stress.

No specific published data on GV20 threshold changes under pathology
comparable to the trunk/limb sensitization data (Kim 2017). The galea
aponeurotica at GV20 lacks the receptor density typical of muscle-rich
acupoint locations — sensitization at this point may involve the
cutaneous nerves (V1, C2) rather than deep tissue receptors.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at GV20:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): supraorbital nerve (CN V1) and
  greater occipital nerve (C2). Both A-beta (light touch) and A-delta
  (needle/pressure) fibers activated. The V1+C2 convergence at this
  point is unique in Phase 1 — it is the ONLY point innervated by
  cranial nerves rather than spinal nerves exclusively.
- A02 (Autonomic Nervous System): indirect via trigeminal-brainstem
  pathway. V1 afferents → trigeminal nucleus → nucleus tractus
  solitarius (NTS) → vagal output
  (same pathway documented for EFT tapping at GV20 in B12). Also:
  PVN activation → oxytocin release → autonomic modulation.
- A03 (Fascia/Connective Tissue): galea aponeurotica = SBL terminus.
  Needle manipulation in this dense aponeurotic tissue may activate
  fascial mechanotransduction (Langevin model), but the galea is
  much thinner and less mechanically responsive than limb fascia.
- A06 (Chemical Signaling): EA at GV20 → PVN oxytocin release
  (confirmed by retrograde tracing and receptor blockade, 2026). This
  is a specific molecular effector pathway.

**Central mechanism — cranial nerve pathway to brainstem (NOT
segmental):**

GV20 represents a fundamentally different neural architecture from all
previous Phase 1 points. All 13 prior points (ST36, ST25, PC6, LI4,
SP6, BL67, BL13/15/18/20/23, HT7, LR3) are innervated by SPINAL
nerves and must traverse the spinal cord to reach the brainstem. GV20
is innervated by CRANIAL nerves (V1, trigeminal) and upper cervical
nerves (C2), both of which have DIRECT projections to brainstem nuclei
without spinal cord intermediary.

```
Trigeminal pathway (V1 at GV20):

Step 1: Needle/EA activates V1 supraorbital nerve afferents at vertex
Step 2: V1 afferents → trigeminal ganglion → trigeminal nucleus
        (brainstem — DIRECT, no spinal cord relay)
Step 3: Trigeminal nucleus connects to:
        → NTS (nucleus tractus solitarius) → vagal output
        → DMV (dorsal motor nucleus of vagus) → parasympathetic
        → LC (locus coeruleus) → norepinephrine modulation
        → DRN (dorsal raphe nucleus) → serotonin modulation
        → PVN (paraventricular nucleus) → oxytocin release
Step 4: Integrated autonomic + neuropeptide response:
        ↓cortisol, ↑5-HT, ↑oxytocin (all confirmed in GV20 studies)
```

```
C2 pathway (greater occipital nerve at GV20):

Step 1: Needle/EA activates C2 greater occipital nerve afferents
Step 2: C2 afferents → trigeminocervical complex (TCC)
        (C2 converges with trigeminal at the TCC — Bartsch & Goadsby
        2003, same convergence documented for migraine pathophysiology)
Step 3: TCC → same brainstem targets as trigeminal pathway above
```

The DUAL innervation (V1 + C2) at GV20 means TWO afferent pathways
converge on the SAME brainstem targets. This creates a "double input"
effect that may explain GV20's prominent role in neuropsychiatric
protocols — stronger brainstem activation through convergent input
from two neural territories.

**Non-segmental gap analysis:** Not applicable in the same sense as
limb/trunk points. GV20's effects on depression, anxiety, and
cognition operate through DIRECT cranial nerve-to-brainstem pathways.
There is no "gap" between peripheral innervation and clinical target
because the brainstem IS the target organ (autonomic regulation,
mood, cognition are all brainstem/cortical functions). This contrasts
with ST36 (L4-L5 → T6-T9 gastric = 5-segment gap) or LR3 (L4-L5 →
T7-T9 liver = 5-segment gap) where the peripheral innervation and
target organ are in different spinal segments.

⚡ **Framework note 1 — 9th mechanism pattern: cranial nerve
convergence → PVN → oxytocin.** GV20 adds a new mechanism to the
Phase 1 catalog that is architecturally distinct from all 8 prior
patterns. All previous mechanisms involved SPINAL nerve input with
varying supraspinal processing. GV20 operates via CRANIAL nerve input
(V1 + C2) with direct brainstem access. The identified molecular
effector (oxytocin from PVN, confirmed by retrograde tracing and
receptor blockade) makes this the 2nd point-specific molecular pathway
after ST36→PROKR2. Updated mechanism catalog:
(1) ST36: PROKR2 → vagal-adrenal
(2) PC6: median nerve → NTS → GABA
(3) LI4: radial nerve → PAG/RVM descending modulation
(4) SP6: dual S2-S4 sacral + PAG
(5) BL67: UNKNOWN
(6) Back-Shu (BL13/15/18/20/23): somatovisceral segmental reflex
(7) HT7: ulnar/dorsal column → cuneate → limbic
(8) LR3: deep peroneal → thalamus → prefrontal cortical
(9) GV20: V1+C2 cranial → brainstem → PVN → oxytocin

⚡ **Framework note 2 — Head points = cranial nerve architecture.**
GV20 reveals that HEAD acupoints operate through a fundamentally
different neural architecture from limb/trunk points. Cranial nerve
pathways (V1, C2 via trigeminocervical complex) have DIRECT brainstem
access without spinal cord intermediary. This may explain why head
points (GV20, Yintang, auricular points) are preferentially used for
neuropsychiatric indications — they have the shortest, most direct
pathway to the brainstem nuclei (NTS, DMV, LC, DRN, PVN) that regulate
mood, anxiety, sleep, and autonomic function. Phase 1 Batch 10
(Yintang) and Batch 11 (auricular points) will test whether this
cranial nerve architecture pattern holds across all head-region points.

⚡ **Framework note 3 — 2nd point-specific molecular effector.** GV20
EA → PVN oxytocin (confirmed by pseudorabies retrograde tracing and
OT receptor blockade) parallels ST36 → PROKR2 (confirmed by receptor
ablation). Two points, two distinct molecular effectors, two different
target pathways. The emerging pattern: acupoint specificity is not
merely a matter of anatomical location selecting different nerve
territories — it extends to MOLECULAR specificity where different
points activate different signaling molecules through different neural
circuits. This strengthens the case for acupoint-specific therapeutic
mechanisms beyond the non-specific "any needling anywhere helps" model.

**fMRI cortical signature for GV20:** EA at GV20 in healthy subjects
produced increased regional homogeneity (ReHo) in orbital frontal
cortex, middle cingulate cortex, precentral cortex, and precuneus;
decreased ReHo in anterior cingulate cortex, supplementary motor area,
thalamus, putamen, and cerebellum (Deng et al. 2016, Journal of
Alternative and Complementary Medicine). In MDD patients, GV20 EA
modulated Default Mode Network connectivity (separate study, Frontiers
in Human Neuroscience 2016).

This is the 4th acupoint-specific cortical fingerprint documented in
Phase 1:
- ST36 → visual network / noradrenaline (Rao 2024 SR)
- HT7 → widest deactivation across limbic/cortical areas (Zheng 2023)
- LR3 → thalamus + prefrontal cortex (Rao 2024 SR)
- GV20 → orbital frontal + cingulate + precuneus / DMN modulation

Four points, four distinct brain activation patterns. The
"acupoint-specific cortical fingerprint" pattern continues to
strengthen with each new point characterized.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S2 (fMRI partial specificity), AP-03 S2 (ST36 fMRI),
AP-05 (HT7 fMRI), AP-12 (LR3 fMRI)

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 5-15 mm oblique/transverse insertion posteriorly along
  the scalp. The galea aponeurotica is the target tissue layer. The
  skull prevents any deeper penetration. Scalp acupuncture technique:
  needle insertion at 15-30° angle, threaded subcutaneously for 15-25
  mm along the scalp surface.
- De Qi: often mild or different from limb points. Patients typically
  report dull distention, warmth, or "electric" sensation at vertex.
  Strong DeQi is less consistently obtained at scalp points compared
  to muscle-rich limb points.
- EA parameters for depression/anxiety: dense-sparse wave at 1.5 mA
  identified as optimal for anxiolytic effect via PVN oxytocin pathway
  (Gong et al. 2026, Chinese Medicine). Standard clinical EA: 2 Hz continuous or
  2/15 Hz dense-sparse, 20-30 min sessions.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02):
- B02 Tier 2 point (10-50 acupressure-specific RCTs). Self-accessible:
  fingertip pressure at vertex.
- Pressure: moderate fingertip or knuckle pressure directly at vertex,
  sustained 30-60 seconds or circular massage. The galea aponeurotica
  transmits pressure to the cutaneous nerve endings (V1, C2).
- Conditions: headache, cognitive impairment, general relaxation.
- No acupressure-specific RCTs for GV20 alone; evidence is from
  multi-point protocols.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Cứu (B08):
- PRIMARY technique for prolapse indications. Indirect moxa (stick moxa
  held above vertex) or moxa cone on ginger/salt slice. Classical
  prescription: 7-14 moxa cones at GV20 for prolapse of rectum, uterus,
  or stomach.
- Warmth at GV20 may activate thermosensitive C-fiber afferents in the
  V1/C2 territory. Chen Rixin's heat-sensitive moxibustion model
  suggests thermal stimulus may preferentially activate certain
  pathways.
→ Cross-ref: B08

Dry needling (B11):
- Not standard. The galea aponeurotica contains no muscle fibers — there
  is no trigger point to target and no local twitch response expected.
  Dry needling at GV20 would functionally duplicate acupuncture needle
  insertion without the framework of point selection.

So sánh: for neuropsychiatric indications (depression, anxiety,
insomnia), EA at GV20 has the strongest evidence base — the molecular
mechanism (oxytocin pathway) was identified using EA parameters.
Moxibustion is the technique of choice for prolapse (classical
consensus, thermal stimulus). Acupressure is accessible but less
studied at this point specifically.

#### An toàn

Vascular risk: parietal emissary veins pass through parietal foramina
near GV20, connecting the scalp venous drainage to the superior
sagittal sinus. Deep perpendicular needling is NOT performed (skull
prevents passage), but the theoretical risk of emissary vein
penetration exists if needling directly into a parietal foramen.
Practically: risk is extremely low with standard oblique insertion
technique. The scalp is highly vascular — minor bleeding is more
common at scalp points than limb/trunk points. Apply pressure
post-needling.

Neural risk: negligible. The supraorbital and greater occipital nerves
are cutaneous sensory nerves — needle contact produces transient
paresthesia, not lasting damage. No motor nerves at the vertex.

Pregnancy: NOT contraindicated. GV20 is not on any classical or modern
list of pregnancy-forbidden points. Used in some pregnancy protocols
for headache and emotional regulation.

Special consideration: scalp hematoma. The scalp's layered anatomy
(rich blood supply in the subcutaneous layer, loose areolar tissue
beneath the galea) means that hematoma formation is possible,
especially in patients on anticoagulants. The loose areolar layer
allows blood to spread widely, potentially causing noticeable swelling.
Clinical significance is low (self-resolving) but patients should be
informed.

### GV21 — Tiền Đỉnh (前顶, Qiándǐng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Before the Vertex" (前 = before/anterior, 顶 = vertex/top). On
the midline, 1.5 cun anterior to GV20 (= 3.5 cun above the
anterior hairline), on the parietal bone along the sagittal suture
approaching the coronal suture. The mirror counterpart of GV19
(Behind the Vertex). Nerve supply is predominantly V1 (supraorbital
nerve) — anterior to the vertex where V1 territory dominates over
C2. Headache, dizziness, rhinorrhea, infantile convulsions.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the head, 1.5 cun directly
anterior to GV20 (= 3.5 cun above the midpoint of the anterior
hairline).
Anatomical landmarks: anterior parietal bone, on the sagittal
suture. The coronal suture (frontal-parietal junction) lies
approximately 1 cun further anterior. The point is anterior to the
vertex, in the zone transitioning toward the frontal bone.
Needle depth: 0.5–0.8 cun (~1.2–2.0 cm) transverse/oblique along
the scalp.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: supraorbital nerve (V1, ophthalmic division of trigeminal)
— anterior branch. At 1.5 cun anterior to the vertex, V1 territory
predominates. Some C2 (GON) contribution may still reach this far
anteriorly, but V1 is the primary innervation.
Cranial nerve: V1.
Vascular: superficial temporal artery branches (from external
carotid). The anterior parietal region is supplied primarily by the
superficial temporal artery rather than the occipital artery.
Fascial: galea aponeurotica. No muscle fibers. The frontalis muscle
begins approximately 2–3 cun further anterior.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A03 S3.3

#### Phân loại huyệt

No special category designations.

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV21 (Qianding). Headache (especially vertex and frontal),
dizziness, rhinorrhea, epistaxis, infantile convulsions. Part of
the "five-needle" vertex protocol (GV20 center + GV19 + GV21 +
bilateral BL7 in cruciform pattern). ✓
Trigger point: no muscle belly — galea aponeurotica. ✗
Ashi: anterior parietal scalp tenderness in frontal headache and
migraines with vertex extension. 🟡
Marma: Adhipati Marma zone (GV20) may extend slightly anterior
from the vertex, but GV21 is not a distinct Marma point. ✗
Head zone: no visceral Head zone (V1 territory). ✗
Anatomy Trains: galea as SBL terminus, diffuse. 🟡
Neurogenic: no data. ✗
Cupping: not on scalp. ✗

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM ✓, Ashi 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: headache, dizziness, rhinorrhea, epistaxis,
facial edema, infantile convulsions. Combined with GV20 in vertex
protocols. The "five-needle" cranial technique (五针法) places
needles at GV20 (center), GV19, GV21, and bilateral BL7 (1.5 cun
lateral) — a cruciform pattern centered on the vertex for headache,
dizziness, and stroke rehabilitation.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No SR/MA specific to GV21. Used as part of scalp acupuncture
protocols (Jiao's motor area, sensory area lines) but no individual
point evidence.
🔴 for all indications.

#### An toàn

Low risk. Parietal bone is solid. Standard transverse/oblique scalp
insertion. Same vascular considerations as other cranial vault
points (scalp hematoma risk).

### GV22 — Tín Hội (囟会, Xìnhuì)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Fontanelle Meeting" (囟 = fontanelle, 会 = meeting/convergence).
On the midline, 2 cun posterior to the anterior hairline (= 3 cun
anterior to GV20). Named after the anterior fontanelle (bregma) —
the diamond-shaped membranous gap at the junction of the coronal
and sagittal sutures, open in neonates until 18–24 months. In
adults, the gap is fully ossified. The point sits at or near the
bregma landmark — the only point in the 361-point system named
after an embryological structure. Headache, dizziness, rhinorrhea,
rhinitis. **Needle prohibited in infants** with open fontanelle.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the head, 2 cun directly
above the midpoint of the anterior hairline.
Anatomical landmarks: on or near the bregma — the junction of the
coronal suture (frontal-parietal junction) and the sagittal suture
(midline parietal junction). The bregma is the site of the anterior
fontanelle in infants. In adults, it is a palpable depression at
the junction of three cranial bones (frontal + two parietal).
Needle depth: 0.5–0.8 cun (~1.2–2.0 cm) transverse/oblique along
the scalp in ADULTS ONLY. Directed posteriorly along the sagittal
suture.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: supraorbital nerve (V1, ophthalmic division of trigeminal).
At 2 cun behind the anterior hairline, this is solidly within V1
territory. The supraorbital nerve exits the supraorbital foramen
and ascends over the forehead to supply the anterior scalp.
Cranial nerve: V1.
Vascular: superficial temporal artery, frontal branch. At the
bregma, emissary veins may pass through the bone connecting to the
superior sagittal sinus (same venous communication as at GV19/GV20
parietal foramina).
Fascial: galea aponeurotica. The frontalis muscle fibers are
approximately 2 cun further anterior (at the forehead). No muscle
at this location.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A03 S3.3

#### Phân loại huyệt

No standard point category designations.

The name 囟 specifically references the fontanelle — making GV22
the only point in the 361-point system named after an embryological
landmark. The anterior fontanelle is the largest of the six
fontanelles (anterior, posterior, 2 mastoid, 2 sphenoid) and the
last to close (18–24 months).

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV22 (Xinhui). Headache, dizziness, rhinorrhea, rhinitis,
epistaxis, infantile convulsions. The fontanelle connection gives

position at the coronal-sagittal suture junction. 🟡
Head zone: no visceral Head zone (V1). ✗

(frontalis is anterior). The bregma region sits between the SBL
(occipitalis → galea) and SFL (frontalis → galea) endpoints. 🟡
Neurogenic: no data. ✗
Cupping: not on scalp. ✗

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM ✓, Ashi 🟡, Marma 🟡, Anatomy
Trains 🟡). Higher than other mid-vault points due to Simantaka

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: headache, dizziness, rhinorrhea, rhinitis,
epistaxis, infantile convulsions, childhood wind disorders. The
fontanelle association makes GV22 a reference point in classical
pediatric TCM — fontanelle palpation is a diagnostic technique in
Chinese pediatric medicine.

**Needle prohibition in infants:** classical texts universally
prohibit needle insertion at GV22 in infants with open fontanelle.
The anterior fontanelle is a membranous gap — needle insertion
penetrates directly to the meninges and brain without cranial bone
protection. Needling is permitted after full ossification (typically
after age 2).

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No SR/MA specific to GV22. No individual point evidence.
🔴 for all indications.

#### An toàn

**In adults:** low risk. The bregma is fully ossified — standard
transverse/oblique scalp insertion is safe. Same vascular
considerations as other cranial vault points.

**In infants and young children (< 2 years):** ⚠️ **NEEDLE
PROHIBITED.** The anterior fontanelle is open — the brain is
separated from the scalp only by the membranous fontanelle, dura
mater, and subarachnoid space. Needle insertion bypasses the
protective skull and can contact brain tissue or the superior
sagittal sinus.

**Age verification:** confirm fontanelle closure before needling
at GV22 in any child under 3 years. The anterior fontanelle closes
at 18–24 months in most children, but delayed closure occurs in
rickets, hypothyroidism, Down syndrome, achondroplasia, and
increased intracranial pressure.

## Forehead–Face Region (GV23–GV28)

Six points from the anterior scalp (GV23, 1 cun above the
anterior hairline) to the inside of the upper gum (GV28). This
region completes Du Mai's descent from the cranial vault over the
face to its intraoral endpoint. The anatomy shifts fundamentally
from scalp to face:

- **Tissue substrate:** galea aponeurotica (GV23) → frontalis
  muscle (GV24) → nasal bone and cartilage (GV25) → orbicularis
  oris muscle (GV26–GV27) → oral mucosa and labial frenulum
  (GV28). The shift from aponeurotic scalp to muscular face
  restores the tissue complexity absent in the cranial vault.
- **Nerve supply:** V1 ophthalmic (supraorbital nerve at GV23,
  GV24) → V1 external nasal nerve (GV25, nose tip) → V2 maxillary
  (infraorbital nerve at GV26, superior labial at GV27) → V2
  maxillary (superior alveolar at GV28). The **V1→V2 transition**
  occurs at the nose, completing the C2→V1→V2 cranial nerve
  gradient that began at GV17.
- **Special designations:** 2 of the 3 GV Ghost Points (GV23 #10,
  GV26 #1), the Du Mai endpoint meeting with Ren Mai and Stomach
  (GV28), and the premier emergency point in TCM (GV26).
- **Insertion technique:** varies dramatically — scalp transverse
  (GV23–GV24) → perpendicular at cartilaginous nose tip (GV25) →
  oblique at philtrum (GV26) → shallow at lip margin (GV27) →
  intraoral at mucosal frenulum (GV28).

### GV23 — Thượng Tinh (上星, Shàngxīng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Upper Star" (上 = upper, 星 = star). On the head, 1 cun directly
above the midpoint of the anterior hairline, on the midline.
**Ghost Point #10 (鬼堂, Gui Tang, "Ghost Hall")** in Sun
Si-miao's 13 Ghost Points sequence — belonging to the 4th and
final group (#10–#13). The forehead location bridges scalp
acupuncture to facial acupuncture: GV23 is the LAST GV point on
the scalp before the trajectory descends onto the face. Primary
traditional indications: nasal obstruction, rhinitis, frontal
headache, eye disorders, and psychiatric conditions (Ghost Point
function). Supraorbital nerve (V1) territory.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the head, 1 cun directly
above the midpoint of the anterior hairline.

Anatomical landmarks: the anterior hairline is a variable
landmark that shifts with age (receding hairline). WHO
standardization: the distance from the anterior hairline to GV20
(vertex) is 5 cun. GV23 at 1 cun above the hairline = 4 cun
anterior to GV20. In practice: palpate the midline 1 cun
posterior to the hairline; the point lies between GV24 (0.5 cun
above hairline, below) and GV22 (2 cun above hairline, above).

Needle depth range: 0.5–0.8 cun (~1.2–2.0 cm) transverse/oblique
insertion, typically directed posteriorly. Same subcutaneous
scalp technique as other cranial vault points.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Supraorbital nerve (V1, ophthalmic division of trigeminal)**
  — the primary sensory nerve of the forehead and anterior scalp.
  The medial branch courses superiorly from the supraorbital
  foramen to innervate the midline scalp up to approximately
  the coronal suture.
- Spinal segment attribution: V1 trigeminal territory, pure
  ophthalmic division.

Fascial plane: galea aponeurotica at its anterior margin,
approaching the frontalis muscle origin. GV23 lies at the
transition zone where the aponeurotic scalp meets the muscular
forehead — the frontalis muscle fibers may extend posteriorly
to or near this point in some individuals.

Vascular: supratrochlear and supraorbital arteries (from
ophthalmic artery, branch of internal carotid) — these ascend
from the orbit to supply the forehead and anterior scalp.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1 (cranial nerves), A03 S3.3 (galea)

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo-Connecting: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
Back-Shu: No. Front-Mu: No. Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No.

**Ghost Point #10 — Gui Tang (鬼堂, "Ghost Hall"):**
Tenth in Sun Si-miao's 13 Ghost Points sequence (Qianjin Yaofang,
652 CE): GV26(#1) → LU11(#2) → SP1(#3) → PC7(#4) → BL62(#5) →
GV16(#6) → ST6(#7) → CV24(#8) → PC8(#9) → **GV23(#10)** →
CV1(#11) → LI11(#12) → EX-HN1(#13).

GV23 belongs to the **4th and final group** (#10–#13):

| Ghost # | Point | Ghost Name | Location | Domain |
|---------|-------|------------|----------|--------|
| **10** | **GV23** | **Ghost Hall (鬼堂)** | **Forehead** | **Frontal lobe / spirit** |
| 11 | CV1 | Ghost Store (鬼藏) | Perineum | Yin–Yang axis endpoint |
| 12 | LI11 | Ghost Minister (鬼臣) | Elbow | Yangming clearing |
| 13 | EX-HN11 | Ghost Seal (鬼封) | Haiquan (sublingual) | Speech / communication |

"Ghost Hall" (鬼堂) = the hall where pathogenic spirits reside.
The forehead location is significant: GV23 overlies the frontal
lobe — the brain region most associated with executive function,
personality, and behavioral regulation. The tradition recognized
the forehead as a "spirit court" (neighboring GV24 is literally
named "Spirit Court"), suggesting intuitive association between
the frontal region and psychiatric function.

**GV Ghost Point axis complete:** GV16 (occiput, #6) → GV23
(forehead, #10) → GV26 (philtrum, #1). The Du Mai's Ghost Points
span from posterior cranium to anterior face, tracing the
meridian's trajectory over the head. The sequence is NOT
anatomical order — GV26 is #1 (the opening) while GV23 is #10,
indicating that Ghost Point numbering follows clinical protocol
sequence (where to needle first) rather than meridian trajectory.
→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV16 (Ghost #6), AP-13 GV26 (Ghost #1),
AP-01 LU11 (#2), AP-04 SP1 (#3), AP-09 PC7 (#4), AP-07 BL62 (#5)

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV23 (Shangxing 上星). Nasal obstruction, rhinitis,
sinusitis, frontal headache, eye diseases (lacrimation, visual
dimness), epistaxis, mania, epilepsy. Traditionally: "Upper Star
opens the nasal passages and clears the head." ✓
Trigger point: frontalis muscle trigger points refer pain to the
forehead and may extend to the anterior hairline region. GV23 is
at the posterior border of frontalis territory — anatomically
close but indirect correspondence. 🟡

Marma: no specific Marma at 1 cun above the anterior hairline.
Sthapani Marma (between the eyebrows, ≈ Yintang EX-HN3) is the
nearest Marma, ~3–4 cun inferior to GV23. ✗
Head zone: V1 ophthalmic territory — no direct visceral reflex
at the anterior scalp midline. ✗
Anatomy Trains: galea aponeurotica at the SBL–SFL transition.
The frontalis–galea junction where the Superficial Front Line
meets the Superficial Back Line is a fascial boundary. 🟡
Neurogenic: no data. ✗
Cupping: not on scalp. ✗

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Anatomy Trains 🟡). Same moderate convergence as other anterior
cranial points, elevated from mid-vault (3/8) by frontalis muscle
proximity allowing trigger point correspondence.

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: nasal obstruction, rhinorrhea, epistaxis,
frontal headache, lacrimation, visual dimness, redness and pain
of the eyes, epilepsy, mania. Qianjin Yaofang adds Ghost Point
designation for psychiatric conditions.

Clinical combinations:
- **Rhinitis:** GV23 + LI20 (Yingxiang) + LI4 (Hegu) — GV23
  from above + LI20 locally + LI4 distally for nasal conditions.
- **Frontal headache:** GV23 + Yintang (EX-HN3) + GV24 — three
  midline forehead points for frontal cephalgia.
→ Cross-ref: AP-02 LI4, LI20

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

GV23 is commonly included in scalp acupuncture protocols for
nasal and neuropsychiatric conditions but no individual point
SRs exist.

Depression: GV23 (Shangxing) is among the frequently selected
acupoints in depression acupuncture research, though less common
than GV20 or GV24. Typically used in multi-point frontal scalp
protocols.

Rhinitis: GV23 is included in standard acupuncture protocols for
allergic rhinitis alongside LI20, LI4, and BL2. No SR/MA
evaluating GV23's individual contribution.

🔴 for all indications (no individual point evidence).

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The forehead at 1 cun above the hairline shows pathology-
dependent sensitization:
- **Nasal congestion/rhinitis:** frontal pressure and headache
  during acute rhinitis may sensitize the V1 supraorbital
  territory. GV23's traditional primary indication for nasal
  obstruction aligns with trigeminoparasympathetic activation
  during nasal inflammation.
- **Frontal sinusitis:** direct frontal sinus inflammation
  produces referred pain and tenderness in the supraorbital
  territory overlying GV23.
- **Frontal headache:** tension-type and migraine headaches
  with frontal predominance may alter palpation findings at
  this location.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: V1 supraorbital nerve → trigeminal nucleus →
nasal mucosa autonomic modulation.**

GV23 is in pure V1 (ophthalmic) territory. The supraorbital
nerve provides afferent input to the trigeminal spinal nucleus
and principal sensory nucleus. Through the trigeminoparasympathetic
reflex (V1 afferent → trigeminal nucleus → superior salivatory
nucleus → pterygopalatine ganglion → nasal mucosa), stimulation
at GV23 can modulate nasal mucosal vasomotor tone, secretion,
and mucociliary function.

This trigeminoparasympathetic pathway is the mechanism underlying
the "nasal-opening" function of forehead acupoints: the V1
afferent directly connects the anterior scalp to the nasal
autonomic control center. The traditional function "opens the
nasal passages" has a direct neuroanatomical basis.

**Ghost Point mechanism:** the frontal lobe underlying GV23 is
the seat of executive function, impulse control, and social
behavior. Prefrontal cortex dysfunction is associated with mania,
disinhibition, and behavioral disturbance — the conditions for
which Ghost Points are prescribed. While the needle does NOT
reach the cortex (separated by skull and meninges), transcranial
galvanic or thermal effects and scalp–cortex autonomic coupling
may provide a pathway.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1 (trigeminal), A02 S2 (autonomic brainstem)

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–0.8 cun transverse/oblique insertion, typically
directed posteriorly along the scalp. DeQi: local distention and
heaviness in the forehead.

Cứu (B08): moxa applicable. Stick moxa over GV23 for chronic
rhinitis and nasal obstruction — 10–15 minutes per session.

Bấm huyệt (B02): easily accessible with fingertip pressure at
the midline above the hairline. Self-applicable for frontal
headache and nasal congestion.

#### An toàn

Low risk. Standard scalp transverse insertion over the intact
adult frontal bone. Same safety profile as other cranial vault
and forehead points.

### GV24 — Thần Đình (神庭, Shéntíng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Spirit Court" (神 = spirit/mind, 庭 = court/courtyard). On the
head, 0.5 cun directly above the midpoint of the anterior
hairline, on the midline. The name places this point at the
entrance to the brain's "palace of spirit" — one of three GV
points containing 神 or 灵 (spirit) in its name (with GV11
Shendao 神道 "Spirit Path" and GV10 Lingtai 灵台 "Spirit Tower").
Located at the frontal scalp at the hairline, GV24 overlies the
prefrontal cortex. Modern clinical significance: **GV20 + GV24
is the MOST COMMONLY USED acupuncture combination for post-stroke
cognitive impairment (PSCI).** Electroacupuncture at GV24
demonstrates specific neuroplasticity effects including PSD-95
upregulation and NLRP3 inflammasome suppression. Meeting point
of Du Mai with BL and ST.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the head, 0.5 cun directly
above the midpoint of the anterior hairline.

Anatomical landmarks: the point is 0.5 cun posterior to the
hairline, on the midline — 0.5 cun BELOW (anterior to) GV23.
In patients with receding hairlines, locate by measuring 4.5 cun
anterior to GV20 on the midline. The point is at the very edge
of the scalp, at the forehead–scalp transition.

Needle depth range: 0.5–0.8 cun (~1.2–2.0 cm) transverse/oblique
insertion, typically directed posteriorly toward GV20.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Supraorbital nerve (V1)** — same territory as GV23. The
  medial branch supplies the paramedian forehead and anterior
  scalp.
- **Supratrochlear nerve (V1)** — the more medial V1 branch,
  supplying the midline forehead. At the hairline level, both
  supraorbital and supratrochlear territories overlap.
- Spinal segment: V1 trigeminal, pure ophthalmic division.

Fascial plane: frontalis muscle at its posterior attachment to
the galea aponeurotica. GV24 at 0.5 cun above the hairline is
more likely within frontalis muscle tissue than GV23 (1 cun),
since the frontalis inserts into the galea at approximately the
hairline level.

Vascular: supratrochlear and supraorbital arteries.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo-Connecting: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
Back-Shu: No. Front-Mu: No. Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No.

**Meeting point (交会穴):** GV24 is a meeting point of the
Governing Vessel with the Bladder channel (Foot Taiyang) and the
Stomach channel (Foot Yangming). Both BL and ST channels have
internal pathways passing through the forehead region — BL2
(Zanzhu) is at the medial eyebrow (~2 cun inferior to GV24),
and ST8 (Touwei) is at the corner of the forehead.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL2, AP-03 ST8

**"Spirit" name significance:** GV24 神庭 "Spirit Court" shares
the 神 character with GV11 神道 "Spirit Path" (T5, Heart level),
while GV10 灵台 "Spirit Tower" uses a different spirit character
(灵). The "Spirit Court" is the most anatomically fitting of
these names — it directly overlies the prefrontal cortex, the
brain region most associated with consciousness, personality,
and executive function. This responds to Architectural Question
#7 from the child plan.
→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV10, GV11

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: GV24 (Shenting 神庭). Insomnia, anxiety, mania, epilepsy,
palpitations with fear, headache, dizziness, rhinitis. The
"Spirit Court" function is calming the spirit and settling the
mind. One of the most important points for emotional disorders
on Du Mai. ✓
Trigger point: frontalis muscle trigger points — the midline
forehead at the hairline corresponds to the superior aspect of
the frontalis. Frontalis trigger points refer pain to the

Marma (≈ Yintang) is ~3 cun inferior. ✗
Head zone: V1 territory — no direct visceral reflex. ✗
Anatomy Trains: frontalis–galea junction, same fascial boundary

Neurogenic: no data. ✗
Cupping: not on scalp. ✗

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: headache, insomnia, palpitations with fear,
mania, epilepsy, fright palpitations, excessive weeping,
rhinorrhea, epistaxis. The name 神庭 directly indicates its
primary domain: disorders of the 神 (spirit/mind).

Clinical combinations:
- **PSCI protocol:** GV20 + GV24 + Sishencong (EX-HN1) + KI3 —
  the standard scalp electroacupuncture protocol for post-stroke
  cognitive impairment.
- **Insomnia:** GV24 + GV20 + Yintang (EX-HN3) + HT7 + SP6 —
  frontal–vertex scalp combination with distal spirit-calming
  points.
- **Anxiety/mania:** GV24 + GV20 + Yintang + PC6 — "calm the

→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV20 (PSCI), AP-05 HT7, AP-04 SP6,
AP-09 PC6

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Post-stroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) — 🟡 Plausible**

GV20+GV24 is the most commonly used combination for PSCI across
multiple systematic reviews. Overview of SRs (Zhou et al. 2023,
Neuropsychiatric Disease and Treatment) confirmed consistent
positive results. SR/MA (Luo et al. 2024, Brain and Behavior,
29 trials): acupuncture for PSCI shows positive effects on MMSE,
MoCA, and P300 latency. GV20+GV24 was the most frequently
selected point combination across all included trials.

EA at GV20+GV24 specifically:
- Upregulates PSD-95 (postsynaptic density protein-95 — essential
  for synaptic plasticity and learning)
- Modulates endogenous melatonin and suppresses ROS/NLRP3
  inflammasome (key mediator of post-stroke neuroinflammation)
- Ameliorates cognitive deficits via Pten/Akt pathway (promoting
  neuronal survival)

**CRITICAL LIMITATION (same as GV20):** 99.92% of stroke
acupuncture RCTs originate from China — extreme geographic bias
affects all PSCI evidence.

Confidence: 🟡 — most-studied point pair for PSCI, with both
clinical (multiple SRs) and mechanistic (animal models with
identified molecular pathways) evidence. Individual contribution
of GV24 cannot be separated from GV20 in clinical trials.

**2. Depression — 🟡 Plausible**

GV24 is among the most frequently used acupoints in depression
studies. SR/MA (electroacupuncture for depression-related
insomnia, 2025, 14 RCTs, N=1,342): EA protocols frequently
include GV24 in combination with GV20. Significant improvement
in both depression and sleep outcomes. Clinical clustering
analysis identified GV24 combined with KI3 (Taixi) and
Sishencong (EX-HN1) as a common combination pattern.

No sole-point GV24 evidence. Confidence: 🟡 — frequently
selected in depression research, consistent inclusion in
effective multi-point protocols.

**3. Insomnia — 🟡 Plausible**

Network meta-analysis (2026, Frontiers in Psychiatry): multiple
acupoint stimulation therapies for primary insomnia. GV24
included in standard protocols. GV24 combined with GV20 and
Sishencong is a common clustering pattern.

Confidence: 🟡 — included in effective protocols. Individual
contribution unknown.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The hairline midline (GV+BL+ST meeting) shows dynamic
properties related to neuropsychiatric conditions:
- **Anxiety/insomnia:** scalp muscle tension (frontalis
  hypertonicity) during anxiety states may increase
  tenderness at the anterior hairline region.
- **Post-stroke cognitive impairment (PSCI):** GV24 is part
  of the GV20+GV24 "cognitive dyad" — the most commonly used
  point combination for PSCI. Whether post-stroke
  neuroinflammation alters local tissue properties at scalp
  points overlying affected cortex is an open question.
- **Electrodermal changes:** scalp acupoints may show altered
  electrical conductance during emotional or cognitive stress.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: V1 → trigeminal nucleus → autonomic modulation
of prefrontal cortex vasculature and frontal neural networks.**

GV24 overlies the prefrontal cortex. The mechanism for PSCI
effects involves:

1. **PSD-95 upregulation:** PSD-95 is essential for synaptic
   plasticity and learning — its upregulation suggests
   acupuncture-induced enhancement of synaptic transmission at
   the damaged cortex.
2. **Melatonin–ROS–NLRP3 axis:** melatonin modulation and NLRP3
   inflammasome suppression reduces post-stroke secondary injury.
3. **Pten/Akt pathway:** promotes neuronal survival in ischemic

**The "cognitive dyad" — GV20+GV24:** this combination provides
synergistic cortical coverage. GV20 (vertex, premotor /
supplementary motor area) + GV24 (forehead, prefrontal cortex) =
anterior–posterior scalp stimulation spanning the cortical
territory most relevant to cognitive function. Both receive V1
afferents; GV20 additionally receives C2 (greater occipital
nerve). Together they bracket the frontal lobe from vertex to
hairline.
→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV20, A01 S1, A02 S2

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–0.8 cun transverse insertion, typically directed
posteriorly toward GV20.

Electroacupuncture (B01): the PRIMARY technique for PSCI. GV20
and GV24 serve as the two poles of the EA circuit. Standard
parameters: 2 Hz continuous wave or 2/100 Hz dense-sparse wave,

Cứu (B08): moxa applicable but not standard in modern cognitive/

Bấm huyệt (B02): easily accessible self-acupressure. Fingertip

#### An toàn

Low risk. Standard scalp transverse insertion over the intact
adult frontal bone. Same safety profile as GV23 and other
forehead/cranial points.

### GV25 — Tố Liêu (素髎, Sùliáo)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"White Bone-Hole" (素 = white/plain, 髎 = bone-hole/foramen).
At the tip of the nose, on the midline. UNIQUE ANATOMY: GV25 is
the only Du Mai point located on cartilage rather than bone —
the nose tip is composed of the lower lateral and alar nasal
cartilages with no underlying bony support. Classical emergency
point for loss of consciousness (secondary to GV26). Traditional
nasal point for obstruction and rhinorrhea. The external nasal
nerve (V1) at GV25 marks the LAST V1-innervated point on Du
Mai before the V1→V2 transition at GV26.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the tip of the nose.

Anatomical landmarks: the apex of the nose. The underlying
structure is the lower lateral cartilages — no bone beneath the
nose tip. GV25 is the transition point where Du Mai leaves the
bony skull and enters the soft tissue structures of the face.

Needle depth range: 0.3–0.5 cun (~0.7–1.2 cm) oblique superiorly
or perpendicular. Limited depth — excessive insertion penetrates
into the nasal cavity.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **External nasal nerve (V1, ophthalmic)** — terminal branch of
  the anterior ethmoidal nerve (from nasociliary nerve →
  ophthalmic division V1). Supplies the skin of the nose tip and
  dorsum of the lower nose. This is the LAST V1 branch before the
  V1→V2 boundary — the next GV point (GV26, philtrum) is
  innervated by V2 (infraorbital nerve).
- Spinal segment: V1 trigeminal.

Fascial plane: nasal cartilage → perichondrium → SMAS
(superficial musculoaponeurotic system) at nose tip. The nasalis
muscle (transverse and alar parts) provides compressor and
dilator functions.

Vascular: lateral nasal artery (from facial artery, external
carotid) and dorsal nasal artery (from ophthalmic artery,
internal carotid). The nose tip is a WATERSHED ZONE between
external carotid and internal carotid vascular territories.

### GV26 — Nhân Trung / Thủy Câu (人中/水沟, Rénzhōng/Shuǐgōu)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**

"Philtrum" / "Water Trough" (人中 = philtrum, literally "middle
of the human" — the point between nose and lip that defines the
human face; 水沟 = water trough, referring to the philtral groove
that channels nasal discharge). On the philtrum, at the junction
of the upper 1/3 and lower 2/3. **THE PREMIER EMERGENCY POINT IN
ALL OF TCM** — the first point applied in acute loss of
consciousness, syncope, epileptic seizure, and cardiopulmonary
collapse. **Ghost Point #1 (鬼宫, Gui Gong, "Ghost Palace")** —
the OPENING point of the entire 13 Ghost Points sequence,
indicating GV26 as the first-line intervention for acute
psychiatric crisis. Meeting point of Du Mai with LI (Hand
Yangming) and ST (Foot Yangming). GV26 sits at the V1→V2 nerve
transition: the infraorbital nerve (V2, maxillary division)
provides afferent input to brainstem arousal centers via the
trigeminal nucleus, providing the neuroanatomical basis for the
consciousness-recovery tradition. SR/MA (2025): 17 studies,
N=1,342 — GCS improvement SMD = 1.12 (95% CI 0.85–1.39,
p<0.001). The EFT tapping sequence independently selects this
same location as point #4 "Under Nose." Dien Chan (Vietnamese
facial reflexology) independently identifies the philtrum as a
therapeutic zone.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the face, at the junction
of the upper one-third and the lower two-thirds of the philtrum.

Anatomical landmarks: the philtrum (vertical groove between nose
and upper lip). The point is at the upper 1/3 – lower 2/3
junction, NOT at the midpoint — placing it closer to the nose
than to the lip. The philtral columns (raised ridges flanking
the groove) frame the point laterally. The philtrum is a visible
facial landmark present from embryonic development (fusion of the
medial nasal prominences with the maxillary prominences).

Needle depth range: 0.3–0.5 cun (~0.7–1.2 cm) oblique superiorly
toward the nasal septum. For emergency stimulation: fingernail
pressure (acupressure) is the standard technique — strong noxious
stimulation with the thumbnail pressing upward into the philtrum.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Infraorbital nerve (V2, maxillary division of trigeminal)** —
  the PRIMARY afferent nerve at GV26. Exits through the
  infraorbital foramen (below the orbit), courses inferiorly to
  supply the philtrum, upper lip, and lateral nose. This is the
  FIRST V2 point on Du Mai — marking the V1→V2 transition.
- **Buccal branch of the facial nerve (CN VII)** — motor nerve
  supplying the orbicularis oris, levator labii superioris, and
  other perioral muscles. GV26 stimulation activates both the
  sensory (V2) and motor (VII) nerves of the philtrum.
- Spinal segment attribution: V2 trigeminal territory.

The V2 infraorbital nerve projects to the spinal nucleus of the
trigeminal nerve in the brainstem. This nucleus connects to:
1. **Reticular formation / ascending reticular activating system
   (ARAS):** the brainstem arousal center for consciousness and
   wakefulness
2. **Rostral ventrolateral medulla (rVLM):** cardiovascular
   control center — GV26 pressor effects are mediated via rVLM
3. **Respiratory center:** infraorbital afferents project to the
   respiratory nucleus, modulating respiratory rate
4. **Dorsal motor nucleus of vagus:** trigeminal → vagal pathway
   enabling parasympathetic modulation

This brainstem connectivity via the trigeminal nucleus is the
anatomical substrate for GV26's emergency arousal function.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1 (trigeminal → parasympathetic), A02 S2
(autonomic brainstem centers)

Fascial plane: orbicularis oris muscle (sphincter muscle
encircling the mouth). The philtrum contains the decussation
(crossing) of fibers from the left and right orbicularis oris.
Layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → orbicularis oris → labial
mucosa.

Vascular: superior labial artery (from facial artery, branch of
external carotid). Well-vascularized — needling may produce
minor bleeding.

Back-Shu: No. Front-Mu: No. Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No.

→ Cross-ref: AP-02 LI19 (same level), LI20 (LI crosses midline),
AP-03 ST1 (ST channel Yangming transition)

**Ghost Point #1 — Gui Gong (鬼宫, "Ghost Palace"):**

BL62(#5) → GV16(#6) → ST6(#7) → CV24(#8) → PC8(#9) →

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Consciousness recovery in ICU — 🟡 Plausible**

SR/MA (2025, Research Square, 17 RCTs, N=1,342): DU-26
stimulation for disorders of consciousness (DOC) in ICU patients.
- **GCS improvement:** SMD = 1.12 (95% CI: 0.85–1.39, p<0.001,
  I²=58%) — a LARGE effect size.
- DU-26 stimulation combined with conventional ICU care
  significantly improved consciousness recovery compared to
  conventional care alone.
- Stimulation methods included manual acupuncture,
  electroacupuncture, and acupressure at GV26.
- Heterogeneity moderate (I²=58%) — consistent direction but
  variable effect magnitude.

This is the largest and most recent SR specifically evaluating
GV26 as an individual point. The large effect size (SMD >1.0) is
notable, though interpretation requires caution due to geographic
bias (all East Asian studies), variable control groups, blinding
challenges, and preprint status.

Confidence: 🟡 — large SR with significant results. Limited by
study quality and geographic bias. Consistent finding across
17 studies strengthens the signal despite individual study
limitations.

**2. Cardiopulmonary cerebral resuscitation — 🟡 Plausible**

SR/MA (Gao et al. 2023, Journal of Integrative Medicine, 7 RCTs,
N=411): acupuncture combined with conventional cardiopulmonary
cerebral resuscitation (CPCR) in patients after return of
spontaneous circulation (ROSC).
- GCS scores significantly higher at days 3, 5, and 7 in
  acupuncture + CPCR vs CPCR alone.
- GV26 was the most commonly used acupoint across included
  studies.
- GRADE certainty: very low.

Confidence: 🟡 — positive direction but very low certainty of
evidence. Small study pool. Supports the consciousness-recovery
signal from SR #1.

**3. Blood pressure / cardiovascular effects — 🟡 Plausible**

Animal studies (multiple): GV26 stimulation increases arterial
blood pressure in hemorrhagic shock and ischemic hypotension.
The pressor effect is mediated through the rostral ventrolateral
medulla (rVLM) — ablation of rVLM abolishes the GV26 pressor
response. GV26 stimulation activates noradrenergic
neurotransmission, reducing the depressive effects of anesthetics
on the central nervous system.

Human evidence: philtrum pressure is used as a standard noxious
stimulus in emergency medicine for consciousness assessment. The
cardiovascular arousal response to philtrum pressure (increased
heart rate and blood pressure) is well documented in emergency
medicine, though not attributed to "acupuncture."

Confidence: 🟡 — strong animal evidence with identified brainstem
mechanism (rVLM). Emergency medicine philtrum pressure testing
provides indirect human validation.

**4. Acute life-threatening bleeding syncope — 🔴 Unvalidated**

First GV26-specific SR (Zhu et al. 2014, Journal of
Acupuncture and Tuina Science): found evidence for efficacy in
acute life-threatening bleeding syncope (ALBS), functional
enuresis, and intractable hiccups. Also noted GV26 effectiveness
in increasing blood pressure and reducing heart rate during
revival. Limited to small studies.

Confidence: 🔴 — mentioned in first SR but evidence quality
insufficient for 🟡.

**5. Acute lumbar sprain — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Classical claim: GV26 as distal point for acute low back pain.
No obvious segmental connection between V2 trigeminal territory
and lumbar spine. Possible explanations: brainstem-mediated
descending pain modulation, attention-redirecting noxious
stimulation, or autonomic reflex. No controlled studies.

Confidence: 🔴 — traditional use only, no evidence, unclear
mechanism.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The philtrum has uniquely high baseline neural density (V2
infraorbital nerve terminal field, mucocutaneous junction)
that distinguishes it from other dynamic acupoint patterns:
- **Inherent high sensitivity:** unlike most acupoints where
  sensitization is pathology-dependent, GV26 maintains
  intrinsically high sensitivity due to dense V2 terminal
  innervation and thin skin over orbicularis oris. This is
  why both TCM and Western emergency medicine independently
  selected this site for consciousness assessment (noxious
  stimulus for GCS).
- **Altered consciousness states:** GV26's primary use is IN
  acute emergency (syncope, coma, seizure) — the point is
  applied TO the unconscious patient rather than becoming
  sensitized BY pathology. This inverts the typical Dynamic
  acupoint model (point sensitization → clinical indication
  → treatment).
- **Perioral sensitization:** trigeminal neuralgia (V2
  branch) can produce hyperalgesia across the philtrum and
  nasolabial fold, potentially increasing GV26 sensitivity.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: V2 infraorbital nerve → trigeminal spinal
nucleus → ascending reticular activating system (ARAS) →
consciousness arousal.**

The mechanism of GV26's emergency function:

1. **Afferent:** strong noxious stimulation at the philtrum
   activates the infraorbital nerve (V2) — a large-diameter nerve
   with high nociceptor density. The philtrum is one of the most
   pain-sensitive areas of the face, reflected in the
   disproportionately large somatosensory cortex representation
   of the perioral area (the sensory "homunculus").

2. **First relay:** V2 afferents project to the spinal nucleus
   of the trigeminal nerve in the brainstem (medulla and upper
   cervical spinal cord).

3. **Arousal pathway:** the trigeminal spinal nucleus has direct
   projections to the reticular formation — the anatomical
   substrate of the ARAS. Noxious trigeminal input activates the
   reticular formation → ARAS → thalamic intralaminar nuclei →
   widespread cortical activation → consciousness.

4. **Cardiovascular pathway:** the trigeminal spinal nucleus
   projects to the rVLM, the primary cardiovascular control
   center. Activation of rVLM → sympathetic vasomotor neurons →
   increased blood pressure and heart rate. Ablation of rVLM
   abolishes the GV26 pressor response (animal studies).

5. **Respiratory pathway:** V2 afferents project to the
   respiratory nucleus. The infraorbital nerve has been identified
   as the specific afferent pathway mediating respiratory
   modulation from GV26 stimulation.

6. **Noradrenergic activation:** electroacupuncture at GV26
   activates noradrenergic neurotransmission. Norepinephrine is
   the primary neurotransmitter of the locus coeruleus — a
   brainstem nucleus projecting widely to the cortex, essential
   for wakefulness and arousal. GV26 → trigeminal → locus
   coeruleus → noradrenergic cortical activation → consciousness
   recovery.

**Why GV26 specifically?** The philtrum is uniquely effective for
arousal because:
- **High nociceptor density:** the perioral region has extremely
  dense sensory innervation
- **Direct brainstem access:** V2 → trigeminal nucleus →
  reticular formation is a SHORT, DIRECT pathway to the ARAS
- **Bilateral activation:** the philtrum is midline —
  stimulation activates BOTH left and right trigeminal nuclei
- **Combined sensory + motor:** simultaneous activation of V2
  (sensory) and CN VII buccal branch (motor) provides dual input

This mechanism explains why philtrum pressure is INDEPENDENTLY
used in both TCM (GV26 acupressure for resuscitation) and Western
emergency medicine (philtrum pressure as GCS noxious stimulus) —
both traditions discovered that strong philtrum stimulation
activates brainstem arousal pathways. The convergent selection of
the same anatomical location for the same clinical purpose across
independent medical traditions is among the strongest convergence
evidence in the entire Atlas.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1 (trigeminal), A02 S2 (autonomic brainstem),
A07 S1 (somatovisceral reflex), C04 S4 (convergent selection)

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

**Emergency acupressure (PRIMARY TECHNIQUE):**
Fingernail pressure at GV26 — press the thumbnail firmly into the
philtral groove, directed upward toward the nasal septum. Apply
STRONG, sustained pressure for 10–30 seconds. Pain IS the
therapeutic mechanism (arousal through nociceptive brainstem
activation). No needle required. Can be applied by bystanders as
first aid.

Châm (B01): 0.3–0.5 cun oblique superiorly toward the nasal
septum. The needle produces strong, sharp pain. Retain for
psychiatric indications (Ghost Point protocol).
Electroacupuncture: continuous wave or dense-sparse 2/100 Hz in
research protocols for consciousness recovery.

Cứu (B08): moxa at GV26 is NOT standard. Philtrum is difficult
to moxa safely (proximity to nose, lips, facial hair). Some
classical texts mention moxa for severe Yang collapse.

Bấm huyệt (B02): GV26 acupressure is the most accessible
emergency technique in the entire acupuncture system. Requires
no equipment, minimal training. Taught in first-aid and martial
arts traditions.

EFT tapping (B12): tapping point #4 "Under Nose" in the standard
EFT sequence — gentle tapping at the philtrum during emotional
processing.
→ Cross-ref: B12

Dien Chan (B05): philtrum zone used in Vietnamese facial
reflexology for lower abdominal and reproductive conditions.
→ Cross-ref: B05

#### An toàn

Low risk. Superficial, well-vascularized site with no deep
dangerous structures.
- Minor bleeding from the superior labial artery — apply pressure
- Pain: GV26 stimulation is INTENTIONALLY noxious for emergency
  use. Pain is the mechanism, not a side effect.
- Depth limited naturally by the nasal septal cartilage
- SR/MA (2025, 17 studies) reported no serious adverse events

**GV26 in emergency context:** GV26 acupressure is an ADJUNCT to
standard emergency care (ACLS/BLS). It does NOT replace
cardiovascular resuscitation, airway management, or
pharmacological intervention. In modern integrated care, apply
GV26 WHILE calling for emergency services, not INSTEAD of them.

### GV28 — Ngân Giao (龈交, Yínjiāo)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Gum Junction" (龈 = gums, 交 = junction/crossing). Inside the
upper lip, at the junction of the upper labial frenulum and the
upper gum (alveolar mucosa). **THE ENDPOINT OF THE GOVERNING
VESSEL** — Du Mai's 28th and final point. **Meeting point of
GV + CV + ST** — the ONLY point in the 361-point system where
the two midline extraordinary vessels (Du Mai and Ren Mai)
directly connect. This junction completes the "Small Heavenly
Circuit" (小周天, Xiǎo Zhōutiān): GV ascending posterior
(coccyx → spine → head) meets CV ascending anterior (perineum →
abdomen → chest → chin → internal pathway → upper gum), creating
a continuous midline orbit.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): inside the upper lip, at the
junction of the frenulum of the upper lip and the upper gum.

Anatomical landmarks: the upper labial frenulum is a midline
fold of oral mucosa connecting the inner surface of the upper
lip to the alveolar mucosa between the central incisors. GV28 is
where this frenulum meets the alveolar ridge — an INTRAORAL
point, requiring the lip to be raised to access it. One of the
few intraoral points in the standard 361-point system.

Needle depth range: 0.2–0.3 cun (~0.5–0.7 cm) oblique superiorly
along the frenulum. Extremely shallow — the frenulum is a thin
mucosal fold.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Superior anterior alveolar nerve (V2)** — branch of the
  infraorbital nerve supplying the upper incisors, gum, and
  labial frenulum. Continuous V2 maxillary territory.
- **Nasopalatine nerve (V2)** — supplies the anterior hard palate
  and gum posterior to the incisors. May contribute to GV28
  innervation at the frenulum–alveolar junction.
- Spinal segment: V2 trigeminal.

Fascial plane: oral mucosa → submucosal connective tissue →
alveolar periosteum. No muscle layer at the frenulum — a mucosal
fold with minimal tissue depth.

Vascular: superior labial artery branches. The incisive artery
(from greater palatine) provides additional supply through the
incisive foramen.

**Meeting point (交会穴) — TRIPLE MIDLINE MEETING:**
GV28 is the meeting point of THREE channels:
1. **Du Mai (GV)** — GV28 is the TERMINUS. The channel ascends
   from GV1 (coccyx), travels the entire posterior midline, passes
   over the head, descends over forehead and nose, and ENDS at the
   upper labial frenulum.
2. **Ren Mai (CV)** — runs the anterior midline from CV1
   (perineum) upward to CV24 (chin). Its internal pathway
   continues upward to meet GV at the upper gum.
3. **Stomach channel (ST)** — the ST internal pathway reaches the
   upper gum.

**Small Heavenly Circuit (小周天):**
The GV–CV meeting at GV28 completes the posterior–anterior midline
circuit:
- GV: ascending posterior — GV1 (coccyx) → spine → head → nose
  → GV28 (upper gum)
- CV: ascending anterior — CV1 (perineum) → abdomen → chest →
  throat → CV24 (chin) → internal → GV28

In Qigong and Daoist meditation, the practitioner connects GV and
CV by placing the tongue tip against the upper palate (near
GV28), closing the circuit. The tongue bridge is called "building
the magpie bridge" (搭鹊桥, dā quèqiáo). This makes GV28 the
critical juncture point for internal energy cultivation.

#### An toàn

Low risk — INTRAORAL point.
- Minor bleeding from the rich mucosal vasculature
- Discomfort (the frenulum is sensitive)
- Infection risk: oral cavity has normal flora — disposable
  sterile needle mandatory, standard aseptic technique for oral
  needling
- Contraindicated in active oral infection, stomatitis, or
  periodontal abscess at the frenulum
- The frenulum varies in size — some individuals have a short or
  minimal frenulum (after frenectomy or naturally), making GV28
  difficult or impossible to access
