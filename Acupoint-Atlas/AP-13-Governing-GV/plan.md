## 1. Meridian Profile

**Trajectory:** GV1 (coccyx tip) → GV2 (sacral hiatus) → GV3–GV5 (L4→L1, lumbar spine) → GV6–GV8 (T11→T9, lower thoracic) → GV9 (T7) → GV10–GV13 (T6→T1, upper thoracic) → GV14 (C7, "Great Vertebra" — meeting of all Yang) → GV15 (C1–C2 junction) → GV16 (below external occipital protuberance) → GV17 (above external occipital protuberance) → GV18–GV19 (posterior cranium) → GV20 (vertex) → GV21–GV22 (anterior cranium) → GV23–GV24 (forehead at hairline) → GV25 (nose tip) → GV26 (philtrum) → GV27 (upper lip) → GV28 (inside upper gum).

**Unique features:**
- **ONLY posterior midline meridian** — ALL 28 points are on the midline (midsagittal plane). No other meridian is exclusively midline. The spinous processes of every vertebra from coccyx to C7 are GV point landmarks.
- **"Sea of all Yang Meridians" (阳脉之海)** — All 6 Yang channels (LI, ST, SI, BL, TE, GB) converge at GV14 (Dazhui, C7). GV20 is the meeting of GV with BL, GB, TE, and LR. Classical tradition: GV governs all Yang Qi in the body.
- **3 Ghost Points — most of any meridian.** GV26 (#1 Gui Gong), GV16 (#6 Gui Zhen), GV23 (#10 Gui Tang). No other meridian has more than 2 Ghost Points. GV has the OPENING point of the entire Ghost Point sequence (GV26 = #1).
- **Window of Heaven (GV16 Fengfu)** — Completes the posterior midline position in the cervical Window of Heaven ring. Previously documented: LI18, ST9, SI16, SI17, TE16, BL10, LU3 (AP-01/02/03/06/07/10). Remaining: GV16, CV22, PC1.
- **Sea of Marrow (髓海)** — GV16, GV17, GV20 access the "Sea of Marrow" (brain). TCM "Marrow" = brain/spinal cord — these points ARE anatomically close to or over the brain.
- **Eight Confluent pair: SI3 (Houxi) + BL62 (Shenmai)** — opens Du Mai. Both points already documented (AP-06, AP-07). SI3 shows thermal effects at posterior neck similar to GV14 (AP-06 L1031).
- **No Five Shu system** — Du Mai is an extraordinary vessel, not a principal meridian. Five Shu (Well-Spring-Stream-River-Sea) applies only to the 12 regular meridians.
- **No Chinese clock assignment** — extraordinary vessels are not part of the 24-hour circadian cycle.
- **Luo-Connecting: GV1 (Changqiang)** — one of the 15 Luo-Connecting points (12 regular + GV + CV + Great Luo of Spleen). GV Luo ascends from coccyx tip alongside the spine, dispersing to the head. This is the 13th Luo in the Atlas (after 12 regular meridian Luo).
- **Moxa prohibition:** NONE at GV points. However, GV15 and GV16 have DEEP NEEDLE prohibitions (danger of brainstem/medulla puncture).
- **Cross-channel meetings (documented):** GV meets BL at BL1, BL11, BL12 (AP-07). GV meets LR internal pathway at vertex (AP-12 L28). GV14 meets all 6 Yang meridians.

**Element:** N/A (extraordinary vessel has no Five Element assignment).

**TCM role:** "Du" (督) = "to govern." Du Mai governs all Yang of the body. Runs through the spine and enters the brain — connects "Kidney Essence" to "Sea of Marrow" (brain). Functions: governs Yang Qi, nourishes brain and spinal cord, strengthens the back. Pathology: stiffness of the spine, opisthotonus, headache, mental disorders, prolapse from Yang deficiency. Du Mai is accessed clinically via SI3+BL62, or directly via GV points along the spine and head.

## 6. Architectural Questions to Monitor

1. **MIDLINE vs PARAVERTEBRAL:** Each spinal GV point (GV2–GV14) has a corresponding BL Back-Shu point 1.5 cun lateral at the same vertebral level. GV accesses supraspinous/interspinous ligament (midline deep fascia); BL accesses erector spinae muscle (paravertebral). Different tissue type → different mechanism? Different clinical indications despite same segmental level? This is the GV-specific version of the "two-line parallel" pattern.

2. **THREE GHOST POINTS = CRANIAL-FACIAL AXIS:** GV has Ghost Points at occiput (GV16), forehead (GV23), and philtrum (GV26). This spans the entire head from posterior to anterior. Does this reflect traditional recognition that the posterior midline has privileged access to the brain? Compare with GV20's cranial nerve architecture (Framework note 2).

3. **GV14 = IMMUNOLOGICAL CROSSROADS?** Meeting of ALL Yang at C7. Al-Kahil (Sunnah cupping) independently selects this same location (B07, C04). Is there an anatomical basis for immunomodulation at the cervicothoracic junction? Thymus proximity (T1–T3), cervical lymph drainage convergence, sympathetic trunk stellate ganglion (C7–T1)?

4. **SAFETY GRADIENT (GV15–GV16):** These points are at the MOST DANGEROUS zone in the entire 361-point system. GV15 at C1–C2 (atlantoaxial junction) and GV16 below external occipital protuberance — needles directed medially can enter the cisterna magna and contact the medulla oblongata. Document maximum safe depths, angle restrictions, and historical accident reports.

5. **MINGMEN (GV4) = ADRENAL GATE?** GV4 at L2 is at the exact level of the renal arteries, adrenal glands, and lumbar splanchnic nerves. TCM "Kidney Yang" = functional system including adrenal function. Does GV4 moxa → paravertebral warming → sympathetic activation → adrenal stimulation provide a mechanism for "warming Kidney Yang"?

6. **CRANIAL NERVE GRADIENT:** GV20 established V1+C2 dual innervation at the vertex. As we move along the GV trajectory from GV17 (occiput) through GV28 (gum), the innervation shifts from C2 → V1 → V2 → superior alveolar. Is there a smooth cranial nerve gradient along the scalp-to-face trajectory?

7. **"SPIRIT" NAMES ON GV:** Multiple GV points contain 神 (spirit) or 灵 (spirit/soul) in their names: GV10 靈臺 (Spirit Tower), GV11 神道 (Spirit Path), GV24 神庭 (Spirit Court). Plus Sea of Marrow = brain access. Does the concentration of spirit-related names on GV reflect a traditional association between the posterior midline and mental/consciousness function? Compare with KI23–25 Spirit names (AP-08).

8. **GV ENDPOINT MEETING (GV28):** GV28 is where Du Mai meets Ren Mai and Stomach channel inside the upper gum. This is the ONLY point where the two midline extraordinary vessels connect. What's the clinical significance? Does it complete a circuit (GV ascending posterior + CV descending anterior = "small heavenly circuit" 小周天)?

9. **CUPPING AT GV14:** GV14 is one of the most commonly cupped points — both in TCM (for Wind-Cold/Heat) and in Sunnah tradition (Al-Kahil). Unlike most cupping points, GV14 is on a bony prominence (C7 spinous process) rather than muscular tissue. How does cupping mechanism differ on bony vs muscular sites?

10. **GV1 PERINEAL PARADOX:** GV1 (Changqiang) is at the coccyx tip — perineal, associated with hemorrhoids and rectal prolapse. Yet it's the Luo-Connecting point of the "Yang Governor." The Luo vessel ascends from the lowest point to the highest (head). Longest Luo pathway in the body? Compare with CV Luo (CV15, ascending from epigastric to chin).

## 10. Special Considerations

1. **Safety zone GV15–GV16:** These two points require the MOST detailed safety documentation in the entire Atlas. Needle directed medially at GV15 or GV16 can penetrate the cisterna magna (subarachnoid space between cerebellum and medulla). Fatal complications are documented in historical literature. Maximum depth, angle restrictions, and contraindications must be prominent.

2. **GV14 cupping convention:** GV14 is the PRIMARY cupping site in both TCM and Sunnah traditions. Unlike most cupping points, it sits on a bony prominence (C7 spinous process). Document cupping-specific technique and the Al-Kahil cross-tradition correspondence.

3. **Trigger point mapping along spine:** Dorsher's trigger point correspondence includes paraspinal trigger points. For GV midline points, the relevant trigger points are in the supraspinous/interspinous ligaments and deep paraspinal muscles. These may map differently than the superficial erector spinae trigger points corresponding to BL Back-Shu points.

4. **GV20 already done — integrate, don't repeat:** GV20 is 635 lines of Phase 1 Tier A entry. During execution, skip GV20 in Batch 5. The Phase 2 review will verify consistency with Phase 2 conventions.

5. **Heading level convention:** GV20 currently uses `## GV20` heading (level 2). Phase 2 entries can use the same level OR follow the BL convention of `##` region headers with `###` individual points. Decision: since GV has natural region groupings (sacral, lumbar, thoracic, cervical, cranial, facial), using `##` for regions and `###` for points is appropriate for the spinal column section. GV20 (currently `##`) will remain as-is since it's a Phase 1 entry.

6. **Moxa prohibition check:** Classical texts prohibit moxa at GV6 (Jizhong, some texts) and GV17 (Naohu, traditional). Verify during execution and note which source(s) specify the prohibition. Compare with TE23 (AP-10) and GB42 (AP-11) moxa prohibitions already documented.
