## 1. Category Profile

**Definition:** Extra points (经外奇穴, literally "extraordinary points outside the channels") are acupoints NOT located on any of the 14 main meridians (12 regular + GV + CV) but recognized for clinical effectiveness through accumulated clinical experience. WHO-WPRO Part 2 (1991) standardized 48 extra point entries with the EX- prefix.

**Unique features:**
- **NO meridian affiliation** — organized by anatomical region, not by channel
- **NO Five Shu system** — no Well/Spring/Stream/River/Sea classification
- **NO Yuan-Source, Luo-Connecting, Xi-Cleft, Eight Confluent** relationships
- **NO Chinese clock** assignment
- **Clinical-empirical origin** — most arose from clinical experience documenting effective non-channel sites; some originated as classical "odd points" (奇穴) in texts like Qianjin Yaofang (652 CE) and later formalized
- **Multi-point groups** — several entries represent groups of points with shared function: Jiaji (34 individual points), Baxie (8), Bafeng (8), Sifeng (4), Shixuan (10), Sishencong (4), Qiduan (10)
- **Anatomical location criterion** — WHO requires clear anatomical location ≥0.5 cm from any classical meridian point (B01 L507-511)
- **Ghost Point #13 EX-HN11 Haiquan** — the LAST Sun Si-miao Ghost Point. Completes the 13/13 Ghost Point inventory in the Atlas.
- **Phase 1 entry: Yintang (EX-HN3)** — already documented (568 lines, Tier A, 2 ⚡ framework notes)

**5 anatomical regions:**
| Region | Code | Count | Body area |
|--------|------|-------|-----------|
| Head & Neck | EX-HN | 15 | Cranium, face, oral, cervical |
| Back | EX-B | 8 | Paravertebral, lumbar, sacral |
| Chest & Abdomen | EX-CA | 1 | Lower abdomen |
| Upper Extremity | EX-UE | 11 | Arm, hand, fingers |
| Lower Extremity | EX-LE | 12 | Hip, knee, ankle, foot, toes |
| **Total** | | **47** | All WHO-WPRO EX codes (Yintang = EX-HN3, already counted in HN 15) |

**Contrast with meridian points:**
- Meridian points: channel theory → functional attribution based on meridian relationships
- Extra points: empirical observation → functional attribution based on anatomical location + clinical experience
- Extra points demonstrate that LOCATION (anatomy) matters more than CHANNEL (theoretical pathway) — consistent with BSA's fundamental asymmetry finding (A09 §3.1: points 🟢 strong evidence, pathways 🟡 weak evidence)

## 5. Architectural Questions to Monitor

1. **JIAJI vs BACK-SHU vs OUTER BL: Three-Position Dorsal Ramus Gradient.** ~~[CORRECTED in EX-B2 entry]~~ Jiaji at 0.5 cun lateral → dorsal ramus trunk / proximal medial branch. Back-Shu at 1.5 cun → medial branch of dorsal ramus (further along course). Outer BL at 3.0 cun → lateral branch of dorsal ramus. All three access the SAME spinal segment but at different positions along the dorsal ramus branching pattern. The medial branch (0.5 + 1.5 cun) targets facet joints + deep paraspinal muscles (multifidus, rotatores), closest to the sympathetic chain. The lateral branch (3.0 cun) innervates the more superficial erector spinae (longissimus, iliocostalis). AP-07 documented the 1.5 vs 3.0 cun pairing; EX-B2 adds the 0.5 cun position to complete the three-line gradient. (Note: original text incorrectly stated Back-Shu = lateral branch; BL file confirms Back-Shu = medial branch.)

2. **DIAGNOSTIC POINTS = PURE DYNAMIC ACUPOINTS.** Dannangxue and Lanweixue have NO therapeutic function in the traditional sense — their value is DIAGNOSTIC (tenderness = organ pathology). This is the purest expression of the Dynamic Acupoint concept (C04 §4): these points literally do not exist as clinically relevant sites until pathology creates them.

3. **GHOST POINT CLOSURE.** EX-HN11 Haiquan is the only Ghost Point NOT on any of the 14 meridians. Its sublingual location is the MOST INTERIOR of all 13 Ghost Points. Does the sequence progress from exterior (#1 GV26 philtrum, exposed skin) to interior (#13 sublingual, hidden under tongue)?

4. **HAND-FOOT SYMMETRY.** Baxie (hand) ↔ Bafeng (foot). Shixuan (fingertips) ↔ Qiduan (toe tips). Extra points recognize hand-foot homology more explicitly than the meridian system (where hand-foot pairings are channel-based, not anatomically direct).

5. **PEDIATRIC-SPECIFIC POINT.** Sifeng is arguably the only acupoint in the 361+48 system with a primary pediatric-specific indication (childhood malnutrition/Gan Ji). The pricking+expression technique is unique.

6. **KNEE EVIDENCE DOMINANCE.** Xiyan pair + Heding + surrounding meridian points (ST34-36, SP9-10, GB34) create the densest evidence cluster of ANY body region in acupuncture research. Knee OA = acupuncture's headline indication. The extra points (Xiyan, Heding) are CORE to this evidence — not peripheral additions.
