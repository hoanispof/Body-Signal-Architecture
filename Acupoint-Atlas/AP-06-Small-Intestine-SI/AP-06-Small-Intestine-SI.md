# AP-06 — Small Intestine Meridian (SI) — Hand Taiyang (手太阳小肠经)

> **Status:** Phase 2 **COMPLETE**. 19/19 points. Review done 2026-08-15.
> 1 Tier A, 10 Tier B, 8 Tier C planned. 19 points total.
>
> **Meridian overview:** The Small Intestine channel (Hand Taiyang)
> runs from the ulnar side of the little fingertip (SI1) → ulnar hand
> → wrist → posterior forearm (ulnar side) → posterior upper arm →
> **scapular zigzag** (SI9–SI15: 7 points on/around scapula — most
> points on a single skeletal structure of any channel) → neck → cheek
> → pre-auricular ear (SI19). 19 points total. Interior-exterior
> paired with Heart (Hand Shaoyin, AP-05 COMPLETE — first fully
> documented interior-exterior pair). Same-name Taiyang pairing with
> Bladder (Foot Taiyang). Peak time in Chinese clock: 13:00–15:00.

## SI1 — Thiếu Trạch (少泽, Shào Zé)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Lesser Marsh" (少泽) — 少 (Shào, lesser/young) identifies this as a
Shaoyin-paired channel point, and 泽 (Zé, marsh/pool) evokes the
Well-point image of Qi emerging. Jing-Well (**Metal**) point of the
Small Intestine channel. Metal on a **Fire** channel = **Ke-cycle subject**
(Fire controls Metal, 火克金) — the point element is dominated by the
channel element. This is the SECOND YANG Jing-Well in the Atlas
(after LI1). All Yang Well points are Metal (vs Wood for Yin).

SI1's primary clinical identity is unique among Jing-Well points:
**CLASSICAL LACTATION POINT** (催乳要穴). The most frequently cited
acupoint for postpartum insufficient lactation. Also part of the
12 Jing-Well emergency bloodletting set (十二井穴放血).

Anatomical mirror: SI1 and HT9 occupy OPPOSITE SIDES of the same
little fingernail — SI1 at the ulnar corner, HT9 at the radial
corner. Distance: ~3–5 mm (width of the nail bed). This is the
closest anatomical proximity of any documented interior-exterior
pair in the Atlas.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the ulnar side of the distal
phalanx of the little finger, at the junction of lines drawn along
the ulnar border of the nail and the base of the nail, approximately
0.1 cun from the ulnar corner of the nail.

Anatomical landmarks: ulnar nail corner of the little finger — the
starting point of the SI channel trajectory. Mirror arrangement with
HT9 (radial nail corner of the same finger): the interior-exterior
pair brackets the 5th fingernail from both sides.

Previously documented Jing-Well locations:
- SP1: medial nail corner, big toe
- LR1: lateral nail corner, big toe
- HT9: radial nail corner, little finger
- PC9: middle fingertip
- LU11: radial nail corner, thumb
- LI1: radial nail corner, index finger
SI1 adds: ulnar nail corner, little finger.

Needle depth: 1–2 mm shallow puncture or prick to bleed. Standard
Jing-Well technique.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Proper palmar digital nerve of the little finger (ulnar nerve,
  C8-T1) — wraps around the ulnar side of the fingertip,
  providing extremely dense sensory innervation at the nail fold
- Dorsal digital nerve from dorsal branch of ulnar nerve (C8-T1)
  — contributes to dorsal/ulnar nail corner sensation
- Spinal segments: C8-T1 (ulnar nerve exclusive — unlike LI1
  which has dual radial+median supply)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Same nerve as HT9:** Both SI1 and HT9 are innervated by the
ulnar nerve (C8-T1). Same spinal segments, same nerve trunk,
essentially the same tissue bed (~3–5 mm apart). Different channels,
different element assignments (SI1 = Metal, HT9 = Wood), different
Five Element roles (Ke Subject vs Tonification).
→ Cross-ref: AP-05 HT9

Vascular: proper palmar digital artery (ulnar side, from ulnar
artery via superficial palmar arch). Rich subungual capillary plexus
at nail bed — anatomical basis for bloodletting technique.

Tissue: thin skin tightly adherent to underlying tissue, minimal
subcutaneous fat, no muscle belly. Periosteum of distal phalanx is
the floor. Meissner and Merkel corpuscle density extremely high at
fingertip — one of the highest mechanoreceptor densities in the body.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Jing-Well (Metal)** — 1st of Five Shu on the Small
Intestine channel. Second Yang Jing-Well in the Atlas (after LI1).

**Metal on Fire channel = Ke-cycle subject** — Fire controls Metal in the
Ke (controlling) cycle (火克金). The channel element dominates the
point element. The "Ke Subject" role means this point represents the
element that Fire subjugates.

**Ke Subject across documented channels:**
- SI1: Well/Metal on Fire. Fire controls Metal → Ke Subject.
- LI3: Stream/Wood on Metal. Metal controls Wood → Ke Subject.
- SP1: Well/Wood on Earth. Wood controls Earth → Ke Controller
  (OPPOSITE — Wood controls Earth, not controlled BY Earth).

Note: Ke Subject ≠ Sedation. Sedation = "child" element (generated
by channel). Ke Subject = "controlled" element (dominated by
channel). SI1 (Ke Subject) vs SI8 (Sedation/Earth — Fire generates
Earth) carry distinct theoretical functions.

**Paired channel Well point comparison:**
- HT9 (Yin Fire): Well/**Wood** → Tonification (mother of Fire)
- SI1 (Yang Fire): Well/**Metal** → Ke Subject (controlled by Fire)
Same channel element (Fire), same Shu position (Well), DIFFERENT
point elements, DIFFERENT Five Element roles — because Yin and Yang
assign different element sequences to the Well position.

Yuan-Source: No (SI4)
Luo-Connecting: No (SI7)
Xi-Cleft: No (SI6)
Special: part of the 12 Jing-Well emergency bloodletting set
(十二井穴放血). Nan Jing Ch.68 — "Well points treat fullness below
the Heart" (井主心下满). Also: primary lactation point in TCM
(催乳要穴).

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI1 Shaoze (少泽). Jing-Well/Metal of Small
Intestine. Functions: promotes lactation (催乳), clears heat,
benefits the eyes, resuscitates. The lactation function is
channel-specific — not shared with other Jing-Well points. Classical
formula: SI1 + CV17 (Danzhong/Shanzhong) + ST18 (Rugen) for
insufficient lactation. SI1 as the DISTAL point in this protocol
follows the "distant point + local point" prescribing principle.

**2. Trigger point:** No established trigger point at digit tip — no
skeletal muscle present. Consistent with all documented Jing-Well
points (SP1, LR1, HT9, PC9, LU11, LI1).

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in little finger trauma, subungual
hematoma, paronychia. Not commonly tender in systemic pain
conditions. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No Marma at little fingertip. Nearest: Kshipra
(web space between thumb and index finger = LI4 territory, not
little finger).

**5. Head zone:** C8-T1 dermatome. The T1 component is significant:
T1 is the UPPER boundary of the thoracic sympathetic chain territory.
For the lactation mechanism, the question is whether T1 input can
reach T4 (mammary innervation) — see Mechanism section.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Back Arm Line (DBAL) — ulnar nerve
pathway runs along the medial arm, but the digit tip is beyond
meaningful myofascial station.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data for hand digit tips
(Kim 2017 studied trunk primarily).

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable (digit).

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi partial). Consistent with all
documented Jing-Well points: convergence score 1–2/8 is a structural
feature of digit-tip points (absent muscle bulk, no cupping possible,
Marma systems map to larger zones).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Lingshu Chapter 1 (Ben Shu): Jing-Well of the Small Intestine.
  "Where Qi emerges is called Well" (所出为井).
- Nan Jing Ch.68: "Well points treat fullness below the Heart"
  (井主心下满) — shared across all 12 Jing-Well points.
- Jiayi Jing (Huangfu Mi, 259 CE): insufficient lactation (乳汁不下),
  headache, sore throat, eye disorders (目生翳), febrile disease,
  loss of consciousness.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: insufficient lactation (产后无乳), headache with
  neck stiffness, sore throat, eye pain, tinnitus, febrile disease.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Promotes lactation (PRIMARY):** insufficient milk supply
  (产后乳少), breast distension without lactation (乳汁不通)
- Clears heat: sore throat, febrile disease, mouth ulcers
- Benefits the eyes: visual disorders, corneal opacity
- Resuscitates: loss of consciousness (Jing-Well emergency)
- Activates channel: headache, stiff neck, finger numbness

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- SI1 is the MOST SPECIFIC single point for lactation in TCM — more
  specific than CV17 (which is general chest/mammary) or ST18 (which
  is local mammary). The classical triad: SI1 (distal) + CV17
  (chest center) + ST18 (local mammary).
- Also combined with SP6 (Blood nourishment) and ST36 (Qi
  tonification) in Qi-Blood deficiency lactation protocols.
- Part of 12 Jing-Well emergency bloodletting — but individually
  selected for lactation (NOT only as part of collective protocol).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Postpartum insufficient lactation — 🟡 Plausible**

Multi-center RCT (Zhongguo Zhen Jiu, 2007): 276 postpartum women
randomized to EA at SI1 (treatment) vs EA at LI1 (active control).
Treatment group: 97.8% cured/markedly effective rate vs 24.3% in
control group. Significant improvement in mammary filling, milk
volume, and serum prolactin level. Single-blind. The LI1 active
control is notable: same stimulation type (EA at Jing-Well), different
channel — testing POINT SPECIFICITY, not just needle effect.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.4 (point specificity)

RCT (Zhen Ci Yan Jiu, 2020): 62 women with postpartum hypogalactia.
EA at SI1 improved breast milk volume AND nutrient composition
(lactose, fat, protein content), breast milk energy density — all
significantly higher than control group after 3 treatment courses.

Systematic review and meta-analysis (Medicine, 2024): 19 RCTs,
2,400 participants. Acupuncture/acupoint stimulation significantly
improved milk production volume (MD 81.30 mL, 95% CI 58.94–103.67)
and serum prolactin levels (MD 41.90 IU/L, 95% CI 28.57–55.22).
SI1 identified as one of the most commonly included and effective
points across studies.

Additional SR (Heliyon, 2024): 24 RCTs, 3,214 participants. TCM-
assisted interventions (including acupuncture at SI1) significantly
improved lactation outcomes.

Rating rationale: 🟡 Plausible (not 🟢) because — (a) most RCTs
are single-center Chinese studies with limited blinding, (b) the
multi-center RCT used an active acupoint control (LI1), not sham,
so placebo effect not fully excluded, (c) SI1 is rarely isolated
as sole point — most protocols combine with CV17, ST18, ST36.
However, the point-specificity evidence (SI1 vs LI1) and the
consistent prolactin data make this the STRONGEST EVIDENCE for any
Jing-Well point in the Atlas.

**2. Emergency consciousness restoration (12 Jing-Well) — 🔴
Unvalidated for SI1 individually**

Same as LI1, HT9, and all Jing-Well points: studied as a CLASS
intervention (12-point bloodletting for acute stroke), not
individually. SI1's specific contribution cannot be isolated.

**3. Eye disorders / sore throat — 🔴 Unvalidated**

No study isolates SI1 for these traditional indications.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No specific sensitization data at SI1. The subungual region has
extremely high baseline receptor density (Meissner and Merkel
corpuscles in fingertip pulp). Whether pathology-dependent dynamic
change occurs at SI1 specifically (e.g., increased sensitivity in
postpartum women with lactation difficulty) is unstudied — but would
be a testable prediction of the Dynamic Acupoint model.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Lactation mechanism (primary):**

SI1 is at C8-T1. Mammary glands are innervated by T4 intercostal
nerve (anterior cutaneous branch). The 3-segment gap (T1→T4) makes
a direct spinal segmental reflex unlikely as the sole mechanism.

The consistent prolactin elevation in RCT data (serum prolactin
increase with EA at SI1) strongly suggests a **supraspinal
hypothalamic pathway**: C8-T1 afferent input → ascending
spinothalamic/spinoreticular tracts → hypothalamus → modulation of
dopamine (prolactin-inhibiting factor) release → increased anterior
pituitary prolactin secretion → mammary gland milk production. This
is a HORMONAL mechanism, not a segmental reflex — fundamentally
different from most acupoint mechanisms documented in the Atlas
(which are primarily spinal or brainstem level).

⚡ Framework note: SI1→lactation via hypothalamic-pituitary prolactin
pathway represents a mechanism class not captured in current BSA
Track A analysis. A01–A09 focus on peripheral nerve → spinal cord
→ brainstem pathways. The hypothalamic-pituitary-end organ axis
(afferent → hypothalamus → hormone → distant organ) may constitute
a distinct mechanism track. Compare: EA at ST36 → vagal → gastric
motility (A07 visceral reflex = spinal/brainstem). SI1 → prolactin
→ mammary (= hypothalamic/hormonal = ?new category). First evidence
in the Atlas for a purely hormonal acupoint mechanism.

**Jing-Well emergency function:**

Same mechanism as documented for LI1 and HT9: A-delta/C fiber
activation from nail fold → ascending nociceptive signal → reticular
activating system arousal. Non-specific alerting function shared
across all Well points.

Gap: the lactation-specific function of SI1 (vs other Jing-Well
points) lacks mechanistic explanation for WHY this particular
fingertip activates the hypothalamic-prolactin axis more effectively
than others (as the SI1-vs-LI1 RCT demonstrates). The channel
concept offers "SI channel connects to breast," but anatomically
both SI1 and LI1 have similar C-fiber afferents ascending to
brainstem/hypothalamus. The specificity mechanism is unknown.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Bloodletting: Jing-Well primary technique. Lancet prick → squeeze
3–10 drops blood from nail fold capillaries. Emergency use: loss of
consciousness, high fever.

Acupuncture: 1–2 mm shallow perpendicular insertion. De Qi: mild
tingling/pricking at fingertip.

Electroacupuncture: the evidence for lactation uses EA at SI1
(typically low frequency, 2–4 Hz). EA may be more effective than
manual acupuncture for the prolactin-stimulating effect — plausible
via 2 Hz → enkephalin pathway (Han) → hypothalamic modulation.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2

Moxa: direct moxa (small cones) at SI1 for lactation is classically
documented for deficiency-type (cold-type) insufficient lactation.
Moxa adds heat — used when the pattern diagnosis is Qi/Yang
deficiency rather than Qi stagnation.

### An toàn

LOW risk. Fingertip — no major vessels or nerves at depth.

Precautions:
- Avoid periosteal contact (sharp pain)
- Nail fold proximity increases infection risk
- Sterile technique essential for bloodletting
- Caution in diabetes/peripheral vascular disease (poor wound
  healing at extremities)
- No pregnancy contraindication at SI1 (unlike LI4, SP6)

## SI2 — Tiền Cốc (前谷, Qián Gǔ)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Front Valley" (前谷) — 前 (Qián, front/anterior) + 谷 (Gǔ,
valley/gorge). The "valley" refers to the depression at the finger
joint, "front" indicates it is distal to SI3 (Houxi/"Back Stream").
Ying-Spring (**Water**) point of the Small Intestine channel. In the
Yang Five Shu sequence, Water occupies the Spring position (same as
LI2 on the other Yang channel). Water on a **Fire** channel =
**Ke-cycle controller** (Water controls Fire, 水克火) — the point element
dominates the channel element. Located on the ulnar side of the 5th
metacarpophalangeal joint, at the junction of the red and white skin
(赤白肉际).

Dual logic: Ke-cycle controller (Five Element role: Water extinguishes
Fire) + HEAT CLEARING (positional function: "Spring points treat
heat in the body," 荥主身热, Nan Jing Ch.68). Both roles converge on
clearing excess heat from the SI/Fire channel.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the ulnar side of the little
finger, in the depression distal to the 5th metacarpophalangeal
joint, at the junction of the red and white skin (赤白肉际).

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the ulnar condyle of the proximal
phalanx base at the 5th MCP joint. The point is in the depression
just distal to the joint, on the ulnar side where thick palmar
(red/glabrous) skin meets thinner dorsal (white/hairy) skin. SI1
is ~2 cm distal (fingertip). SI3 is on the opposite side of the
MCP joint (proximal to metacarpal head).

Red-white skin junction (赤白肉际): a recurring landmark in
acupuncture — LI2 (index finger, radial), LU10 (thenar eminence),
SP1-SP3 (medial foot). This transition zone has distinct
mechanoreceptor profiles across the boundary.

Needle depth: 5–8 mm perpendicular or oblique. Limited by
periosteum of proximal phalanx.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Proper palmar digital nerve of the little finger (ulnar nerve,
  C8-T1) — sensory on palmar/ulnar aspect
- Dorsal digital nerve from dorsal branch of ulnar nerve (C8-T1)
  — sensory on dorsal/ulnar aspect
- At the red-white skin junction: single nerve source (ulnar)
  with distinct terminal branches on palmar vs dorsal sides
- Spinal segments: C8-T1 (ulnar nerve exclusive)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: thin subcutaneous tissue over periosteum. At the
red-white skin junction — transition from glabrous palmar skin
(Meissner's + Pacinian corpuscles) to hairy dorsal skin (free
nerve endings). Distinct mechanoreceptor profile at boundary.

Vascular: proper palmar digital artery (ulnar side). Small caliber.
Low vascular risk.

Muscle/tissue: no significant muscle belly at this level.
Abductor digiti minimi does not extend this far distally. Thin skin
over joint capsule, flexor tendon sheath of flexor digitorum
profundus/superficialis, periosteum.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Ying-Spring (Water)** — 2nd of Five Shu on the Small
Intestine channel.

**Water on Fire channel = Ke-cycle controller** — Water controls Fire in
the Ke (controlling) cycle (水克火). The point element dominates the
channel element. "Ke Controller" means this point embodies the
element that restrains the channel's Fire.

**Same element, same position, DIFFERENT role — SI2 vs LI2:**
- SI2 (Yang Fire): Spring/Water → Ke Controller (Water controls
  Fire, 水克火)
- LI2 (Yang Metal): Spring/Water → Sedation (Metal generates
  Water = child, 金生水)
Same element (Water) at same Shu position (Ying-Spring) on two Yang
channels, but different Five Element roles because the CHANNEL
element differs (Fire vs Metal). The element-at-position is
constant across all Yang channels; the ROLE depends on the
relationship to the channel's own element.

**Ying-Spring comparison across all documented channels:**

All Yin Ying-Spring = Fire:
- HT8, PC8 (Fire/Fire): Horary
- LR2 (Fire/Wood): Sedation
- SP2 (Fire/Earth): Tonification
- LU10 (Fire/Metal): Ke Controller

All Yang Ying-Spring = Water:
- LI2 (Water/Metal): Sedation
- SI2 (Water/Fire): Ke Controller

The Yang pattern emerges: same element (Water) at Spring, with the
role systematically determined by the Ke/Sheng relationship to the
channel element. With SI2, both Yin and Yang Ke Controller roles are
now documented at the Spring position: LU10 (Fire controls Metal)
and SI2 (Water controls Fire).

Also: Nan Jing Ch.68 — "Spring points treat heat in the body"
(荥主身热). Position-based heat-clearing function, independent of
element role. SI2 carries CONVERGENT logic: Ke Controller (Water
quenches Fire) + Heat Clearing (positional) — both point toward
the same therapeutic direction. Compare LI2, where Sedation
(draining excess) + Heat Clearing also converge functionally.

Yuan-Source: No (SI4)
Luo-Connecting: No (SI7)
Xi-Cleft: No (SI6)

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI2 Qiangu (前谷). Ying-Spring/Water of Small
Intestine. Functions: clears Small Intestine Heat, benefits eyes
and ears, reduces swelling. Primary use: excess Heat in the SI
channel — sore throat, mouth ulcers, tinnitus, eye redness. The
Water-on-Fire configuration makes SI2 the primary heat-clearing
point on the SI channel.

**2. Trigger point:** No trigger point — no muscle belly at finger
base level. Abductor digiti minimi is proximal (→ SI3–SI4 area).

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in 5th MCP joint arthritis
(osteoarthritis, rheumatoid). Also reactive in ulnar-side hand
trauma or repetitive strain. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No Marma at little finger joint. Kshipra
(web space) is at the 1st–2nd finger web = LI4 territory.

**5. Head zone:** C8-T1 dermatome. No direct visceral
correspondence (C8-T1 is below the cervical but above the thoracic
visceral reflex zone; T1 marks the transition).
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Back Arm Line (DBAL) — ulnar nerve
pathway along medial arm. The 5th MCP joint is a distal terminus,
not a meaningful myofascial station.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data for hand digits
(Kim 2017 studied trunk primarily).

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable (digit).

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi partial). Same digit-point
convergence pattern as SI1 and all documented Jing-Well/Ying-Spring
hand points.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Lingshu Chapter 1 (Ben Shu): Ying-Spring of the Small Intestine.
  "Where Qi glides is called Spring" (所溜为荥).
- Nan Jing Ch.68: "Spring points treat heat in the body" (荥主身热).
- Jiayi Jing: febrile disease without sweating, headache with neck
  stiffness, tinnitus, deafness, eye redness, sore throat, mumps
  (腮肿), insufficient lactation (乳汁不足), finger numbness.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: sore throat, tinnitus, eye pain, headache,
  febrile disease, nasal obstruction, finger stiffness.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Clears Heat: febrile disease, sore throat, mouth ulcers, eye
  redness (Ying-Spring heat-clearing function)
- Benefits ears and eyes: tinnitus, deafness, eye pain
- Activates channel: headache, stiff neck, finger/hand numbness
- Additional: epistaxis, nasal obstruction, mumps
- Note: insufficient lactation listed in some classical texts for
  SI2, but SI1 is the PRIMARY lactation point — SI2's lactation
  indication is secondary/supplementary

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- Rarely selected individually in modern practice — SI3 (Shu-Stream
  + Eight Confluent) dominates distal SI point selection.
- When selected: for acute Heat conditions on the SI channel,
  leveraging the Ke Controller + Ying-Spring convergent heat-
  clearing logic.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review, no RCT for SI2 as an individual point. No
study isolates SI2's contribution to any clinical outcome.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

**Ke Controller evidence desert:** SI2 extends the pattern — NO
Ke Controller point in the Atlas has point-specific evidence for
the controlling function. Previously documented Ke Controllers:
LU10 (Fire controls Metal — tested for throat but as part of LU
channel, not for Ke function), SP1 (Wood controls Earth — 🔴).
LI3 (Ke Subject), LI5 (Ke Controller of LI) also 🔴. The entire
Ke (controlling) cycle functional assignment is empirically
unvalidated at the point level.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No sensitization data. The 5th MCP joint region becomes tender in
rheumatoid arthritis and ulnar-side hand pathology (Ashi/joint
phenomenon), not a channel-specific dynamic change.

### Cơ chế giải thích

Same non-segmental challenge as all distal hand points: C8-T1
input → throat/eye/ear effects require supraspinal relay. No
mechanism data specific to SI2.

The heat-clearing function (convergent from both Ke Controller
role and Ying-Spring position) has no identified neurobiological
correlate. Whether "clearing SI Heat" corresponds to a measurable
anti-inflammatory, sympatholytic, or thermoregulatory mechanism
at SI2 is unknown.

Gap: same fundamental question as raised at LI2 — whether
stimulating different Five Shu points on the same channel activates
different central pathways (as Five Element theory predicts) or
produces equivalent effects (suggesting the system is organizational
rather than physiological). SI2 vs SI1 vs SI3 on the same ulnar
nerve territory (C8-T1) is an ideal test case: SAME nerve input,
DIFFERENT categorical assignments. Any functional difference would
require receptor-density or depth-dependent explanations rather
than gross nerve identity.
→ Cross-ref: AP-02 LI2 (same question raised for LI channel)

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 5–8 mm perpendicular or oblique. Joint capsule and
periosteum nearby — shallow. De Qi: mild distension/tingling.

Bloodletting: Ying-Spring bloodletting for acute SI channel Heat
is classically documented but uncommon in modern practice.

Moxa: generally NOT indicated at SI2 — as a Ke Controller (Water
quenching Fire) + heat-clearing point, adding heat via moxa is
contraindicated in Heat patterns. Exception: deficiency-cold
patterns where the "controlling" function is irrelevant.

### An toàn

LOW risk. Finger joint level — no major structures at standard
depth.

Precautions:
- Avoid periosteal contact and joint space penetration at 5th MCP
- Caution in MCP joint arthritis (inflamed capsule — tenderness
  may be Ashi phenomenon, not channel-specific)

## Batch 1 Architectural Observations

> **Obs. 1 — INTERIOR-EXTERIOR NAIL-CORNER PAIR:** SI1 (ulnar corner)
> and HT9 (radial corner) occupy opposite sides of the SAME little
> fingernail, ~3–5 mm apart. This is the closest anatomical proximity
> of any documented interior-exterior pair in the Atlas. Both are
> C8-T1 (ulnar nerve), yet carry different element assignments
> (Metal vs Wood) and different Five Element roles (Ke Subject vs
> Tonification). The clinical divergence — SI1 = lactation, HT9 =
> cardiac resuscitation — from near-identical anatomical substrates
> represents a maximal challenge to the "anatomy determines function"
> principle.

> **Obs. 2 — FIRST JING-WELL WITH POINT-SPECIFIC EVIDENCE:** SI1 is
> the first Jing-Well point in the Atlas with point-specific RCT
> evidence (lactation, 🟡 Plausible). All previously documented
> Jing-Well points (LI1, LU11, SP1, LR1, HT9, PC9) are 🔴
> Unvalidated — tested only as part of the collective 12 Jing-Well
> protocol. The SI1 vs LI1 RCT specifically tests POINT SPECIFICITY
> at the Jing-Well level, with dramatically different outcomes
> (97.8% vs 24.3%). This is the first Jing-Well evidence supporting
> point specificity (C04 S3.4).

## SI3 — Hậu Khê (后溪, Hòu Xī)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**
"Back Stream" (后溪) — 后 (Hòu, back/posterior) identifies the
proximal (ulnar-dorsal) side of the metacarpophalangeal joint,
contrasting with SI2 前谷 (Qiangu, "Front Valley") which is distal.
溪 (Xī, stream/creek) reflects the Shu-Stream designation —
"where Qi pours is called Stream" (所注为输). Shu-Stream (**Wood**)
point of the Small Intestine channel. Wood on a **Fire** channel =
**TONIFICATION** (mother of Fire in the Sheng/generating cycle,
木生火) — stimulating the Wood element nourishes Fire. This is the
MOST IMPORTANT FUNCTIONAL ROLE in the Five Element point system
for treating deficiency of the channel's own element.

SI3 holds **TRIPLE DESIGNATION** — the densest category membership
of any SI channel point:

1. **Shu-Stream/Wood (Tonification):** Five Shu point + Five Element
   therapeutic role. Nan Jing Ch.68: "Stream points treat heaviness
   in the body and joint pain" (输主体重节痛); Yang addition: "also
   treat intermittent diseases" (时病).
2. **Eight Confluent Point (Du Mai + Yang Qiao Mai):** One of 8
   points on regular meridians that access Extraordinary Vessels.
   SI3 opens the Du Mai (Governing Vessel, 督脉) — the midline
   spinal channel. Paired with BL62 (Shenmai) on Foot Taiyang.
   Both are Taiyang channel points: Hand Taiyang (SI) + Foot
   Taiyang (BL) = same meridian family accessing the same vessel.
3. **EFT (Emotional Freedom Techniques) Karate Chop Point:** Point #0 in Clinical EFT's 9-point
   tapping protocol — the Setup Statement point where cognitive-
   somatic integration begins.

SI3 is the **MOST-REFERENCED SI POINT** in the BSA corpus (6+
documents: A09, B01, B12, C01, AP-05, AP-13). This convergence from
multiple independent analytical frameworks — Five Shu theory,
Eight Confluent system, and EFT tapping — makes SI3 the anchor
point of the SI channel, analogous to LI4 on LI or PC6 on PC.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the ulnar side of the hand,
in the depression proximal to the ulnar side of the 5th
metacarpophalangeal joint, at the end of the transverse crease of
the hand (when a loose fist is made), at the junction of the red
and white skin (赤白肉际).

Anatomical landmarks: ask the patient to make a loose fist. The
most prominent transverse crease on the ulnar border of the hand
ends at a depression just proximal to the head of the 5th
metacarpal bone. SI3 lies in this depression, at the red-white skin
junction (palmar glabrous skin meeting dorsal hairy skin). The
point overlies the proximal edge of the hypothenar eminence — the
belly of abductor digiti minimi.

Spatial relationships:
- SI2 is ~2 cm distal (at the MCP joint, distal side)
- SI4 is ~2 cm proximal (at the junction of 5th metacarpal and
  hamate, ulnar wrist border)
- HT7 (Shenmen) is on the SAME WRIST but palmar/radial: at the
  ulnar end of the distal wrist crease, approximately 3–4 cm
  proximal to SI3. The Host-Guest pairing SI4 (Yuan) + HT5 (Luo)
  bridges this gap between the interior-exterior channels.

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular insertion. At depth, the
needle enters the belly of abductor digiti minimi. Oblique insertion
toward the palm (0.5–1 cun) to reach deeper tissue is described in
some classical techniques.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Dorsal branch of ulnar nerve (C8-T1) — primary sensory
  innervation of the dorsal/ulnar hand surface at this level.
  The dorsal branch separates from the ulnar nerve trunk ~5 cm
  proximal to the wrist, courses around the ulnar head, and
  provides dorsal digital nerves to the ulnar 2.5 fingers. SI3
  lies on the course of this branch.
- Deep branch of ulnar nerve (C8-T1) — motor innervation to
  abductor digiti minimi, flexor digiti minimi, opponens digiti
  minimi (hypothenar muscles). The deep branch enters the
  hypothenar eminence at Guyon's canal, approximately at the
  level of SI3–SI4.
- Proper palmar digital nerve of the little finger (ulnar nerve,
  C8-T1) — sensory on the palmar aspect, wrapping around to the
  red-white skin junction.
- Spinal segments: C8-T1 (ulnar nerve exclusive — same as SI1,
  SI2, and the entire distal SI channel)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Transition from digit to hand:** SI1–SI2 are digit-tip points
with negligible muscle. SI3 is the FIRST SI point with significant
muscle tissue (abductor digiti minimi belly). This provides:
(a) greater tissue volume for needle stimulation, (b) a myofascial
trigger point substrate (absent at digit tips), (c) a proprioceptive
input pathway (muscle spindles in ABD digiti minimi). The tissue
gradient from SI1→SI3 parallels LI1→LI3 (digit→metacarpal) and
represents increasing therapeutic "leverage" per the tissue-depth
model.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S4.2

Fascial plane: the hypothenar eminence contains a defined fascial
compartment (hypothenar compartment) bounded by the palmar
aponeurosis superficially and the 5th metacarpal periosteum deep.
The red-white skin junction at SI3 marks the boundary between the
palmar fascial system and the dorsal hand fascia — a fascial
transition zone with potential mechanical significance.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: ulnar artery proper palmar digital branch (5th finger,
ulnar side). The ulnar artery itself passes through Guyon's canal
~1–2 cm proximal to SI3. Small caliber at SI3 level. Dorsal carpal
branch of ulnar artery provides dorsal supply. Low vascular risk.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (thicker than digit, moderate fat)
- Abductor digiti minimi (ABD, hypothenar eminence — primary
  muscle at SI3, origin from pisiform bone)
- Flexor digiti minimi brevis (deeper, smaller)
- Opponens digiti minimi (deepest hypothenar muscle)
- 5th metacarpal periosteum (floor)

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI3 Houxi (后溪). Shu-Stream/Wood, Tonification,
Eight Confluent (Du Mai). Primary functions: opens and regulates
the Du Mai (spine/posterior midline), benefits the occiput/neck/

calms the spirit, activates the channel. SI3 is the PRIMARY DISTAL
POINT for cervical and spinal disorders in classical acupuncture —
the spine equivalent of LI4's role for the face. Classical formula:
SI3 + BL62 for Du Mai disorders (spine stiffness, headache, back
pain). Combined with HT3 (Shaohai) for upper limb paired-channel
problems.

**2. Trigger point:** Abductor digiti minimi trigger point. Referred
pain: ulnar side of hand and 5th finger, sometimes extending to
the ulnar wrist. SI3 overlies the proximal attachment area of ABD
digiti minimi, within the trigger point's typical referral zone.
This is the first trigger point correspondence on the SI channel
(SI1–SI2 had none — no muscle at digit tips). The correspondence

→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in hypothenar overuse syndromes,
ulnar hand trauma, 5th MCP joint pathology, Guyon's canal syndrome

tender points. Clinically, SI3 tenderness is sought in cervical
spondylosis assessment in some acupuncture traditions — a distal
diagnostic point. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No direct Marma at ulnar hand border. Nearest
correspondences: Kurchashira (at hypothenar eminence in some
Ayurvedic texts — variable placement) and Kshipra (1st–2nd web
space = LI4 territory). The mapping is uncertain. Talahridaya
(palm center = PC8) has strong correspondence documented, but it
is at a different location (palm center vs ulnar border). No
confident correspondence.

**5. Head zone:** C8-T1 dermatome. Same as SI1, SI2, and the
entire distal SI channel. No direct visceral correspondence (C8-T1
is the upper boundary of the thoracic sympathetic territory but
below the T2–T12 viscerotome range for most organs).
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Back Arm Line (DBAL) — the ulnar nerve
pathway running along the medial arm, from hypothenar eminence
→ medial forearm → medial upper arm → axilla. SI3 at the hypothenar
eminence is a more meaningful station on this line than SI1–SI2
(digit tips). The DBAL connects hypothenar → triceps medial head
→ rhomboids → thoracolumbar fascia. This fascial chain runs
POSTERIOR — paralleling the Du Mai trajectory from hand to spine.
Whether this fascial continuity contributes to SI3's spinal effects
is speculative but architecturally suggestive.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4 (Anatomy Trains)

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data for hand points
(Kim 2017 studied trunk primarily).

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable (hand surface too small/curved
for standard cups). Flash cupping on the hypothenar eminence is
occasionally described but not standard practice.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Trigger Point partial, Ashi).
Higher than SI1–SI2 (both 2/8) due to the first trigger point
correspondence on the SI channel. Still below muscular points like
LI4 (5/8) — hand points generally score lower than forearm/upper
arm points because several systems (Marma, cupping, neurogenic spots)
do not map well to the hand.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Acute stiff neck / neck pain — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Sun et al. 2014 (BMJ Open): pilot RCT PROTOCOL for SI3 vs massage
for acute neck pain (落枕). 36 participants planned, two-parallel-
group, assessor-blinded. Acupuncture at SI3 + massage vs massage
alone, 3 days treatment. The study was designed to evaluate
feasibility and preliminary efficacy. Published as a protocol paper
only — NO PUBLISHED RESULTS found as of current search. The most
widely used clinical indication of SI3 (stiff neck) has no
completed, published clinical trial.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.4 (point specificity gap)

**2. Du Mai thermal connection — 🟡 Plausible**

Trinh et al. 2024 (Cureus): 60 healthy volunteers, self-controlled
crossover design. Acupuncture at SI3 vs LU10 (control acupoint on
a different channel). Posterior neck skin surface temperature
measured by infrared thermal camera (FLIR C5™) at 5 time points
with 5-minute intervals. Result: **statistically significant
increase in posterior neck temperature (p < 0.05) during
acupuncture at both left and right SI3** — no significant change
during acupuncture at LU10 control points.

This is the first OBJECTIVE PHYSIOLOGICAL MEASUREMENT supporting
the Eight Confluent access concept: stimulating a hand point (SI3,
C8-T1) produces a measurable thermal response at the posterior neck
(Du Mai territory, cervical spine). The LU10 control rules out
non-specific needle effects — the response is POINT-SPECIFIC.

Zhen Ci Yan Jiu (2023): infrared thermal imaging comparing SI3 vs
GV14 (Dazhui — a point actually ON Du Mai). SI3 generated thermal
effects at facial acupoints similar to GV14's thermal pattern,
providing additional support for the SI3→Du Mai connection claim.

Rating rationale: 🟡 Plausible (not 🟢) because — (a) temperature
change is a physiological biomarker, not a clinical outcome,
(b) mechanism of the thermal response (vasomotor? sympathetic?)
is not established, (c) healthy volunteers, not patients,
(d) single study with control acupoint.

⚡ Framework note: SI3→posterior neck thermal response = first
objective measurement-based evidence in the Atlas for an Eight
Confluent point's access to its extraordinary vessel. All previous
Eight Confluent entries (SP4/Chong Mai, PC6/Yin Wei Mai) relied on
clinical observation and traditional theory. This creates a testable
prediction: if SI3→Du Mai has a measurable thermal signature, do
the other 3 pairs produce analogous physiological signals at their
respective vessel territories? (SP4→abdominal temperature for
Chong Mai? LU7→anterior cervical for Ren Mai? TE5→lateral trunk
for Dai Mai?) The Eight Confluent system may be empirically
verifiable via thermal imaging. Extends A09 S2 (meridian substrates)
and C04 S3.4 (point specificity).

**3. Lumbar disc herniation / spinal nerve root inflammation —
🔴 Unvalidated (animal studies only)**

Multiple rat model studies using SI3+GB30 (Huantiao) combination:

(a) NF-κB / iNOS pathway: acupuncture at SI3+GB30 improved spinal
nerve root morphology, inhibited NF-κB and iNOS protein expression
in spinal nerve roots, reduced serum NO levels, and relieved pain
in rats with lumbar disc herniation. Effects greater than using
local points alone.

(b) HMGB1 pathway: SI3+GB30 inhibited HMGB1 protein and mRNA
expression in spinal nerve trunk, reduced IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8
production in rat LDH model.

(c) PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway: SI3+GB30 modulated autophagy in nucleus
pulposus cells via this signaling pathway in a rat disc degeneration
model.

Rating: 🔴 because — (a) all animal studies (rat), (b) SI3 never
isolated from GB30 — the combination is tested, not SI3 individually,
(c) no human clinical data. However, the MECHANISTIC DATA is rich:
three distinct anti-inflammatory pathways identified. If translated
to humans, these would represent the first molecular-level mechanism
evidence for a Du Mai confluent point treating spinal pathology.

**4. EFT protocol component — 🔴 for SI3 specifically**

EFT (Emotional Freedom Techniques) evidence is protocol-level,
not point-specific. Clinical EFT has SR/MA evidence for PTSD
(Sebastian & Nelms 2017: 7 RCTs, large effect sizes) and anxiety
(Clond 2016: 14 RCTs, d = 1.23). But SI3 (Karate Chop) is one of
9 tapping points — its individual contribution cannot be isolated.
B12 analysis shows EFT's somatic component activates only rapidly-
adapting Aβ mechanoreceptors (Meissner, Pacinian) — the WEAKEST
somatic stimulus in Track B. The therapeutic signal likely comes
from cognitive components (exposure + acceptance) rather than the
somatic tapping.
→ Cross-ref: B12 S2.2 (receptor analysis), B12 S2.3 (cognitive)

**5. All other traditional indications — 🔴 Unvalidated**

No study isolates SI3 for eye disorders, tinnitus, epilepsy,
malaria, or night sweating.

**Evidence-importance paradox:** SI3 is arguably the most clinically
important SI point — Eight Confluent, Tonification, most-referenced,
most frequently used in practice — yet has NO COMPLETED clinical
trial with published results. Not even a single published RCT
outcome. This extends the pattern first noted at LU9 (triple
designation, zero RCTs) and represents an extreme example of the
evidence-clinical importance gap in acupuncture research.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No specific sensitization data at SI3. However, the hypothenar
eminence (ABD digiti minimi) becomes tender in ulnar nerve
entrapment (Guyon's canal syndrome), cervical radiculopathy (C8-T1),
and repetitive hand strain. Whether SI3 specifically shows pathology-
dependent threshold changes (as predicted by the Dynamic Acupoint
model) — particularly in cervical spondylosis patients, where SI3's
primary traditional indication lies — is unstudied but would be a
clinically relevant test.

Testable prediction: cervical spondylosis patients should show
lower pressure-pain thresholds at SI3 compared to healthy controls,
AND compared to non-SI3 hand points at similar innervation levels.
This would parallel SP8's Xi-Cleft sensitization finding (Chen 2015)
and extend the Dynamic Acupoint model to Eight Confluent points.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

### Cơ chế giải thích

**The central question: HOW does a hand point (C8-T1) affect the
entire spine (C1–S5)?**

SI3's primary clinical function — treating cervical and spinal
disorders — requires traversing the ENTIRE spinal column from a
single entry point at C8-T1. Unlike LI4's non-segmental gap
(C6-T1→trigeminal, ~5 segments), SI3's functional claim spans
potentially 20+ segments (C8-T1→any spinal level including lumbar
and sacral). This is the widest non-segmental gap for any single
point's primary indication in the Atlas.

**Proposed mechanism — supraspinal descending modulation:**

```
Step 1: Needling SI3 activates A-beta/A-delta fibers of ulnar
        nerve (C8-T1, dorsal branch + deep branch via ABD digiti
        minimi proprioceptors/nociceptors)
Step 2: Afferents ascend via spinothalamic and spinoreticular
        tracts to brainstem
Step 3: Brainstem activation: PAG (periaqueductal gray) and RVM
        (rostroventromedial medulla)
Step 4: Descending inhibition via dorsolateral funiculus projects
        to MULTIPLE spinal levels — potentially the entire
        dorsal horn from C1 to S5
Step 5: At each spinal level: modulation of nociceptive
        processing, muscle tone regulation (via ventral horn
        effects), and sympathetic outflow modification
```

This is the same general pathway as LI4→trigeminal (PAG→RVM→
descending) and LI11→RVLM (sympathoinhibition), but with a
critical difference: SI3's clinical effects span the ENTIRE spine,
not a specific territory. The Du Mai access claim effectively
predicts that SI3 activates a WHOLE-SPINE descending modulation
system, not a segmentally restricted one.

**Thermal imaging evidence supports the mechanism:**

The Trinh 2024 finding (SI3→posterior neck temperature increase)
is consistent with sympathetic modulation via supraspinal relay.
Posterior neck temperature change implies vasomotor regulation in
cervical territory — a sympathetic response. If SI3 activates
descending sympathetic modulation from brainstem to cervical cord
(via superior cervical ganglion → external carotid/vertebral artery
territory), this would explain both the thermal imaging result AND
the clinical effect on neck/cervical disorders.

**Animal evidence adds molecular detail:**

The rat studies (SI3+GB30) identify three anti-inflammatory pathways
(NF-κB/iNOS, HMGB1/IL-1β/IL-6/IL-8, PI3K/AKT/mTOR) at the
spinal nerve root level. These molecular targets are consistent
with peripheral nerve inflammation modulation — potentially mediated
by descending anti-inflammatory signals or by reflexive modulation
of local inflammatory cascades via sympathetic efferents.

**EFT mechanism context:**

In the EFT tapping protocol, SI3 (Karate Chop) provides C8-T1
ulnar nerve input — the DEEPEST spinal level entry point among
the 9 EFT points (56% trigeminal, 11% cervical plexus C3-C4, 11%
thoracic T2). The Setup Statement is performed AT SI3 while
combining cognitive reframing — the only EFT step that sustains
tapping at a single point for the full duration of a cognitive
exercise. B12 analysis shows tapping intensity is too light for
significant mechanoreceptor activation, suggesting any SI3-specific
contribution to EFT would be minimal. The cognitive component
(exposure + acceptance) likely carries the therapeutic load.
→ Cross-ref: B12 S2.2

**Gap analysis:**

1. The specificity of SI3 for the SPINE (vs generalized pain
   modulation) is unexplained. If SI3 activates PAG→RVM→descending
   inhibition, why doesn't it treat all pain equally (as LI4 does
   for facial pain generally, not specific facial structures)?
   Classical answer: Du Mai access. Modern answer: unknown.

2. The thermal imaging result (posterior neck only measured) does
   not confirm whether the effect extends to thoracic and lumbar
   levels. A whole-spine thermal imaging study after SI3 stimulation
   would be informative.

3. The SI3+GB30 animal data cannot distinguish SI3's contribution
   from GB30's (which independently has evidence for sciatic nerve
   modulation via strong manual acupuncture).

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 10–15 mm perpendicular insertion into ABD digiti
minimi belly. De Qi: distension, heaviness, or electric-like
sensation along the ulnar hand border, sometimes radiating to the
wrist or up the forearm. De Qi quality at SI3 is generally
moderate to strong — more robust than SI1/SI2 due to muscle tissue.

Classical technique for acute stiff neck (落枕): needle
contralateral SI3 (opposite side from the painful neck), retain
needle, and instruct patient to actively rotate the neck through
the painful range during needle retention. This combination of
distal needling + active movement is a classical treatment
principle (远道取穴 + 运动疗法).

Eight Confluent technique: SI3 + BL62 needled simultaneously
(bilateral or ipsilateral, depending on tradition). SI3 needled
first (upper limb opens the vessel), BL62 second (lower limb
completes the access). Retain both 20–30 minutes.

EA: applicable but less commonly used at SI3 than at SI1
(lactation). When used, typically low-frequency (2–4 Hz) for
musculoskeletal indications.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Acupressure: self-applicable. Firm pressure with opposite thumb
on the ulnar border of the hand, pressing into the depression
proximal to the 5th MCP joint. 30–60 seconds sustained pressure.
Commonly recommended as a self-care point for neck stiffness and
upper back tension.

Moxa: applicable. Indirect moxa or moxa stick at SI3 for
deficiency-type (cold) neck/back pain. Not a primary moxa point.

EFT tapping: light percussive tapping (~2 taps/second, 7 taps per
cycle) with 2–3 fingertips of the opposite hand on the hypothenar
eminence (side of hand). In EFT, this is sustained for the full
Setup Statement (approximately 15–20 seconds per round, 3
repetitions = 45–60 seconds total). Stimulus intensity is
SUPERFICIAL — activates only Meissner/Pacinian mechanoreceptors,
no nociceptor or proprioceptor engagement.
→ Cross-ref: B12 S2.2

### An toàn

LOW risk. Hypothenar eminence — no major vessels or nerves at
standard needle depth.

Precautions:

### Discussion

**1. THE EVIDENCE-IMPORTANCE PARADOX AT ITS MOST EXTREME**

SI3 represents the most extreme example in the Atlas of the
evidence-importance gap. Compare:

| Point | Designations | Clinical use | Completed RCTs |
|-------|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| LI4 | Yuan-Source, Command, Ma Dan-yang | Most used | Multiple (dental, labor, etc.) |
| PC6 | Luo, Eight Confluent (Chong Mai) | Very high | Multiple (PONV) |
| SP6 | Three Yin Crossing | Very high | Multiple (labor pain, insomnia) |
| LU9 | Yuan-Source, Shu-Stream, Hui-Meeting | High | Zero |
| **SI3** | **Shu-Stream, Tonification, Eight Confluent (Du Mai), EFT** | **Very high** | **Zero** |

SI3 and LU9 share the same paradox: maximum theoretical importance,
zero clinical trial validation. LU9 has triple designation but
moderate clinical use. SI3 has quadruple functional identity AND
is one of the most frequently used points in clinical practice
(落枕 prescription taught to every acupuncture student). The gap
between clinical ubiquity and evidence absence is maximal.

**2. DU MAI ACCESS — FROM CLASSICAL CLAIM TO TESTABLE HYPOTHESIS**

The Eight Confluent system's central claim — that stimulating a
hand point (SI3) can regulate the entire spinal channel (Du Mai)
— has been treated as either accepted classical theory or dismissed
as pre-scientific metaphor. The thermal imaging evidence (Trinh
2024) transforms this into a testable hypothesis with a measurable
physiological marker. The finding is modest (temperature change at
posterior neck in healthy volunteers), but its implications are
large: if SI3→Du Mai has a thermal signature, the ENTIRE Eight
Confluent system becomes empirically testable via systematic thermal
imaging of all 4 pairs at their respective vessel territories.

**3. CROSS-SYSTEM CONVERGENCE AT SI3**

SI3 is the only point in the Atlas where three independent
therapeutic systems converge on the same location:

- **TCM acupuncture:** Eight Confluent (Du Mai) + Five Shu
  (Tonification) = highest-category designation
- **EFT tapping:** Karate Chop point = Setup Statement location
  (cognitive-somatic integration)
- **Trigger point therapy:** ABD digiti minimi = hypothenar
  trigger point for ulnar hand pain

These systems were developed independently across different
cultures, centuries, and theoretical frameworks. Their convergence
at the ulnar border of the 5th MCP joint — a specific anatomical
location overlying the ulnar nerve's deep branch entry into the
hypothenar compartment — is architecturally significant. Whether
this convergence reflects shared anatomical substrates or
independent discovery of the same clinically reactive zone cannot
be determined from current data, but it elevates SI3's cross-
tradition score to 3/8 with qualitatively stronger signals than
the numerical score alone suggests.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.5 (convergence analysis)

## SI4 — Uyển Cốt (腕骨, Wàn Gǔ)

is host; SI4 (Yuan) + HT5 (Luo) when SI is host.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the ulnar side of the hand,
in the depression between the junction of the 5th metacarpal base
and the hamate bone, at the junction of the red and white skin
(赤白肉际).

Anatomical landmarks: from SI3 (hypothenar eminence at MCP joint),
move ~2 cm proximally along the ulnar border. SI4 lies at the
depression where the 5th metacarpal shaft meets the carpus (hamate
bone). The hook of the hamate is palpable deep to this point.
SI5 is ~1.5 cm proximal-ulnar (at the ulnar styloid).

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular. At depth, the needle contacts
the periosteum of the 5th metacarpal base or hamate.

→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

EXIT of Guyon's canal — the most anatomically significant neural
landmark in the SI4 vicinity.

Fascial plane: transition from hypothenar fascial compartment

→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

## SI6 — Dưỡng Lão (养老, Yǎng Lǎo)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Nourishing the Aged" (养老) — 养 (Yǎng, nourish/support) +
老 (Lǎo, old/elderly). The MOST DISTINCTIVE point name on the
SI channel — the only acupoint in the standard 361 named
explicitly for a demographic group (the elderly). **Xi-Cleft
point** (郄穴) of the Small Intestine channel — where channel
Qi accumulates deeply for acute conditions. Located on the
dorsal wrist, in a bony cleft on the radial side of the
styloid process of the ulna.

SI6 is the SECOND Yang Xi-Cleft in the Atlas (after LI7).
Classical theory: Yang Xi-Cleft → acute PAIN and INFLAMMATORY
conditions (vs Yin Xi-Cleft → acute BLOOD conditions). SI6's
traditional emphasis on vision and musculoskeletal pain in the
elderly is unique — most Xi-Cleft points are defined by their
ACUTE function, not by patient population.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the dorsal aspect of the
forearm, in the bony cleft on the radial side of the styloid
process of the ulna.

Anatomical landmarks: supinate the forearm with the palm facing
the chest. Palpate the ulnar styloid — on its RADIAL (lateral/
dorsal) surface, there is a bony cleft between the styloid
process and the head of the ulna. SI6 is in this cleft. The
point location changes with pronation/supination — traditionally
located with the palm pressed against the chest (full
supination), which opens the cleft.

**SI5-SI6 relationship:** both at the ulnar styloid but on
OPPOSITE sides. SI5 = distal/medial depression (ulnar styloid
to triquetral). SI6 = proximal/radial cleft (on the styloid
itself). Separated by ~1 cm, the ulnar styloid acting as the
bony landmark between them.

Needle depth: 5–10 mm oblique, following the bony cleft.
Perpendicular insertion is limited by bone. Oblique insertion
toward the ulnar shaft can reach 10–15 mm along the periosteal
surface.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Dorsal branch of ulnar nerve (C8-T1) — sensory. The dorsal
  branch wraps around the ulnar head at approximately this level
  — SI6 is near the transition point where the dorsal branch
  separates from the main ulnar trunk.
- Posterior interosseous nerve (C7-C8) — terminal branches.
  The posterior interosseous nerve (sensory terminal) lies on
  the interosseous membrane and radioulnar joint capsule,
  deep to SI6. Provides proprioceptive feedback from the
  distal radioulnar joint.
- Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (C8-T1) — terminal
  branches at dorsal wrist.
- Spinal segments: C7-T1 (broadening — first SI point with
  C7 contribution via posterior interosseous nerve)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural transition:** SI6 is the first SI point where the
posterior interosseous nerve (C7-C8) becomes accessible — a
motor/proprioceptive input source absent from SI1–SI5. This
adds a new spinal segment (C7) to the SI channel's neural
repertoire.

Fascial plane: within the bony cleft, tissue is primarily
periosteum and joint capsule of the distal radioulnar joint
(DRUJ). The extensor retinaculum overlies the area. Limited
fascial layering — bone-dominated site.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: dorsal carpal arterial arch (from radial and ulnar
arteries). Small branches at this level. Low vascular risk.

Muscle/tissue:
- Skin → thin subcutaneous tissue
- Extensor retinaculum (crossing the area)
- Tendon sheaths (ECU in 6th compartment, ulnar side)
- Bony cleft: periosteum of ulnar styloid / ulnar head
- Deep: DRUJ capsule, posterior interosseous nerve terminal

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- VISION indication is what distinguishes SI6 from the other
  wrist-level SI points (SI4, SI5). "Nourishing the Aged" is
  specifically invoked in prescriptions for elderly patients
  with presbyopia or age-related visual decline.
- For acute pain (Xi-Cleft function), SI6 is used for
  shoulder/upper back acute pain — particularly acute
  torticollis (overlapping with SI3's 落枕 indication).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review, no RCT for SI6 as an individual point.
A systematic review on acupuncture for age-related macular
degeneration (AMD) exists (Li et al. 2023, Complementary
Therapies in Medicine) but SI6 was not among the most commonly
used points — BL1, GB37, and local eye points dominated.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

The vision indication for SI6 is architecturally interesting:
C8-T1 at the wrist has NO anatomical pathway to the visual
system. Not segmental (optic nerve is CN II, visual cortex is
occipital — neither connected to C8-T1). Not trigeminal (unlike
BL1/GB1 which are in CN V1 territory). Any visual effect from
SI6 would require a supraspinal mechanism with NO plausible
intermediate pathway — among the most extreme non-segmental
claims in the Atlas.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No sensitization data. The Xi-Cleft model predicts SI6 should
sensitize during acute SI channel conditions — but what
constitutes an "acute SI condition" in modern terms is itself
unclear (small intestinal inflammation? acute neck pain as
channel pathology?). SP8 is the only Xi-Cleft with
sensitization evidence (Chen 2015).
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Acute pain (Xi-Cleft function):** segmentally adjacent
effects (wrist, forearm) are explicable via C8-T1 gate control.
Shoulder/neck effects require supraspinal relay — same
PAG/RVM descending modulation pathway proposed for SI3.

**Vision mechanism — extreme non-segmental gap:** C8-T1 → visual
system has no identified pathway. Not explained by:
- Segmental reflex (spinal levels entirely unrelated to vision)
- Trigeminal pathway (SI6 is on ulnar nerve, not CN V)
- Vagal pathway (ulnar nerve is not vagal)
- Sympathetic pathway (no direct C8-T1 → ciliary ganglion)

The ONLY plausible route: supraspinal relay → cortical/
subcortical modulation of visual processing (occipital cortex),
analogous to how acupuncture at LI4 (C6-C7) activates visual
cortex in fMRI studies. But LI4→visual cortex has been
demonstrated (Cho 1998/2006, later debated); SI6→visual cortex
has no data.

**"Nourishing the Aged" as theory vs mechanism:** The name
encodes a PATIENT POPULATION, not a mechanism. Whether age-
related conditions have different acupoint response profiles
than the same conditions in younger patients is an unexplored
question. The name may reflect clinical observation — elderly
patients may respond better to SI6 — but this would be a
patient-specific modifier, not a point-specific mechanism.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 5–10 mm oblique, following the bony cleft on the
radial side of the ulnar styloid. Full supination (palm toward
chest) opens the cleft. De Qi: mild distension/aching at the
wrist, sometimes radiating along the dorsal forearm.

Classical technique for eye disorders: needle SI6, retain
20–30 minutes while the patient performs eye exercises (looking
in all four directions). Parallels SI3's active movement
technique for stiff neck.

Moxa: applicable. Small cones or moxa stick for deficiency-
type elderly conditions.

Acupressure: press into the bony cleft with the opposite
thumb, palm facing the chest. Less intuitive location than
SI3 or SI4.

### An toàn

LOW risk. Bony cleft — limited depth, no major structures.

Precautions:
- Dorsal branch of ulnar nerve in the vicinity — paresthesia
  possible
- ECU tendon sheath (6th compartment) immediately adjacent
- Periosteal contact expected at standard depth — may cause
  sharp pain
- Localization requires correct forearm position (supination)
  — mislocalization with pronation may miss the bony cleft

## SI7 — Chi Chính (支正, Zhī Zhèng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Branch of the Upright" (支正) — 支 (Zhī, branch/diverge) + 正
(Zhèng, upright/correct). The "branch" refers to the Luo
vessel that diverges from the main SI channel at this point.
**Luo-Connecting point** (络穴) of the Small Intestine channel
— links SI to its paired Heart (HT) channel. Located on the
posterior forearm, 5 cun proximal to SI5, on the line connecting
SI5 and SI8. SI7 is the SECOND Yang Luo-Connecting point in the
Atlas (after LI6) and the SEVENTH Luo-Connecting overall.

The SI7-HT5 Luo pair completes the bilateral Luo network for
the HT-SI interior-exterior pair: SI7 connects SI→HT, HT5
connects HT→SI. Combined with Yuan-Source points in Host-Guest
technique (主客配穴法): HT7 (Yuan) + SI7 (Luo) when Heart is
the host channel.
→ Cross-ref: AP-05 HT5, AP-05 HT7

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterior aspect of
the forearm, on the line connecting SI5 and SI8, 5 cun proximal
to SI5.

Anatomical landmarks: from SI5 (ulnar styloid), measure 5 cun
proximally along the posterior-ulnar forearm. SI7 is between the
flexor carpi ulnaris (ulnar/palmar) and the extensor carpi
ulnaris (dorsal). The ulna is palpable medially. SI6 is 5 cun
distal (at wrist). SI8 is 5 cun proximal (at elbow/olecranon).
SI7 is approximately at the mid-forearm level on the ulnar side.

Needle depth: 10–20 mm perpendicular or oblique. Greater muscle
mass than wrist-level points (SI4–SI6) allows deeper insertion.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (C8-T1) — primary
  sensory. This nerve (from medial cord of brachial plexus)
  provides cutaneous sensation to the medial forearm.
- Ulnar nerve (C8-T1) — the main trunk runs between FCU and
  FDP at this level, on the medial forearm. Deeper than standard
  needle depth but at risk with aggressive insertion.
- Posterior interosseous nerve (C7-C8) — motor branches to
  forearm extensors. Accessible at depth.
- Spinal segments: C7-T1 (same broadened range as SI6)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural corridor transition:** SI7 is the first SI point where
the ulnar nerve TRUNK is nearby (vs the dorsal and digital
branches at SI1–SI6). The main trunk runs along the medial
forearm at ~15–25 mm depth, between FCU and FDP. This
represents a shift from terminal sensory branches to the main
nerve trunk — increasing both therapeutic leverage and safety
considerations.

Fascial plane: antebrachial fascia between FCU (ulnar/palmar)
and ECU (dorsal). Inter-muscular septum between flexor and
extensor compartments. This transition zone between
compartments is a defined fascial plane.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: ulnar artery runs with the ulnar nerve on the medial
forearm — medial to SI7, deeper. Posterior interosseous artery
on the dorsal interosseous membrane. Both below standard needle
depth. Low-moderate vascular risk.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue → antebrachial fascia
- Flexor carpi ulnaris (ulnar/palmar side)
- Extensor carpi ulnaris (dorsal side)
- Flexor digitorum profundus (deeper, ulnar head)
- Interosseous membrane → ulna periosteum (floor)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Luo-Connecting: YES** — Luo point (络穴) of the Small
Intestine channel. One of 15 Luo-Connecting points (12 regular
+ GV + CV + Great Luo of Spleen).

**Luo vessel pathway (Ling Shu Ch.10):**
Separates from the SI main channel at SI7, connects to the

## SI8 — Tiểu Hải (小海, Xiǎo Hǎi)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Small Sea" (小海) — 小 (Xiǎo) refers to the Small Intestine
(小肠) and 海 (Hǎi, sea) identifies this as the He-Sea (合穴)
point. The most LITERAL naming of any Five Shu point in the
Atlas — the name directly encodes both channel and Shu category.
**He-Sea/Earth** point of the Small Intestine channel — the 5th
and FINAL Five Shu position. Earth on a **Fire** channel =
**SEDATION** — Fire generates Earth in the Sheng cycle (火生土),
making Earth the child element. Nan Jing: "for excess, sedate
the child" (实则泻其子). Located between the olecranon of the
ulna and the medial epicondyle of the humerus, in the ulnar
groove — directly over the **cubital tunnel**, where the ulnar
nerve is most superficially exposed in the body.

SI8 COMPLETES all Five Element roles on the SI channel:
- SI1/Metal = Ke Subject
- SI2/Water = Ke Controller
- SI3/Wood = Tonification
- SI5/Fire = Horary
- **SI8/Earth = Sedation**

This is the 8th He-Sea and 2nd Yang He-Sea (after LI11)
documented in the Atlas. All Yin He-Sea = Water (5/5); all
Yang He-Sea = Earth (2/2) — a systematic consequence of the
fixed Five Shu element sequences.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): between the olecranon of
the ulna and the medial epicondyle of the humerus, in the
depression of the ulnar groove.

Anatomical landmarks: flex the elbow. Palpate the olecranon
(posterior point of the elbow) and the medial epicondyle
(medial bony prominence). SI8 lies in the groove between
these two landmarks — the ulnar groove (cubital tunnel). This
is the site where the ulnar nerve is palpable as a cord-like
structure rolling under the finger (the "funny bone" sensation
when tapped).

SI7 is 5 cun distal (mid-forearm). SI9 is on the posterior
axillary fold — a substantial anatomical gap (no SI points on
the upper arm between elbow and shoulder).

Needle depth: 5–10 mm perpendicular. SHALLOW — the ulnar nerve
is immediately deep (3–5 mm subcutaneous). Classical technique:
oblique insertion along the groove, parallel to the nerve, to
minimize nerve impalement risk.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Ulnar nerve TRUNK (C7-C8-T1) — the defining structure. The
  ulnar nerve passes through the cubital tunnel between the
  olecranon and medial epicondyle, covered only by skin,
  subcutaneous tissue, and Osborne's ligament (cubital tunnel
  retinaculum). This is the MOST SUPERFICIALLY EXPOSED
  position of the ulnar nerve in the body — depth to nerve:
  ~3–5 mm.
- Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (C8-T1) — terminal
  branches in the medial elbow region.
- Medial brachial cutaneous nerve (C8-T1) — supplies skin of
  medial upper arm, terminal branches reach medial elbow.
- Spinal segments: C7-T1 (same broadened range as SI6-SI7)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Cubital tunnel architecture:** SI8 sits directly over the
cubital tunnel — the osteo-fibrous channel formed by the
medial epicondyle (anteriorly), olecranon (posteriorly), and
Osborne's ligament (roof). The ulnar nerve is the sole content.
During elbow flexion, the tunnel narrows and intraneural
pressure increases — this is why cubital tunnel syndrome is the
second most common upper limb entrapment neuropathy (after
carpal tunnel). SI8 is the ONLY point in the Atlas located
directly over a named nerve entrapment tunnel.

Fascial plane: cubital tunnel retinaculum (Osborne's ligament)
— a thin fascial band spanning from medial epicondyle to
olecranon, roofing the ulnar nerve. Deeper: medial collateral
ligament of the elbow joint capsule.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: posterior ulnar recurrent artery (branch of ulnar
artery) accompanies the ulnar nerve through the cubital tunnel.
Small caliber but consistent presence.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (thin over bony landmarks)
- Osborne's ligament / cubital tunnel retinaculum
- Ulnar nerve trunk (content of the cubital tunnel)
- FCU humeral and ulnar heads (originate from medial
  epicondyle and olecranon respectively, ulnar nerve passes
  between them)
- Medial collateral ligament → elbow joint capsule (floor)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **He-Sea (Earth)** — 5th and final Five Shu position
on the Small Intestine channel.

**Earth on Fire = SEDATION** — Fire generates Earth in the Sheng
cycle (火生土). Earth is the CHILD of Fire. Nan Jing Difficulty
69: "for excess, sedate the child" (实则泻其子). SI8 drains
excess Fire from the Small Intestine channel — excess-type
conditions: acute inflammation, Heat, fever.

**He-Sea across documented channels:**
| Channel | He-Sea | Element | Five Element Role |
|---------|--------|---------|-------------------|
| LU | LU5 | Water | Sedation |
| LI | LI11 | Earth | Tonification |
| SP | SP9 | Water | Ke Subject |
| LR | LR8 | Water | Tonification |
| HT | HT3 | Water | Ke Controller |
| PC | PC3 | Water | Ke Controller |
| **SI** | **SI8** | **Earth** | **Sedation** |

**He-Sea element pattern:** Yin He-Sea = ALWAYS Water (5/5).
Yang He-Sea = ALWAYS Earth (2/2). Algebraic consequence of the
fixed Five Shu element sequences: Yin sequence ends with Water

completely determined by Yin/Yang — not by channel element.

Yuan-Source: No (SI4)
Luo-Connecting: No (SI7)
Xi-Cleft: No (SI6)

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, TrP strong, Ashi strong,

at C7-T1 dorsal horn levels.

**He-Sea neural access pattern:** SI8 (cubital tunnel, ulnar

→ Cross-ref: AP-02 LI11

**Spirit-calming/epilepsy — non-segmental:** C7-T1 → cortical

**Fire channel He-Sea spirit cluster:** All three Fire channel

Acupuncture: 5–10 mm perpendicular or oblique. Elbow flexed.

paresthesia radiates to 4th-5th fingers (ulnar nerve

nerve stimulation.

Moxa: applicable. Indirect moxa for cold-damp medial elbow

Acupressure: press into the ulnar groove between olecranon and

### An toàn

**HIGH risk** — highest safety-concern point on the SI channel.

- Immediate: intense paresthesia/pain to 4th-5th fingers

**Clinical safety notes:**
- ALWAYS palpate the ulnar nerve cord before needling
- Insert the needle radial or ulnar to the nerve
- Brief paresthesia during insertion is COMMON and generally

- Avoid deep perpendicular insertion into groove center

**Vascular:** posterior ulnar recurrent artery — small caliber,
low risk.

**Overall:** HIGH risk due to ulnar nerve exposure. Requires
nerve palpation skill. FIRST point on SI rated HIGH (SI1-SI3
LOW, SI4 MODERATE, SI5-SI6 LOW, SI7 LOW-MODERATE).

- Spinal segments: C5-C7 (DRAMATIC SHIFT from SI8's C7-T1)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

  for frozen shoulder. Zheng et al. 2023 used pricking and
  cupping at this zone for frozen stage periarthritis — but
  SI9's individual contribution is not isolated.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SI9 individually. Appears in
most multi-point shoulder pain protocols but NEVER studied as a
single point. The Jianbo area approach (SI9+SI10+SI11, Zheng
et al. 2023) showed improvement for frozen stage periarthritis,
but this is regional treatment, not point-specific evidence.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**MODERATE risk.** Posterior shoulder with neurovascular
structures.

Precautions:
- Axillary nerve in quadrilateral space — deep/lateral
  insertion risks nerve contact
- Circumflex scapular and posterior circumflex humeral
  arteries — vascular risk at depth
- Glenohumeral joint capsule accessible at depth — avoid
  intra-articular penetration
- No pneumothorax risk (scapula shields thorax)

## SI10 — Nhu Du (臑俞, Nào Shū)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Upper Arm Transport" (臑俞) — 臑 (Nào, upper arm) + 俞 (Shū,
transport/point). Located on the posterior shoulder, directly
below the scapular spine, in the infraspinous fossa, when the
arm is adducted. **Meeting point** (交会穴) of the Small
Intestine channel with **Yang Wei Mai** (阳维脉) and **Yang
Qiao Mai** (阳跷脉) — the FIRST meeting point with
extraordinary vessels on the SI channel.

SI10 forms the lateral vertex of the **Jianbo area** triangle
(with SI9 inferior and SI11 medial). Part of the scapular
zigzag (SI9–SI15).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterior aspect of
the shoulder, with the arm adducted, directly below the
scapular spine, in the depression above SI9.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the scapular spine (bony ridge
across the scapula). SI10 is directly inferior to the lateral
end of the scapular spine, in the infraspinous fossa. SI9 is
inferior (posterior axillary fold level). SI11 is medial
(center of infraspinous fossa). SI10 sits at the lateral-
superior corner of the infraspinous fossa.

Needle depth: 10–20 mm perpendicular to oblique. Infraspinatus
provides adequate tissue depth. Scapular bone = safety floor
(protects against pneumothorax).

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Suprascapular nerve (C5-C6) — motor to infraspinatus. The
  suprascapular nerve passes through the spinoglenoid notch
  from supraspinous to infraspinous fossa at approximately
  this level.
- Lateral cutaneous branches of T1-T2 intercostal nerves may
  contribute superficial sensory supply.
- Spinal segments: C5-C6 (suprascapular nerve primary)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: circumflex scapular artery (ascending branch),
suprascapular artery (descending branch).

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous → infraspinatus (primary
muscle) → scapular periosteum (floor).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Meeting point (交会穴):** SI with Yang Wei Mai (阳维脉)
and Yang Qiao Mai (阳跷脉). Meeting points represent
locations where regular channel Qi intersects with
extraordinary vessel Qi. SI10 is the first SI point with
this designation.

Note: the exact extraordinary vessel attribution varies
between classical sources. Jiayi Jing lists Yang Wei Mai;
Deadman lists "meeting of SI, Yang Qiao Mai, and Yang Wei
Mai." Some modern compilations differ.

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI10 Naoshu (臑俞). Functions: activates
channel, benefits shoulder. Meeting point function connects SI
Qi with Yang Wei Mai (linking all Yang channels) and Yang Qiao
Mai (Yang motility). Primary indications: shoulder pain, arm
weakness, scapular pain, inability to raise the arm.

**2. Trigger point:** Infraspinatus TrP — classic trigger point
location in the infraspinous fossa. Refers pain to anterior
shoulder and lateral arm. SI10 is at the lateral portion, near
but not identical to TrP1 (more central, closer to SI11).
Partial correspondence. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in infraspinatus strain,
rotator cuff pathology (posterior component), scapular
dyskinesia. Moderate reactivity. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Amsa Marma (shoulder) — regional overlap.

**5. Head zone:** C5-C6 dermatome (shoulder region).
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Spiral Line via infraspinatus → rotator
cuff connection. Moderate station.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Scapular surface adequate for cupping.
Commonly cupped for shoulder/scapular pain. 🟡

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP partial, Ashi partial).
Slightly lower than SI9 — the infraspinous fossa is less
clinically distinctive as a palpation site than the posterior
axillary fold.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: shoulder pain, arm pain, inability to raise arm,
  scapular pain.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: shoulder pain, arm weakness, cold shoulder
  with wind invasion.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Benefits shoulder (PRIMARY):** shoulder pain, weakness,
  restricted movement — same shoulder cluster as SI9
- **Activates channel:** scapular pain, arm heaviness
- **Wind-damp:** shoulder pain with cold/wind (Yang Wei Mai
  connection — Yang Wei governs exterior defense)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- Part of the **Jianbo area** triangle (SI9+SI10+SI11).
  Functionally overlapping with SI9 and SI11 for shoulder/
  scapular conditions. In practice, SI11 (Tianzong, center
  of infraspinous fossa) is the most commonly selected of the
  three.
- The Yang Wei Mai meeting status is the theoretical
  distinguishing feature, but meeting point protocols are
  rarely the primary selection criterion in modern practice.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SI10 individually. Same as
SI9 — appears in shoulder protocols but never isolated. The
Jianbo area study (Zheng et al. 2023) included SI10 as part
of the triangular zone.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

LOW-MODERATE risk. Infraspinous fossa with scapular bone floor.

Precautions:
- Suprascapular nerve at spinoglenoid notch — deep medial
  insertion risks nerve contact
- Suprascapular artery accompanies the nerve — vascular risk
  at depth
- Scapular bone floor provides pneumothorax protection
- Standard perpendicular needling (10–20 mm) is generally safe

> pre-bifurcation at the lateral elbow) is striking: both He-Sea
> points access major nerve trunks at the elbow, suggesting the
> He-Sea anatomical position systematically selects for nerve
> trunk access. If this pattern holds across leg He-Sea points
> (SP9 at popliteal fossa, LR8 at medial knee), it would
> constitute a structural principle of He-Sea point placement.

> Corresponds to the point-free upper arm gap: no named SI point

> are explicitly grouped as a REGIONAL unit rather than selected

> traditional point system may function more as zone markers
> than as independently significant points — all three share
> the same primary indication (shoulder pain) with minimal

> **Obs. 25 — NEURAL ZONE 3 BEGINS: SCAPULAR INNERVATION:**
> Zone 1: C8-T1 pure (SI1–SI5, digital/palmar ulnar branches).
> Zone 2: C7-T1 (SI6–SI8, forearm/elbow ulnar trunk + posterior

> (hand → forearm/elbow → shoulder/scapula) and with brachial
> plexus organization (medial cord → posterior cord). The Zone
> 2→3 jump is dramatically larger than Zone 1→2 (1 segment vs

## SI11 — Thiên Tông (天宗, Tiān Zōng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Celestial Gathering" (天宗) — 天 (Tiān, celestial/heavenly) +
宗 (Zōng, ancestor/gathering). Three SI points carry the 天
prefix (SI11, SI16, SI17). SI11 is located at the center of
the infraspinous fossa — the anatomical midpoint of the
scapula's posterior surface. No special point category. SI11
is the clinical anchor of the scapular region — the most
commonly selected scapular acupoint in practice and the most
reliably tender palpation point on the scapula.

SI11 completes the **Jianbo area** (肩胛区) triangle as the
medial vertex (SI9 inferior, SI10 lateral-superior, SI11
center-medial). Among the three Jianbo points, SI11 has the
strongest cross-tradition validation: it precisely overlays
**infraspinatus trigger point 1** (TrP1) — one of the most
extensively documented trigger points in the myofascial
literature. A pronounced tender point is "almost universally
found here" in patients with shoulder, neck, and upper back
tension (Travell & Simons).

Classical sources also attribute **breast pain and insufficient
lactation** to SI11 — the same mammary indications as SI1 at
the opposite end of the channel, but at completely different
spinal segments (SI11: C5-C6 vs SI1: T1).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the scapular region, at
the junction of the upper and middle thirds of the distance
between the inferior angle of the scapula and the midpoint of
the spine of the scapula, in a depression on the infraspinatus.

Anatomical landmarks: with the patient seated, locate the
scapular spine (horizontal ridge) and the inferior angle of
the scapula. SI11 is at the junction of the upper 1/3 and
lower 2/3 of the line connecting the midpoint of the scapular
spine to the inferior angle. This corresponds to the center
of the infraspinous fossa. A depression is palpable,
especially with the arm crossed over the chest.

SI10 is superior (below the scapular spine). SI12 is above
the scapular spine (supraspinous fossa). SI9 is inferolateral
(posterior axillary fold).

Needle depth: 10–20 mm perpendicular or slightly oblique.
Scapular bone provides a solid floor — no pneumothorax risk.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Suprascapular nerve (C5-C6) — motor to infraspinatus.
  The suprascapular nerve enters the infraspinous fossa via
  the spinoglenoid notch at the lateral end of the scapular
  spine, then branches medially into the infraspinatus
  muscle body. SI11 lies over the distal motor branches.
- Lateral cutaneous branches from T2-T3 intercostal nerves
  provide superficial sensory supply.
- Spinal segments: C5-C6 (primary motor), T2-T3 (cutaneous)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: circumflex scapular artery (ascending branch),
suprascapular artery (descending branch through infraspinous
fossa).

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous fat (often thin) →
trapezius (lower fibers, variable) → infraspinatus muscle
(primary bulk, ~15–25 mm thick) → scapular periosteum (floor).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No special category. Standard channel point — the most
clinically significant SI point without a formal designation.

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
Meeting: No. Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI11 Tianzong (天宗). Functions: benefits
shoulder and scapula (PRIMARY), activates channel, clears heat
from breast, promotes lactation. Indications: scapular pain,
shoulder pain, arm pain, inability to raise arm, breast pain,
insufficient lactation, chest/lateral costal pain.

**2. Trigger point:** Infraspinatus TrP1 — STRONG correspondence.
This is a textbook myofascial trigger point (Travell & Simons):
located at the center of the infraspinous fossa, at the densest
part of the muscle belly. Referred pain pattern: deep aching
in the anterior shoulder and lateral arm, sometimes extending
to forearm and hand (radial side). "Almost universally found"
as a tender point in patients with shoulder/neck/upper back
tension. The STRONGEST trigger point correspondence on the SI
channel and one of the strongest in the Atlas. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Highly reactive. One of the most
reliably tender scapular palpation points in clinical
examination — consistent tenderness in rotator cuff pathology,
shoulder impingement, scapular dyskinesia, and postural
tension. Self-palpation by reaching over the opposite shoulder
frequently identifies this point. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Amsa Marma (shoulder region) — regional
overlap. Not point-specific.

**5. Head zone:** C5-C6 dermatome (lateral shoulder/arm).
The infraspinous fossa is supplied by C5-C6 motor (deep)
and T2-T3 cutaneous (superficial) — dual-segment coverage.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Spiral Line via infraspinatus →
subscapularis → serratus anterior chain. Back Functional
Line through latissimus dorsi → thoracolumbar fascia.
Moderate station — force transmission through rotator cuff.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data. The infraspinous
fossa has not been systematically mapped for neurogenic spots
(Kim 2017 focused primarily on extremity points).

**8. Cupping point:** Flat scapular surface is ideal for
cupping — one of the most commonly cupped body areas.
Cupping at SI11 for shoulder/scapular pain is standard in
both TCM and modern clinical practice. 🟡

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, TrP strong, Ashi strong,
Cupping). Same count as SI8 and SI9 but with qualitatively
STRONGER trigger point and Ashi correspondence. The
infraspinatus TrP1 match may be the strongest individual
TrP-acupoint correspondence on the SI channel.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Ling Shu (implied via channel trajectory): shoulder and
  scapular pain, arm heaviness.
- Jiayi Jing: shoulder and scapular pain, arm pain, inability
  to raise arm, chest and lateral costal pain, breast pain,
  insufficient lactation, cough with qi ascending.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: scapular pain (PRIMARY), shoulder pain,
  arm heaviness, breast pain/swelling, mastitis, asthma,
  chest tightness.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Benefits shoulder and scapula (PRIMARY):** scapular pain,
  shoulder pain, frozen shoulder, rotator cuff dysfunction,
  arm pain with restricted movement
- **Clears breast heat:** breast pain, mastitis, insufficient
  lactation — the BREAST CLUSTER with SI1 (both SI points
  with lactation indication)
- **Activates channel:** arm/elbow pain, lateral costal pain
- **Regulates qi:** chest tightness, asthma, cough

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- SI11 is the MOST COMMONLY SELECTED scapular acupoint —
  if a practitioner uses only one scapular point, SI11 is
  the default choice. Combined with SI9, SI10 for the Jianbo
  area protocol, or used alone for focal scapular treatment.
- In dry needling practice, the infraspinatus TrP at SI11
  location is one of the most frequently treated sites for
  shoulder pain, rotator cuff dysfunction, and referred
  anterior shoulder pain.
- The breast/lactation indication is used adjunctively
  (primary lactation points: CV17, ST18, SI1) but appears
  consistently in classical and modern source compilations.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SI11 as an individual point.

SI11 appears in most multi-point shoulder pain protocols but
has NEVER been studied as a single point with point-specific
controls. Ongoing RCT protocols (2026: Baduanjin + tuina for
chronic neck pain; acupressure + cervical movement for
cervical spondylosis) include SI11 as one of several
treatment points — results pending or not isolating SI11.

A sharp-hook acupuncture study for periarthritis (PMC4657093)
showed improvement with a protocol including scapular points,
but SI11's individual contribution was not isolated.

**Trigger point dry needling evidence (indirect):** The
infraspinatus TrP1 (anatomically corresponding to SI11) has
been studied in randomized trials for chronic shoulder pain.
Trigger point acupuncture at the infraspinatus showed pain
reduction and improved function in a preliminary RCT for
chronic shoulder pain (2013). However, this evidence applies
to the TRIGGER POINT location, not to the TCM acupoint
indication — the treatment rationale differs even though the
anatomical site overlaps.

All TCM-specific indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.
Trigger point indications at the same location: 🟡 Plausible
(supported by TrP research, not TCM-specific evidence).

**Evidence anchoring pattern continued:** SI11 joins the
scapular evidence desert — no individual point evidence for
SI9, SI10, or SI11. The scapular region is treated as a
zone, not as discrete evidence-bearing points.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No disease-state sensitization data specific to SI11. However,
the infraspinatus TrP1 literature demonstrates clear
sensitization under pathology: the trigger point becomes
MORE tender and MORE active with shoulder use, rotator cuff
dysfunction, and postural strain. This TrP sensitization
is the closest proxy for acupoint dynamic behavior at SI11
— the location reproducibly shifts from latent to active
under shoulder pathology.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Shoulder/scapular pain (segmentally explicable):**
Infraspinatus innervation via suprascapular nerve (C5-C6) →
dorsal horn → gate control for shoulder/scapular afferents.
The infraspinatus TrP mechanism is well-characterized:
sustained sarcomere contraction → energy crisis → local
ischemia → sensitized nociceptors → referred pain pattern
to anterior shoulder. Needling → local twitch response →
sarcomere release → pain resolution. This is the most
mechanistically understood effect at SI11.

**Breast/lactation (non-segmentally explicable):**
SI11 (C5-C6) has no segmental connection to mammary
innervation (intercostal T3-T5). SI1 (T1) is also
non-segmental for mammary effects — the ⚡ framework note
at SI1 proposed a hypothalamic-hormonal pathway. SI11's
mammary indication REINFORCES the non-segmental challenge:
two SI points at opposite ends of the channel (finger and
scapula), at different spinal segments (T1 and C5-C6),
both claiming mammary effects. Possible explanations:
(a) channel-level signaling (traditional), (b) supraspinal
pathway via ascending afferents → hypothalamus → prolactin,
(c) non-specific stress/relaxation effect, (d) coincidental
inclusion in classical records. No data distinguishes these.
→ Cross-ref: AP-06 SI1 ⚡ note

Gap: breast/lactation mechanism at SI11 (and SI1) is entirely
unexplained by segmental models. The core BSA framework
(A01, A07) does not account for mammary effects from C5-C6
stimulation.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 10–20 mm perpendicular into infraspinatus belly.
Scapular bone = depth limiter (safe floor). De qi: deep
aching/heaviness in the scapular region, often with referred
sensation to the shoulder or lateral arm. In TrP-informed
practice, the goal is to elicit a LOCAL TWITCH RESPONSE (LTR)
in the infraspinatus — visible fasciculation indicating
therapeutic sarcomere release.

EA: 2 Hz low-frequency at SI11 commonly used for chronic
scapular/shoulder pain. Sometimes paired with SI9 or SI10
(Jianbo area bilateral EA). No point-specific frequency data.

Dry needling: SI11 location is one of the most commonly dry-
needled sites in sports medicine and musculoskeletal practice.
Technique: perpendicular insertion, piston-like movement to
elicit LTR. Multiple insertions (fan technique) through
infraspinatus muscle fibers. May elicit referred pain to
anterior shoulder — confirms TrP activation.

Cupping: commonly applied to the infraspinous fossa area.
The flat scapular surface accommodates standard cup sizes.
Both retained cupping and sliding cupping are used for
scapular/shoulder pain.

Moxa: indicated for cold-damp shoulder patterns. Moxa stick
at SI11 for chronic scapular stiffness. No contraindication.

Acupressure: self-accessible by reaching over the opposite
shoulder with the hand. Pressure into the infraspinous fossa.
One of the most practical self-treatment sites — patients
frequently discover this tender point independently.

### An toàn

LOW risk. The infraspinous fossa provides one of the safest
needling environments on the body.

**Safety advantages:**
- Scapular bone floor prevents over-penetration — NO
  pneumothorax risk (unlike intercostal or paravertebral
  points)
- Infraspinatus muscle provides adequate tissue buffer
  (15–25 mm)
- No major vascular structures at standard needling depth
- No major nerve trunks at the point (suprascapular nerve
  branches are intramuscular)

**Minor precautions:**
- Suprascapular nerve branches within infraspinatus —
  deep aggressive needling may contact nerve branches,
  producing shoulder weakness or deep aching
- Circumflex scapular artery branches — minor vascular
  risk at depth
- Thin patients: reduced tissue buffer, adjust depth

**Overall:** LOW risk — the safest point in the scapular
SI series (SI9 MODERATE, SI10 LOW-MODERATE, SI11 LOW).
Scapular bone floor is the definitive safety feature.

## SI12 — Bỉnh Phong (秉风, Bǐng Fēng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Grasping Wind" (秉风) — 秉 (Bǐng, grasp/hold) + 风 (Fēng,
wind). The name references Wind pathology (风邪), one of the
six external pathogens in TCM — this point traditionally
expels wind from the shoulder and neck. Located in the
supraspinous fossa, directly above the scapular spine from
SI11. No special point category. SI12 crosses from the
infraspinous fossa (SI9–SI11) to the supraspinous fossa —
above the scapular spine.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the shoulder, in the
suprascapular fossa, in the depression superior to the
midpoint of the spine of the scapula, directly above SI11.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the scapular spine (horizontal
ridge). SI12 is directly above the midpoint of the scapular
spine, in the supraspinous fossa. A depression is palpable
when the arm is raised. SI11 is directly below (infraspinous
fossa). SI13 is medial (toward the vertebral border).

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular or oblique. Supraspinous
fossa is shallower than infraspinous fossa — less tissue depth.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Suprascapular nerve (C5-C6) — motor to supraspinatus.
  The nerve passes through the suprascapular notch (under
  the superior transverse scapular ligament) to enter the
  supraspinous fossa, then crosses the scapular spine via
  the spinoglenoid notch to reach infraspinatus. SI12 is
  in the supraspinous fossa segment of this course.
- Supraclavicular nerves (C3-C4) provide superficial
  sensory supply to the supraspinous region.
- Spinal segments: C5-C6 (motor), C3-C4 (cutaneous)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: suprascapular artery (transverse cervical artery
branch) — travels with the suprascapular nerve but ABOVE
the superior transverse scapular ligament.

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous → trapezius (upper fibers)
→ supraspinatus muscle → scapular periosteum (floor).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
No special category. Standard channel point.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI12 Bingfeng (秉风). Functions: expels
wind, benefits shoulder, activates channel. Indications:
shoulder pain, scapular pain, inability to raise arm,
numbness/heaviness of upper extremity.

**2. Trigger point:** Supraspinatus TrP — moderate
correspondence. Supraspinatus TrPs refer pain to the
lateral deltoid and mid-deltoid region. The TrP location
varies (often more lateral, near the musculotendinous
junction), making the SI12 match less precise than SI11's
infraspinatus TrP1 match. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in supraspinatus
tendinopathy, shoulder impingement syndrome. Less
consistently reactive than SI11 — the supraspinous fossa
is a smaller, less muscular area. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Amsa Marma (shoulder) — regional overlap.

**5. Head zone:** C5-C6 dermatome (motor), C3-C4 (cutaneous).
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Spiral Line through trapezius →
supraspinatus chain. Moderate station.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Supraspinous fossa is narrower than

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: shoulder pain, inability to raise arm, arm

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Expels wind from shoulder (PRIMARY):** shoulder pain,
  arm pain, upper extremity numbness — the "wind" concept

- **Activates channel:** scapular pain, restricted movement
- **Wind-damp conditions:** shoulder/neck stiffness with
  weather sensitivity

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- Used adjunctively in shoulder protocols, rarely selected

LOW-MODERATE risk. Supraspinous fossa with bony floor.

- Scapular bone floor provides pneumothorax protection
- Less tissue depth than infraspinous fossa — adjust
  needle depth accordingly for thin patients

## SI13 — Khúc Viên (曲垣, Qū Yuán)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Crooked Wall" (曲垣) — 曲 (Qū, curved/crooked) + 垣 (Yuán,

the medial scapular spine, or the "wall-like" quality of the
scapula itself. Located at the medial end of the supraspinous
fossa, in the depression at the medial end of the scapular
spine. No special point category. SI13 is the most medial
scapular SI point (closest to the vertebral border), marking
the channel's approach toward the spine before continuing to
SI14 and SI15 (near BL territory).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the shoulder, at the
medial end of the suprascapular fossa, in the depression on
the medial end of the scapular spine.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the scapular spine medially
toward its root at the vertebral (medial) border of the
scapula. SI13 is in the depression at this medial junction.
SI12 is lateral (center of supraspinous fossa). SI14 is
medial and inferior (near T1-T2 spinous process level,
approaching BL territory).

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular or oblique medially.
Scapular bone provides floor near the vertebral border.
Medial to the scapula's edge → potential loss of bony floor
if needle angles too medially.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Dorsal scapular nerve (C5) — motor to rhomboid minor
  and major. The dorsal scapular nerve descends along the
  medial border of the scapula under the rhomboids. SI13
  is near the superior portion of this course.
- Suprascapular nerve (C5-C6) — medial branches may reach
  the medial supraspinous fossa.
- Accessory nerve (CN XI) — motor to trapezius, which
  overlies this region.
- Spinal segments: C5-C6 (primary), CN XI (trapezius motor)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: dorsal scapular artery (deep branch of transverse
cervical artery) — descends along the medial scapular border.

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous → trapezius (upper/middle
fibers) → supraspinatus (medial portion) → rhomboid minor
(deep, medial border) → scapular periosteum.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
No special category. Standard channel point.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI13 Quyuan (曲垣). Functions: activates
channel, relaxes sinews, benefits shoulder and scapula.
Indications: scapular pain (especially medial/interscapular),
shoulder pain, stiffness of suprascapular region.

**2. Trigger point:** Rhomboid TrP or medial supraspinatus
TrP — partial correspondence. Rhomboid TrPs (medial scapular

**3. Ashi correspondence:** May be tender in interscapular

**4. Marma point:** Amsa Marma (shoulder) — regional overlap.

**5. Head zone:** C5 dermatome (medial scapular via dorsal

→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Back Line through upper
trapezius → rhomboid connection. Lateral Line may contribute.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Medial supraspinous fossa — possible
but narrow. More commonly, cupping spans the broader
interscapular area including SI13.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP partial, Ashi partial).
Same as SI12. Medial scapular points have less distinctive
cross-tradition identity compared to the infraspinous center
(SI11).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: scapular pain, shoulder pain, inability to
  raise arm, arm heaviness.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: interscapular pain, shoulder stiffness,
  upper back pain.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Benefits scapula (PRIMARY):** medial scapular pain,
  interscapular pain, shoulder stiffness
- **Activates channel:** arm pain, restricted shoulder
  movement
- **Relaxes sinews:** upper back tension, scapular stiffness

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- Least commonly selected of the scapular SI cluster.
  Rarely used alone — appears in comprehensive scapular

  Back-Shu points (paravertebral) than with medial
  scapular SI points.
- The medial scapular location blurs the boundary between
  SI and BL channel territories — SI14 and SI15 continue
  this convergence toward BL.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SI13 individually. One of
the least-studied scapular acupoints. Rarely appears even
in multi-point protocols — SI11 and lateral shoulder points
(LI15, TE14) are preferred.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

### An toàn

LOW-MODERATE risk. Medial supraspinous fossa near scapular
border.

Precautions:

- Loss of bony floor: if needle angles medially past the
  vertebral border of the scapula, the intercostal space

  angle to keep scapular bone as depth limiter.
- Dorsal scapular nerve along medial border — nerve contact
  possible with deep medial insertion
- Standard perpendicular needling (10–15 mm) with scapular
  bone contact is safe

## SI14 — Kiên Ngoại Du (肩外俞, Jiān Wài Shū)

外 (Wài, outer/external) + 俞 (Shū, transport/point). The
俞 suffix links SI14 to the Back-Shu transport point naming
convention, though SI14 is NOT a Back-Shu point — it uses the
same character but belongs to the SI channel, not the BL channel.
Located at the level of T1, 3 cun lateral to the midline — this
places SI14 at the same lateral distance as the **BL second
line** (BL41–BL54 territory). SI14 thus occupies BL TERRITORIAL
SPACE: the SI channel crosses into the paravertebral zone that
is conventionally BL territory.

No special point category. SI14 marks the end of the scapular
zigzag (SI9–SI14) and the channel's transition toward the neck.

to the lower border of the spinous process of the first thoracic
vertebra (T1), on the medial border of the scapula.

Anatomical landmarks: locate C7 (vertebra prominens) and T1
spinous processes. Measure 3 cun laterally from T1 — this
reaches the medial (vertebral) border of the scapula. SI14
lies in the musculature between the medial scapular border
and the spine.

SI13 is lateral (medial supraspinous fossa). SI15 is superior
(C7 level, 2 cun lateral). BL41 Fufen is at T2 level, same
3 cun lateral distance — SI14's nearest BL neighbor.

Needle depth: 10–15 mm oblique medially or laterally. Caution
with depth — lung apex is deep.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:

  to paravertebral skin.

  contribute at the medial scapular border.
- Accessory nerve (CN XI) — motor to trapezius (superficial
  layer).
- Spinal segments: T1-T2 (dorsal rami), C5 (dorsal scapular)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: dorsal scapular artery (transverse cervical branch),
intercostal arteries (posterior branches at T1-T2).

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous → trapezius (middle fibers)
→ rhomboid minor → posterior superior serratus → erector spinae
group → ribs/intercostal muscles (deep).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
No special category. Standard channel point.

Note: SI14 uses the 俞 (Shū) character shared with Back-Shu
points (背俞穴) but is NOT a Back-Shu point. The Back-Shu
system belongs exclusively to the BL channel (1.5 cun lateral).
SI14 at 3 cun lateral is at the BL second line distance.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI14 Jianwaishu (肩外俞). Functions:
activates channel, expels wind-cold, benefits shoulder and
back. Indications: shoulder and back pain, neck stiffness,
scapular pain.

**2. Trigger point:** Levator scapulae TrP (medial scapular
border attachment) and rhomboid TrPs — moderate correspondence.
Both refer pain to the medial scapular border and interscapular
area. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** May be tender in upper back pain,
interscapular pain, postural strain. Less distinctive than
scapular fossa points. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No specific correspondence at T1
paravertebral.

**5. Head zone:** T1-T2 dermatome (upper thoracic).
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Back Line through
trapezius/rhomboid chain.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Upper back surface adequate for cupping.
Commonly cupped for upper back/interscapular pain. 🟡

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP partial, Cupping).
The paravertebral zone has less distinctive cross-tradition
identity — BL Back-Shu points dominate this territory.

- **Benefits shoulder and back (PRIMARY):** upper back pain,

- **Activates channel:** scapular pain, arm heaviness

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- Rarely selected as a primary point. Upper back pain is more

makes a MAJOR directional change from posterior back to lateral
neck between SI15 and SI16.

Needle depth: 10–15 mm oblique. Lung apex risk at depth.

## SI16 — Thiên Song (天窗, Tiān Chuāng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Celestial Window" (天窗) — 天 (Tiān, celestial/heaven) + 窗
(Chuāng, window). The name directly encodes the **Window of
Heaven** (天牖穴) concept — SI16 is one of the original Window
of Heaven points from **Ling Shu Ch.2**. The ~10 Window of Heaven points
form a cervical ring around the neck at the body-head transition
zone (see AP-02 LI18 Obs. 26). SI16 occupies the **lateral
neck position** in this ring — posterior to the SCM, between
the anterolateral points (LI18, ST9) and the posterolateral
point (TE16).

SI16 is the SECOND Window of Heaven point documented in the
Atlas (after LI18) and the third 天-named point on the SI
channel (after SI11 天宗 and with SI17 天容 — SI has the MOST
天-prefixed points of any channel, 3 total).

SI16 is the first SI point on the NECK — marking a major
trajectory change from the posterior back (SI14–SI15) to the
lateral cervical region. The channel runs along the posterior
border of the SCM from SI16 upward.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral aspect of
the neck, on the posterior border of the sternocleidomastoid
(SCM) muscle, at the level of the laryngeal prominence
(Adam's apple).

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the posterior border of the SCM
at the level of the thyroid cartilage (laryngeal prominence).
SI16 is in the depression between the SCM and the anterior
scalene muscle. SI15 is on the posterior back (C7, 2 cun
lateral). SI17 is superior, below the angle of the mandible.
LI18 is on the ANTERIOR border of the SCM at the same level
— SI16 and LI18 are separated by the width of the SCM muscle.

Needle depth: 5–15 mm perpendicular, shallow. Carotid sheath
structures are deep to the SCM at this level.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Great auricular nerve (C2-C3) — sensory to ear, angle of
  mandible, and lateral neck. Emerges at the posterior border
  of SCM at approximately this level (Erb's point region).
- Lesser occipital nerve (C2) — sensory to posterior ear
  and lateral scalp.
- Accessory nerve (CN XI) — crosses the posterior triangle
  superficially. At SI16 level, CN XI has entered or is
  entering the posterior triangle after exiting the SCM.
- Cervical plexus branches (C3-C4) — sensory to lateral neck.
- Deep: vagus nerve (CN X) within the carotid sheath, phrenic
  nerve (C3-C5) on the anterior scalene surface.
- Spinal segments: C2-C4 (cervical plexus), CN XI (accessory)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural zone transition:** SI14–SI15 (T1-C7, dorsal rami) →
SI16 (C2-C4, cervical plexus) = second major neural transition
on the SI channel. The channel shifts from paravertebral dorsal
rami to the cervical plexus — a transition from spinal nerve
dorsal branches to ventral branches (cervical plexus is formed
by ventral rami of C1-C4).

Vascular: external jugular vein (crosses SCM superficially),
transverse cervical artery (superficial branch). Deep: carotid
sheath containing common carotid artery, internal jugular vein,
vagus nerve (CN X).

Tissue layers: skin → platysma → SCM (posterior border,
aponeurosis) → space between SCM and scalenes → deep cervical
fascia → carotid sheath (deep).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Window of Heaven (天牖穴)**

Classical source: **Ling Shu Ch.2 (本输 Běn Shū)**. SI16 is
listed as a Window of Heaven point in the ORIGINAL Ling Shu
texts — one of the classical Window of Heaven points (alongside ST9, LI18,
TE16, BL10, LU3 in various Ling Shu chapters).

SI16's position in the cervical ring (AP-02 LI18 Obs. 26):
**LATERAL** — between LI18/ST9 (anterolateral) and TE16
(posterolateral). The ring provides access to the critical
body-head transition zone from all angles:
- Anterior midline: CV22
- Anterolateral: ST9, LI18
- **Lateral: SI16** (this point)
- Posterolateral: TE16
- Posterior: BL10
- Posterior midline: GV16
- Submandibular: SI17 (debated)
- Medial arm (exception): LU3

SI16 accesses the lateral cervical neurovascular bundle from
the posterior SCM border — complementing LI18's access from
the anterior SCM border. Together, SI16 and LI18 "bracket"
the SCM muscle.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1, AP-02 LI18

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI16 Tianchuang (天窗). Functions: benefits
ears (PRIMARY), clears throat, descends rebellious Qi, calms
spirit. Window of Heaven function: treats conditions where Qi
ascends excessively or fails to flow between trunk and head
— sudden deafness, loss of voice, throat obstruction,
disorientation. Indications: deafness, tinnitus, sore throat,
loss of voice, facial edema, stiff neck, lockjaw.

**2. Trigger point:** SCM TrP (posterior border region) —
referred pain to ear, temporal area, and throat. The SCM
posterior border is a well-documented TrP site with ear/
hearing referral pattern that closely matches SI16's
traditional auditory indications. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Reactive in torticollis, acute
neck stiffness (especially rotational restriction). Tender
when SCM is taut or in spasm. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Manya Marma (मन्या, "pride/cervical") —
lateral neck over the carotid region. Classified as Sira Marma
(vascular) in Ayurveda, consistent with SI16's proximity to
the carotid sheath. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** C2-C3 dermatome. Cervical viscerotome for
larynx, pharynx — segmental support for throat/voice
indications.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line via SCM component.
SI16 is at the posterior SCM border — the boundary between
the Lateral Line (SCM) and the Deep Back Arm Line
(scalenes/posterior triangle).

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — cervical region with
major vascular structures deep. Cupping contraindicated on
lateral neck.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi, Marma). Same
as LI18 — the lateral neck has moderate cross-tradition
convergence due to vascular/muscular significance.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Ling Shu Ch.2 (本输): listed as Window of Heaven point
  for treating conditions of ascending Qi, deafness, throat
  obstruction.
- Jiayi Jing: sudden deafness, tinnitus, sore throat, loss of
  voice, neck stiffness, facial edema, lockjaw (trismus).
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: deafness (PRIMARY), tinnitus, sore throat,
  loss of voice, swollen cheek, stiff neck.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Benefits ears (PRIMARY):** sudden deafness, tinnitus,
  ear pain — the dominant indication, reflecting the great
  auricular nerve (C2-C3) territory which includes the ear
- **Benefits throat:** sore throat, loss of voice, goiter
- **Window of Heaven function:** disorientation, sudden
  onset sensory changes (hearing, vision)
- **Activates channel:** neck stiffness, lockjaw, facial
  edema

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- SI16 is primarily selected for EAR conditions — its
  traditional specialization within the Window of Heaven group. While
  most Window of Heaven points have general throat/sensory indications,
  SI16's great auricular nerve (C2-C3) location provides
  segmental justification for ear-specific effects.
- Combined with TE17 (Yifeng, behind the ear) and TE3
  (Zhongzhu, hand Shaoyang) for tinnitus/deafness.
- Combined with SI17 and LU11 for sore throat with swelling.
- In the Window of Heaven cervical ring, SI16 represents the LATERAL
  access point — relevant when lateral neck pathology or
  lateral cervical lymphadenopathy is present.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SI16 as an individual point.

SI16 appears occasionally in multi-point tinnitus and
deafness protocols but has never been studied in isolation.
The Window of Heaven group as a whole has no systematic
evidence for its traditional functions (ascending Qi
regulation, sensory restoration). This is consistent with
the Window of Heaven evidence pattern established at LI18: a coherent
traditional concept with zero modern validation.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

**Window of Heaven evidence profile:** 0/10 Window of Heaven points have
individual evidence for Window of Heaven-specific functions (ascending Qi
regulation, consciousness restoration, throat/sensory). The
entire Window of Heaven concept is an evidence desert. Some Window of Heaven points
have evidence for OTHER indications (ST9 for hypertension via
baroreceptor mechanism), but none for their Window of Heaven-specific role.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No disease-state sensitization data for SI16. The SCM
posterior border area becomes tender in acute torticollis
and cervicogenic headache — a clinical equivalent of
sensitization. Whether SI16 specifically (vs the broader
posterior SCM region) demonstrates point-specific
sensitization is unknown.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Ear indications (segmentally explicable):** The great
auricular nerve (C2-C3) provides sensory supply to the
external ear, lobule, and periauricular region. Needling
at SI16 stimulates C2-C3 afferents → dorsal horn → gate
control for ear-referred pain. The C2-C3 segmental level
also receives input from CN IX (glossopharyngeal) and CN X
(vagus) via auricular branches — spinal-cranial nerve
convergence at the dorsal horn may explain ear/throat effects
through a single segmental mechanism.

**Throat/voice (segmentally explicable):** C2-C4 cervical
plexus provides motor (via ansa cervicalis to infrahyoid
muscles) and sensory supply to the anterior neck/throat
region. Needling at SI16 may modulate throat function through
this segmental pathway.

**Window of Heaven — ascending Qi (speculative):** The
traditional Window of Heaven concept (treating conditions where Qi fails
to reach the head) maps to the anatomical bottleneck where
all ascending pathways converge (AP-02 LI18 Obs. 26). SI16's
deep access to the carotid sheath provides a theoretical
pathway to modulate vagal tone (CN X) and carotid blood flow
— but NO evidence connects SI16 needling to cerebral
perfusion or vagal modulation.

Gap: the Window of Heaven mechanism remains entirely speculative. The
cervical ring architecture provides an anatomical rationale
but no demonstrated pathway from needle stimulation to the
proposed effects.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 5–15 mm perpendicular, shallow. Insert at the
posterior border of the SCM, directing the needle into the
fascial space between SCM and scalenes. AVOID deep insertion
— carotid sheath structures (common carotid artery, internal
jugular vein, vagus nerve) are 15–25 mm deep at this level.
De qi: distension at the lateral neck, sometimes radiating to
the ear or throat — consistent with great auricular nerve
stimulation.

EA: NOT commonly applied at SI16 due to HIGH cervical risk.
If used, low intensity only. No frequency-specific data.

Moxa: indirect moxa or moxa stick at a distance. Direct moxa
inappropriate at the lateral neck (thin skin, vascular
structures).

Acupressure: accessible at the posterior SCM border.
Self-palpation by turning the head to identify the SCM
posterior border, then pressing into the depression behind it.
Used in self-treatment for neck stiffness and tinnitus.

### An toàn

**HIGH risk** — one of the highest-risk points on the SI
channel (alongside SI8 for different reasons).

**Vascular structures:**
- External jugular vein — crosses the SCM superficially.
  Visible and palpable in many patients. Identify and avoid.
- Deep: carotid sheath (common carotid artery + internal
  jugular vein + vagus nerve CN X) — 15–25 mm deep to the
  posterior SCM border. Deep insertion → major vascular/
  neural risk.

**Neural structures:**
- Accessory nerve (CN XI) — crosses the posterior triangle
  superficially. Injury → trapezius weakness/winging.
- Great auricular nerve (C2-C3) — superficial at Erb's point.
  Brief paresthesia to the ear is common and generally
  harmless.
- Phrenic nerve (C3-C5) — on anterior scalene surface, deep.
  Bilateral phrenic nerve injury → diaphragmatic paralysis.

**Safety protocol:**
- Palpate the external jugular vein and avoid
- Insert at the posterior SCM border, SHALLOW (5–15 mm)
- NEVER needle deeply toward the midline
- NO bilateral simultaneous deep needling (phrenic nerve risk)

**Overall:** HIGH risk. Same risk category as LI18. SI16 and
LI18 bracket the SCM from opposite sides — both require
extreme caution. Risk shifts from STRUCTURAL (pneumothorax
at SI14–SI15) to VASCULAR/NEURAL at SI16.

## SI17 — Thiên Dung (天容, Tiān Róng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Celestial Appearance" (天容) — 天 (Tiān, celestial) + 容
(Róng, appearance/countenance/face). Located posterior to the
angle of the mandible, on the anterior border of the SCM.
SI17 is the third 天-named point on the SI channel (SI11 天宗,
SI16 天窗, SI17 天容) — SI has the MOST 天-prefixed points of
any channel (3 total).

**Window of Heaven debate:** SI17 appears in SOME modern
compilations of Window of Heaven points (Deadman includes it) but is NOT
in the original Ling Shu Ch.21 listing (ST9, LI18, TE16, BL10,
LU3). Its Window of Heaven status is debated — some traditions list ~10 Window of Heaven
points including SI17, others limit the group to 8–9. This
makes SI17 the only Window of Heaven point with contested attribution in
the Atlas. If included, SI becomes the only channel with TWO
Window of Heaven points.

SI17 is the LAST point before the SI channel crosses to the
face (SI18, SI19). It marks the mandibular-to-facial
transition.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): posterior to the angle of
the mandible, in the depression on the anterior border of the
sternocleidomastoid muscle.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the angle of the mandible (the
bony corner where the mandibular ramus meets the body).
SI17 is in the depression just posterior and inferior to this
angle, on the anterior border of the SCM. SI16 is inferior
(lateral neck at laryngeal level). SI18 is superior and
anterior (zygomatic bone, on the face).

Needle depth: 5–15 mm perpendicular, shallow. Multiple
cranial nerves and major vessels at depth.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Great auricular nerve (C2-C3) — sensory to the angle
  of mandible and ear lobule.
- Facial nerve (CN VII) — marginal mandibular branch passes
  near the angle of mandible. Motor to lower facial muscles.
  VULNERABLE to needling injury.
- Deep: glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX) — sensory to pharynx,
  motor to stylopharyngeus. In the parapharyngeal space.
- Deep: vagus nerve (CN X) — within the carotid sheath.
- Deep: hypoglossal nerve (CN XII) — motor to tongue muscles.
  Passes through the submandibular region.
- Spinal segments: C2-C3 (great auricular), CN VII, IX, X,
  XII (cranial nerves)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural zone transition:** SI16 (cervical plexus C2-C4) →
SI17 (cervical plexus C2-C3 + cranial nerves CN VII, IX, XII).
The transition from spinal nerve dominance to CRANIAL NERVE
dominance begins here — SI17 is the first SI point where
cranial nerve motor branches are at primary risk.

Vascular: external carotid artery (ascending branch),
retromandibular vein, facial artery. The external carotid
bifurcation occurs near the mandibular angle.

Tissue layers: skin → platysma → parotid gland (lower pole)
→ SCM (anterior border) → deep cervical fascia →
parapharyngeal space (deep).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Window of Heaven — DEBATED STATUS**

SI17 is listed as a Window of Heaven point in Deadman (A Manual of
Acupuncture) and several modern compilations. However, it is
NOT in the original Ling Shu Ch.21 listing of 5 Window of Heaven points.
The Ling Shu Ch.2 listing (which includes SI16) does not
consistently include SI17.

If SI17 IS a Window of Heaven point, then SI has TWO Window of Heaven
points — the most of any channel. In the cervical ring model
(AP-02 LI18 Obs. 26), SI17 would occupy the SUBMANDIBULAR
position — the highest point on the ring, approaching the
mandible. This Atlas records the debate without resolving it.

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI17 Tianrong (天容). Functions: benefits
throat, clears heat, resolves swelling, calms spirit.
Indications: sore throat, tonsillitis, swelling of cheek,
mumps, deafness, tinnitus, neck stiffness, goiter.

**2. Trigger point:** Digastric muscle TrP (posterior belly)
and medial pterygoid TrP (deep to ramus) — partial
correspondence. Both refer pain to throat and submandibular
area. Less precise than SI16's SCM TrP match. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in acute tonsillitis,
submandibular lymphadenopathy, TMJ disorders (medial
component). Reactive when submandibular gland is swollen. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Manya Marma (cervical/carotid) —
regional overlap at the upper cervical vascular zone.

**5. Head zone:** C2-C3 dermatome (great auricular territory
at mandibular angle).
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Line via digastric →
hyoid → pharyngeal chain. Key junction in the Deep Front
Line's cervical segment.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — mandibular angle with
major vascular structures. Cupping contraindicated.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP partial, Ashi partial).
The submandibular area has less distinctive cross-tradition
identity than the lateral neck (SI16).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: sore throat, tonsillitis, swelling of cheek and
  submandibular region, deafness, tinnitus, neck stiffness.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: sore throat (PRIMARY), swelling below the
  mandible, mumps, goiter, deafness, loss of voice.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Benefits throat (PRIMARY):** sore throat, tonsillitis,
  pharyngitis — the dominant indication. Combined with LU10
  and LU11 for acute tonsillitis with toxic heat.
- **Resolves swelling:** submandibular swelling, mumps,
  scrofula (cervical lymphadenopathy), goiter
- **Benefits ears:** deafness, tinnitus — shared with SI16
  (great auricular nerve territory overlap)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- SI17 is primarily a THROAT point — selected for acute
  sore throat and tonsillitis, often combined with LU10
  and LU11 (heat-clearing + throat-benefiting protocol).
- Throat points are selected by location: SI17 for
  posterior/submandibular throat, CV22 for suprasternal,
  LI18 for lateral throat.
- The Window of Heaven function (if attributed) adds a broader role
  but is rarely the primary selection criterion.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SI17 individually. Appears
in some tonsillitis/pharyngitis protocols but never isolated.
Combined with LU10 and LU11 in classical prescriptions for
acute sore throat — widely taught but unstudied.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

### An toàn

**HIGH risk** — one of the highest-risk points on the SI
channel. Multiple cranial nerves and major vessels converge
at the mandibular angle.

Precautions:
- **Facial nerve (CN VII):** marginal mandibular branch at
  the mandibular angle. Injury → lower lip weakness/drooping
  (asymmetric smile). The most consequential nerve risk.
- **External carotid artery:** ascending branch and
  bifurcation. Palpate for pulsation — avoid the pulse.
- **Retromandibular vein:** deep to parotid, vascular risk
- **Deep cranial nerves (CN IX, X, XII):** in the
  parapharyngeal space. Deep insertion toward the midline
  risks contact with glossopharyngeal, vagus, or hypoglossal
  nerves.
- **Parotid gland:** lower pole overlies the SCM at the
  mandibular angle. Deep insertion into the parotid is
  painful.

**Safety protocol:**
- SHALLOW insertion only (5–15 mm)
- Palpate for arterial pulsation before needling
- NEVER insert deeply toward the midline
- Monitor for facial asymmetry after needling (CN VII)

**Overall:** HIGH risk. Three consecutive HIGH-risk SI points
(SI16, SI17, plus SI8 at elbow) — reflecting the convergence
of major neurovascular structures at the cervical-mandibular
transition.

> **Obs. 30 — SI-BL TERRITORIAL OVERLAP: SI14–SI15 IN

> body trajectories. The paravertebral overlap is where their

> fully documented (after LI18). In the cervical ring model

> on the posterior border of the SCM, complementing LI18 on the
> anterior border. Together, SI16 and LI18 "bracket" the SCM,
> providing bilateral access to the carotid sheath from opposite
> sides. The Window of Heaven ear-specific indications at SI16 (vs throat-

> territories: great auricular nerve C2-C3 at SI16 (ear sensory)

> but their segmental anatomy predicts DIFFERENT specializations
> within the group — a segmental differentiation that the
> classical tradition may not have explicitly recognized.

> Window of Heaven status is the only contested Window of Heaven attribution in the Atlas.
> If included, SI becomes the only channel with TWO Window of Heaven points
> (SI16 lateral neck + SI17 submandibular). The 天-prefix cluster

> especially strong association with the "celestial/heavenly"

> significance (celestial = head/consciousness-related) or

> CRANIAL NERVE TRANSITION:** SI14–SI15 introduce Zone 4: T1-C7
> dorsal rami (paravertebral posterior body). SI16 introduces

## SI18 — Quyền Liêu (颧髎, Quán Liáo)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Cheekbone Crevice" (颧 = cheekbone/zygomatic bone, 髎 = bone
crevice/foramen). Located in the depression at the lower border
of the zygomatic bone, directly below the outer canthus of the
eye. The 髎 character denotes a bony depression or foramen —
the same naming convention as the Eight Liao (八髎, BL31–BL34)
at the sacral foramina. SI18 is the FIRST SI point on the face
proper — after ascending from the neck (SI16–SI17), the channel
crosses the mandible and arrives at the zygomatic bone. SI18
marks the transition from cervical-cranial nerve territory
(Zone 5: C2-C4 + CN VII/IX/XII) to trigeminal territory
(Zone 6: CN V).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): directly below the outer
canthus of the eye, in the depression at the lower border of
the zygomatic bone.

Anatomical landmarks: locate the outer canthus of the eye.
Drop directly downward to the lower border of the zygomatic
arch. SI18 is in the depression on the inferior zygomatic
border. SI17 is inferior and posterior (mandibular angle).
SI19 is posterior (pre-auricular, at tragus level).

Needle depth: 5–10 mm perpendicular or oblique inferiorly.
Some sources recommend oblique insertion toward the masseter
for facial pain protocols.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Zygomaticofacial nerve (branch of the zygomatic nerve,
  from CN V2 maxillary division) — cutaneous sensory to the
  malar (cheekbone) region. Emerges through the
  zygomaticofacial foramen on the zygomatic bone.
- Infraorbital nerve (CN V2) — terminal branches from the
  infraorbital foramen extend laterally toward the zygomatic
  area.
- Motor: zygomatic and buccal branches of the facial nerve
  (CN VII) — innervating zygomaticus major and minor muscles.
- Spinal segments: N/A — cranial nerve territory (CN V2, CN VII)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular:
- Transverse facial artery (branch of superficial temporal
  artery) — runs below the zygomatic arch
- Zygomaticoorbital artery (small branch)

Tissue layers: skin → orbicularis oculi (inferior fibers) →
zygomaticus major origin → periosteum of zygomatic bone.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
Back-Shu: No. Front-Mu: No. Eight Confluent: No.
Hui-Meeting: No.

**Meeting point:** SI18 = meeting of Hand Taiyang (SI) and
Hand Shaoyang (TE) in some classical texts (recorded in
Deadman). Not universally accepted. If correct, this is the
first SI-TE meeting point before the definitive triple-
channel meeting at SI19.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI18 Quanliao (颧髎). Functions: expels
Wind, clears Heat, relieves pain, reduces swelling. Selected
for facial conditions in the zygomatic region.

**2. Trigger point:** zygomaticus major TrP — referred pain
to the malar area and along the zygomatic arch. Partial
correspondence. Masseter TrP2 refers to the zygomatic area
from below. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** tender in TMJ dysfunction
(masseter hypertonicity), trigeminal neuralgia attacks in
V2 distribution, and facial spasm. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** no direct correspondence at the
zygomatic bone.

**5. Head zone:** V2 (maxillary) dermatome — same dermatomal
territory as LI20 (nasolabial). Both points access the
maxillary division of the trigeminal nerve.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** no significant fascial line
involvement at the zygomatic bone.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** no specific data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — face.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP partial, Ashi).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: deviation of mouth (口喎, facial
  paralysis), toothache, swelling of the cheek
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: facial pain (面痛), facial edema,
  lockjaw (牙关紧闭), twitching of eyelids

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Facial paralysis (PRIMARY):** Bell's palsy — combined
  with ST4, ST6, LI4, TE17 in standard facial paralysis
  protocols. SI18 targets the middle face (zygomatic zone)
  while ST4-ST6 target the lower face (mandibular zone).
- **Trigeminal neuralgia:** V2 (maxillary) distribution pain
- **Toothache:** upper teeth (maxillary nerve territory)
- **Facial swelling:** sinusitis-related malar edema

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- SI18 is a LOCAL facial point — selected for conditions in
  the zygomatic/malar region specifically. Less commonly used
  than the adjacent auditory points (SI19, TE21, GB2) or
  the nasal point (LI20).
- Standard facial paralysis protocol: ST4 + ST6 + SI18 +
  TE17 + LI4 + ST36. SI18 provides zygomatic-level
  stimulation within this multi-point layout.
- Some TMJ protocols include SI18 alongside SI19 and ST7
  for comprehensive jaw-face coverage.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SI18 individually. Included
in facial paralysis (Bell's palsy) protocols but never
isolated from multi-point treatments. Appears in some thread
embedding acupuncture protocols for TMJ alongside SI19, GB2,
and TE21.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

### An toàn

**LOW-MODERATE risk.** The zygomatic bone provides a
protective floor beneath the point.

Precautions:
- **Infraorbital foramen:** located ~1 cm superior to SI18,
  below the infraorbital margin. Deep superior-directed
  needling could reach the orbital floor. Direction should
  be perpendicular or inferior.
- **Transverse facial artery:** runs below the zygomatic
  arch — bruising is the main consequence of vessel puncture.
- **Parotid duct (Stensen's duct):** crosses the masseter
  approximately at the zygomatic arch level. Deep lateral
  insertion could contact the duct.

**Overall:** LOW-MODERATE risk. Zygomatic bone floor provides
protection. Lower risk than adjacent SI17 (HIGH, mandibular
angle) and SI19 (MODERATE, TMJ/parotid). The safest SI face
point.

## SI19 — Thính Cung (听宫, Tīng Gōng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Palace of Hearing" (听 = hear/listen, 宫 = palace/court) —
one of the most descriptively named acupoints in the system.
Located anterior to the tragus, in the depression formed when
the mouth is opened, between the tragus and the TMJ condyle.
SI19 is the **TERMINAL POINT (止穴)** of the Small Intestine
channel — the final destination of the Hand Taiyang trajectory
from the little fingertip (SI1) to the ear, spanning the widest
anatomical range of any documented channel. The name directly
documents the primary function: treating ear and hearing
conditions — parallel to LI20 "Welcome Fragrance" documenting
olfactory function at the LI terminus. Both channel-terminal
names encode the sensory function of their destination.

SI19 is also the **MEETING POINT of three channels**: Hand
Taiyang (SI), Hand Shaoyang (TE), and Foot Shaoyang (GB). This
triple-channel convergence produces the densest multi-channel
concentration dedicated to a single sensory function (hearing)
in the acupuncture system. The three pre-auricular auditory
points — SI19, TE21 (Ermen 耳门, "Ear Gate"), and GB2 (Tinghui
听会, "Auditory Convergence") — are arranged vertically within
~2 cm around the tragus, each on a different channel but all
targeting the same organ (ear) via the same nerve
(auriculotemporal, V3).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): anterior to the tragus and
posterior to the condyloid process of the mandible, in the
depression formed when the mouth is opened.

Anatomical landmarks: with the mouth OPEN, the mandibular
condyle translates anteriorly, creating a palpable depression
between the condyle and the tragus. SI19 is in this depression.
TE21 (Ermen) is superior to SI19, at the supratragic notch.
GB2 (Tinghui) is inferior to SI19, anterior to the intertragic
notch. The three points form a vertical stack around the tragus.
SI18 is anterior and superior (zygomatic bone).

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular, with the mouth OPEN to
separate the condyle from the ear canal. De Qi commonly felt
as fullness or aching radiating into the ear canal — consistent
with auriculotemporal nerve stimulation.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Auriculotemporal nerve** (branch of V3, mandibular division
  of CN V) — the PRIMARY nerve at SI19. This single nerve
  provides sensory innervation to: (1) the tragus and anterior
  ear, (2) the external auditory canal, (3) the tympanic
  membrane (outer surface), (4) the TMJ capsule, (5) the
  temporal region. Connects SI19's surface location directly
  to its primary treatment targets (ear + TMJ).
- Branches of the facial nerve (CN VII) — the main trunk exits
  the stylomastoid foramen posterior to SI19. Temporal and
  zygomatic branches cross the pre-auricular area superficially.
- Great auricular nerve (C2-C3) — sensory to the ear lobule
  and lower auricle.
- Spinal segments: primarily CN V3 (trigeminal), with C2-C3
  contribution
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

**Neural zone transition:** SI16–SI17 (C2-C4 cervical plexus +
CN VII/IX/XII) → SI18–SI19 (CN V trigeminal) = third cranial
nerve transition. Zone 6: trigeminal territory. The channel's
final two points share V2 (SI18) and V3 (SI19) innervation —
both branches of the SAME parent nerve (CN V), but different
divisions with different dermatomal coverage.

Vascular:
- Superficial temporal artery — passes anterior to the ear,
  palpable at the root of the zygomatic arch. The main vessel
  to palpate before needling.
- Superficial temporal vein — accompanies the artery
- Deep: maxillary artery — within the infratemporal fossa

Tissue layers: skin → parotid gland (tail/accessory lobe) →
TMJ capsule → mandibular condyle (mouth closed) or articular
disc space (mouth open).

Receptor environment: the TMJ capsule is densely innervated
with mechanoreceptors and nociceptors from V3. The auricular
region has high innervation density — the anatomical basis for
the auricular acupuncture microsystem (AP-16).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
Back-Shu: No. Front-Mu: No. Eight Confluent: No.
Hui-Meeting: No.

**Channel terminal point (止穴):** SI19 is the final point on
the SI channel. From SI19, qi flow transitions to **BL1
(Jingming)** at the inner canthus of the eye — establishing
the **Hand Taiyang (SI) → Foot Taiyang (BL)** same-name
continuity. Compare with LI20 → ST1 (Hand Yangming → Foot
Yangming). Both terminus-to-start transitions are face-to-face
connections between same-name pairs: Yangming crosses at the
nose (LI20→ST1), Taiyang crosses at the eye (SI19→BL1).

**Meeting point (交会穴):** SI19 = meeting of **Hand Taiyang
(SI)**, **Hand Shaoyang (TE)**, and **Foot Shaoyang (GB)**.
Three channels converge at this single point — the most
channels meeting at any point on the SI channel and one of the
few triple-channel meetings in the system. All three channels
pass through the pre-auricular region in their classical
trajectories.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SI19 Tinggong (听宫). Functions: benefits
the ears (利耳), opens ear orifices (通耳窍), calms the spirit
(安神). THE primary acupoint for ear conditions in classical
TCM. Combined with TE21 and GB2 as the "Three Pre-auricular
Points" (耳前三穴) — a standard grouping in clinical education
and practice.

**2. Trigger point:** lateral pterygoid TrP — referred pain to
the TMJ and pre-auricular area. Masseter TrP1 — referred pain
to the ear region. The TMJ trigger point complex includes
multiple muscles (lateral pterygoid, masseter, temporalis) that
refer pain to the pre-auricular area. Moderate correspondence.
🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** strongly tender in TMJ dysfunction,
otitis externa, and pre-auricular lymphadenitis. 🟡 Palpation of
the TMJ condyle movement (mouth open/close) is a standard
diagnostic maneuver — SI19's location directly on the TMJ makes
it a reliable ashi point for jaw disorders.

**4. Marma point:** Vidhura Marma (विधुर) — located below and
behind the ear, near the mastoid process. Classified as Snayu
Marma (ligament/tendon). PARTIAL correspondence — Vidhura is
more posterior than SI19 but shares auditory indications.
Different anatomical position, overlapping functional target.
→ Cross-ref: C04

**5. Head zone:** V3 (mandibular) dermatome — auriculotemporal
nerve territory. The ear canal and tympanic membrane receive
sensory supply from V3 — SI19 is in the SAME dermatomal
territory as its internal treatment target, providing direct
segmental correspondence. Same principle as LI20's V2
correspondence with the nasal mucosa.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** no significant fascial line involvement
at the pre-auricular area.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** no specific data. The pre-
auricular area may show neurogenic changes (C-fiber
sensitization) in TMJ inflammation, but not formally studied.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — pre-auricular face.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi, Head zone). Strong
convergence for a facial point. The TMJ/pre-auricular area is
a universal treatment target across traditions for ear and jaw
conditions.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Ling Shu: described in the SI channel trajectory as the
  terminal point. Meeting of three channels documented.
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: deafness (耳聋), tinnitus (耳鸣), ear
  discharge (耳中脓), toothache, manic psychosis (癫狂)
- Nanjing: listed as an ear treatment point
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: ear pain, deafness, tinnitus, lockjaw
  (牙关紧闭), loss of voice, epilepsy (癫痫)

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Benefits the ears (PRIMARY):** deafness (all types),
  tinnitus, otitis media/externa, ear discharge, ear pain
- **Opens the jaw:** TMJ dysfunction, lockjaw, dental pain
- **Calms the spirit:** mania, epilepsy — a classical spirit
  indication at the "palace" point. The 宫 (palace) naming
  may reference the Ling Shu concept of sensory "palaces"
  (五官) as doorways to the spirit.

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- SI19 is the CENTRAL point in the "Three Pre-auricular Points"
  (SI19 + TE21 + GB2) — THE standard protocol for all ear
  conditions. Virtually every acupuncture treatment for tinnitus,
  deafness, or ear pain includes some or all three points.
- Point differentiation in practice: SI19 is the most commonly
  selected when only ONE ear point is used; TE21 is added for
  "opening the ear gate"; GB2 is added for additional Shaoyang
  influence.
- TMJ protocol: SI19 + ST7 (Xiaguan, at TMJ) + ST6 (Jiache,
  masseter) + LI4. SI19 accessed with mouth OPEN to expose the
  condylar depression.
- The "single needle through multiple points" (透刺) technique
  threads one needle from TE21 through SI19 to GB2 — treating
  all three pre-auricular points with one insertion (Ekofitranto
  2026 case report).
- Bilateral SI19 for bilateral tinnitus; unilateral for
  unilateral hearing loss — following laterality principle.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**Tinnitus:**
- Systematic review and meta-analysis (Kim et al. 2020, 12
  RCTs): acupuncture for tinnitus. SI19 identified as one of
  the most frequently used acupoints across trials. Overall:
  no significant difference vs control on visual analogue scale
  (8 studies), but combination with medication showed higher
  effective rate vs medication alone (3 studies, RR favorable).
  Evidence quality: LOW (GRADE). SI19 never isolated from
  multi-point protocols. 🟡

**Sudden sensorineural hearing loss (SSNHL):**
- Systematic review and meta-analysis (2024, PMC, 12 RCTs):
  acupuncture combined with medication vs medication alone for
  SSNHL. SI19 among the top 4 most frequently used acupoints.
  Combined treatment showed significantly higher effective rate
  for hearing improvement. Evidence quality: LOW to MODERATE.
  SI19 not isolated from multi-point protocols. 🟡

**TMJ dysfunction:**
- Thread embedding acupuncture RCT protocol (2025, preprint):
  SI19 in the core protocol for temporomandibular disorder
  alongside SI18, GB2, TE21. Registered but results not yet
  published. 🔴
- SI19 is routinely included in TMJ acupuncture protocols but
  no study has isolated its individual contribution.

**Single-needle threading technique:**
- Ekofitranto et al. (2026, SAGE): single needle threaded
  TE21→SI19→GB2 for tinnitus. Case report — described
  technique feasibility and immediate symptom improvement in
  one patient. No controlled comparison. 🔴

Overall evidence: **🟡 Plausible** for tinnitus and SSNHL (as
part of multi-point protocols). SI19 is the most frequently
selected auditory acupoint across RCTs, but its individual
contribution has never been isolated from the standard "Three
Pre-auricular Points" combination — the same "protocol inclusion
without isolation" pattern documented at LI15 (shoulder, AP-02
Obs. 27) and LI20 (rhinitis). The auriculotemporal nerve (V3)
provides a biologically plausible local mechanism connecting the
surface point to its auditory treatment targets.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No formal sensitization data using the dynamic acupoint
methodology (Chen Rixin, Kim 2017). Clinically, the pre-
auricular area shows increased tenderness in TMJ dysfunction
and during acute ear infections — consistent with disease-state
sensitization. The TMJ capsule is densely innervated with
mechanoreceptors (V3), and capsular inflammation lowers
palpation thresholds at SI19. The mouth-open positioning
itself changes tissue tension at the point — a form of
mechanical provocation that exposes the condylar depression
and alters receptor loading.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Auditory effects (tinnitus, hearing loss):**
LOCAL trigeminal pathway — the auriculotemporal nerve (V3) at
SI19 directly innervates the external ear canal and the outer
surface of the tympanic membrane. Needle stimulation activates
V3 afferents → trigeminal nucleus (brainstem) → potential
modulation of auditory processing areas. The tympanic membrane
receives dual innervation: V3 (outer surface, via
auriculotemporal nerve) and CN IX/X (inner surface, middle ear
mucosa) — SI19 accesses the V3 component specifically.

**TMJ effects:**
DIRECT mechanical — SI19 is AT the TMJ capsule. Needling with
mouth open places the needle adjacent to the TMJ capsule,
directly stimulating capsular mechanoreceptors and nociceptors.
The lateral pterygoid muscle inserts on the articular disc —
deep needling may reach this muscle, paralleling trigger point
dry needling for lateral pterygoid dysfunction.

**Spirit-calming (mania, epilepsy):**
Mechanism unknown. Classical theory associates sensory "palaces"
(五官) with spirit function. No modern evidence or proposed
pathway. Trigeminal-vagal connections (V3 → NTS via auricular
vagal afferents) could theoretically provide an autonomic
route, but this is speculative.

Gap: the primary gap is isolating SI19's individual contribution
from the three-point combination. The auriculotemporal nerve
provides a biologically plausible pathway for ear effects, but
the specific mechanism (anti-inflammatory? vascular? neural
modulation of auditory processing?) is not characterized.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

**Châm (needling):**
- MOUTH OPEN is essential — moves the condyle anteriorly,
  creating the depression and reducing risk of condylar
  impaction.
- Perpendicular insertion, 10–15 mm.
- De Qi: commonly felt as fullness or aching radiating into
  the ear canal — consistent with auriculotemporal nerve
  stimulation.
- Some practitioners direct the needle slightly posteriorly
  toward the ear canal for maximal auditory effect.

**EA (electroacupuncture):**
- SI19 + TE21 or SI19 + GB2 bipolar EA: used in tinnitus and
  SSNHL protocols across multiple RCTs. Frequency: typically
  2 Hz (low frequency) for chronic tinnitus. Intensity: below
  comfort threshold (facial nerve proximity).
  → Cross-ref: B01 S5.2

**Threading technique (透刺):**
- Single needle threaded TE21→SI19→GB2: one insertion covers
  all three pre-auricular points. Described in case reports
  for tinnitus. Requires precise angulation along the
  vertical pre-auricular line.

**Acupressure:**
- Self-applicable with mouth open — press in the depression
  anterior to the tragus. Used in self-care tinnitus protocols.
  The mouth-open requirement makes sustained pressure awkward.
  → Cross-ref: B02 S4

**Moxa:**
- Indirect moxa (stick) used in some traditions for chronic
  or cold-type ear conditions. Caution: proximity to hair
  and ear canal. Not the primary modality for SI19.
  → Cross-ref: B08

### An toàn

**MODERATE risk.** The TMJ area has significant neurovascular
structures, but the condylar depression (mouth open) provides
a relatively safe needling window.

Precautions:
- **Superficial temporal artery:** palpable anterior to the
  ear at the zygomatic arch root. PALPATE before needling —
  avoid the arterial pulse. Hematoma is the most common
  adverse event at this point.
- **Facial nerve (CN VII):** main trunk exits the stylomastoid
  foramen posterior to SI19. Temporal and zygomatic branches
  cross superficially. Deep posterior-directed insertion risks
  contact. Injury → facial weakness.
- **External auditory canal:** medial to SI19. Needling
  directed medially could enter the ear canal — direction
  should be perpendicular to the skin surface.
- **Parotid gland:** superficial lobe overlies the pre-
  auricular area. Needle passes through or near glandular
  tissue — generally well tolerated.
- **TMJ disc:** with mouth open, the needle may contact the
  articular disc. Utilized intentionally in some dry needling
  protocols for TMJ but requires precision.

**Safety protocol:**
- MOUTH OPEN during needling (moves condyle, creates space)
- Palpate superficial temporal artery BEFORE insertion
- Perpendicular insertion — avoid medial or posterior angulation
- Moderate depth only (10–15 mm)

**Overall:** MODERATE risk. Lower than the cervical SI points
(SI16 HIGH, SI17 HIGH) but higher than SI18 (LOW-MODERATE,
zygomatic bone protection). The mouth-open technique is both
a clinical requirement AND a safety measure — it simultaneously
creates the treatment window and reduces structural risk.

> Shaoyang (TE), and Foot Shaoyang (GB). All three are Yang
