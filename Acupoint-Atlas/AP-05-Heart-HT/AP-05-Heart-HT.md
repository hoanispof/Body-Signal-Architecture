# AP-05 — Heart Meridian (HT) — Hand Shaoyin (手少阴心经)

> **Status:** Phase 2 COMPLETE — 9/9 points.
>
> **Meridian overview:** The Heart channel (Hand Shaoyin) is the shortest
> of the 12 regular meridians with only 9 points. It originates internally
> in the heart, emerges at the axilla (HT1 Jiquan) → runs along the
> posteromedial arm → medial forearm (ulnar side) → wrist crease
> (HT7 Shenmen) → palm → ends at the radial nail corner of the little
> finger (HT9 Shaochong). Paired with Small Intestine (Hand Taiyang).
> Peak time in Chinese clock: 11:00–13:00.
>
> **Total points:** 9 standard | Entries: HT7 (Phase 1), HT3, HT8 (Batch 1), HT5, HT6 (Batch 2), HT9, HT4, HT1, HT2 (Batch 3)

## HT7 — Thần Môn (神門, Shén Mén)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**
The "Spirit Gate" — most important point for Shen (heart-mind) disturbances
in TCM. Yuan-Source AND Shu-Stream (Earth) point of the Heart channel.
Primary point for insomnia and anxiety across both acupuncture and
acupressure traditions. Kim et al. 2013 identified a distinctive mechanism:
HT7 signals via A-beta fibers (Pacinian/Meissner corpuscles) through the
dorsal column-medial lemniscal pathway, NOT through pain fibers (A-delta/C)
— unique among well-studied acupoints. Kim et al. 2017 confirmed HT7 as
a neurogenic inflammatory spot matching cardiac disease state.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): at the wrist joint, on the radial side
of the tendon of flexor carpi ulnaris, in the depression at the proximal
border of the pisiform bone, on the transverse crease of the wrist.

Anatomical landmarks: on the palmar surface of the wrist, at the wrist
crease, in the small depression between the tendon of flexor carpi ulnaris
(ulnar side) and the tendons of flexor digitorum superficialis (radial
side), just proximal to the pisiform bone. Palpate the FCU tendon by
having the patient flex the wrist against resistance with ulnar deviation
— HT7 is immediately radial to this tendon.

Needle depth range: 3–8 mm perpendicular insertion. Shallow point — the
ulnar artery and ulnar nerve are in close proximity.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Dorsal cutaneous branch of ulnar nerve (C8-T1) — primary sensory
  innervation of the skin at this location
- Ulnar nerve proper passes deep to HT7 through Guyon's canal
  (between pisiform and hook of hamate) — mixed nerve carrying motor
  and sensory fibers to hand intrinsics and digits 4–5
- Palmar cutaneous branch of ulnar nerve — superficial sensory branch
  to hypothenar skin
- Spinal segments: C8-T1
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Kim et al. 2013 (PLoS ONE): HT7 effects are mediated specifically by
large myelinated A-beta fibers of the ulnar nerve (NOT A-delta or C
fibers). Pacinian corpuscles (~200 Hz) and Meissner corpuscles (~50 Hz)
are the primary mechanoreceptors. Severing the ulnar nerve completely
abolishes HT7 effects; blocking C/A-delta fibers with resiniferatoxin
does NOT alter effects.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.1, A02 S4.3

Fascial plane: between the tendons of flexor carpi ulnaris and flexor
digitorum superficialis at the wrist crease. Superficial fascia over the
flexor retinaculum. The point accesses the loose connective tissue of
the carpal tunnel entrance from the ulnar side.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: ulnar artery passes immediately lateral (radial side) to HT7.
Close proximity — vascular risk at deeper insertions. Ulnar venous
tributaries in the superficial tissue.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (thin at wrist crease)
- Flexor retinaculum / deep fascia
- Between flexor carpi ulnaris tendon (ulnar) and flexor digitorum
  superficialis tendons (radial)
- Deep: ulnar nerve and artery in Guyon's canal

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Shu-Stream (Earth)** — 3rd of the Five Shu points on the
Heart channel. For Yin channels, the element sequence is Wood → Fire →
Earth → Metal → Water (Jing-Well → Ying-Spring → Shu-Stream → Jing-River
→ He-Sea). Heart = Fire organ in Five Element theory. Earth is the "child"
of Fire in the generating cycle. Therefore HT7 (Earth point on Fire
channel) is the **sedation point** of the Heart per Mother-Child
tonification/sedation rule (Nanjing Chapter 69): sedate excess Heart
Fire → HT7. This aligns with clinical use: HT7 calms excess (anxiety,
palpitations, mania) rather than tonifying deficiency.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Yuan-Source: **Yes.** Yuan-Source point (原穴) of the Heart channel. For
the 6 Yin channels, the Yuan-Source point coincides with the Shu-Stream
point at the same location. This differs from Yang channels, where
Yuan-Source and Shu-Stream are separate points (e.g., LI4 is Yuan but LI3
is Shu-Stream). HT7 thus serves a dual function: Shu-Stream (Five Shu
therapeutic logic) AND Yuan-Source (access to Heart's Original Qi).
Combined with Luo-Connecting point of paired channel in Host-Guest
technique (主客配穴法): Heart as host → HT7 (Yuan) + SI7 (Luo of paired
Small Intestine).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Luo-Connecting: No (HT5 Tongli is Luo-Connecting of Heart)

Xi-Cleft: No (HT6 Yinxi is Xi-Cleft of Heart — used for acute cardiac
conditions, hemostasis per Yin Xi-Cleft → blood disorder rule)

Back-Shu: No (BL15 Xinshu is Back-Shu of Heart, T5 — see AP-07)

Front-Mu: No (CV14 Juque is Front-Mu of Heart)

Eight Confluent: No

Hui-Meeting: No

Special designations: none among the standard special categories (not in
Four Command Points, Ma Dan-yang Twelve Stars, or Sun Si-miao Ghost
Points). However, HT7's status as the "Spirit Gate" gives it a de facto
special role as the primary Shen-calming point across all TCM traditions
— functionally equivalent to a command designation for mental and
emotional conditions.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** HT7 Shenmen (神門, "Spirit Gate"). Yuan-Source AND
Shu-Stream of Heart. Primary function: calms the Shen (heart-mind),
nourishes Heart blood, clears Heart fire, opens Heart orifices. The single
most important point for all Shen-related disturbances in TCM. "Heart
houses the Shen" (心藏神) — Heart is the organ system governing
consciousness, cognition, emotional regulation, and sleep in TCM theory.

**2. Trigger point:** Flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU) distal trigger point /
tendon attachment point (Dorsher correspondence mapping). Referred pain
pattern: ulnar side of wrist, palmar/volar aspect. HT7 at the radial
border of the FCU tendon corresponds anatomically — within 1 cm. The main
FCU belly trigger point is in the proximal forearm; the distal attachment
point near the pisiform provides the closest spatial match to HT7.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Commonly tender on palpation in patients with
anxiety-related forearm tension, ulnar-sided wrist pain, and stress-
related muscle guarding. The wrist crease ulnar depression is a frequent
site of spontaneous tenderness, especially in patients reporting insomnia
or emotional distress.

**4. Marma point:** Manibandha Marma (मणिबन्ध) — wrist Marma in the
107-point Ayurvedic system (Sushruta Samhita, Frawley). Classified as
Sandhi (joint) Marma. HT7 is directly at the wrist crease — closer
anatomical correspondence (~1 cm) than PC6 (2 cun proximal, noted as
"proximal boundary" in AP-09). Both TCM and Ayurveda identify the wrist
as a therapeutically significant zone for cardiac and emotional conditions.

**5. Head zone:** C8-T1 dermatome (ulnar nerve territory at wrist).
**PARTIAL CONVERGENCE for cardiac effects:** Heart viscerotome = T1-T5.
The T1 component of the ulnar nerve sensory field overlaps with the upper
boundary of cardiac autonomic innervation → partial segmental basis for
cardiac effects. Same dermatomal situation as PC6 (also C8-T1, also T1
overlap with cardiac). For insomnia/anxiety effects: NO segmental basis —
these are brain-mediated conditions requiring supraspinal pathways.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2, S4.4

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Arm Line (DFAL, Myers 2001). This
myofascial line runs: anterior chest → pectoralis minor → coracobrachialis
→ medial intermuscular septum → medial upper arm → forearm flexors
(including flexor carpi ulnaris, flexor digitorum superficialis/profundus)
→ hand. HT7 at the FCU tendon is a key distal station on this line. The
Heart meridian trajectory closely parallels the DFAL along the medial
arm — one of the strongest meridian-Anatomy Train correspondences
documented by Dorsher 2009.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** **CONFIRMED.** Kim et al. 2017
(Scientific Reports): hypertensive rats showed neurogenic inflammatory
spots concentrated at C8-T2 dermatome (spinal segments of cardiac
innervation extending via stellate ganglion connections to C8). 67% of
these spots matched acupoint locations, specifically including HT7
alongside PC6 and PC7. This is direct objective evidence that cardiac
disease creates measurable neurogenic inflammation at HT7's anatomical
location — the body "activates" this acupoint in response to cardiac
pathology.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3, C04 S3.2.3

⚡ Framework note: Kim 2017 showed cardiac neurogenic spots at C8-T2
range (not limited to T1-T5 as assumed in the PC6 entry AP-09). The C8
extension is likely via stellate ganglion connections (C7-T1). Both HT7
and PC6 are at C8-T1, and both are NAMED as matched cardiac neurogenic
spots. The PC6 entry (AP-09) rated neurogenic spot correspondence as
"weak" based on T1-T5 assumption — this should be upgraded to "confirmed"
during Phase 5 review. Cross-ref: AP-09 neurogenic spot section.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable. Wrist surface too small and
anatomically unsuitable for standard cupping application.

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Marma, Anatomy
Trains, Neurogenic Spot). Head zone provides partial convergence at T1
only (not counted as full match, consistent with PC6 scoring in AP-09).
Very high convergence — matches or exceeds most Phase 1 limb points.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:

Neijing Lingshu (Chapter 1, Ben Shu — 本输): identified as Shu-Stream
point of the Heart channel. Heart "stores the Shen" (心藏神) — HT7 as
the Yuan-Source point is the primary access point to the Heart's Shen
function.

Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing (针灸甲乙经, Huangfu Mi, 259 CE): palpitations,
fear, fright, insomnia, forgetfulness, manic behavior (癫狂), anxiety
with chest oppression, dry throat, cardiac pain, shortness of breath.

Nanjing (难经, Classic of Difficulties): elaborates Yuan-Source theory for
Yin channels — the Shu-Stream and Yuan-Source coincide at the same point.
HT7 accesses the Heart's source Qi for constitutional treatment of Heart
patterns.

Zhenjiu Dacheng (针灸大成, Yang Jizhou, 1601): insomnia (不寐),
palpitations (心悸), dream-disturbed sleep (多梦), mania and withdrawal
(癫狂), poor memory, tongue stiffness with aphasia, cardiac pain,
jaundice with lack of appetite.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, Manual of Acupuncture):
- Calms the Shen — insomnia, anxiety, fright, palpitations, poor memory,
  mania, depression, epilepsy
- Nourishes Heart blood — palpitations, dizziness
- Clears Heart fire — mouth/tongue ulcers, bitter taste, insomnia with
  vivid dreams
- Benefits the throat and voice — aphasia, tongue stiffness, stuttering
- Regulates and strengthens the Heart — cardiac pain, chest tightness

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật: HT7 is the single most consistently
prescribed point for insomnia and anxiety across ALL TCM traditions and
modern clinical practice. It appears in virtually every acupuncture
protocol for sleep disorders and most protocols for anxiety, palpitations,
and emotional disturbances. In modern clinical rankings, HT7 is among the
top 10 most frequently prescribed acupoints worldwide. B02 identifies HT7
+ auricular Shen Men as the most studied point combination for sleep
quality.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1, B02 S4

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Insomnia / sleep quality — 🟡 Plausible**

Systematic review with HT7 as sole point (European Journal of Integrative
Medicine, 2017 — 4 RCTs, 436 participants): limited evidence that
acupressure and moxibustion at HT7 alone improves insomnia symptoms with
some persistence of effect. Also decreased anxiety and improved general
wellbeing. Limitation: only 4 trials met criteria; poor methodological
quality; notably, no needle acupuncture RCTs on HT7 alone qualified for
inclusion.

Nordio & Romanelli 2008 (Minerva Medica, double-blind RCT, N=40, 20
nights): H7 acupressure wrist device worn overnight vs placebo device.
Improved sleep quality (PSQI) and reduced anxiety (STAI) vs placebo.
Melatonin metabolite rhythm normalized in higher percentage of treated
patients — suggests HT7 stimulation modulates the suprachiasmatic nucleus
→ pineal gland → melatonin pathway.

fMRI evidence (Ning et al. 2022, Frontiers in Neurology, N=30 healthy):
bilateral HT7 acupuncture after sleep deprivation modulated inter-network
functional connectivity — specifically somatomotor-ventral attention,
somatomotor-default mode, and somatomotor-frontoparietal connections. HT7
reversed specific brain connectivity disruptions caused by sleep
deprivation.

Synergy study (Qiao et al. 2023, Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine,
rat model): HT7 alone activated PKA/CREB pathway in hippocampus; SP6
alone activated BDNF/TrkB pathway. Combined HT7+SP6+GV20 produced
synergistic effects activating BOTH pathways with superior outcomes.

B02 evidence (acupressure): 41 studies, 3,680 inpatients (2025 SR) and
23 RCTs, 1,689 patients (2024 SR) show HT7 + auricular Shen Men + SP6
combination improves PSQI scores, total sleep time, sleep efficiency,
and sleep onset latency.

C02 evidence: acupuncture for insomnia PSQI improvement = -2.60 (🟡 in
condition-response map). No therapy reaches 🟢 for sleep as primary
outcome — sleep improvements across therapies are often secondary to pain
or anxiety reduction.

Confidence: 🟡 — one SR (sole-point, positive but small and low quality),
one positive double-blind RCT (N=40, wrist device), fMRI evidence of
brain mechanism, and multiple positive meta-analyses for HT7 as part of
sleep protocols. However: the sole-point SR included only acupressure/moxa
(no needle RCTs qualified); multi-point protocol evidence cannot isolate
HT7's specific contribution.

**2. Anxiety — 🟡 Plausible**

B02 meta-analysis (Chen SR et al. 2023 — 24 RCTs, 2,537 participants):
acupressure for preoperative anxiety SMD **-1.30** (95% CI: -1.54 to
-1.06). Very large effect. Points used: primarily HT7, PC6, EX-HN3
(Yintang). Individual HT7 contribution not isolated.

Au et al. 2015 (PubMed): SR of acupressure for anxiety. Medium effect
size for immediate relief. HT7 among most commonly selected points.

Dhanushya Devi et al. 2025 (American Journal of Chinese Medicine, RCT):
needling HT7 + GV20, 20 min/session × 10 days. Reduced anxiety in
younger and middle-aged GAD patients without affecting cardiovascular
parameters.

Du et al. 2022 (PubMed): EA at HT7+SP6 reduced serum norepinephrine and
dopamine levels, improved sleep quality, reduced anxiety and depression
scores in insomnia patients.

**NEGATIVE evidence:** Fleckenstein et al. 2018 (PLoS ONE, randomized
placebo-controlled pilot, N=25 male medical students): bilateral HT7
acupuncture vs sham laser acupuncture for test anxiety. NO significant
group differences — STAI-S p=0.927, salivary cortisol p=0.811, alpha-
amylase p=0.325. Conclusion: treatment-specific effects not demonstrated;
reductions may reflect nonspecific therapeutic attention.

Confidence: 🟡 — positive as part of multi-point protocols (large effect
for preoperative anxiety), but HT7 as sole point showed NULL result in
one small but well-designed pilot (Fleckenstein 2018). The pattern
parallels other high-evidence acupoints: large effects vs no treatment,
modest-to-null vs sham. HT7 likely contributes to anxiety reduction but
its specific contribution is amplified by combination and therapeutic
context.

**3. Cardiac autonomic regulation — 🟡 Plausible**

Huang et al. 2015 (Acupuncture in Medicine, RCT, N=120, 4 groups: HT7
verum, non-penetrating sham at HT7, sham point acupuncture, no-acupuncture
control): HT7 verum group showed higher VLF, LF, and HF power during
stimulation vs controls. Effects not sustained post-acupuncture. Primarily
parasympathetic mechanism.

Liu et al. 2009 (preclinical, hypertensive rats): HT7 reduced systolic and
diastolic blood pressure and improved HRV.

Preclinical cardiac protection: Jiang et al. 2020 — HT7 decreased ST
segment changes in acute MI rats (via ferroptosis inhibition); Cai et al.
2007 — increased heart rate and contractility in acute ischemia rabbits.

Neural tracing evidence: co-labeled neural signals from mouse ventricular
wall and HT7 region found at ALL levels of CNS (cerebral cortex,
hypothalamus, midbrain, pons, medulla oblongata) — demonstrating a
literal neural pathway connecting HT7 anatomical region, heart, and brain.

Cardiac pathway mechanism: EA at HT7-HT5 → hippocampus → NTS → vagus
nerve → heart (Heliyon review, 2024). The NTS transmits acupuncture
signal to hippocampus; vagal signal then relayed to heart for rate and
rhythm modulation.

Confidence: 🟡 — one well-designed 4-arm RCT (N=120) showing acute
parasympathetic activation with sham comparison, preclinical cardiac
protection data, and neural tracing evidence. Limited by: effects not
sustained post-treatment; no systematic review for HT7-specific cardiac
outcomes; preclinical findings not replicated in human cardiac studies.

**4. Substance abuse — 🔴 Unvalidated (clinically)**

Strong preclinical evidence (Kim et al. 2013, PLoS ONE + follow-up
studies): HT7 suppresses cocaine-induced, morphine-induced, AND ethanol
self-administration in rats. Mechanism: ulnar nerve A-beta fibers →
cuneate nucleus (dorsal column pathway) → modulates GABAergic neurons in
ventral tegmental area (VTA) → suppresses dopamine release in nucleus
accumbens (NAc). Also suppresses c-Fos overexpression and enhances
GABAergic inhibition in mesolimbic circuits.

Severing the ulnar nerve COMPLETELY blocks all HT7 effects on substance-
seeking behavior (Kim 2013). This is the clearest anatomical demonstration
of pathway dependence for any acupoint's behavioral effects.

No human systematic reviews or RCTs for HT7 alone in substance abuse. The
NADA protocol (auricular, not body acupuncture) is the clinical standard
for addiction acupuncture.

Confidence: 🔴 — exceptional preclinical evidence (mechanism fully mapped:
peripheral nerve → brainstem relay → specific limbic circuit), but no
clinical translation. Preclinical → clinical gap remains wide for
addiction applications.

**5. Emergence agitation (pediatric) — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Mihara et al. 2023 (PLoS ONE — MA, 6 RCTs, 489 pediatric patients): all
6 studies stimulated HT7. Pooled RR = **0.62** (95% CI: 0.26–1.48) — NOT
statistically significant. Evidence quality rated "very low." Bilateral
HT7 may be more effective than unilateral (Nakamura et al. 2018: unilateral
HT7 = no effect, 28% vs 24%, p>0.99).

Confidence: 🔴 — MA pooled effect non-significant; evidence quality
very low.

**Sham comparison note:** HT7 shows the same pattern as other high-
evidence acupoints: positive effects in multi-point protocols vs no
treatment, but modest-to-null specific effects when isolated as sole point
vs sham (Fleckenstein 2018). The Huang 2015 cardiac study is an
exception — HT7 verum showed significant HRV changes vs non-penetrating
sham. This is consistent with the 3-layer sham resolution (AP-00 §3.3):
HT7's therapeutic effects involve multiple simultaneous mechanisms, with
the point-specific contribution being one component alongside therapeutic
context effects.

**Negative evidence recorded:** Fleckenstein 2018 test anxiety (sole
point, null result) and Mihara 2023 emergence agitation (MA non-
significant) per AP-00 §3.2 rule 4.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization: Kim et al. 2017 demonstrated that cardiac
disease (hypertension) creates neurogenic inflammatory spots at HT7's
anatomical location (C8-T2 dermatome, 67% matching acupoints including
HT7). This is among the strongest evidence for any acupoint that disease
states create measurable physiological changes at specific point
locations — consistent with the dynamic acupoint model (AP-00 §1.2).
The mechanism: cardiac visceral afferents → dorsal horn convergent
neurons → antidromic activation of somatic C fibers → CGRP + Substance P
release → neurogenic inflammation at skin surface (Kim 2018: these
neuropeptides underlie acupoint electrical properties).

Clinical observation: HT7 is often more tender on palpation in patients
with insomnia, anxiety, or cardiac symptoms — consistent with the
sensitization model. However, specific published data on HT7 pressure-
pain threshold changes under pathology are limited.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon), A07 S4.3

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at HT7:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): **UNIQUE FIBER TYPE** — HT7 activates
  large myelinated A-beta fibers through Pacinian (~200 Hz) and Meissner
  (~50 Hz) corpuscles (Kim et al. 2013). This is fundamentally different
  from most studied acupoints which primarily engage A-delta/C fibers for
  therapeutic effects. Both superficial (1 mm) and deep (3 mm) insertion
  equally effective — consistent with A-beta mechanoreceptor activation
  rather than deep-tissue nociceptor engagement.
- A02 (Autonomic Nervous System): cardiac autonomic regulation via NTS →
  hippocampus → vagus pathway. Parasympathetic upregulation (increased HF
  power on HRV, Huang 2015). HPA axis modulation: downregulates ACTH and
  cortisol.
- A04 (Vascular-Lymphatic): ulnar artery in close proximity. Local
  vascular effects at wrist.
- A06 (Chemical Signaling): reduces norepinephrine and dopamine release;
  increases serotonin and melatonin. Modulates suprachiasmatic nucleus →
  pineal gland → melatonin pathway for sleep regulation. PKA/CREB pathway
  activation in hippocampus (Qiao 2023, preclinical).
- A07 (Dermatome): C8-T1 sensory field partially overlaps cardiac
  autonomic innervation at T1 → partial segmental basis for cardiac
  effects. Confirmed by Kim 2017 neurogenic spot data.

**Central mechanism — dorsal column-medial lemniscal pathway:**

Kim et al. 2013 established the definitive peripheral pathway for HT7:

```
Step 1: Needling/pressing HT7 activates A-beta fibers of ulnar nerve
        via Pacinian and Meissner corpuscles in wrist tissue
Step 2: A-beta afferents ascend via DORSAL COLUMN (fasciculus cuneatus)
        — NOT spinothalamic tract
Step 3: Synapse at cuneate nucleus in medulla
Step 4: From cuneate nucleus, signals modulate:
        a) VTA GABAergic neurons → suppresses dopamine in NAc
           (reward/motivation circuit)
        b) Hippocampal pathways → memory/emotional regulation
        c) NTS → vagal output → cardiac modulation
Step 5: Downstream effects:
        - Mesolimbic dopamine regulation → calming, anti-addictive
        - HPA axis modulation → ↓ cortisol, ↓ stress response
        - SCN/melatonin → sleep regulation
        - Vagal cardiac → parasympathetic activation

Key evidence:
- Ulnar nerve severing COMPLETELY blocks all HT7 effects (Kim 2013)
  = peripheral pathway is necessary, not optional
- Blocking C/A-delta fibers does NOT alter effects (Kim 2013)
  = mechanism is NOT pain-fiber-dependent
- Both 1 mm and 3 mm depth equally effective (Kim 2013)
  = A-beta mechanoreceptor activation, not depth-dependent nociception
- fMRI: HT7 produces WIDEST deactivation pattern among tested
  acupoints (Hu et al. 2025, N=51) — broader than ST36 or KI4
  = distinct cortical "fingerprint" for each acupoint
```

**7th distinct mechanism pattern documented across Phase 1:**

```
  # │ Point(s)  │ Mechanism                      │ Afferent     │ Central relay
  ──┼───────────┼────────────────────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────
  1 │ ST36      │ PROKR2 vagal-adrenal           │ A-delta/C    │ NTS/DMV
  2 │ PC6       │ Median nerve → NTS → GABA      │ A-delta      │ NTS → DMV
  3 │ LI4       │ PAG/RVM descending modulation  │ A-beta/delta │ PAG/RVM
  4 │ SP6       │ Dual S2-S4 + PAG               │ Mixed        │ Sacral + PAG
  5 │ BL67      │ UNKNOWN                        │ Unknown      │ Unknown
  6 │ Back-Shu  │ Somatovisceral reflex          │ A-delta/C    │ Segmental
  7 │ HT7       │ Dorsal column → cuneate →      │ A-BETA       │ Cuneate nucleus
    │           │ limbic modulation              │              │
```

HT7 is unique in using A-beta fibers (touch/proprioception) rather than
A-delta/C fibers (pain/nociception). This means HT7's mechanism operates
through the discriminative touch pathway, not the pain pathway — which
explains why acupressure (non-painful touch) at HT7 can be effective, and
why both shallow and deep insertion produce equal results.

**Contrast with PC6 — two wrist points, two different pathways:**
PC6 and HT7 are both at C8-T1, both at the wrist, both used for cardiac
and emotional conditions. Yet they use different nerves (median vs ulnar),
different fiber types (A-delta vs A-beta), and different central pathways
(NTS → GABA for PC6; cuneate → limbic for HT7). Their clinical overlap
may reflect anatomical proximity (shared spinal segments) rather than
shared mechanism.

**Gap:** The dorsal column pathway has been mapped in detail for substance
abuse behavior (Kim 2013), but the specific relay stations for insomnia
and anxiety effects are less defined. The fMRI evidence (widespread
cortical deactivation, inter-network connectivity modulation after sleep
deprivation) supports supraspinal action, but the anatomical path between
cuneate nucleus and cortical effects for sleep/anxiety requires further
mapping. Also: Kim 2013 used rat models — human replication of the A-beta
specificity finding is needed.

⚡ Framework note 1: HT7 represents the 7th distinct mechanism pattern in
Phase 1 and the FIRST to use A-beta fibers (dorsal column-medial lemniscal
pathway) rather than A-delta/C fibers (spinothalamic/pain pathway). If
generalizable, this means different acupoints can engage DIFFERENT sensory
modalities (touch vs pain) for therapeutic effects — not all acupuncture
works through "pain fiber" activation. This extends A01 S2.1 discussion
of peripheral nerve fiber types and challenges the assumption that De Qi
(A-delta-mediated) is universally required for acupuncture efficacy.
For HT7, De Qi sensation may be incidental rather than mechanistically
necessary.

⚡ Framework note 2: fMRI evidence shows acupoint-specific cortical
"fingerprints" — HT7 produces the widest cortical deactivation pattern
among tested acupoints (Hu 2025), different from ST36's pattern. This
provides neuroimaging evidence for acupoint specificity BEYOND peripheral
anatomy — different acupoints produce different brain activation
signatures. Extends A09 S2.2 convergence model with cortical-level evidence.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 3–8 mm perpendicular (shallow — wrist crease, close to
  ulnar artery and nerve). Some classical texts describe oblique insertion
  toward HT3 (medial) for 5–8 mm.
- De Qi: moderate — typically produces heaviness, distention, or mild ache
  at wrist, sometimes radiating along ulnar forearm or toward little
  finger. Per Kim 2013, HT7's mechanism does NOT require nociceptive
  De Qi — A-beta activation (touch/pressure sensation) is sufficient.
- EA frequency: 2 Hz preferred for calming/anxiolytic effects (Han
  Jisheng model: 2 Hz → enkephalin/beta-endorphin). Du 2022: EA at HT7+SP6
  reduced norepinephrine and dopamine. Frequency-dependent effects at HT7
  specifically are under-studied compared to ST36 and LI4.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02):
- B02 Tier 2 point (10–50 acupressure-specific RCTs).
- Self-accessible: contralateral thumb presses into depression at ulnar
  wrist crease. Easy to locate by palpating FCU tendon.
- Pressure: moderate sustained pressure, 30–60 seconds per application.
- Wrist device (Nordio 2008): H7 compression device worn overnight —
  passive sustained stimulation during sleep.
- HT7 + auricular Shen Men = most studied acupressure point combination
  for sleep quality (B02 synthesis).
- Given Kim 2013 A-beta finding, acupressure may be particularly well-
  suited for HT7: surface pressure activates the same mechanoreceptors
  (Pacinian, Meissner) identified as HT7's primary transducers. This
  may explain why HT7 has positive acupressure evidence when many other
  acupoints show weaker acupressure vs acupuncture effects.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4, S10

Cứu (B08):
- Included in sole-point SR for insomnia (European Journal of Integrative
  Medicine 2017): moxa at HT7 showed positive effects for sleep.
- Indirect moxa applicable (mild warming). Direct moxa less common at
  this thin-skinned wrist location.
→ Cross-ref: B08

Dry needling (B11):
- FCU distal trigger point: needle near pisiform attachment of FCU. Local
  twitch response possible but often minimal at this tendinous location.
- Effects limited to local wrist pain — does NOT reproduce the calming,
  insomnia, or cardiac effects that require supraspinal pathways.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

So sánh: for insomnia and anxiety, acupressure (wrist device or manual)
may be comparably effective to acupuncture at HT7, given the A-beta
mechanism (Kim 2013): surface pressure activates the same fiber type as
needle insertion. HT7 is thus an ideal candidate for patient self-care
applications. For cardiac autonomic effects, EA may provide stronger
stimulus. For substance abuse applications (preclinical only), both
superficial and deep insertion are equally effective (Kim 2013).

### An toàn

Vascular risk: **ulnar artery** passes immediately radial to HT7. Risk
of arterial puncture at deeper insertions (>8 mm). Apply firm pressure
post-needling for 1–2 minutes. Avoid deep perpendicular insertion —
shallow perpendicular (3–5 mm) or oblique is safer. Higher risk in
patients on anticoagulation.

Neural risk: **ulnar nerve** passes deep to HT7 through Guyon's canal.
Excessive depth could contact the nerve trunk — causing electric shock
sensation radiating to digits 4–5. Risk is LOW with standard 3–8 mm depth
but requires awareness. Avoid aggressive needle manipulation at this point.

Pneumothorax risk: none (wrist location).

Pregnancy: **not contraindicated.** HT7 is not among the traditionally
"forbidden" pregnancy points (LI4, SP6, BL60, BL67, GB21). Safe for use
during pregnancy for insomnia and anxiety management.

Infection: standard clean needle technique. Thin skin at wrist crease
requires care with needle gauge selection.

Overall safety: HT7 is a safe point with LOW complication risk when
standard depth guidelines (3–8 mm) are followed. The primary concern is
ulnar artery proximity — shallow insertion and post-needling pressure are
the key precautions.

## HT3 — Thiếu Hải (少海, Shào Hǎi)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Lesser Sea" — He-Sea (Water) point of the Heart channel. Water
controls Fire in the Five Element control cycle, making HT3 the
primary point for sedating Heart Fire excess. One of the most
important Heart channel points for mental disorders (mania,
depression, schizophrenia). Medial elbow location near the cubital
tunnel = direct ulnar nerve access. Classical bloodletting point
at the cubital fossa veins.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): when the elbow is flexed, HT3
is at the midpoint between the medial end of the transverse cubital
crease and the medial epicondyle of the humerus.

Anatomical landmarks: with the elbow flexed to approximately 90°,
locate the medial epicondyle of the humerus. HT3 is in the depression
just anterior to the medial epicondyle, between the insertions of the
pronator teres (radial side) and the biceps brachii tendon (lateral).
Palpate with the elbow flexed — the depression is most evident in
this position.

Needle depth range: 10–15 mm perpendicular insertion. The ulnar nerve
runs in the cubital tunnel posterior to the medial epicondyle — needle
ANTERIOR to the epicondyle to avoid the nerve.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Medial cutaneous nerve of forearm (C8-T1) — primary sensory
  innervation of the skin at the medial elbow
- Ulnar nerve passes through the cubital tunnel (between the
  medial epicondyle and olecranon) — posterior and slightly deep
  to HT3. The nerve is superficial here and vulnerable; HT3 is
  positioned anterior to avoid it
- Median nerve passes through the cubital fossa more laterally
  (under the bicipital aponeurosis) — not directly at HT3
- Spinal segments: C8-T1
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: superficial fascia over the medial epicondyle. Deep
to the point: antebrachial fascia covering the pronator teres origin.
The common flexor tendon origin (medial epicondyle) anchors multiple
forearm flexor muscles.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: inferior ulnar collateral artery (branch of brachial artery)
supplies the medial elbow region. Basilic vein crosses superficially —
visible for bloodletting. Brachial artery is more lateral in the
cubital fossa (not directly at HT3 but within the region).
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (moderate thickness at elbow crease)
- Pronator teres (humeral head origin from medial epicondyle)
- Brachialis muscle (deep)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **He-Sea (Water)** — 5th and deepest of the Five Shu points
on the Heart channel. For Yin channels: Wood → Fire → Earth → Metal →
Water (Jing-Well → Ying-Spring → Shu-Stream → Jing-River → He-Sea).
Heart = Fire organ. Water is the element that CONTROLS Fire in the Five
Element control cycle (克, Ke cycle). Therefore HT3 (Water point on
Fire channel) is the point for sedating Heart Fire through the control
cycle — distinct from HT7 (Earth/sedation via generating cycle) and
HT8 (Fire/horary — same-element clearing).

Ling Shu chapter 1 (Ben Shu): "the He point is where Qi enters deeply,
like water flowing into the sea." He-Sea points on Yin channels treat
fullness/excess of the Zang organ.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Yuan-Source: No (HT7 is Yuan-Source)

Luo-Connecting: No (HT5 is Luo-Connecting)

Xi-Cleft: No (HT6 is Xi-Cleft)

Back-Shu: No (BL15 at T5)

Front-Mu: No (CV14 Juque)

Eight Confluent: No

Hui-Meeting: No

Special designations: None among standard categories. However, HT3's
role as the Water/He-Sea point on the Fire channel gives it unique
therapeutic logic — no other Five Element control-cycle relationship
is as clinically exploited as Water-controlling-Fire at HT3.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** HT3 Shaohai (少海, "Lesser Sea"). He-Sea/Water
of Heart. Primary functions: clears Heart Fire, calms the Shen,
transforms Phlegm-Fire, activates the channel. Maciocia identifies
HT3 and HT7 as the two most important Heart channel points: HT7 for
calming the Shen (sedation via Earth), HT3 for clearing Heart Fire
disturbing the Shen (control via Water). Classical bloodletting point —
pricking superficial veins at the cubital fossa for acute Heat
conditions (same tradition as PC3 Quze at the cubital fossa).

**2. Trigger point:** Pronator teres trigger point at its humeral head
origin (medial epicondyle). Referred pain pattern: medial elbow, volar
forearm, and wrist — matching golfer's elbow presentation. Also
brachialis distal trigger point (deep). Dorsher correspondence: the
medial elbow region shows consistent trigger point-acupoint overlap.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Commonly tender on palpation in medial
epicondylitis ("golfer's elbow"), cubital tunnel syndrome, and
patients with chronic forearm tension. The medial elbow depression
is a frequent site of pressure sensitivity.

**4. Marma point:** Kurpara Marma (कूर्पर) — elbow joint Marma in the
107-point Ayurvedic system (Sushruta Samhita). Classified as Sandhi
(joint) Marma, size: 3 anguli. Kurpara Marma encompasses the entire
elbow joint area — HT3 at the medial elbow falls within this zone.
Both TCM and Ayurveda identify the elbow joint as therapeutically
significant for upper limb conditions.

**5. Head zone:** C8-T1 dermatome (medial cutaneous nerve territory at
medial elbow). Same partial cardiac overlap at T1 as HT7 and PC6 —
Heart viscerotome = T1-T5, and the T1 component of C8-T1 reaches the
lower boundary of cardiac autonomic innervation.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2, S4.4

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Arm Line (DFAL, Myers 2001). The DFAL
passes through the medial intermuscular septum → medial elbow →
forearm flexors. HT3 at the medial elbow is a proximal station on this
line — the point where the line transitions from arm to forearm flexor
compartment. Heart meridian trajectory closely parallels the DFAL
(Dorsher 2009).
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** Not individually confirmed. HT3 is
within the C8-T1 dermatome range documented by Kim et al. 2017 for
cardiac neurogenic spots, but the medial elbow location is less likely
than the wrist (HT7) to manifest disease-state inflammation. Kim 2017
did not specifically name HT3 as a matched spot.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable. Medial elbow over bony prominence
(medial epicondyle) = anatomically unsuitable for cupping.

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Marma, Anatomy
Trains). Head zone provides partial convergence at T1 only. Consistent
with other elbow points.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:

Neijing Lingshu (Chapter 1, Ben Shu): identified as He-Sea point of
the Heart channel. "Where Qi enters deeply" — the most interior of
the Five Shu points, treating internal/deep organ pathology.

Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing (Huangfu Mi, 259 CE): mania and withdrawal (癫狂),
epilepsy (痫), head and neck pain, tremor of hands, toothache, cardiac
pain with palpitations, vomiting.

Jiao Explanation of Acupuncture Points: "When Heart-Qi rebels upwards
there is belching or vomiting: this means Heart-Qi is full; tonifying
the Water point [HT3 Shaohai] will make Qi descend."

Zhenjiu Dacheng (Yang Jizhou, 1601): mania, forgetfulness, arm
numbness and pain, tremors, elbow contracture.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, Manual of Acupuncture):
- Calms the Shen — mania, depression, poor memory, epilepsy,
  schizophrenia, agitation
- Clears Heart Fire — palpitations, cardiac pain, mouth ulcers
- Transforms Phlegm-Fire — dizziness, nausea, vomiting
- Activates the channel — medial elbow pain, arm numbness, tremor
- Clears Heat — toothache (lower jaw), scrofula, axillary swelling

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật: Maciocia emphasizes HT3 as the
central point for treating Heart Fire disturbing the Shen — particularly
in mood disorders with somatic manifestations (tremors, palpitations,
sleep disorders). In clinical practice, HT3 and HT7 form a pair: HT3
for excess/Fire patterns (reduce), HT7 for deficiency/calming patterns
(regulate). HT3 is also commonly combined with SI3 (Houxi, Back-Stream
of paired Small Intestine) for upper limb channel problems — activating
both paired channels at their He-Sea and Shu-Stream points respectively.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Mental disorders / epilepsy — 🔴 Unvalidated (clinically)**

Preclinical: Zhen et al. 2014 (Molecular Medicine Reports) — EA at
HT3 showed neuroprotective effects in cerebral ischemia rats, reducing
neuronal apoptosis via regulation of Bcl-2/Bax ratio. Relevant for
stroke-related cognitive impairment, not directly for primary mental
disorders.

Clinical: HT3 appears in multi-point protocols for depression and
anxiety (typically combined with HT7, GV20, PC6), but no systematic
review or randomized controlled trial has isolated HT3 as a sole point
for any psychiatric indication. The point's importance for mental
disorders is primarily based on classical tradition and clinical
experience (Maciocia, Deadman).

Confidence: 🔴 — strong classical tradition and modern clinical
experience support HT3 for mental disorders, but zero controlled
evidence for HT3 specifically. Preclinical neuroprotection data exists
but addresses a different mechanism (ischemia, not psychiatric).

**2. Medial epicondylitis / elbow pain — 🔴 Unvalidated**

No RCT or systematic review for acupuncture specifically at HT3 for
medial epicondylitis. The point is used clinically for medial elbow
pain and is within the anatomical zone of the pathology, but evidence
is limited to case reports and clinical experience.

Confidence: 🔴 — anatomically logical but no controlled evidence.

**Sham comparison note:** No sham-controlled studies exist for HT3
specifically. The point's clinical importance is derived from classical
tradition and the Five Element control-cycle logic (Water controlling
Fire), not from modern evidence. This is a significant evidence gap
for one of the most classically important Heart channel points.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization: HT3 is within the C8-T1 dermatome range
where Kim et al. 2017 demonstrated cardiac neurogenic inflammatory
spots in hypertensive rats. However, the medial elbow location is
anatomically different from the wrist — the medial epicondyle region
is more bony and less likely to manifest the same neuropeptide-mediated
inflammation as the wrist crease area. No specific published data on
HT3 sensitization under cardiac or psychiatric pathology.

Clinical observation: HT3 is frequently tender on palpation in patients
with anxiety, tension-related arm pain, and medial epicondylitis.
Whether this represents disease-state sensitization or mechanical
sensitivity from muscle tension at the common flexor origin is unclear.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at HT3:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): medial cutaneous nerve of forearm
  (C8-T1) for superficial sensation. Ulnar nerve trunk passes
  posterior to the point through the cubital tunnel — deeper insertion
  could access the nerve, but standard technique avoids it. Fiber type:
  NOT studied at HT3. Kim 2013 demonstrated A-beta pathway at HT7;
  whether HT3 uses the same fiber type or engages different afferents
  (A-delta at the deeper pronator teres insertion) is unknown.
- A02 (Autonomic): T1 spinal segment partially overlaps cardiac
  autonomic innervation. Mechanism for Shen-calming effects likely
  involves supraspinal pathways via ulnar nerve → dorsal column →
  cuneate nucleus (extrapolated from HT7 pathway, Kim 2013), but
  this has not been confirmed for HT3.
- A03 (Fascial): at the common flexor tendon origin = fascial
  convergence zone. Transition from arm to forearm compartments.

**Five Element control-cycle mechanism:**
HT3's traditional logic (Water controlling Fire) maps to a specific
clinical pattern: Heart Fire excess conditions (mania, agitation,
insomnia with vivid dreams, mouth ulcers, palpitations with anxiety).
The Water/He-Sea position traditionally "draws Qi downward and inward"
— potentially corresponding to a parasympathetic shift or cortical
inhibition pattern, though this is speculative.

Contrast with HT7: both calm the Shen, but through different
traditional mechanisms — HT7 (Earth/sedation point via generating
cycle) for deficiency and regulation, HT3 (Water/control cycle) for
excess and fire. Whether this traditional distinction reflects different
neural mechanisms is unknown. Both are on the ulnar nerve trajectory.

**Gap:** HT3 has no fiber-type study, no fMRI data, and no mechanism
mapping comparable to HT7 (Kim 2013) or PC6 (NTS → GABA). This is
one of the most important Heart channel points clinically yet one of
the least studied mechanistically. The He-Sea location at the cubital
fossa is anatomically distinct from the wrist — different depth,
different surrounding structures, potentially different afferent
engagement — but no study has compared HT3 vs HT7 neural pathways.

  palmar skin (Pacinian, Meissner corpuscles). Fiber type: NOT studied

  predominate, similar to HT7's mechanism (Kim 2013). If confirmed,
  this would extend the A-beta/dorsal column pathway along the entire
  distal Heart channel.
- A02 (Autonomic): T1 partial overlap with cardiac autonomic
  innervation. Mechanism for Heat-clearing effects (if any) would
  require supraspinal relay — no pathway mapped.

**Five Element horary-point concept:**
In traditional theory, the horary point (same element as channel)
treats the channel's own-element pathology most directly. For Heart
(Fire channel), HT8 (Fire point) addresses Heart Fire. This concept
has no modern mechanistic basis — there is no known reason why the
Ying-Spring anatomical location would specifically affect Heat/Fire
pathology differently from adjacent points. The therapeutic distinction
may reflect empirical clinical observation accumulated over centuries
rather than a mechanism that current anatomy can explain.

**Comparison with PC8 (Laogong):**
Both HT8 and PC8 are palmar points on Fire-element channels, both
used for clearing Heat. Key difference: PC8 has DUAL nerve innervation
(median + ulnar) while HT8 is ulnar-only. PC8 also has stronger
cross-tradition support (Talahridaya Marma strong match). The question
of whether dual innervation at PC8 provides mechanistically broader
effects than single innervation at HT8 parallels the multi-nerve
convergence pattern identified in Phase 1 and Phase 2 AP-09.

**Gap:** No mechanism study exists for HT8. The "Heart Fire clearing"
concept is clinically applied but has no modern attribution. The
Heart-Small Intestine Fire transfer (Heart Fire → dark urine) has no
identified neural or vascular mechanism — TCM interior-exterior channel
relationships do not map to known anatomical pathways.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

- Needle depth: 5–8 mm perpendicular. Shallow — palmar digital

→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Nguồn cổ:

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Post-stroke aphasia — 🔴 Unvalidated (clinically)**

patients (Frontiers in Neurology, 2022). The study demonstrated
changes in language-related cortical areas. An earlier fMRI study
(Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine, 2015) showed functional
activation in distributed cortical areas upon HT5 and GB39
stimulation.

Clinical trial protocol: a multicenter RCT protocol (Trials, 2022)
has been registered for acupuncture + language training for motor
aphasia after stroke, using HT5 among other points with 2 Hz EA for
30 minutes — outcome data not yet published.

Confidence: 🔴 — neuroimaging evidence supports cortical language
area activation, but no published clinical outcome RCTs isolating
HT5 specifically. The fMRI studies are mechanistic, not clinical
outcome data.

**2. Cardiac rhythm / palpitations — 🔴 Unvalidated**

The cardiac pathway via HT5 was documented in a Heliyon review
(2024): EA at HT7-HT5 → hippocampus → NTS → vagus nerve → heart.
This provides a theoretical neural pathway for cardiac effects, but
the study described the pathway based on multiple points in
combination, not HT5 alone.

No RCT or systematic review found for HT5 as sole point for cardiac
rhythm conditions.

Confidence: 🔴 — theoretical pathway mapped in combination with
HT7, but no clinical evidence for HT5-specific cardiac effects.

**Sham comparison note:** No sham-controlled data exists for HT5
specifically. All evidence derives from multi-point protocols or
neuroimaging mechanistic studies.

suggests a supraspinal cortical pathway, but the specific relay
stations between C8-T1 peripheral afferents and cortical language

  would engage the same pathway.
- A02 (Autonomic): T1 partial overlap with cardiac autonomic
  innervation. Same segmental basis as HT7 for cardiac effects.

**Xi-Cleft "Qi and Blood accumulation" concept:**
Traditional theory describes Xi-Cleft points as locations where
channel Qi and Blood converge deeply — hence their use for acute
conditions. In modern anatomical terms, there is no known basis for
why a point 0.5 cun proximal to HT7 would have different
"accumulation" properties from HT7 itself. The same nerve, same
fascia, same vessel, same depth. The Xi-Cleft concept may reflect:
1. Empirical observation that needling this slightly proximal
   location produces measurably different clinical effects
2. Theoretical classification imposed on an anatomical continuum
3. A combination of both

**Night sweats mechanism:**
Yin-deficiency night sweats in TCM correlate with autonomic
dysregulation of thermoregulation during sleep. Heart Yin
deficiency → Empty Heat → sympathetic activation → nocturnal
sweating. If HT6 stimulation produces parasympathetic upregulation
via the same pathway as HT7 (A-beta → cuneate → limbic → vagal),
this could suppress the sympathetic overdrive causing nocturnal
sweating. However, this mechanistic chain is entirely speculative —
no study has examined HT6's autonomic effects on thermoregulation.

**Gap:** HT6 has no mechanism study, no fMRI, no fiber-type data.
The fundamental question is whether HT6 is mechanistically distinct
from HT7 (0.5 cun away, same anatomy) or whether the distinction
is an empirical/theoretical convention within TCM. This question
applies to the entire FCU quartet (HT4-HT5-HT6-HT7) — four points
on the same tendon border, 0.5–1.5 cun apart, same nerve, different
traditional functions.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 3–8 mm perpendicular. Same technique and depth as
  HT7.
- De Qi: similar to HT7 — distention, heaviness at wrist/forearm.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02):
- Self-accessible: contralateral thumb pressure on radial border of
  FCU tendon, 0.5 cun above wrist crease. Very close to HT7 —
  acupressure at HT6 may inadvertently stimulate HT7 and vice versa.

Cứu (B08):
- Applicable. Moxa at HT6 for Yin-deficiency night sweats. Some
  traditions prefer needle with reducing method rather than moxa at
  HT6 for Empty-Heat patterns.

### An toàn

Vascular risk: ulnar artery lateral to the point, same as HT7.
Apply post-needling pressure.

Neural risk: ulnar nerve deep to the point. Same LOW risk as HT7
with standard depth.

Pneumothorax risk: none (forearm location).

Pregnancy: not contraindicated.

Overall safety: LOW complication risk. Identical safety profile to
HT7.

## HT9 — Thiếu Xung (少冲, Shào Chōng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Lesser Surge" — Jing-Well (Wood) point and **tonification point**
of the Heart channel. Wood is the mother of Fire in the generating
cycle → needling HT9 tonifies Heart Qi/Yang deficiency (Nanjing
Chapter 69). Also an emergency resuscitation point — Jing-Well
bloodletting at all 12 hand fingertip points (Shixuan / twelve
Jing-Well) is a classical first-aid technique for loss of
consciousness, stroke, and coma.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the radial side of the little
finger, 0.1 cun from the corner of the nail.

Anatomical landmarks: at the junction of lines drawn along the
radial border and base of the little fingernail. Prick point for
bloodletting — the fingertip capillary bed.

Needle depth: 1–2 mm shallow puncture, or prick to bleed.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Proper palmar digital nerve of little finger (ulnar nerve branch,
  C8-T1) — extremely dense sensory innervation at fingertip
- Fingertip Meissner and Merkel corpuscles — highest
  mechanoreceptor density in the body
- Spinal segments: C8-T1
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: proper palmar digital artery terminal branches. Rich
capillary network at nail bed — basis for bloodletting technique.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Jing-Well (Wood)** — 1st of the Five Shu. For Yin
channels: Wood → Fire → Earth → Metal → Water. Heart = Fire organ.
Wood is the "mother" of Fire in the generating cycle → HT9 is the
**tonification point** of the Heart. Needled for Heart Qi/Yang
deficiency (Nanjing Chapter 69: "tonify the mother to strengthen
the child").
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Ling Shu: "Jing-Well points are where Qi emerges, like water
welling from a spring." Used for acute fullness, emergency revival,
and transitions between Yin and Yang (loss/restoration of
consciousness).

Yuan-Source: No (HT7)
Luo-Connecting: No (HT5)
Xi-Cleft: No (HT6)
Special: part of the **Shixuan** (十宣, "Ten Diffusions") — the 10
fingertip Jing-Well points used collectively for emergency
bloodletting.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** HT9 Shaochong. Jing-Well/Wood of Heart.
Functions: opens Heart orifices, restores consciousness, clears
Heat, tonifies Heart Qi. Emergency combination: HT9 + GV26
(Renzhong) + PC9 (Zhongchong) + KI1 (Yongquan) for acute loss of
consciousness.

**2. Trigger point:** No established trigger point correspondence
at the fingertip — digital pulp has no skeletal muscle.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in patients with little finger
pain, Raynaud's phenomenon, or peripheral neuropathy.

**4. Marma point:** Kshipra Marma (क्षिप्र) — located at the web
space between thumb and index finger, NOT at the little finger nail.
No direct correspondence.

**5. Head zone:** C8 dermatome (little finger). No direct
viscerotome overlap.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** DFAL terminal — the line ends in the hand
and digits.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No fingertip data in Kim 2017.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable.

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi partial). Lowest convergence
on the Heart channel — fingertip points rarely overlap with non-TCM
traditions.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ (Jiayi Jing, Dacheng): loss of consciousness, febrile
disease, cardiac pain, palpitations, chest fullness, mania,
epilepsy, wind-stroke.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Opens Heart orifices — loss of consciousness, wind-stroke, coma
- Clears Heat — febrile disease, tongue stiffness, dry throat
- Tonifies Heart — palpitations from Heart Qi deficiency (Wood
  tonification point)
- Jing-Well emergency — combined with other fingertip points for
  acute resuscitation

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**Jing-Well bloodletting (collective, not HT9 alone) — 🔴
Unvalidated for HT9 specifically**

Preclinical: bloodletting at hand 12 Jing-Well points improved
neurological recovery and ameliorated coagulopathy in TBI mice
(Frontiers in Neuroscience, 2020). Blood-brain barrier protection
via tight junction preservation in cerebral ischemia rats (2017).
Autophagy activation via PINK1/Parkin pathway in altitude hypoxia
rats (Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine, 2023).

Clinical: multi-center RCT showed bloodletting at hand 12 Jing-Well
points improved consciousness in acute stroke patients (Chinese
Journal of Integrative Medicine, 2021). Safe and beneficial. However,
this uses ALL 12 hand Jing-Well points — HT9's individual
contribution cannot be isolated.

Confidence: 🔴 — collective Jing-Well bloodletting has emerging
clinical evidence (one multi-center RCT for acute stroke), but HT9
as an individual point has no isolated evidence.

### An toàn

LOW risk. Fingertip location — no major vessels or nerves. Pricking
is briefly painful due to dense sensory innervation. Apply brief
pressure after bloodletting. Standard clean technique to prevent
nail bed infection.

## HT4 — Linh Đạo (灵道, Líng Dào)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Spirit Pathway" — Jing-River (Metal) point of the Heart channel.
Located 1.5 cun above the wrist crease, the most proximal point of
the FCU quartet (HT4-HT5-HT6-HT7). Jing-River points traditionally
treat voice and respiratory conditions. Primarily used for sudden
loss of voice, cardiac pain, and emotional disturbances.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the radial side of the tendon
of flexor carpi ulnaris, 1.5 cun proximal to HT7, on the anterior
aspect of the forearm.

Anatomical landmarks: with palm facing upward, locate the FCU tendon
at the wrist crease (HT7), measure 1.5 cun proximal. Same radial
border of FCU tendon as HT5 (1 cun), HT6 (0.5 cun), and HT7
(wrist crease).

Needle depth: 5–8 mm perpendicular, or oblique proximal/distal
5–10 mm.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Medial cutaneous nerve of forearm (C8-T1) — superficial sensory
- Ulnar nerve deep between FCU and FDP — same trajectory as HT5-7
- Spinal segments: C8-T1

→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: ulnar artery lateral to the point.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue → between FCU and FDS →
FDP → ulnar nerve and artery.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Jing-River (Metal)** — 4th of the Five Shu on the
Heart channel. For Yin channels: Wood → Fire → Earth → Metal →
Water. Jing-River points (Nan Jing Chapter 68): "River points treat
cough and dyspnea" — and by extension voice and throat. Metal is
the "grandchild" of Fire (Fire → Earth → Metal) — no direct
tonification/sedation significance at this position.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Yuan-Source: No (HT7)
Luo-Connecting: No (HT5)
Xi-Cleft: No (HT6)
Special: none.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** HT4 Lingdao. Jing-River/Metal of Heart.
Functions: regulates Heart Qi, benefits the voice, calms the Shen.
The name "Spirit Pathway" reflects its role as the conduit for Heart
Shen to manifest through voice and speech.

**2. Trigger point:** FCU proximal tendon / muscle belly transition
trigger point (Dorsher). Referred pain: medial forearm.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in forearm tension, similar to
HT5-7. At 1.5 cun, palpation finds slightly more muscular tissue
than at the more tendinous distal points.

**4. Marma point:** Proximal to Manibandha (wrist). No direct match.

**5. Head zone:** C8-T1 dermatome. Same as HT5-7.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** DFAL (Myers 2001).
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** Not individually confirmed.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Anatomy
Trains). Same as HT5 and HT6 — consistent for FCU quartet points.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ (Jiayi Jing, Dacheng): sudden loss of voice, sadness and
fear, heart palpitations, cardiac pain with elbow/arm contracture,
restless Zang disorder with fluctuating emotions.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Benefits the voice — sudden loss of voice (aphonia), throat
  disorders, tongue stiffness
- Regulates Heart Qi — cardiac pain, palpitations, chest tightness
- Calms the Shen — anxiety, sadness, insomnia, restlessness,
  mania-depression

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review, RCT, or preclinical study found for HT4
specifically. Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

LOW risk. Same safety profile as HT5-7. Ulnar artery and nerve in
proximity — standard depth guidelines (5–8 mm) avoid both. Not
contraindicated in pregnancy.

## HT1 — Cực Tuyền (极泉, Jí Quán)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Summit Spring" — first point on the Heart channel, located at the
center of the axilla. The only Heart channel point at the proximal
trunk. Brachial plexus + axillary artery = **highest-risk point on
the Heart channel**. Clinically used for Heart Yin nourishment,
arm pain/numbness, and cardiac pain, but limited in modern practice
due to neurovascular risk.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): in the center of the axilla, on
the medial side of the axillary artery, with the arm abducted.

Anatomical landmarks: with the arm raised and axilla exposed, HT1
is in the deepest depression of the axillary fossa, medial to the
palpable pulse of the axillary artery. Palpate the axillary artery
first — needle insertion is on the medial side.

Needle depth: 3–5 mm oblique or perpendicular. AVOID deep insertion
— brachial plexus and axillary vessels directly beneath. Some
sources recommend avoiding needle altogether, using acupressure or
plucking the tendons (弹拨法, tanbo fa) instead.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Medial cutaneous nerve of arm (T1-T2) — superficial sensory
- Medial cutaneous nerve of forearm (C8-T1) — superficial
- **Brachial plexus** (C5-T1) — entire plexus passes through the
  axilla: median nerve, ulnar nerve, radial nerve, musculocutaneous
  nerve all accessible here. This is the PROXIMAL ORIGIN of the
  ulnar nerve that HT3-HT9 all access distally
- Intercostobrachial nerve (T2) — sensory to medial arm
- Spinal segments: mixed C5-T1 + T1-T2 cutaneous
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: **axillary artery** — major vessel, directly lateral to
HT1. Axillary vein medial/deep. Risk of hemorrhage with deep
needling.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers: skin → axillary fascia → fat pad → neurovascular
bundle (brachial plexus + axillary vessels) surrounded by
coracobrachialis, subscapularis, pectoralis major, latissimus dorsi,
teres major.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **No.** HT1 is not one of the Five Shu points (which are
located distal to the elbow for upper limb channels).

Yuan-Source: No
Luo-Connecting: No
Xi-Cleft: No
Special: none among standard categories. HT1 is notable as the
emergence point (出) of Heart channel Qi from the internal pathway
to the surface — the channel originates in the heart organ and
surfaces at the axilla.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** HT1 Jiquan. Functions: nourishes Heart Yin,
clears Empty-Heat, removes channel stagnation, calms the Shen.
Also used for arm pain, numbness, and shoulder limitation via
channel activation. The name "Summit Spring" = the highest point
where Heart channel Qi wells up to the surface.

**2. Trigger point:** Coracobrachialis trigger point (deep) and
subscapularis axillary border trigger point (posterior). Both refer
pain to the anterior arm and medial scapula. Dorsher mapping shows
axillary trigger point-acupoint overlap.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Axilla commonly tender in patients with
frozen shoulder, thoracic outlet syndrome, and lymphadenopathy.

**4. Marma point:** Kakshadhara Marma (कक्षधर, "axilla holder") —
axillary Marma in the 107-point Ayurvedic system. Classified as Sira
(vessel) Marma, size: 1 anguli. Both TCM and Ayurveda identify the
axilla as a vital point with cardiac and upper limb significance.
Good anatomical correspondence.

**5. Head zone:** T1-T2 dermatome (medial arm cutaneous nerves).
T1-T2 overlaps with upper cardiac autonomic innervation → STRONGER
segmental basis for cardiac effects than wrist points (which rely on
T1 alone).
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Arm Line (DFAL) — HT1 at the
axilla is the most proximal station, where the DFAL begins its
course through the medial arm. Also connects to the Deep Front Line
(DFL) via the chest fascia.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** T1-T2 dermatome — within
cardiac neurogenic spot range documented by Kim 2017 (C8-T2). The
axilla represents the proximal extent of this zone.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable. Axillary fossa unsuitable.

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Marma,
Anatomy Trains). Head zone partial (T1-T2). Neurogenic spot
plausible. Highest convergence of the Tier C points — driven by the
Marma match (Kakshadhara).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ (Jiayi Jing, Dacheng): cardiac pain, dry throat, thirst,
sadness with desire to cry, hypochondriac pain and distension,
inability to raise the shoulder, axillary pain.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Nourishes Heart Yin — dry mouth, thirst, night sweats, insomnia,
  mental restlessness
- Clears Empty-Heat — five-center heat, malar flush
- Removes channel stagnation — frozen shoulder, arm pain, upper
  limb numbness, axillary swelling
- Calms the Shen — anxiety, sadness, palpitations

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: in modern practice, HT1 is often stimulated
via the **tendon-plucking technique** (弹拨法) — a rapid strumming
of the tendons/nerves in the axilla — rather than conventional
needling, to reduce neurovascular risk. Used for post-stroke upper
limb paralysis (pluck to activate brachial plexus).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for HT1 specifically. The tendon-
plucking technique for post-stroke upper limb rehabilitation is
used clinically but not systematically studied at HT1 alone.
Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**HIGH RISK** — the most dangerous point on the Heart channel.

- **Vascular:** axillary artery immediately lateral. Deep or lateral
  deviation → arterial puncture → hemorrhage, hematoma. ALWAYS
  palpate the pulse first and insert medial to it.
- **Neural:** brachial plexus (entire upper limb nerve supply) passes
  through the axilla. Nerve injury risk with deep insertion.
- **Lung:** medial-directed deep insertion risks entering the thorax
  → pneumothorax. Keep insertion shallow and avoid medial angulation.
- **Precautions:** shallow insertion only (3–5 mm). Avoid vigorous
  manipulation. Apply firm pressure post-needling for 2+ minutes.
  Tendon-plucking technique preferred over needle insertion by many
  modern practitioners.

## HT2 — Thanh Linh (青灵, Qīng Líng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Cyan Spirit" — located on the upper arm, 3 cun above the medial
elbow crease (HT3). No special point category designation. Several
classical texts prohibited needling at this point due to proximity
to the brachial artery and ulnar nerve. The least clinically used
and least studied point on the Heart channel.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the medial aspect of the
upper arm, 3 cun above the medial end of the transverse cubital
crease (HT3), in the groove medial to the biceps brachii muscle.

Anatomical landmarks: with elbow flexed, locate HT3 at the medial
elbow, then measure 3 cun proximal along the medial arm. HT2 is in
the depression between the biceps brachii (anterior) and the medial
head of the triceps (posterior), on the medial aspect of the
humerus.

Needle depth: 5–10 mm perpendicular. Classical prohibition: the
Introduction to Medicine (入门, Yixue Rumen) specifically
contraindicates needling at this point. Many early classics discuss
only moxibustion.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Medial cutaneous nerve of arm (T1-T2) — superficial sensory
- Ulnar nerve passes along the medial intermuscular septum — in the
  sulcus posterior to the medial epicondyle distally, but more
  superficial here in the upper arm
- Median nerve is more lateral (with brachial artery)
- Spinal segments: C8-T1 (ulnar), T1-T2 (cutaneous)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: **brachial artery** passes anteromedially — major vessel
in proximity. Superior ulnar collateral artery (branch) also in the
region. Basilic vein superficially.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue → between biceps brachii
(anterior) and medial head of triceps (posterior) → medial
intermuscular septum → brachialis (deep) → humerus.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **No.** HT2 is proximal to the elbow — outside the Five
Shu range (which lies distal to the elbow/knee for limb channels).

Yuan-Source: No
Luo-Connecting: No
Xi-Cleft: No
Special: none. HT2 has no special point category designation — one
of only two Heart channel points (with HT1) that hold no category
membership.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** HT2 Qingling. Functions: activates the channel,
alleviates pain. Limited therapeutic scope compared to other Heart
points — primarily used as a local point for medial arm pain and
headache. Rarely selected for cardiac or Shen indications when
distal Heart points (HT3-HT9) are available.

**2. Trigger point:** Medial intermuscular septum region — biceps
brachii medial border trigger point (partial). Referred pain:
medial arm, medial forearm.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Limited. The medial upper arm is less
commonly a site of spontaneous tenderness than the elbow or
forearm.

**4. Marma point:** Urvi Marma (ऊर्वि) — mid-arm/mid-thigh Marma.
However, Urvi is more commonly applied to the thigh. Ani Marma
(अणि) at the lower arm/forearm boundary is closer but still not a
precise match. Weak correspondence.

**5. Head zone:** T1-T2 dermatome (medial cutaneous nerve of arm).
Same as HT1.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Arm Line (DFAL) — runs through
the medial intermuscular septum at HT2's location.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** T1-T2 within Kim 2017 range
(C8-T2) but upper arm location not individually named.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable. Upper arm medial surface over
bony/muscular prominence unsuitable.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Trigger Point partial, Anatomy
Trains). Low convergence — consistent with the pattern that
proximal limb points between joint and trunk have fewer cross-
tradition overlaps.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ (Jiayi Jing, Dacheng): headache, arm pain and numbness,
shoulder inability to raise, hypochondriac pain, cardiac pain.
Limited classical record compared to other Heart points.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Activates the channel — medial arm pain, shoulder pain, upper limb
  numbness
- Alleviates pain — headache (lateral, referred through medial arm
  channel connection)
- Cardiac pain — rarely used for this when HT3-HT7 are available

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review, RCT, or preclinical study found for HT2.
The least studied acupoint on the Heart channel. Evidence: 🔴
Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**MODERATE risk** due to brachial artery proximity.

- **Vascular:** brachial artery anteromedial to the point. Avoid
  lateral deviation toward the artery. Apply post-needling pressure.
- **Neural:** ulnar nerve in the region along the medial
  intermuscular septum. Electric sensation possible.
- **Classical prohibition:** some texts (Yixue Rumen) contraindicate
  needling — moxibustion preferred. Modern practice allows careful
  needling but HT2 is rarely selected.
- Not contraindicated in pregnancy.
