## §1 — Overview & Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

This document synthesizes mechanism data from all 520 Acupoint Atlas
entries (Phases 1-2) and 55 ⚡ Framework notes (Phases 1-3) into a
unified mechanism analysis. Where Phase 3 (AP-Cross-Tradition.md)
analyzed acupoints along the CONVERGENCE axis — how many traditions
independently identify each point — this document analyzes the
orthogonal MECHANISM axis — WHY each point produces its clinical effects.

Together, these two axes form the **dual-axis model** identified in
Phase 3:

```
Clinical importance = Convergence breadth × Mechanism specificity
```

Neither axis alone predicts clinical significance. BL67 demonstrates
this: 2/8 convergence (lowest among Tier A points) yet Cochrane-level
evidence — mechanism specificity, not convergence breadth, drives its
clinical importance. The Back-Shu points demonstrate the opposite:
3-6/8 convergence (mean 4.75) with the clearest mechanism (segmental
reflex), where convergence breadth predicts both mechanism clarity and
clinical utility.

### 1.3 The Core Epistemological Principle

Per AP-00 S1.1, the Atlas maintains a strict separation:

- **Traditional clinical observations = DATA**: "ST36 treats stomach
  disorders." Valued as observation.
- **Traditional explanations ≠ MECHANISM**: "Because Qi flows through
  the Stomach meridian." Recorded as tradition, not endorsed as mechanism.
- **Modern mechanism = Track A attribution**: "PROKR2+ neurons at ST36 →
  vagal-adrenal pathway → gastric motility via NTS/DMVN." Attributed to
  specific body systems with evidence.

The 6 Track A body systems referenced in mechanism attribution (AP-00
S2.1): A01 Neural, A02 PROKR2, A03 Fascial, A04 Vascular, A05 Immune,
A06 Chemical.

### 1.4 The Headline Finding

**There is no universal acupuncture mechanism.**

The five highest-evidence acupoints in the Atlas — ST36, PC6, LI4, SP6,
BL67 — each use a DIFFERENT mechanism to produce their primary clinical
effects:

| Point | Primary mechanism | Segmental % |
|-------|-------------------|-------------|
| ST36 | PROKR2-vagal-adrenal | 0% |
| PC6 | NTS-GABAergic vagal | ~30-40% |
| LI4 | PAG-descending inhibition | 0% |
| SP6 | Partial segmental + dual-pathway | ~50% |
| BL67 | UNKNOWN | 0% |

If a universal mechanism existed, these five points would share it.
They do not. Each accesses a different neural pathway, determined by
local anatomy — which nerve, which fiber types, which spinal segment,
which brainstem nucleus. The mechanism is in the LOCATION, not in the
technique.

This document catalogs 19 distinct mechanism patterns identified across
the Atlas, organized into a 6-level taxonomy.

## §2 — Mechanism Taxonomy

### 2.1 Organizational Principle

Acupoint mechanisms operate across multiple levels simultaneously. ST36
activates local adenosine release (tissue level), PROKR2+ sensory neurons
(peripheral), and supraspinal NTS/DMVN circuits (brainstem level) — three
levels from a single needle insertion.

The taxonomy below classifies each mechanism pattern by its PRIMARY
explanatory level — the level that explains the NON-OBVIOUS clinical
effect. For ST36, local adenosine explains local pain relief (obvious),
but the PROKR2-vagal-adrenal pathway explains distant anti-inflammatory
effects (non-obvious). The primary level is supraspinal, not local.

### 2.2 The Six-Level Hierarchy

```
Level 1: LOCAL TISSUE
  Effects at the needling site
  ├─ Adenosine release (cell damage → A1R activation)
  ├─ Mechanical fascial signaling (needle grasp → CT strain)
  └─ Neurogenic inflammation (mast cell degranulation → local milieu)

Level 2: SPINAL SEGMENTAL
  Dorsal horn convergence, somatovisceral reflex
  ├─ Somatovisceral segmental reflex (~100% segmental)
  └─ Spinal gate control (A-beta → inhibitory interneurons)

Level 3: SUPRASPINAL BRAINSTEM
  Specific brainstem nuclei as relay/effector
  ├─ NTS/DMVN vagal pathway (→ autonomic organ modulation)
  ├─ PAG/RVM descending pathway (→ endogenous analgesia)
  ├─ RVLM sympathoinhibition (→ cardiovascular regulation)
  └─ Non-specific brainstem arousal (→ consciousness/alertness)

Level 4: CRANIAL NERVE DIRECT
  Bypasses spinal cord entirely → direct brainstem access
  ├─ Ophthalmic V1 pathway (→ trigeminovascular → brainstem)
  ├─ ABVN vagal surface (→ NTS direct, only vagal skin access)
  ├─ Triple convergence (V3 + ABVN + C2-C3 → multiple nuclei)
  └─ C2-C3 → posterior circulation specificity

Level 5: CORTICAL
  Modulates specific cortical areas
  ├─ Thalamic-cortical relay (→ specific cortical fingerprint)
  ├─ Segment-specific cortical modulation (within-line somatotopy)
  └─ Cortical priming + neuroplasticity (activity-dependent)

Level 6: HORMONAL/NEUROENDOCRINE
  Hypothalamic-pituitary axis engagement
  ├─ PVN → oxytocin (cranial input → neuroendocrine)
  └─ Hypothalamic → prolactin (afferent → hormonal cascade)
```

### 2.3 Cross-Cutting Dimensions

The taxonomy levels describe WHERE the mechanism operates. Four
additional dimensions describe HOW:

**A. Molecular Effector**

Six point-specific molecular effectors are identified in the Atlas:

| Molecule | Point | Pathway | Evidence |
|----------|-------|---------|----------|
| PROKR2 | ST36 | C-LTMR → vagal-adrenal | Causal (Nature 2021, ablation) |
| GABA | PC6 | Median n. → NTS → GABAergic | Supported (preclinical) |
| Oxytocin | GV20 | V1+C2 → PVN → OT release | Causal (receptor blockade) |
| Adenosine | ST36 (local) | Cell damage → ectonucleotidase → A1R | Supported (Goldman 2010) |
| p47phox | LI11 | Radial n. → RVLM → NADPH oxidase | Preclinical |
| 5-HT2AR/KCC2 | MS6 | Scalp → cortical → spinal KCC2 | Preclinical (motion-style scalp acupuncture 2025) |

Most acupoint mechanisms have NO identified molecular effector (see §7
Gap Analysis).

**B. Fiber Type**

| Fiber | Modality | Point examples |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| PROKR2+ C-LTMR | Deep tissue mechanical | ST36 (specific C-fiber subtype) |
| A-delta/C | Nociceptive (De Qi) | Most body acupoints |
| A-beta | Tactile/proprioceptive | HT7 (dorsal column pathway) |
| Cutaneous A-beta/A-delta | Shallow touch/pain | Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture, Abdominal, Korean Hand Therapy microsystems |
| TRPV1 C-fiber | Thermal nociceptive | BL67 (moxibustion-specific?) |

HT7 is notable: the FIRST point identified using A-beta fibers (dorsal
column-medial lemniscal pathway) rather than nociceptive A-delta/C
fibers. De Qi may be incidental rather than mechanistically necessary
at some points (⚡ note #12).

**C. Segmental Contribution**

The Atlas documents a 0-100% range of segmental contribution, not a
fixed percentage:

| Range | Points | Pattern |
|-------|--------|---------|
| ~100% | Back-Shu (BL13, BL15, BL18, BL20, BL23) | Pure segmental reflex |
| ~50% | SP6 | Dual segmental + supraspinal |
| ~30-40% | PC6 | Partial segmental via stellate ganglion |
| 0% | ST36, LI4, BL67, KI1, LR3, GV20 | Non-segmental dominant |

C04's "60-70% segmental" estimate (A07 S8 Q1) is approximately correct
as a POPULATION AVERAGE across all acupoints but is misleading as a
PER-POINT prediction. Individual variance spans the full 0-100% range.
The segmental model is a SPECIFIC predictor for points where dermatome-
viscerotome overlap exists (primarily truncal and paravertebral points).
For distal limb points, non-segmental mechanisms dominate entirely
(⚡ note #21).

**D. Modality Dependence**

Most mechanism patterns are modality-independent — the neural pathway
is activated regardless of whether the stimulus is needle, pressure, or
electrical. Two exceptions:

| Point | Modality | Mechanism implication |
|-------|----------|----------------------|
| BL67 | Moxa-specific | TRPV1 thermal pathway, not mechanical |
| SP6 | Acupressure > acupuncture for dysmenorrhea | Sustained fascial chain engagement |

PC6 is the strongest evidence for modality-INDEPENDENCE: ALL stimulation
methods (needle, EA, acupressure, TENS, laser, capsicum) are effective
for PONV (⚡ note #27). The mechanism is neural activation at a specific
location, not technique-specific.

### 2.4 Relationship to BSA Track A Systems

The taxonomy maps onto the BSA Track A body systems (A01-A06):

| Taxonomy level | Primary Track A system(s) |
|----------------|--------------------------|
| Local tissue | A03 Fascial, A05 Immune, A06 Chemical |
| Spinal segmental | A01 Neural (dorsal horn), A07 Somatotopy |
| Supraspinal brainstem | A01 Neural, A02 PROKR2 |
| Cranial nerve direct | A01 Neural (CN V, X) |
| Cortical | A01 Neural (cortical) |
| Hormonal | A06 Chemical (neuroendocrine) |

The A02 PROKR2 system, while molecularly defined, operates at the
supraspinal level — PROKR2 identifies the specific peripheral neurons,
but the therapeutic mechanism is the supraspinal vagal-adrenal pathway
those neurons access.

## §3 — The Mechanism Catalog

This section documents all 19 distinct mechanism patterns identified
across the Atlas. Each pattern includes: pathway, molecular basis,
representative points, segmental contribution, evidence level, and
⚡ note references.

Patterns are organized by primary taxonomy level (§2.2).

### 3.0 Reading Guide

Each catalog entry follows this format:

```
PATTERN [#]: [Name]
Point(s): [Representative acupoint(s)]
Taxonomy: Level [N] — [sublevel]
Pathway:  [Afferent → central relay → effector/outcome]
Molecular: [Identified effector, or "Not identified"]
Segmental: [0-100%]
Evidence:  [Causal / Supported / Preclinical / Neuroimaging / Unknown]
⚡ Notes:  [Reference numbers]
BSA refs:  [Framework cross-references]
```

Evidence levels (primary):
- **Causal**: Ablation/blockade eliminates effect (gold standard)
- **Supported**: Consistent preclinical + clinical data
- **Preclinical**: Animal model evidence only
- **Neuroimaging**: fMRI/EEG correlation, not causal
- **Unknown**: No identified mechanism

Additional evidence descriptors used where primary levels are
insufficient:
- **Established**: Multiple decades of convergent evidence across
  modalities (e.g., segmental reflex: Sherrington 1906 → present)
- **Traditional**: Classical clinical use without modern validation
- **Limited**: Some positive data but insufficient for higher tier
- **Cochrane**: Cochrane-level systematic review evidence exists but
  mechanism is unknown (unique to BL67)

### 3.1 Level 2 — Spinal Segmental

#### PATTERN 1: Somatovisceral Segmental Reflex

**Point(s):** Back-Shu group (BL13 Lung T3-T4, BL15 Heart T5, BL18
Liver T9-T10, BL20 Spleen T11-T12, BL23 Kidney L2-L3)

**Taxonomy:** Level 2 — Somatovisceral reflex

**Pathway:** Dorsal rami at paravertebral site → dorsal horn Lamina V
WDR neurons (convergence with visceral afferents from same segment) →
sympathetic/parasympathetic outflow → target organ modulation

**Molecular:** Segmental (A-delta/C fiber → dorsal horn interneurons →
autonomic outflow). No unique molecular effector — the mechanism is
structural: skin and organ share the same spinal segment.

**Segmental:** ~100%. Each Back-Shu point sits at the spinal level
whose dermatome-viscerotome convergence matches its named organ. 9/12
traditional Back-Shu assignments match modern viscerotome mapping (odds
of chance: ~1 in 5 billion, A09 S1.2).

**Evidence:** Established. 130+ years of convergence neurophysiology
(Sherrington 1906, Ruch 1946, Pomeranz 1987). Confirmed in spinal
preparations (effect persists after spinal transection, proving
spinal-level mechanism).

**Clinical profile:** Highest convergence among Phase 1 groups (5-6/8
for Phase 1 subset; full 12-point Back-Shu group: 3-6/8, mean 4.75).
Strongest mechanism-convergence correlation: high convergence predicts
high mechanism clarity for segmental points (⚡ note #22).

**BL20 annotation:** Segmental match requires including lesser
splanchnic nerve (T10-T11) contribution to celiac plexus. A07 S1.6
viscerotome table lists Spleen at T6-T8 (primary innervation only);
extended range T6-T11 via both greater and lesser splanchnic nerves
should be annotated (⚡ note #20).

**⚡ Notes:** #20 (BL20 viscerotome), #21 (0-100% range confirmation),
#22 (convergence-mechanism correlation)

**BSA refs:** A07 S1.6, C04 S3.4 rank 2

#### PATTERN 2: Partial Segmental + Supraspinal Dual-Pathway

**Point(s):** SP6 (Sanyinjiao)

**Taxonomy:** Level 2-3 — Segmental + supraspinal (dual)

**Pathway:** Tibial nerve → S2-S4 dorsal horn (segmental component:
overlap with pelvic parasympathetic supply to uterus, bladder, rectum) +
ascending pathway → PAG (supraspinal component: descending modulation)

**Molecular:** Mixed. No single identified effector.

**Segmental:** ~50%. S2-S4 overlap between tibial nerve territory and
pelvic organ innervation provides substantial segmental explanation for
reproductive/urinary effects. Non-segmental component needed for
analgesic effects beyond pelvic organs.

**Evidence:** Supported. Multiple RCTs for dysmenorrhea, labor pain,
urinary function. PTNS (Posterior Tibial Nerve Stimulation) = FDA-cleared
medical device using the same nerve at the same site for overactive
bladder — independent modern medical validation of this anatomical
location (⚡ note #9).

**Key finding:** SP6 acupressure outperforms acupuncture for
dysmenorrhea (p=0.001 vs p=0.371, Chen & Chien 2013) — the ONLY known
condition where pressure beats needle at the same point. Possible
mechanism: sustained broad pressure engages the Deep Front Line fascial
chain (tibialis posterior → pelvic floor) more effectively than focal
needle penetration (⚡ note #10).

**⚡ Notes:** #8 (dual-pathway pattern), #9 (PTNS convergence),
#10 (acupressure > acupuncture)

**BSA refs:** C04 S3.4, A07 S1, A03 (fascial chain)

### 3.2 Level 3 — Supraspinal Brainstem

#### PATTERN 3: PROKR2-Vagal-Adrenal Axis

**Point(s):** ST36 (Zusanli)

**Taxonomy:** Level 3 — NTS/DMVN vagal pathway

**Pathway:** PROKR2+ C-LTMR sensory neurons at ST36 deep connective
tissue → vagus nerve afferents → NTS/DMVN brainstem relay → vagal
efferents → adrenal medulla → catecholamine/dopamine release → systemic
anti-inflammatory response

**Molecular:** PROKR2 (Prokineticin Receptor 2). CAUSAL: ablating
PROKR2+ neurons eliminates electroacupuncture anti-inflammatory effects
at ST36 (Liu et al., Nature 2021). The ONLY acupoint mechanism with full
molecular causal chain from peripheral receptor to clinical outcome.

**Also present:** Local adenosine release (Goldman 2010) — ATP →
ectonucleotidase → adenosine → A1R activation. Operates at Level 1
(local tissue) simultaneously with Level 3 PROKR2 pathway. Demonstrates
multi-system convergence at a single site (A09 S2.2).

**Segmental:** 0%. ST36 at L4-L5; stomach at T6-T9. No segmental
overlap. The paradigmatic non-segmental acupoint.

**Evidence:** CAUSAL (strongest in entire Atlas). Nature 2021 + Neuron
2020 (Liu lab). Ablation study + dose-response + pathway mapping.

**fMRI signature:** Distinct cortical fingerprint — visual network +
noradrenaline receptor map correlation (Frontiers in Neuroscience 2025).
Adjacent GB34 shows different brain network (⚡ note #2).

**Key finding:** ST36 resolves the primary non-segmental gap (A07 S8 Q1,
C04 S3.5.6) for this specific point. The resolution is POINT-SPECIFIC —
other non-segmental effects require their own pathway identification
(⚡ note #1).

**⚡ Notes:** #1 (point-specific resolution), #2 (fMRI specificity),
#3 (adenosine dual-level)

**BSA refs:** A02 S2, A07 S8 Q1, A09 S2.2, C04 S3.4 rank 1

#### PATTERN 4: NTS-GABAergic Vagal

**Point(s):** PC6 (Neiguan)

**Taxonomy:** Level 3 — NTS/DMVN vagal pathway (different from ST36)

**Pathway:** Median nerve (C8-T1) → spinal cord → NTS (nucleus tractus
solitarius) → GABAergic inhibition → anti-emetic center suppression.
Both ST36 and PC6 terminate at NTS, but through different afferent
molecules and neural circuits.

**Molecular:** GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid). NTS-GABAergic pathway is
the proposed effector mechanism for anti-emetic effects.

**Segmental:** ~30-40%. Partial segmental contribution via stellate
ganglion connections (C7-T1 → cardiac sympathetic). Kim 2017 showed
cardiac neurogenic spots extend to C8-T2 (⚡ note #11), supporting
partial segmental explanation for cardiac indications.

**Evidence:** Supported. Cochrane evidence: NNT~4 for PONV (59 RCTs),
PC6 = equivalent to antiemetic drugs. Cochrane conclusion: further
comparative trials are "futile" — unprecedented in acupuncture research
(⚡ note #26).

**Key finding — modality independence:** ALL stimulation methods are
effective for PONV: needling, electroacupuncture, acupressure, TENS,
laser, capsicum plaster. This validates that the mechanism is neural
activation at a specific anatomical location, not technique-specific
(⚡ note #27).

**⚡ Notes:** #25 (second NTS pathway), #26 (Cochrane evidence),
#27 (modality independence)

**BSA refs:** A07 S8 Q1, A09 S4.3, C04 S3.5.6

#### PATTERN 5: PAG-Descending Inhibition

**Point(s):** LI4 (Hegu)

**Taxonomy:** Level 3 — PAG/RVM descending pathway

**Pathway:** Radial nerve (C5-T1) → spinal cord → PAG (periaqueductal
gray) → RVM (rostral ventromedial medulla) → descending inhibitory
pathway → spinal dorsal horn → wide-field analgesia

**Molecular:** Endorphin (endogenous opioid). PAG-RVM pathway is the
primary endogenous analgesia system.

**Segmental:** 0%. LI4 at C6-C7; face (primary clinical target) at
V1-V3 (trigeminal, not spinal). 5+ segment gap between hand and face
cannot be explained by segmental convergence.

**Evidence:** Preclinical (multiple animal studies confirm PAG
activation). Clinical: extensive RCT evidence for dental/facial pain.

**Key divergence — LI4 vs LI11:** Both use the SAME peripheral nerve
(radial, C5-T1) but activate DIFFERENT brainstem nuclei: LI4 → PAG
(analgesia), LI11 → RVLM (cardiovascular). This is the strongest
evidence that POINT LOCATION on a nerve — not merely nerve identity —
determines which central mechanism is engaged (⚡ note #7). The
implication: specificity operates at finer grain than "nerve territory,"
at the level of fiber populations accessed at each anatomical location.

**⚡ Notes:** #5 (third non-segmental pattern), #6 (LI4 pregnancy
safety), #7 (LI4/LI11 divergence)

**BSA refs:** A07 S8 Q1, A07 S1, C04 S3.5.6

#### PATTERN 6: RVLM Sympathoinhibition

**Point(s):** LI11 (Quchi)

**Taxonomy:** Level 3 — RVLM sympathoinhibition

**Pathway:** Radial nerve (C5-T1, same as LI4) → spinal cord → RVLM
(rostral ventrolateral medulla) → sympatholysis → cardiovascular
regulation (blood pressure reduction)

**Molecular:** p47phox (NADPH oxidase subunit). Electroacupuncture at
LI11 reduces p47phox expression in RVLM, decreasing oxidative stress-
driven sympathetic overactivity.

**Segmental:** 0%. C5-T1 → cardiovascular (T1-T5 sympathetic cardiac).
Minimal overlap; the mechanism is supraspinal.

**Evidence:** Preclinical. Same nerve as LI4 (Pattern 5) but different
brainstem target — the defining evidence for within-nerve location
specificity.

**⚡ Notes:** #7 (LI4/LI11 divergence: strongest location specificity
evidence in Atlas)

**BSA refs:** C04 S3.5.6

#### PATTERN 7: Intensity-Dependent Non-Specific Brainstem

**Point(s):** KI1 (Yongquan)

**Taxonomy:** Level 3 — Non-specific brainstem arousal

**Pathway:** Plantar nociceptors (densely innervated sole) → strong
nociceptive afferent barrage → RAS (reticular activating system) + PAG +
LC (locus coeruleus) + NTS → brainstem arousal → consciousness recovery

**Molecular:** Non-specific. Unlike Patterns 3-6 which each identify a
specific brainstem target, KI1's effects arise from the INTENSITY of
nociceptive input activating multiple brainstem nuclei simultaneously.

**Segmental:** 0% for traditional indications. L4-S1 has no organ-
specific convergence relevant to emergency consciousness recovery.

**Evidence:** Traditional + case reports. Primary use: emergency
consciousness recovery (syncope, coma, shock). C04 S3.5.7 identifies
foot sole as the WEAKEST neural access region for organ-specific therapy
— correct. But KI1's target is BRAINSTEM AROUSAL, not organ-specificity,
and for this purpose the sole's high nociceptor density becomes an
advantage (⚡ note #24).

**Key distinction:** KI1 is the ONLY mechanism pattern where the
mechanism is NON-SPECIFIC — defined by stimulus intensity rather than
pathway specificity. All other patterns work through identified specific
pathways.

**⚡ Notes:** #23 (10th mechanism pattern), #24 (foot sole paradox)

**BSA refs:** C04 S3.5.7, A09 S2

### 3.3 Level 4 — Cranial Nerve Direct

The Atlas documents that head and ear acupoints operate through a
fundamentally different neural architecture than limb and trunk points.
Cranial nerve pathways (V1, V3, ABVN, C2-C3) have DIRECT brainstem
access without spinal cord intermediary. This architectural difference
may explain why head/ear points are preferentially used for
neuropsychiatric indications — they have the shortest pathway to
brainstem nuclei (NTS, DMV, LC, DRN, PVN) that regulate mood, anxiety,
sleep, and autonomic function.

Three distinct cranial nerve access patterns form a "triptych"
(⚡ note #39):

```
(a) GV20  = V1 + C2         (dual cranial nerve input)
(b) Yintang = pure V1       (single cranial nerve)
(c) Auricular = ABVN + V3 + C2-C3  (triple input, unique vagal)
```

#### PATTERN 8: Cranial V1+C2 → PVN → Oxytocin

**Point(s):** GV20 (Baihui)

**Taxonomy:** Level 4 → Level 6 (cranial input → neuroendocrine output)

**Pathway:** Ophthalmic branch V1 (supraorbital nerve) + C2 (greater
occipital nerve) → trigeminocervical complex → brainstem → PVN
(paraventricular nucleus of hypothalamus) → oxytocin release

**Molecular:** Oxytocin. CAUSAL: confirmed by pseudorabies retrograde
tracing (identifying the pathway) and oxytocin receptor blockade
(eliminating the anti-depressant effect). The 2nd point-specific
molecular effector after ST36 → PROKR2 (⚡ note #34).

**Segmental:** N/A. Cranial nerve input — no spinal segments involved.

**Evidence:** Causal (receptor blockade eliminates anxiolytic effect).

**Key finding:** GV20 demonstrates that acupoint specificity extends
beyond anatomical location to MOLECULAR specificity — different points
activate different signaling molecules through different neural circuits
(⚡ note #34). Two points (ST36, GV20), two distinct molecular effectors
(PROKR2, oxytocin), two completely different pathways.

**⚡ Notes:** #32 (9th pattern), #33 (cranial architecture), #34
(molecular specificity)

**BSA refs:** A09 S2.2, C04 S3.5.1

#### PATTERN 9: Pure V1 Cranial

**Point(s):** Yintang (EX-HN3)

**Taxonomy:** Level 4 — Cranial nerve direct

**Pathway:** V1 supratrochlear nerve → trigeminovascular system →
brainstem (NTS, LC, DRN) → anxiolytic/analgesic effects

**Molecular:** Not identified. However, the eTNS (external trigeminal
nerve stimulation) device Cefaly targets the SAME nerve territory (V1
supratrochlear + supraorbital) and is FDA-approved for migraine. This
validates the neural pathway for therapeutic effect with a different
modality (⚡ note #36).

**Segmental:** N/A. Pure cranial nerve, no spinal cord involvement.

**Evidence:** Supported. 5 dedicated RCTs for anxiety + eTNS FDA
validation at same nerve territory.

**Key distinction from GV20:** Yintang is pure V1 (supratrochlear
only), while GV20 combines V1 + C2 (greater occipital). The C2
component at GV20 may explain its broader indication range
(neuropsychiatric + autonomic) vs Yintang's more focused anxiolytic
profile (⚡ note #35).

**⚡ Notes:** #35 (confirms cranial architecture), #36 (eTNS
convergence)

**BSA refs:** C04 S3.5.1

#### PATTERN 10: ABVN-Vagal Direct

**Point(s):** Auricular cymba conchae (CO4 Stomach zone, taVNS target)

**Taxonomy:** Level 4 — Cranial nerve X surface access

**Pathway:** ABVN (auricular branch of vagus nerve, 100% innervation at
cymba conchae) → NTS → locus coeruleus → dorsal raphe nucleus → vagal
circuit activation

**Molecular:** Via NTS/LC/DRN activation (specific effectors depend on
indication).

**Segmental:** N/A. The ONLY place on the body surface where the vagus
nerve is directly accessible. Cranial nerve X reaches the skin surface
at the cymba conchae and nowhere else.

**Evidence:** Supported. taVNS fMRI studies (Frangos 2015, Farmer 2020)
confirm NTS/LC activation at cymba conchae.

**Critical limitation — zone-level vs point-level specificity:** Within
the cymba conchae, the 100% ABVN coverage means there is no
neuroanatomical basis for distinguishing between named auricular "points"
(CO4 Stomach vs CO5 Esophagus vs CO6 Cardia). The therapeutic effect may
be ZONE-LEVEL (concha stimulation → vagal activation), not POINT-LEVEL
(specific organ regulation). Supporting evidence: Yeo 2013 found no
significant difference between 5-point auricular protocol and single-
point stimulation (⚡ note #40).

**ABVN gradient across auricular zones:**

| Zone | ABVN % | Vagal access |
|------|--------|-------------|
| Cymba conchae | 100% | Full |
| Antihelix body | 73% | High |

#### PATTERN 11: Triple Cranial Nerve Brainstem Convergence

**Point(s):** Auricular mixed innervation zones (Shen Men TF4,
Sympathetic AH6a, Point Zero, antihelix, antitragus)

**Taxonomy:** Level 4 — Multi-cranial-nerve convergence

**Pathway:** V3 (auriculotemporal) + ABVN (partial) + C2-C3 (great
auricular) → multiple brainstem nuclei simultaneously. The RATIO of
input from each nerve determines the zone-specific "brainstem
fingerprint":

| Zone | Dominant nerve | Brainstem target | Clinical profile |
|------|---------------|------------------|-----------------|
| Cymba conchae | ABVN 100% | NTS, LC, DRN | Vagal/autonomic |
| Antihelix body | ABVN 73% + C2-C3 | NTS → vlPAG | Analgesic (dual-pathway) |
| Cavum conchae | ABVN 45% + V3 + C2-C3 | NTS (cardiac-specific) | Cardiovascular |
| Antitragus | C2-C3 primary | Nucleus cuneatus | Somatosensory |
| Crura antihelix | ABVN 9% + C2-C3 | Non-vagal dominant | Limited evidence |
| Scaphoid fossa | C2-C3 100% | No vagal access | Somatotopic (2-4 segment gap) |

**Molecular:** Zone-dependent. Not individually characterized.

**Evidence:** Supported. Frangos 2015 fMRI demonstrates different
brainstem activation patterns for different auricular zones.

**Key findings:**

1. **Inter-zone specificity:** Cavum conchae (45% ABVN) activates
   cardiac-related NTS neurons while cymba conchae (100% ABVN) does NOT.
   Paradoxically, less ABVN produces more organ-specific effects. The
   MIXED innervation may be the key to organ specificity, not pure ABVN
   (⚡ note #41).

2. **Innervation corrections:** Phase 2 corrected several errors from
   prior literature: antitragus has no V3 (C2-C3 primary, ⚡ note #43),
   scaphoid fossa has no ABVN (C2-C3 100%, ⚡ note #44).

3. **Anterior-posterior duality:** Five organs mapped twice (anterior
   concha = ABVN pathway, posterior ear = C2-C3 pathway). Neural
   pathway, not somatotopic label, determines mechanism strength
   (⚡ note #46).

4. **Lobule-scaphoid fossa parallel:** Identical nerve supply (100%
   C2-C3) but different mechanism plausibility — lobule→head/face has
   direct C2-C3→trigeminocervical convergence (moderate-high), scaphoid
   fossa→upper limb has 2-4 segment gap (low-moderate). Same peripheral
   input, different central anatomy (⚡ note #45).

**⚡ Notes:** #39 (triptych complete), #41 (inter-zone specificity),
#42 (ABVN gradient), #43 (antitragus correction), #44 (scaphoid
correction), #45 (lobule-scaphoid parallel), #46 (anterior-posterior
duality)

### 3.6 Cross-Level Pathway

#### PATTERN 18: Subcutaneous-Only PROKR2-Independent

**Point(s):** Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture (Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture), Abdominal Acupuncture, Korean Hand Therapy
(Korean Hand Therapy)

**Taxonomy:** Cross-level — Level 2 (spinal gate) + Level 3 (supraspinal
opioid)

**Pathway:** Cutaneous A-beta/A-delta afferents (shallow insertion,

**Molecular:** NOT PROKR2. These microsystems use shallow needling that
does NOT penetrate deep connective tissue, does NOT trigger De Qi, and
therefore does NOT activate PROKR2+ C-LTMRs (located in deep tissue, Liu

meta-analyses (positive for stroke rehabilitation), Korean Hand Therapy 4+ RCTs
(positive for PONV). Evidence still limited but consistently positive.

**Key implication:** PROKR2 is SUFFICIENT (ST36) but NOT NECESSARY for
acupuncture analgesia. The microsystem evidence demonstrates that
therapeutic effects can be achieved without deep tissue penetration and
without PROKR2 receptor activation.

**⚡ Notes:** #53 (PROKR2 sufficient but not necessary)

**BSA refs:** A02 S2, C04 S3

#### ST25 vs ST36: Same Meridian, Different Mechanism

ST25 and ST36 both treat gastrointestinal conditions on the Stomach
meridian, but use COMPLETELY different mechanisms:
- ST25: segmental somatovisceral reflex (T10, direct segmental)

Same target organ, same meridian, opposite mechanisms. This confirms
that traditional meridian organization does NOT predict mechanism
(⚡ note #4). Points are real and mechanistically distinct; the
connecting meridian pathway does not explain why they work.

SI3 → posterior neck thermal response = first objective measurement for
an Eight Confluent point's access to its extraordinary vessel. Creates
testable predictions for the other 7 Confluent pairs. The Eight
Confluent system may be empirically verifiable via thermal imaging
(⚡ note #15).

#### MS7 Motor-Sensory Evidence Asymmetry

MS6 (motor) has segment-specific fMRI validation (Zheng 2016) but MS7
(sensory) does not — despite being anatomically parallel and routinely
used together. The asymmetry likely reflects research priorities (motor
outcomes are more measurable than sensory recovery in stroke) rather than
a genuine mechanistic difference (⚡ note #50).

### 3.10 The "No Universal Mechanism" Conclusion

The 19-pattern catalog conclusively demonstrates that acupuncture has no
single mechanism. The diversity is systematic, not random:

1. **Different nerves → different brainstem nuclei:** Radial nerve at
   LI4 → PAG; radial nerve at LI11 → RVLM. Same nerve, different
   central targets depending on WHERE on the nerve the stimulus is
   applied.

2. **Different levels → different clinical domains:** Segmental
   mechanisms (Back-Shu) → organ modulation. Supraspinal mechanisms
   (ST36, LI4) → systemic effects. Cranial mechanisms (GV20, Yintang) →
   neuropsychiatric. Cortical mechanisms (MS6) → neurological
   rehabilitation.

3. **Different molecules → different outcomes:** PROKR2 →
   anti-inflammatory. Oxytocin → anti-depressant. GABA → anti-emetic.
   Adenosine → local analgesia.

4. **Different body regions → different architectures:** Trunk =
   segmental dominant. Limb = supraspinal dominant. Head/ear = cranial
   nerve direct. Scalp = cortical.

The mechanism IS the anatomy. Each acupoint is a unique access point to a
specific neural pathway determined by:
- Which nerve(s) are present
- Which fiber types are activated
- Which spinal segment receives the input
- Which brainstem nucleus integrates the signal
- Which molecular effectors are engaged

This is not a failure to find "the" mechanism — it is the ANSWER. The
mechanism of acupuncture is anatomical specificity: the body's nervous
system provides different therapeutic access at different locations, and
acupuncture traditions have empirically mapped which locations are useful
for which conditions.

## §4 — Segmental vs Non-Segmental Distribution

### 4.1 The Central Claim Under Examination

A07 S8 Q1 and C04 S3.4 estimate that segmental convergence explains
"~60-70% of acupoint-organ effects." This has been the primary
mechanistic framework throughout the BSA analysis. Phase 4 data from
520 Atlas entries now allows quantification of this claim.

**Verdict:** The ~60-70% figure is approximately correct as a
POPULATION-WEIGHTED AVERAGE across all body acupoints, but it is
misleading as a per-point prediction. Individual acupoints range from
0% to ~100% segmental contribution, with the value determined primarily
by body region.

### 4.2 Quantitative Distribution Across 520 Entries

Based on mechanism section analysis across all 18 Atlas files:

| Category | Entries | % of 520 | Description |
|----------|---------|----------|-------------|
| Segmental primary | ~75-90 | 14-17% | Back-Shu, outer BL line, GV thoracolumbar, Front-Mu with clear viscerotome overlap |
| Non-segmental primary | ~130-150 | 25-29% | Distal limb points with organ indications, specific pathways identified for ~10 points |
| Mixed/dual pathway | ~35-50 | 7-10% | Transition zone points (shoulder, hip, partial overlap) |
| Mechanism gap | ~80-110 | 15-21% | No mechanism section or explicit "unknown/unresolved" |
| Cranial nerve / microsystem | ~115-130 | 22-25% | Auricular (83), scalp (20), other microsystems (9), plus ~62 standard head/face points |

**Key observations:**

1. **Pure segmental points are a MINORITY** (~14-17% of all entries).
   These cluster in the trunk paravertebral region (Back-Shu, BL outer
   line, GV midline, Front-Mu). Outside this region, pure segmental
   explanation is rare.

2. **Non-segmental dominance at distal limb points.** ~130-150 entries
   identify their primary organ/distant mechanism as requiring
   supraspinal pathways. However, only ~10 specific pathways have been
   identified (the 19 patterns in §3). The remaining ~120+ entries state
   "requires supraspinal" without specifying which pathway.

3. **The cranial nerve category** (~22-25%) operates outside the
   segmental/non-segmental framework entirely. Head, ear, and scalp
   points use cranial nerve pathways with direct brainstem access — the
   segmental model does not apply (see §5).

4. **Mechanism gap density** (~15-21%) reflects the current state of
   knowledge. BL67 is the most extreme case (Cochrane evidence, zero
   mechanism), but ~80-110 entries have inadequate or absent mechanism
   attribution.

### 4.3 Mechanism Section Coverage by Tier

The depth of mechanism attribution correlates strongly with evidence tier:

| Tier | Entries | With formal mechanism section | Coverage |
|------|---------|------------------------------|----------|
| A (Deep Analysis) | 36 | 36 | 100% |
| B (Standard Entry) | 185 | ~140-160 | 76-86% |
| C (Minimum Viable) | 299 | ~50-70 | 17-24% |
| **Total** | **520** | **~217** | **~42%** |

**Tier A** entries have full Track A system attribution with identified
pathways, gap analysis, and ⚡ Framework notes where relevant.

**Tier B** entries typically follow the template: (1) local
musculoskeletal mechanism = segmental, HIGH clarity; (2) distant/organ
mechanism = requires supraspinal, LOW-MODERATE clarity; (3) classical
category-specific mechanisms (Yuan-Source, Xi-Cleft) = UNRESOLVED.
Tier B entries do not say "mechanism unclear" — they identify the gap
precisely.

**Tier C** entries have markedly thinner mechanism attribution.
Approximately 75-80% either lack a formal mechanism section or provide
only anatomy-based notes. Where present, they follow the LOCAL/DISTANT
template in condensed form but almost never identify specific
supraspinal pathways.

### 4.4 Body Region × Segmental Contribution

The body region is the primary predictor of segmental contribution:

```
REGION                    SEGMENTAL %     MECHANISM TYPE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Paravertebral (Back-Shu)  ~100%          Pure somatovisceral reflex
Trunk midline (GV/CV)     ~60-90%        High segmental + some supraspinal
Trunk lateral (ST/KI/SP)  ~40-70%        Mixed, depends on organ target
Shoulder/hip transition   ~20-50%        Mixed/dual pathway
Proximal limb (elbow-knee) ~10-30%       Mostly non-segmental for organ
Distal limb (below knee)  ~0-10%         Non-segmental dominant
Digit tips (Jing-Well)    ~0%            Non-segmental (if any mechanism)
Head/ear                  N/A            Cranial nerve architecture
Scalp                     N/A            Trigeminovascular / cortical
```

This gradient explains the ~60-70% population average: the trunk
contains a high density of acupoints (Back-Shu ×12, Front-Mu ×12, GV
×15, CV ×20+, KI/ST/SP abdominal clusters) that are mostly segmental,
which mathematically weights the average upward. But for any INDIVIDUAL
distal limb point (ST36, LI4, LR3, etc.), the segmental contribution
is near zero.

### 4.5 The Bladder Meridian: A Complete Proximal-Distal Case Study

The BL meridian (67 entries) spans from head to foot on a single
channel, providing the clearest view of how segmental contribution
changes with body region:

| BL region | Points | Segmental % | Mechanism clarity |
|-----------|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| Head (BL1-BL10) | 10 | N/A (cranial) | Trigeminal/C1-C3 |
| Upper back medial (BL11-BL17) | 7 | ~80-100% | HIGH |
| Back-Shu (BL18-BL28) | 11 | ~100% | HIGH (textbook) |
| Outer back line (BL41-BL54) | 10 | ~80-90% | MODERATE-HIGH |
| Gluteal/sacral (BL30-BL35) | 6 | ~50-70% | MODERATE |
| Posterior thigh (BL36-BL40) | 5 | ~20-40% | MODERATE (local) / LOW (organ) |
| Posterior leg (BL55-BL57) | 3 | ~10-20% | LOW for organ |
| Ankle (BL58-BL63) | 6 | ~0-10% | LOW, "requires supraspinal" |
| Foot (BL64-BL67) | 4 | 0% | LOW to UNKNOWN (BL67 = max gap) |

The gradient from BL18 (~100% segmental) to BL67 (0% segmental, unknown
mechanism) is the single most complete demonstration in the Atlas of how
body position determines mechanism type. It also demonstrates the
inverse relationship: as segmental contribution decreases, mechanism
CLARITY decreases — except for BL67, where high evidence coexists with
zero mechanism understanding.

### 4.6 Revising the "60-70% Segmental" Framework

C04 S3.4's estimate should be revised from a single number to a
**regional model**:

| Statement | Status |
|-----------|--------|
| "~60-70% of acupoint effects are segmental" | APPROXIMATELY CORRECT as population average |
| "Segmental convergence predicts acupoint-organ effects" | CORRECT for paravertebral/trunk points |
| "The remaining 30-40% is non-segmental" | MISLEADING — for distal limb points, it is 90-100% non-segmental |
| "Segmental contribution is a fixed property" | INCORRECT — it varies continuously from 0% to ~100% by region |

**Proposed framework update for Phase 5:**

Replace "~60-70% segmental" with:
- Trunk paravertebral: ~80-100% segmental (somatovisceral reflex)
- Trunk lateral/anterior: ~40-70% segmental (mixed)
- Proximal limb: ~10-30% segmental (mostly non-segmental)
- Distal limb: ~0-10% segmental (non-segmental dominant)
- Head/ear/scalp: N/A (cranial nerve architecture)

## §5 — Cranial Nerve Architecture

### 5.1 The Architectural Divide

The body's acupoints divide into two fundamentally different neural
architectures:

```
SPINAL ARCHITECTURE (trunk + limb points)
  Peripheral nerve → spinal cord dorsal horn → ascending pathways
  → brainstem nuclei → effector response
  Multiple relay levels. Segmental model applies.
  ~275-390 standard acupoints (AP-01 through AP-15)

CRANIAL ARCHITECTURE (head + ear + scalp points)
  Cranial nerve (V1, V3, ABVN, C2-C3) → brainstem DIRECT
  No spinal cord intermediary. Segmental model does not apply.
  ~62 standard head/face points + 83 auricular + 20 scalp + 9 other
  = ~174 entries (~33% of Atlas)
```

Cranial nerve pathways are SHORTER (fewer synapses between skin and
brainstem), FASTER (direct brainstem access), and preferentially
target NEUROPSYCHIATRIC nuclei (NTS, DMV, LC, DRN, PVN). This may
explain the empirical observation that head/ear points are preferentially
used for anxiety, depression, insomnia, and autonomic regulation.

### 5.2 The Three Cranial Nerve Access Patterns

Phase 1-2 documents three distinct cranial nerve access patterns, forming
a "triptych" of cranial acupoint architecture (⚡ note #39):

#### Pattern A: Dual Cranial Input (GV20 model)

```
V1 (supraorbital) + C2 (greater occipital)
  ↓                    ↓
Trigeminal ganglion   Trigeminocervical complex (TCC)
  ↓                    ↓
  └────── CONVERGE ────┘
           ↓
  Brainstem targets: NTS, DMV, LC, DRN, PVN
           ↓
  Integrated response: ↓cortisol, ↑5-HT, ↑oxytocin
```

The DUAL input from two neural territories creates "double input" to the
same brainstem targets, potentially producing stronger brainstem
activation than single-nerve input (⚡ note #32).

**GV midline C2→V1 gradient:** The posterior-to-anterior GV line
demonstrates a systematic transition:

| GV point | Innervation | Zone |
|----------|------------|------|
| GV17 | C2 greater occipital (pure) | Posterior |
| GV18-GV19 | C2/V1 overlap (transition) | Vertex approach |
| GV20 | V1 + C2 (dual convergence) | Vertex |
| GV21-GV22 | V1 supraorbital (predominant) | Anterior vertex |
| GV23-GV24 | V1 pure (supraorbital + supratrochlear) | Frontal |
| GV25 | V1 external nasal (last V1) | Nasal bridge |
| GV26+ | V2 transition → V3 | Lower face |

This gradient means different GV head points access different
proportions of V1 vs C2 input, potentially producing different brainstem
response profiles along the midline.

#### Pattern B: Pure V1 Cranial (Yintang model)

```
V1 supratrochlear nerve ONLY
  ↓
Trigeminovascular system
  ↓
Brainstem: NTS, LC, DRN
  ↓
Anxiolytic / analgesic response
```

Validated by eTNS (Cefaly device): FDA-approved for migraine using the
SAME nerve territory (V1 supratrochlear + supraorbital). Different
modality (electrical vs mechanical), same anatomical pathway, same
clinical domain (⚡ note #36).

GV24 (Shenting) also uses pure V1. The "cognitive dyad" GV20+GV24
provides synergistic cortical coverage: GV20 engages V1+C2, GV24
engages pure V1 overlying prefrontal cortex. Combined EA at GV20+GV24
upregulates PSD-95 and modulates the melatonin-ROS-NLRP3 axis.

#### Pattern C: Triple Cranial Input with Vagal Component (Auricular model)

```
ABVN (CN X) + Auriculotemporal (V3) + Great auricular (C2-C3)
  ↓              ↓                       ↓
NTS direct    Trigeminal nucleus     Cervical dorsal horn → TCC
  ↓              ↓                       ↓
  └─────────── CONVERGE ────────────────┘
                 ↓
  Multiple brainstem nuclei (zone-specific ratio determines fingerprint)
```

The auricular system is UNIQUE: it is the ONLY body surface where 3
cranial nerve sources converge AND the ONLY surface access to the vagus
nerve (ABVN). This triple convergence creates the richest cranial nerve
input of any acupoint region.

### 5.3 The ABVN Gradient: Mapping Vagal Access Across the Ear

Peuker & Filler (2002, 7 cadavers, 14 ears), confirmed by Kaniusas
et al. (2019, Frontiers in Neuroscience):

```
ZONE                    ABVN %    VAGAL ACCESS    BRAINSTEM TARGET
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Cymba conchae           100%      Full            NTS, LC, DRN
Antihelix body          73%       High            NTS → vlPAG
Cavum conchae           45%       Moderate        NTS (cardiac-specific)
Tragus                  45%       Moderate        NTS (weaker) + ACC
Crura of antihelix      9%        Minimal         Non-vagal dominant
Scaphoid fossa          0%        None            No vagal access
Lobule                  0%        None            No vagal access
```

**Clinical correlates of ABVN gradient:**
- The 8-fold difference between antihelix body (73%) and crura (9%)
  explains why spine/trunk auricular points (AH9-AH13) have stronger
  evidence than lower limb points (AH1-AH5): dual-pathway analgesia
  (vagal + somatic) vs somatic-only (⚡ note #42).
- taVNS devices target cymba conchae (100% ABVN) specifically. Evidence
  from taVNS cannot be directly transferred to predict effects at
  non-concha auricular points with lower ABVN innervation (⚡ note #38).

### 5.4 Zone-Specific Brainstem Fingerprints

Frangos et al. (2015) fMRI demonstrates that different auricular zones
activate DIFFERENT brainstem nuclei, creating zone-specific "brainstem
fingerprints":

| Zone | Dominant nerve | Primary brainstem activation |
|------|---------------|----------------------------|
| Cymba conchae | ABVN 100% | NTS + locus coeruleus + dorsal raphe |
| Antihelix body | ABVN 73% + C2-C3 | NTS → vlPAG (dual analgesic) |
| Cavum conchae | ABVN 45% + V3 + C2-C3 | Cardiac-specific NTS neurons |
| Tragus | ABVN 45% + V3 | NTS (weaker) + bilateral ACC/frontal |
| Antitragus | C2-C3 primary | Nucleus cuneatus (somatosensory) |

The fingerprints are determined by the RATIO of nerve contributions at
each zone. This provides the first neuroanatomical explanation for why
different auricular zones treat different conditions — the nervous system
routes signals from each zone to different brainstem processing centers.

**The cavum conchae paradox (⚡ note #41):** Cavum conchae (45% ABVN)
activates cardiac-specific NTS neurons while cymba conchae (100% ABVN)
does NOT. Less ABVN produces more organ-specific effects. The MIXED
innervation (ABVN + V3 + C2-C3) at cavum may be the key to cardiac
specificity — perhaps the non-ABVN components (V3, C2-C3) route signals
to cardiac-specific NTS sub-regions that pure ABVN stimulation does
not reach.

### 5.5 Scalp Acupuncture: Trigeminovascular, Not Vagal

Scalp acupuncture (AP-17, 20 entries) operates through a distinct
cranial pathway with NO vagal component (unlike auricular):

```
Scalp afferents (V1/V2 + C2/C3 at vertex)
  ↓
Trigeminovascular system activation
  ↓
Three parallel effector mechanisms:
  1. Antidromic CGRP/substance P release → vasodilation → ↑rCBF
  2. Parasympathetic via sphenopalatine ganglion → NO → vasodilation
  3. Central RVLM pathway → blood pressure modulation
  ↓
Cortical area modulation (see §3 Pattern 15-16)
```

**Innervation-vascular correspondence (⚡ note #52):**

| Scalp region | Nerve territory | Vascular territory | Cortical area |
|-------------|----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Anterior (forehead) | V1 supraorbital | Anterior cerebral artery | Prefrontal |
| Lateral (temple) | V2/V3 | Middle cerebral artery | Motor/sensory/language |
| Vertex | V1 + C2 overlap | ACA-MCA watershed | Motor/sensory strip |
| Posterior (occiput) | C2/C3 | Posterior cerebral / vertebrobasilar | Cerebellar / visual |

The innervation territory determines which cerebrovascular territory is
modulated: anterior scalp → anterior/middle cerebral blood flow;
posterior scalp → posterior circulation. This is why different scalp
regions treat different neurological conditions (Liu 2023).

**Cortical projection hypothesis revision:** The original theory (Jiao
1971) proposed that scalp needling directly stimulates underlying cortex.
Jin 2023 critiqued this: needle tip to cortex = 8-16+ mm through bone;
>57% of scalp zones don't correspond to underlying cortex (Ye 2023).
The revised model: scalp acupuncture works through peripheral
trigeminovascular pathways that INDIRECTLY modulate cortical function via
cerebral blood flow changes and neuroplasticity cascades (BDNF/TrkB →
PI3K/AKT + MAPK/ERK → synaptogenesis).

### 5.6 Standard Meridian Head Points: Cranial Architecture Within the
Standard System

Approximately 62 standard meridian head/face points also operate through
cranial nerve architecture despite being classified within the spinal
meridian system:

| Meridian | Head/face points | Cranial nerves |
|----------|-----------------|----------------|
| BL | BL1-BL10 | V1 (orbital), C1-C3 (suboccipital) |
| ST | ST1-ST8 | V2 (infraorbital), V3 (mandibular) |
| GB | GB1-GB20 | V1-V3 (orbital-temporal), C2-C3 (occipital) |
| GV | GV17-GV28 | C2 → V1 gradient (see §5.2) |
| SI | SI16-SI19 | C2-C3 (cervical), V3 (auricular) |
| LI | LI19-LI20 | V2 (infraorbital) |
| TE | TE17, TE21-TE23 | V3, C2-C3 (periauricular) |
| CV | CV23-CV24 | C2-C3 (submental), CN XII (hypoglossal) |

These ~62 points are counted under the standard meridian system but
their mechanism discussions in the Atlas use the cranial nerve framework,
not the segmental model. This reinforces §4's finding: the segmental
model applies to ~14-17% of entries (trunk paravertebral), while the
cranial nerve model applies to ~33% of all entries.

## §6 — Molecular Specificity

### 6.1 The Current State of Molecular Knowledge

Of the 19 mechanism patterns identified in §3, only 6 (32%) have an
identified molecular effector. Only 2 (11%) have CAUSAL evidence
(ablation or blockade eliminates the effect):

| Molecule | Point | Evidence level | Method |
|----------|-------|---------------|--------|
| **PROKR2** | **ST36** | **CAUSAL** | PROKR2+ neuron ablation eliminates EA anti-inflammatory effect (Liu, Nature 2021) |
| **Oxytocin** | **GV20** | **CAUSAL** | OT receptor blockade eliminates anxiolytic effect (Gong, Chinese Medicine 2026; pseudorabies retrograde tracing confirms pathway) |
| GABA | PC6 | Supported | NTS-GABAergic inhibition proposed for anti-emetic mechanism |
| Adenosine | ST36 (local) | Supported | ATP → ectonucleotidase → A1R (Goldman 2010); operates at local level simultaneously with PROKR2 pathway |
| p47phox | LI11 | Preclinical | NADPH oxidase subunit reduction in RVLM |
| 5-HT2AR/KCC2 | MS6 | Preclinical | motion-style scalp acupuncture 2025: scalp → 5-HT2AR → spinal KCC2 → restored GABAergic tone |

The remaining 13 patterns (68%) have NO identified molecular effector.
This represents the largest single gap in acupoint mechanism research.

### 6.2 Two Points, Two Molecules, Two Circuits

The ST36 and GV20 molecular data together establish a fundamental
principle: **acupoint specificity extends to the molecular level**.

| Dimension | ST36 | GV20 |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Peripheral receptor** | PROKR2+ C-LTMR neurons | Not identified |
| **Afferent pathway** | PROKR2 → vagus → NTS/DMVN | V1+C2 → TCC → brainstem → PVN |
| **Molecular effector** | Catecholamine/dopamine (adrenal) | Oxytocin (hypothalamic) |
| **Clinical domain** | Anti-inflammatory, GI | Anti-depressant, anxiolytic |
| **Architecture** | Spinal (L4-L5, non-segmental) | Cranial (V1+C2, direct brainstem) |
| **Causal proof** | Neuron ablation | Receptor blockade |

These are not variations of the same mechanism — they are completely
different molecular circuits accessed at different body locations. This
is the strongest evidence for point-specific therapeutic mechanisms
beyond the non-specific "any needling anywhere helps" model (⚡ note #34).

### 6.3 fMRI Cortical Fingerprints

Three acupoints have been characterized by fMRI meta-analysis, each
showing a DISTINCT brain activation signature:

| Point | Study | N | Primary cortical pattern |
|-------|-------|---|------------------------|
| ST36 | Frontiers in Neuroscience 2025 | Multiple | Visual network + noradrenaline receptor map correlation |
| HT7 | Hu 2025 | — | Widest cortical DEACTIVATION pattern (broadest inhibition) |
| LR3 | Rao 2024 meta-analysis | 319 (10 studies) | Thalamus + prefrontal cortex activation |

The distinct fingerprints converge with the molecular and pathway
evidence: different acupoints produce different brain states. These
cortical fingerprints are reproducible across studies and subjects,
supporting acupoint specificity at the neuroimaging level (⚡ notes
#2, #13, #30).

**Adjacent-point specificity:** ST36 vs GB34 (2025, Frontiers in
Neuroscience) — adjacent points on different meridians show distinct
brain networks correlated with different neurotransmitter receptor maps.
This suggests specificity BEYOND somatotopy, potentially at the
neurochemical level (⚡ note #2).

### 6.4 PROKR2: Sufficient but NOT Necessary

The PROKR2 discovery (Liu 2021, Nature) established that PROKR2+
C-LTMR sensory neurons in deep fascia/periosteum are the peripheral
receptors for electroacupuncture anti-inflammatory effects at ST36.
PROKR2 ablation = complete loss of effect. This is the gold standard
causal proof.

However, the Atlas documents evidence that PROKR2 activation is NOT the
only therapeutic pathway:

**Microsystem evidence (⚡ note #53):** Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture, Abdominal Acupuncture, and
Korean Hand Therapy all use shallow (subcutaneous/cutaneous) needling that does NOT
penetrate deep connective tissue, does NOT trigger De Qi, and therefore
does NOT activate PROKR2+ C-LTMRs (which reside in deep tissue). Yet:
- Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture: 7+ systematic reviews positive for pain
- Abdominal: 3 meta-analyses positive for stroke rehabilitation
- Korean Hand Therapy: 4+ RCTs positive for PONV

If these effects are real, at least one PROKR2-independent therapeutic
pathway must exist, operating through cutaneous A-beta/A-delta afferents
→ spinal gate control + supraspinal opioid modulation.

**The PROKR2 depth question (B02 S10 Q1):** This is identified as the
"single most important open question for acupressure." Can surface
finger pressure at ST36 activate PROKR2+ neurons that sit at periosteal
depth? If NO: acupressure fundamentally cannot access the vagal-adrenal
anti-inflammatory pathway at body points. If YES: acupressure's
mechanism profile is significantly richer than currently attributed.
Resolution requires electrophysiological recording from PROKR2+ neurons
during calibrated surface pressure vs needle insertion.

**Mechanism modality scorecard (B02):**
- 11 mechanisms SHARED or STRONGER for pressure vs needle (gate control,
  somatovisceral reflex, CT afferent reward, oxytocin pathway, fascial
  mechanotransduction, vagal tone enhancement, etc.)
- 9 mechanisms REDUCED or ABSENT for pressure (PROKR2 vagal-adrenal,
  adenosine 24x, needle grasp/collagen winding, frequency-dependent
  EA opioid, micro-wound healing, etc.)
- 3 mechanisms UNCERTAIN (PROKR2 depth, endocannabinoid threshold, mast
  cell activation threshold)

The critical distinction: location-dependent but NOT depth-dependent
mechanisms are shared across modalities. Depth-dependent mechanisms
(PROKR2, adenosine, collagen winding) require needle penetration.

### 6.5 HT7 and the A-Beta Fiber Challenge

HT7 introduces a molecular-level challenge: it appears to use A-beta
(touch/proprioceptive) fibers rather than A-delta/C (pain/nociceptive)
fibers (⚡ note #12). If confirmed, this means:

1. De Qi sensation (A-delta-mediated tingling/aching) is NOT
   universally required for therapeutic effect.
2. Different acupoints may engage different SENSORY MODALITIES — touch
   vs pain — for their effects.
3. The PROKR2 model (which depends on C-LTMR activation via deep tissue
   mechanical stimulation) may not apply to A-beta-dominant points.

The A-beta pathway (dorsal column-medial lemniscal → cuneate nucleus →
thalamus → cortex) is a fundamentally different sensory channel from the
A-delta/C pathway (spinothalamic → brainstem → cortex). HT7's widest
cortical deactivation pattern (fMRI) may reflect this distinct sensory
modality engaging a different cortical processing network.

### 6.6 The Molecular Gap: Research Priorities

13 of 19 mechanism patterns (68%) lack identified molecular effectors.
This gap is not uniform — it clusters at specific levels:

| Taxonomy level | Patterns | With molecular | Gap |
|----------------|----------|----------------|-----|
| Level 2 (Segmental) | 2 | 0 specific | Segmental reflex uses standard neurotransmission — no point-specific molecule expected |
| Level 3 (Supraspinal) | 5 | 4 (PROKR2, GABA, endorphin, p47phox) | KI1 non-specific by nature |
| Level 4 (Cranial) | 5 | 0 | Largest gap — 5 cranial patterns, zero molecular data |
| Level 5 (Cortical) | 4 | 1 (5-HT2AR/KCC2) | LR3, HT7 cortical fingerprints but no molecules |
| Level 6 (Hormonal) | 1 | 1 (prolactin) | Covered |
| Cross-level | 1 | 0 | Subcutaneous pathway uncharacterized |
| Unknown | 1 | 0 | BL67 — the #1 unsolved question |

**Highest-priority molecular targets for future research:**

1. **BL67** — zero mechanism, Cochrane evidence. Any molecular
   identification would be transformative.
2. **Auricular ABVN zones** — the zone-specific brainstem fingerprints
   (Frangos 2015) suggest different molecular cascades at different
   zones, but none are characterized.
3. **LR3** — fMRI meta-analysis (10 studies) shows thalamic-PFC
   activation, but the molecular basis of this thalamic relay is unknown.
4. **HT7** — if the A-beta pathway is confirmed, identifying the
   downstream molecular effector would establish a new mechanism class.
5. **Yintang/V1 pathway** — eTNS FDA validation confirms the pathway
   works, but the molecular effector within the trigeminovascular system
   is uncharacterized for acupuncture specifically.

This section ranks the major mechanism gaps identified across the Atlas,
from most impactful to least. Each gap is assessed on two dimensions:
(1) clinical importance of the affected points, and (2) how much the

### 7.1 Ranked Gap List

#### GAP 1: BL67 Breech — Zero Mechanism, Cochrane Evidence

**Severity:** CRITICAL
**Points:** BL67 (Zhiyin)
**The gap:** S1 dermatome → uterine function (T10-L2). Zero segmental

**4 ⚡ notes** — more than any other single point.

#### GAP 2: HT5 → Speech — C8-T1 to CN XII Bridge

**Severity:** HIGH
**Points:** HT5 (Tongli)
**The gap:** Ulnar nerve at C8-T1 has no anatomical connection to
tongue motor function (CN XII hypoglossal) or sensory function (CN V3

stations between C8-T1 afferents and cortical language areas are unmapped

#### GAP 3: Ghost Points — 13 Points, No Unified Mechanism

**Severity:** HIGH
**Points:** GV26, LU11, SP1, PC7, BL62, GV16, ST6, CV24, PC8, GV23,

psychiatric crisis rather than the underlying pathology through

## §9 — Pattern Discovery

This section identifies novel patterns that emerged from per-point
mechanism data across the Atlas — findings that were not visible at the
system level in the original BSA framework and only became apparent
through the accumulation of 520 individual entries.

### 9.1 Ten Discovered Patterns

#### DISCOVERY 1: Location-on-Nerve Specificity

**Evidence:** LI4 and LI11 share the SAME peripheral nerve (radial,
C5-T1) but activate DIFFERENT brainstem nuclei — LI4 → PAG (analgesia),
LI11 → RVLM (cardiovascular). (⚡ note #7)

**Implication:** Acupoint specificity operates at finer grain than "nerve
territory." The fiber POPULATIONS accessed at each anatomical location
determine which central mechanism is engaged. This means two points on
the same nerve, separated by only 10-15 cm, can have fundamentally
different therapeutic targets.

**Mechanism:** At the elbow (LI11), the radial nerve contains different
proportions of sympathetic efferent-related afferents vs nociceptive
fibers compared to the hand (LI4). The local anatomy — muscle bellies,
fascial planes, depth of nerve, surrounding vascular structures —
selects which fiber sub-populations are preferentially activated.

**Strength:** Preclinical. Both LI4-PAG and LI11-RVLM pathways are
demonstrated in animal models. Human confirmation needed.

---

#### DISCOVERY 2: Multi-Nerve Convergence Predicts Clinical Breadth

**Evidence:** Acupoints innervated by multiple nerve sources consistently
show broader clinical indication repertoires:

| Point | Nerve convergence | Indication breadth |
|-------|------------------|-------------------|
| GV20 | V1 + C2 (2 sources) | Broad neuropsychiatric + autonomic |
| Ear Shen Men | V3 + ABVN + C2-C3 (3 sources) | Broadest auricular (6/6 systems) |
| LI4 | Radial + median territory (2 sources) | Broadest non-visceral |
| PC8 | Median + ulnar (2 sources) | Mental + cardiac + oral |

Single-nerve points (e.g., HT7 ulnar-only) tend to have narrower, more
specific indication profiles. (⚡ note #28)

**Implication:** Neural convergence at a point may be a structural
PREDICTOR of clinical breadth — a point that receives input from
multiple nerve territories can send signals through multiple central
pathways simultaneously, producing a wider range of therapeutic effects.
This extends A09 S2.2's multi-system convergence model from tissue-level
(neural + fascial + vascular) to pathway-level (multiple neural
pathways at one site).

---

#### DISCOVERY 3: Mechanism-Convergence Dissociation

**Evidence:** Phase 3 convergence data (407 scored points) combined with
Phase 4 mechanism data reveals that convergence predicts mechanism
CLARITY (how well we understand WHY a point works) but NOT clinical
EFFICACY (WHETHER it works):

| Point | Convergence | Mechanism clarity | Clinical efficacy |
|-------|------------|------------------|-------------------|
| Back-Shu | 3-6/8 (high, mean 4.75) | HIGH (textbook segmental) | Established |
| BL67 | 2/8 (lowest Tier A) | ZERO (unknown) | Cochrane-level |
| Jing-Well | 1-3/8 (lowest) | ZERO | Limited |

BL67 proves that high efficacy can exist with low convergence and zero
mechanism understanding. Jing-Well points have low convergence AND
low efficacy — for them, convergence correctly predicts clinical utility.
The dissociation is specific to points with high mechanism-specificity on
the orthogonal axis (the dual-axis model). (⚡ notes #16, #22)

---

#### DISCOVERY 4: Three Architectural Regions

**Evidence:** The 520 Atlas entries naturally partition into three
regions, each with fundamentally different mechanism architecture:

```
TRUNK (paravertebral + midline + lateral)
  Architecture: Spinal segmental
  Primary mechanism: Somatovisceral reflex (dorsal horn convergence)
  Segmental contribution: 40-100%
  Mechanism clarity: HIGH
  ~140-180 entries

LIMB (proximal + distal + digits)
  Architecture: Spinal supraspinal
  Primary mechanism: Point-specific brainstem pathways
  Segmental contribution: 0-30%
  Mechanism clarity: LOW-MODERATE
  ~135-175 entries

HEAD/EAR/SCALP
  Architecture: Cranial nerve direct
  Primary mechanism: Direct brainstem access (no spinal cord)
  Segmental contribution: N/A
  Mechanism clarity: MODERATE (emerging)
  ~165-205 entries (incl. microsystems)
```

This three-region model is more predictive than the traditional
12-meridian system for understanding mechanism type. A point's body
REGION determines its mechanism class more reliably than its meridian
membership. (⚡ notes #21, #33, #39)

---

#### DISCOVERY 5: Cortical Priming Principle

**Evidence:** Scalp acupuncture works as a neuroplasticity PRIMER, not a
standalone therapy. The principle appears across two independent clinical
domains:

| Domain | scalp acupuncture alone | scalp acupuncture + concurrent rehab | Gap |
|--------|---------|----------------------|-----|
| Motor (motion-style scalp acupuncture) | Standard effect | Superior effect | Significant |
| Speech (aphasia) | SUCRA 17.8% | SUCRA 97.9% | ~80 points |

The ~80-point SUCRA gap in aphasia is the largest treatment combination
effect documented in the Atlas. The proposed mechanism (motion-style scalp acupuncture 2025):
scalp needling creates a CBF-augmented, 5-HT2AR-mediated
"neuroplasticity window" that concurrent motor/speech training maximally
exploits. (⚡ notes #49, #51)

**Implication:** Scalp acupuncture should be evaluated primarily as a
COMBINATION therapy, not as a monotherapy. RCTs testing scalp acupuncture alone may
systematically underestimate its therapeutic potential.

---

#### DISCOVERY 6: Modality Importance is Point-Specific

**Evidence:** Five distinct modality-effect relationships documented:

| Relationship | Point | Finding |
|-------------|-------|---------|
| Modality-INDEPENDENT | PC6 | All methods equally effective for PONV |
| Modality-REVERSED | SP6 | Acupressure > acupuncture for dysmenorrhea |
| Modality-DEPENDENT | BL67 | Moxa-only for breech (thermal pathway) |
| Fiber-DIVERSE | HT7 | A-beta pathway, De Qi may be incidental |
| Depth-INDEPENDENT | Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture/Korean Hand Therapy | Subcutaneous works, PROKR2 not required |

No universal rule governs which technique is "best." The optimal
modality depends on the specific mechanism at each point: where the
mechanism is neural activation at a location (PC6), any stimulus works;
where the mechanism requires thermal nociception (BL67), only heat
works; where the mechanism engages fascial chains (SP6 dysmenorrhea),
sustained pressure outperforms focal needle. (⚡ notes #10, #12, #18,
#27, #53)

---

#### DISCOVERY 7: Molecular Diversity Beyond Pathway Diversity

**Evidence:** The Atlas documents not just different pathways but
different MOLECULES at different points:

- ST36 → PROKR2 → catecholamine/dopamine (anti-inflammatory)
- GV20 → oxytocin (anti-depressant/anxiolytic)
- PC6 → GABA (anti-emetic)
- ST36 local → adenosine (analgesic)
- LI11 → p47phox (cardiovascular)
- MS6 → 5-HT2AR/KCC2 (motor recovery)

Each uses a different signaling molecule through a different circuit to a
different clinical endpoint. This extends specificity from anatomy
(which nerve) to biochemistry (which molecule). (⚡ notes #3, #34)

---

#### DISCOVERY 8: PROKR2 is Necessary Context, Not Complete Answer

**Evidence:** The PROKR2 discovery (Liu 2021, Nature) was transformative
for ST36 mechanism, but three Atlas findings show it is one mechanism
among many:

1. Adenosine operates simultaneously at ST36 via a DIFFERENT pathway
   (Goldman 2010) — multi-system convergence at one site (⚡ note #3)
2. HT7 may use A-beta fibers, not C-LTMRs — different fiber type
   entirely (⚡ note #12)
3. Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture/Korean Hand Therapy microsystems work subcutaneously without PROKR2 activation —
   at least one PROKR2-independent therapeutic pathway exists (⚡ note
   #53)

**Implication:** PROKR2 is sufficient (proven by ablation at ST36) but
NOT necessary (proven by microsystem evidence). The field should not
generalize PROKR2 to all acupoints — each point may use a different
molecular receptor, and some therapeutic pathways may not require
specific molecular receptors at all.

---

#### DISCOVERY 9: Zone vs Point in Microsystems

**Evidence:** The auricular microsystem data challenges whether
individual named "points" are meaningful therapeutic units or whether
ZONES with characteristic innervation patterns are the real units:

- Within cymba conchae (100% ABVN), there is no neuroanatomical basis
  for distinguishing CO4 (Stomach) from CO5 (Esophagus) — same nerve,
  same NTS projection (⚡ note #40)
- Yeo 2013: no significant difference between 5-point protocol and
  single-point stimulation within the same zone
- BUT between zones (cymba vs cavum), differential NTS activation IS
  documented (He 2012, ⚡ note #41)

**Implication:** Auricular therapy may operate at ZONE granularity
(concha, antihelix, tragus, etc.), not POINT granularity (CO4 vs CO5 vs
CO6). The organ-specific naming within each zone may be a clinical
scaffold that does not map to neuroanatomical reality. This does not
invalidate auricular therapy — but it reframes the specificity claim
from "organ-specific points" to "zone-specific neural pathways."

---

#### DISCOVERY 10: Innervation-Vascular Correspondence in Scalp

**Evidence:** Scalp acupuncture reveals a topographic correspondence
between nerve territory and cerebrovascular territory:

- Anterior scalp (V1/V2/V3) → anterior/middle cerebral artery → motor,
  sensory, language cortex
- Posterior scalp (C2/C3) → posterior cerebral/vertebrobasilar → 
  cerebellar, visual cortex

Liu 2023: C2/C3 stimulation enhances posterior (vertebrobasilar) but NOT
middle cerebral artery flow. (⚡ note #52)

**Implication:** The mechanism is NOT direct cortical stimulation (Jiao
1971 hypothesis — critiqued: needle tip 8-16+ mm from cortex, >57% of
zones don't correspond to underlying cortex). The mechanism IS
trigeminovascular-mediated blood flow modulation, where the peripheral
nerve territory determines which cerebrovascular territory is enhanced.
This provides a testable, falsifiable mechanism for scalp zone
selection.

### 9.2 Patterns NOT Found

Equally important as what was discovered is what was NOT found:

1. **No universal acupuncture mechanism.** 19 distinct patterns, not one.
2. **No meridian-based mechanism prediction.** ST25 and ST36 (same
   meridian) use completely different mechanisms. Meridian membership
   does not predict mechanism type.
3. **No De Qi requirement.** HT7 (A-beta), PC6 (all modalities), Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture
   (subcutaneous) — De Qi is not universally required.
4. **No depth hierarchy.** "Deeper = more effective" is not supported.
   SP6 acupressure > acupuncture; microsystems work subcutaneously.
5. **No single anatomical substrate.** Acupoints are multi-system
   convergence zones (A09 S2.2) — no single tissue type (nerve, fascia,
   blood vessel) is "the" acupoint.

## §10 — Framework Implications & Proposed Updates

### 10.1 What Phase 4 Changes About the BSA Framework

Phase 4's mechanism analysis produces five structural revisions to the
BSA framework's understanding of acupoint mechanisms:

**Revision 1: From "60-70% segmental" to regional range model.**
The segmental estimate in C04 S3.4 should be replaced by a body-region
model where segmental contribution varies continuously from ~100%
(paravertebral) to 0% (distal limb) with head/ear points outside the
model entirely (cranial nerve architecture).

**Revision 2: From "convergence zone" to "dual-axis model."**
A09 S2.2's convergence zone model correctly identifies multi-system
overlap as the BASIS for acupoint specificity, but convergence alone
does not predict clinical importance. A second axis — mechanism
specificity — is needed. Together: clinical importance = convergence
breadth × mechanism specificity.

**Revision 3: From implicit "one mechanism" to explicit "19 patterns."**
The BSA framework discusses mechanisms at the system level (A01-A06
Track A systems). Phase 4 demonstrates that the mechanism catalog at the
PER-POINT level contains 19 distinct patterns across 6 taxonomy levels.
No single Track A system explains all acupoint effects.

**Revision 4: From "spinal pathway" assumption to "three architectures."**
The BSA framework implicitly assumes spinal pathway architecture for
all acupoints. Phase 4 documents that ~33% of Atlas entries operate
through cranial nerve architecture with direct brainstem access. This
should be added to the framework as a distinct pathway class.

**Revision 5: From "PROKR2 discovery" to "molecular diversity."**
A02 correctly highlights PROKR2 as a breakthrough molecular mechanism.
Phase 4 shows this is one of at least 6 identified molecular effectors,
each at a different point through a different circuit. PROKR2 is
sufficient but not necessary for therapeutic effect.

### 10.4 Final Assessment

The Acupoint Atlas Phase 4 (Mechanism Attribution) demonstrates that:

1. **Acupuncture has 19 distinct mechanisms**, not one. The diversity
   is systematic, determined by anatomy.
2. **The segmental model covers 14-17% of entries** (~100% segmental),
   not 60-70%. The average is approximately correct but misleading
   per-point.
3. **~33% of entries operate through cranial nerve architecture**,
   a pathway class not represented in the current BSA framework.
4. **Only 2 of 19 patterns have causal molecular evidence** (ST36
   PROKR2, GV20 oxytocin). 68% lack any molecular characterization.
5. **BL67 remains unsolved** — Cochrane evidence, zero mechanism, four
   ⚡ notes, and the single biggest challenge to every framework model.
6. **The dual-axis model** (convergence × mechanism specificity) is the
   key structural addition for Phase 5.
7. **17 specific BSA file updates** are needed across 8+ files.

The mechanism IS the anatomy. The body's nervous system provides
different therapeutic access at different locations. The traditions that
mapped these locations empirically encoded real neuroanatomical
relationships — but through a framework (meridians, Qi, Five Elements)
that does not correspond to the actual mechanism. The Atlas separates
the DATA (which locations work for which conditions) from the
EXPLANATION (why they work), and Phase 4 demonstrates that the "why"
is point-specific anatomical pathway access.
