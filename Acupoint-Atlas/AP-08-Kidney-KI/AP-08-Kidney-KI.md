## KI1 — Dũng Tuyền (涌泉, Yǒngquán)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**

"Gushing Spring" — first/lowest point on the Kidney channel. Kidney Qi
"emerges" here like spring water gushing from the earth (Lingshu Ch.2).
The ONLY Jing-Well point located on the sole of the foot (all other
Jing-Well points are at finger/toe tips). One of the most painful
acupoints to needle — traditionally reserved for emergency and severe
conditions.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO 2008: On the sole, in the depression when the foot is in plantar
flexion, at the junction of the anterior 1/3 and posterior 2/3 of the
line connecting the base of the 2nd and 3rd toes with the heel.

Anatomical landmarks: between the 2nd and 3rd metatarsal bones, over
the plantar aponeurosis. The depression is palpable when toes are
curled.

Needle depth: 5–10 mm perpendicular. PAINFUL — foot sole has extremely
high mechanoreceptor and nociceptor density. Traditional texts
recommend strong stimulation for emergency use only; acupressure or
moxibustion preferred for routine clinical applications.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2, B02 S4

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

**Nerve supply:**

Dual plantar nerve innervation (Kim et al. 2024, Medicina — cadaver
dissection, 6 specimens):
- Medial plantar nerve (L4–L5) — sensory to medial sole
- Lateral plantar nerve (S1–S2) — sensory to lateral sole

Both are terminal branches of the tibial nerve (from sciatic nerve,
L4–S2). KI1 is positioned at the interface of medial and lateral
plantar nerve territories, providing DUAL nerve access — analogous in
principle to SP6's dual tibial nerve access but at a different spinal
level.

Spinal segments: L4–S2

**Fascial plane:**

Plantar aponeurosis (plantar fascia) — thick, dense connective tissue
sheet. One of the strongest fascial structures in the body. Continuous
with the Superficial Back Line (A03 §4.2): plantar fascia →
gastrocnemius → hamstrings → sacrotuberous ligament → erector spinae →
epicranial fascia = foot sole to scalp.

KI1 is at the START of the Superficial Back Line — the lowest origin
point of this continuous fascial chain.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S4.2

**Vascular:** Plantar arterial arch (deep plantar arch from dorsalis
pedis artery + lateral plantar artery). Rich arterial anastomosis in
foot sole.

**Muscle layers (superficial → deep):**
1. Plantar aponeurosis (fascia, no muscle fibers at KI1 surface)
2. Flexor digitorum brevis
3. Second layer: flexor digitorum longus tendons, quadratus plantae
   (flexor accessorius)
4. Third layer: flexor hallucis brevis, adductor hallucis

**Skin type:** GLABROUS — thick keratinized epidermis. Dense
Meissner corpuscles (light touch), Merkel cells (sustained pressure),
Pacinian corpuscles (deep vibration). These receptors serve BALANCE AND
LOCOMOTION, not organ sensing (A07 §3.4).

**CT afferents: ABSENT.** Glabrous skin = no C-tactile afferents =
no affective touch pathway = no oxytocin via CT→paraventricular nucleus
(PVN) pathway. This is a fundamental mechanistic constraint: KI1
cannot activate the pleasant/affective touch pathway available at
hairy-skin acupoints.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.5.7, A01 S4.3, C01 S4.7

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Jing-Well (Wood)** — the first of the Five Shu Transport
points on the Kidney channel. UNIQUE among all Jing-Well points: located
on the foot SOLE rather than at a finger/toe tip. This deviation from
the standard Jing-Well pattern is anatomically significant — sole vs
toe tip means different tissue depth, different receptor density, and
different fascial plane access.

Yuan-Source: no (KI3 Taixi is Yuan-Source of Kidney)
Luo-Connecting: no
Xi-Cleft: no
Back-Shu: no
Front-Mu: no
Eight Confluent: no
Hui-Meeting: no

Special: **First point of Kidney channel** — Kidney Qi originates
here (Lingshu Ch.2: "腎出於湧泉"). **Emergency/resuscitation point** —
classical combination KI1 + GV26 (Shuigou) + PC8 (Laogong) for
fainting, shock, yang collapse. NOT a Ghost Point. NOT a Command Point.
NOT a Ma Dan-yang Star.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**TCM acupoint:** KI1 Yongquan — Jing-Well (Wood) point of Kidney
channel. Primary function: descend excess from the head, revive
consciousness, calm the spirit. The "root" of the Kidney channel where
Yin Qi enters from the earth.

**Trigger point:** No correspondence found. Flexor digitorum brevis
has trigger points but they refer pain to the metatarsal heads, not to
the KI1 depression. Dorsher mapping does not identify a trigger point
at this specific sole location.

**Ashi correspondence:** Limited. Foot sole tenderness at KI1 is not a
clinically meaningful ashi finding in the same way as tenderness at
limb/trunk points. The foot sole is generally tender to deep pressure
in most people, making ashi diagnosis unreliable here.

**Marma point: Talahridaya (तलहृदय, "Heart of the Sole")** — one of
107 Marma points in Ayurvedic system. Located at the center of the
sole. Two Talahridaya points total (one per foot, plus two in palms =
4 Talahridaya total). Classification: Mamsa (muscle) Marma. Associated
with Pranavaha Srotas (respiratory system), circulation, and Bhrajata
Pitta (heat regulation). Classical function: calming, grounding,
settling Vata. Pada Abhyanga (foot oiling and massage) targeting
Talahridaya = one of the most referenced classical practices for
supporting sleep and grounding (parallels KI1's insomnia indication).
Injury classification: Vaikalyakara (causes deformity/disability).

**Head zone:** No correspondence. L4–S2 dermatomes have NO visceral
projection to the kidneys (T10–L1), brain, or cardiovascular system
(T1–T5). The segmental mismatch is fundamental and unresolvable through
known convergence mechanisms.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S3.4

**Anatomy Trains: Superficial Back Line (SBL) — START POINT.** KI1
sits on the plantar fascia, which is the origin of the SBL: plantar
fascia → gastrocnemius → hamstrings → sacrotuberous ligament → erector
spinae → epicranial fascia. This continuous fascial chain connects
the foot sole to the scalp — notably, KI1 (foot sole) to the GV20
region (scalp vertex). Traditional Chinese medicine also links KI1 to
vertex symptoms (headache, dizziness). The SBL provides anatomical
continuity but the speed and magnitude of fascial force transmission
over this distance is uncertain for acute therapeutic effects.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S4.2

**Neurogenic spot:** No data. Kim 2017 did not map foot sole neurogenic
spots.

**Cupping point:** Not applicable. Foot sole is curved, thick-skinned,
and unsuitable for cup suction.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Marma Talahridaya, Anatomy Trains
SBL). Lower than limb points (LI4 5/8, SP6 4/8) — anatomically
expected for foot sole which lacks trigger points, ashi significance,
Head zone projections, neurogenic spot data, and cupping applicability.
The Talahridaya correspondence is nonetheless significant: both
traditions independently identified the center of the sole as a
therapeutically important point for calming/sleep.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA)

**Nguồn cổ:**
- Lingshu Ch.2 (Ben Shu): "腎出於湧泉，湧泉者足心也" — "Kidney Qi
  emerges at Yongquan; Yongquan is the center of the foot." Establishes
  KI1 as the root/origin of the Kidney channel.
- Nanjing Ch.68: Jing-Well points indicated for "fullness below the
  heart" (心下滿) — emergency/acute excess patterns.
- Jiayi Jing (Systematic Classic): vertex headache (巔頂痛), dizziness,
  infantile convulsions, loss of consciousness, throat pain, loss of
  voice.

**Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):**
Loss of consciousness, mania, epilepsy, infantile convulsions; vertex
headache, dizziness, blurred vision; sore throat, dry tongue, loss of
voice; difficulty urinating, constipation; hypertension; menopausal hot
flushes; nausea/vomiting.

**Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:**

*Emergency/resuscitation point:* The most consistent historical use of
KI1 is as an emergency point for loss of consciousness and yang
collapse. Classical combination: KI1 + GV26 + PC8 — all three are
PAINFUL points, suggesting the mechanism involves noxious arousal
stimulation. Clinical trials since 1987 applied KI1 stimulation after
standard cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) failure (Alvarez 2010,
Resuscitation — "Lazarus phenomenon" paper). The technique involves
strong manual stimulation of KI1 in conscious patients and is described
as "very painful" — acupuncture at KI1 is traditionally reserved for
severe/emergency conditions only.

*Hypertension:* KI1 = most commonly used single acupoint for
hypertension in traditional protocols. Moxa and herbal fomentation at
KI1 are standard complementary approaches.

*Insomnia:* Moxa at KI1 = classical treatment for insomnia, paralleling
the Ayurvedic Pada Abhyanga (foot sole massage) practice for sleep.
Both traditions independently arrive at "stimulate the foot sole center
for sleep" — a cross-cultural convergence worth noting.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Hypertension — 🔴 Unvalidated (KI1-specific)**

Yang et al. 2014, Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine
— systematic review of 6 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) for
moxibustion at KI1 for essential hypertension. Results: moxa at KI1 +
antihypertensive drugs > antihypertensive drugs alone for systolic blood
pressure (SBP). NO significant difference for diastolic blood pressure
(DBP). Limitations: all Chinese studies, variable trial design, NO sham
moxibustion control. Quality assessment: high risk of bias.

Tong & Yu 2012, Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine —
herbal fomentation at KI1 (N=102, ages 39–55). Reduced daytime SBP and
DBP but NOT nighttime or 24-hour blood pressure. No control group for
the fomentation component.

Confidence: 🔴 — no high-quality sham-controlled trial exists for KI1
specifically for hypertension. The systematic review (SR) evidence is
confounded by
combination therapy and lack of sham controls. General acupuncture for
hypertension = 🟡 (C02: systolic blood pressure -6.73 mmHg), but KI1's
specific contribution is undemonstrated.

**2. Insomnia / sleep quality — 🟡 Plausible**

He F et al. 2018, Zhongguo Zhen Jiu (Chinese Acupuncture &
Moxibustion) — literature analysis: 27 clinical papers on moxibustion
at KI1 for insomnia, including 14 RCTs. Few papers used
KI1 moxibustion as sole intervention; most combined with acupuncture,
massage, or other points (commonly HT7, GV20). Acupressure sleep
quality meta-analysis: standardized mean difference (SMD) = -0.81 (95%
confidence interval: -1.26 to -0.36, P < 0.0001, I² = 78.6%) — but
pooled across multiple acupressure points, not KI1-specific.

KI1 appears consistently in insomnia treatment protocols alongside HT7
(Shenmen) and GV20 (Baihui). The combination reflects traditional
theory (Kidney Yin nourishes Heart Shen for sleep), but clinical data
does not isolate KI1's individual contribution.

Confidence: 🟡 — consistent traditional use, cross-tradition convergence
(Ayurvedic Talahridaya for sleep), 14 RCTs available, but no sole-point
KI1 trial with sham control for insomnia specifically.

**3. Disorders of consciousness (DOC) — 🟡 Plausible**

Feng et al. 2014, Neural Regeneration Research — positron emission
tomography (PET) study (N=10 DOC patients). Acupuncture at KI1 induced
stronger neuronal activity than sham acupoints. Activated regions:
putamen, cingulate cortex, frontal lobe, cerebellum — areas associated
with conscious processing.

Clinical study: electroacupuncture (EA) at GV20 + GV26 + KI1 combined
with medication → 73.3% (11/15) patients regained consciousness vs
28.6% (4/14) in medication-only control. Small sample, no sham, multi-
point protocol.

Resuscitation context: Alvarez 2010, Resuscitation — reports on
clinical trials since 1987 applying KI1 stimulation after CPR failure
(58 patients, ~85% survival). Described as "Lazarus phenomenon." NO
randomized controlled trial, historical controls only. Intense pain from
KI1 stimulation likely functions as a noxious arousal stimulus rather
than a specific acupoint mechanism.

Confidence: 🟡 — PET imaging shows KI1 > sham for brain activation in
DOC patients; however, sample sizes are small, no replication, and the
arousal response may be attributable to PAIN INTENSITY rather than
point-specific pathway. The resuscitation data is interesting but
uncontrolled.

**4. Epilepsy — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Animal model data: EA at ST40 (Fenglong) + KI1 at 10 Hz reduced
seizure frequency and intensity. No human systematic review or RCT for
KI1-specific epilepsy treatment. Traditional indication only.

Confidence: 🔴 — animal data only, always combined with ST40, no human
clinical evidence for KI1's contribution.

**Tier assessment note:** Phase 1 plan flagged KI1 as "A→B?" pending
evidence survey. Verdict: maintain Tier A. Although clinical evidence
is weaker than points like ST36, PC6, or LI4, the MECHANISM PUZZLE
(intensity-dependent vs pathway-specific) and the FRAMEWORK INSIGHTS
justify deep analysis. Comparable to BL67 — limited mechanistic
explanation but the puzzle itself is important.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Limited data. Foot sole mechanoreceptors have well-characterized
sensitivity curves for balance and locomotion (Kennedy & Inglis 2002)
but no specific disease-state sensitization data exists for KI1 in
the way that trunk points show visceral pathology-related threshold
changes (Kim 2017).

The 2024 cadaver study (Kim et al. 2024, Medicina) found gender
differences in the exact anatomical position of KI1 relative to
plantar nerves, suggesting that individual variation in point location
may be more relevant at KI1 than at limb points with clearer anatomical
landmarks.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4, C05 S3

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at KI1:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): Medial plantar nerve (L4–L5) +
  lateral plantar nerve (S1–S2). Dense Aδ and C fiber activation in
  glabrous foot sole (high nociceptor density). Strong Aβ activation
  from Meissner/Merkel/Pacinian mechanoreceptors. CT afferents ABSENT
  (glabrous skin).
- A03 (Fascia-Connective Tissue): Plantar aponeurosis = SBL start
  point. Continuous fascial chain to scalp.
- A07 (Dermatome): L4–S2 territory. NO segmental connection to
  Kidney (T10–L1), brain, or cardiovascular system (T1–T5).

**The non-segmental gap at KI1 — THE LARGEST IN PHASE 1:**

```
KI1 spinal segments:      L4–S2 (tibial nerve branches)
Traditional targets:
  Kidney (TCM organ):     T10–L1 = 6-segment gap (minimum)
  Brain/consciousness:    Brainstem/cortex = CROSS-SYSTEM (spinal →
                          supraspinal, no direct pathway)
  Cardiovascular (BP):    T1–T5 = 9-segment gap (minimum)
  Vertex headache:        Trigeminal V1 + C2 = CROSS-SYSTEM

Comparison with other Phase 1 non-segmental gaps:
  ST36:  L4–L5 → T6–T9   = 5 segments
  LR3:   L4–L5 → T7–T9   = 5 segments
  PC6:   C8–T1 → T6–T9   = 6 segments
  LI4:   C6–T1 → CN V    = cross-system (spinal → cranial)
  GV20:  V1/C2 → brain   = NO gap (cranial nerve → brainstem direct)
  KI1:   L4–S2 → T10–L1 / brain / T1–T5 = 6–9+ segments AND
         cross-system
```

KI1 has the LARGEST non-segmental gap of any Phase 1 point for its
PRIMARY traditional indications. C04 §3.5.7 identifies the foot sole
as the WEAKEST neural access region — NO special brainstem pathway,
NO CT afferents, segmental mismatch with all major target organs.

**Proposed mechanism — INTENSITY-DEPENDENT NON-SPECIFIC rather than
PATHWAY-SPECIFIC:**

Unlike the 9 prior Phase 1 mechanism patterns, which each identified a
specific neural pathway, specific brainstem target, or specific
molecular effector, KI1 has NO identified point-specific pathway. The
mechanism may operate through STIMULUS INTENSITY rather than PATHWAY
SPECIFICITY:

```
Step 1: Needling/strong pressure at KI1 activates dense Aδ/C fibers
        in foot sole (glabrous skin = very high nociceptor density,
        thick plantar fascia creates firm substrate for deep pressure)
Step 2: INTENSE nociceptive signal ascends via spinothalamic +
        spinoreticular tracts to brainstem
Step 3: Brainstem arousal centers activated non-specifically:
        → Reticular activating system (RAS) → arousal/consciousness
        → Periaqueductal gray (PAG) → endogenous opioid release
        → Locus coeruleus (LC) → norepinephrine → arousal/alertness
        → Nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) → cardiovascular regulation
Step 4: Effects mediated by INTENSITY of input rather than
        SPECIFICITY of pathway

Key supporting evidence:
- KI1 needling is "very painful" — traditionally only for emergency
  or severe conditions (Alvarez 2010)
- Classical emergency combination (KI1 + GV26 + PC8) = all three
  are PAINFUL points, consistent with noxious arousal mechanism
- PET study (Feng et al. 2014): KI1 activated putamen, cingulate,
  frontal lobe, cerebellum — general consciousness/arousal areas,
  NOT organ-specific areas
- Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) study (Hsieh et al.
  2011, Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine — laser
  at KI1): primary somatosensory cortex, precuneus, medial frontal
  gyrus — memory, attention, self-consciousness functions
- Foot sole has the HIGHEST mechanoreceptor density of any body
  surface area but serves BALANCE functions, not organ connection
```

**Contrast with pathway-specific Phase 1 mechanisms:**

```
Pattern     Point   Pathway
─────────── ─────── ──────────────────────────────────────────
1. PROKR2   ST36    PROKR2+ neurons → vagal-adrenal → anti-
                    inflammatory (specific molecule, specific
                    nerve, specific organ response)
2. NTS-GABA PC6     Median nerve → NTS → GABA → dorsal motor
                    nucleus of vagus (DMV) → vagal cardiac
                    (specific neurotransmitter at specific
                    brainstem nucleus)
3. PAG/RVM  LI4     Radial nerve → PAG/rostroventromedial
                    medulla (RVM) → descending modulation →
                    trigeminal territory (specific descending
                    target)
9. Cranial  GV20    V1/C2 → trigeminal nucleus → PVN →
                    oxytocin (specific molecule from
                    specific brainstem nucleus)
─────────── ─────── ──────────────────────────────────────────
10. INTENSITY KI1   Dense Aδ/C → general brainstem arousal
                    (NON-specific: RAS, PAG, LC, NTS all
                    activated by high-intensity input,
                    without identified point-specific
                    selectivity)
```

**The key distinction:** Patterns 1–9 identify something SPECIFIC about
the acupoint (specific nerve branch, specific brainstem target, specific
molecular effector) that explains WHY THAT POINT produces THOSE EFFECTS
and not others. Pattern 10 (KI1) may represent a fundamentally
different mechanism class: effects driven by the INTENSITY of sensory
input from a densely innervated area, activating brainstem arousal
centers in a NON-SPECIFIC manner. This means any sufficiently painful
stimulus at any body location might produce similar consciousness/
arousal effects — the "point specificity" at KI1 may be the ANATOMICAL
ACCESSIBILITY of intense stimulation (foot sole = firm substrate +
dense nociceptors) rather than a specific neural pathway.

**Gap:** This intensity-dependent model predicts that strong painful
stimulation at a non-acupoint on the foot sole would produce similar
emergency/consciousness effects. The PET study (Feng et al. 2014) found
KI1 > sham, but the sham location and stimulation intensity are
critical details. If sham received LESS painful stimulation, the
difference could reflect intensity rather than location. This is the
key experiment needed to resolve KI1's mechanism question.

**SBL fascial connection — secondary mechanism?** KI1 sits at the start
of the Superficial Back Line, with continuous fascial connectivity to
the scalp vertex (GV20 territory). Traditional indications include
vertex headache and dizziness — targets at the OTHER END of the SBL.
Mechanical force transmission through fascia over this distance is
documented (A03 §4.2) but the speed and magnitude of therapeutic
effects via this pathway remain uncertain. This fascial pathway may
contribute to chronic/postural effects (foot-to-head tension patterns)
but is unlikely to explain acute effects like consciousness recovery.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S4.2

⚡ Framework note 1: KI1 = 10th mechanism pattern — INTENSITY-DEPENDENT
NON-SPECIFIC brainstem activation. Unlike patterns 1–9 which each
identify a specific pathway/molecule/brainstem target, KI1's effects may
arise from the INTENSITY of nociceptive input from the densely
innervated foot sole, activating brainstem arousal centers (reticular
activating system, periaqueductal gray, locus coeruleus, nucleus tractus
solitarius) non-specifically. This proposes a NEW mechanism class:
some acupoints may work through intensity-dependent activation rather
than pathway-specific activation. Extends A09 §2 mechanism catalog.

⚡ Framework note 2: C04 §3.5.7 identifies foot sole as the WEAKEST
neural access region — correct for organ-specific therapy. But KI1's
primary traditional use (emergency consciousness recovery) bypasses
organ-specificity entirely. The target is BRAINSTEM AROUSAL, accessible
from any strong nociceptive input. "Weakest region" applies to
segmental organ-targeting; it does not predict efficacy for intensity-
dependent brainstem activation where the foot sole's HIGH NOCICEPTOR
DENSITY becomes an advantage rather than a limitation. The framework
assessment should distinguish organ-targeting (where foot sole is
weakest) from arousal-targeting (where foot sole may be adequate).
Extends C04 §3.5.7.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 5–10 mm perpendicular insertion.
- De Qi: INTENSE — sharp pain, electric-like sensation radiating through
  the sole. Unlike most acupoints where De Qi is described as dull
  distention/heaviness, KI1 De Qi is predominantly PAINFUL (Aδ/C fiber
  dominant rather than Aβ dominant).
- Clinical use: traditionally reserved for emergency or severe
  conditions due to pain intensity. Bleeding technique (Jing-Well
  tradition): prick to bleed at KI1 for acute heat conditions, mania,
  loss of consciousness.
- EA: rarely applied at KI1 due to pain intolerance. When used (DOC
  studies), combined with GV20 and GV26 in multi-point protocols.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02):
- B02 Tier 2 point. PRIMARY technique for routine KI1 clinical use
  (avoids the extreme pain of needling).
- Technique: firm thumb pressure into the foot sole depression,
  sustained 30–60 seconds. Patient can self-apply.
- Conditions: insomnia (pre-sleep foot acupressure), anxiety, headache.
- KI1 acupressure has "some clinical evidence for specific effects
  (anxiety, insomnia) possibly via strong Aβ + Aδ stimulation" (B06
  §5.3).
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Cứu (B08):
- Moxa at KI1 = most commonly studied KI1 technique. Indirect moxa
  (moxa stick held above sole) or herbal fomentation preferred over
  direct moxa (foot sole sensitivity).
- Yang et al. 2014 SR: moxa at KI1 for hypertension (6 RCTs).
- Literature analysis 2018: moxa at KI1 for insomnia (14 RCTs).
- Traditional protocol: moxa at KI1 + salt-filled moxa at CV8 (Shenque)
  for Kidney Yang deficiency.
→ Cross-ref: B08

Dry needling (B11):
- Not applicable. No trigger point at KI1 location. Flexor digitorum
  brevis trigger points exist in the foot sole but not at the KI1
  depression (Dorsher mapping). Dry needling at KI1 would produce
  only pain without the local twitch response that defines trigger
  point treatment.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

So sánh: Acupressure and moxibustion are the primary evidence-based
techniques at KI1. Acupuncture is reserved for emergency/severe
conditions. EA rarely used at KI1 alone. The technique hierarchy at
KI1 is INVERTED compared to most Tier A points (where acupuncture is
primary) — reflecting the extreme pain sensitivity of the foot sole.

### An toàn

Vascular risk: Plantar arterial arch branches are present but deep
to the needle insertion zone at standard depth (5–10 mm). Low vascular
risk at recommended depth. Bleeding technique (Jing-Well prick) is
intentional and controlled.

Neural risk: Medial and lateral plantar nerves are adjacent to KI1
(Kim et al. 2024 cadaver study). Excessive depth beyond 10 mm could
contact nerve trunks. Risk is LOW at standard 5–10 mm depth but higher
than at most acupoints due to the proximity of major plantar nerves.

Pneumothorax: None. Foot sole location.

Pregnancy: NOT listed as contraindicated in pregnancy. KI1 is not among
the traditionally forbidden pregnancy points (LI4, SP6, BL60, BL67,
GB21).

Pain: THE PRIMARY SAFETY CONCERN. KI1 acupuncture is extremely painful.
In conscious patients, strong stimulation can cause vasovagal syncope
(fainting from pain, paradoxically opposite to the consciousness-
restoring intent). Must be used with patient awareness and consent.
For routine clinical use, acupressure or moxibustion is preferred.

Infection: Foot sole requires careful skin preparation before needling.
Higher microbial load than upper body points. Standard clean needle
technique with thorough antiseptic preparation.

## KI2 — Nhiên Cốc (然谷, Rángu)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Blazing Valley" — Ying-Spring (Fire) point of the Kidney channel.
The Fire element on the Water channel creates a natural Five Element
opposition: Water controls Fire (水克火), making KI2 the Ke Subject
point. Classical function: clears deficiency heat (虚热), the
pathological "fire from water" pattern central to Kidney Yin
depletion. Spring points indicated for "changes in complexion"
(变于色) and heat in the body (Nan Jing Ch.68).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO 2008: On the medial aspect of the foot, below the tuberosity of
the navicular bone, at the border of the red and white skin.

Anatomical landmarks: inferior to the navicular tuberosity, at the
junction of plantar (glabrous) and dorsal (hairy) skin — the 赤白肉际
(red-white skin boundary). Palpable as a depression below the bony
prominence of the navicular on the medial arch.

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular insertion. The relatively thin
tissue over the navicular tuberosity limits depth.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

**Nerve supply:** Medial plantar nerve (L4–L5), terminal branch of
tibial nerve. Same nerve territory as KI1 (medial plantar) but at a
more dorsal position — closer to the red-white skin boundary where
receptor density transitions between thick glabrous sole and thinner
medial skin.

Spinal segments: L4–L5

**Fascial plane:** Abductor hallucis muscle belly (superficial layer of
medial foot). Deep to abductor hallucis: plantar fascia medial band
continuous with the spring ligament (calcaneonavicular ligament) —
keystone of the medial longitudinal arch.

**Vascular:** Medial plantar artery (branch of posterior tibial artery).
Smaller caliber than the posterior tibial trunk at KI3.

**Muscle layers:**
1. Skin + subcutaneous tissue (thin at red-white junction)
2. Abductor hallucis (muscle belly)
3. Spring ligament / medial plantar fascia (deep)

**Skin type:** Red-white skin junction (赤白肉际) — transitional zone.
Glabrous side: dense mechanoreceptors (Meissner, Pacinian). Hairy side:
potential CT afferent presence. This transitional position means KI2
may access BOTH receptor populations (unlike KI1, which is purely
glabrous).
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.5.7, A01 S4.3

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Ying-Spring (Fire)** — 2nd of Five Shu Transport points on
the Kidney channel. Ke Subject: Water controls Fire (水克火 — the organ
element controls the Shu-point element). Spring points in Nan Jing
Ch.68: "for heat in the body" (身热). Fire on Water = internal
contradiction central to Kidney pathology (deficiency heat, Yin-Fire).

Yuan-Source: no (KI3 Taixi)
Luo-Connecting: no (KI4 Dazhong)
Xi-Cleft: no (KI5 Shuiquan)
Back-Shu: no (BL23 Shenshu)
Front-Mu: no (GB25 Jingmen)
Eight Confluent: no
Hui-Meeting: no

### Cross-tradition mapping

**TCM acupoint:** KI2 Rangu — Ying-Spring (Fire). Clears deficiency
heat, cools Kidney Fire, regulates the lower Jiao. Classical: "five
hearts heat" (五心烦热), night sweats, bone-steaming fever (骨蒸潮热).

**Trigger point:** Abductor hallucis trigger points occur in this
region (Travell & Simons). TrP in abductor hallucis refers pain along
the medial heel and arch — partial overlap with KI2 territory. The
correspondence is anatomically approximate, not precise. 🟡

**Ashi correspondence:** Medial arch tenderness at the red-white skin
junction = clinically meaningful finding. Tenderness here indicates
plantar fasciitis, medial arch strain, or posterior tibialis
tendinopathy — conditions with clear musculoskeletal pathology. 🟢

**Marma point: Kurchashira (कूर्चशिर, "Head of the Bow")** — located at
the junction of tarsal and metatarsal region on the medial foot. Sira
(vessel) Marma. Associated with foot mobility and structural integrity.
Approximate overlap with KI2 region. 🟡

**Head zone:** L4–L5 dermatome. No visceral projection to Kidney
(T10–L1). Minimum 5-segment gap. ✗

**Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Back Line (SBL) — plantar fascia
extends medially through the medial arch. KI2 sits at the medial
extension of the SBL where plantar fascia connects to the spring
ligament and navicular. Also: Deep Front Line (DFL) — tibialis
posterior tendon inserts at the navicular, and KI2 overlies this
insertion area. Dual-line access (SBL medial band + DFL tibialis
posterior insertion). 🟡

**Neurogenic spot:** No specific data for medial foot arch (Kim 2017
did not map this region). ✗

**Cupping point:** Not applicable. Curved medial arch surface. ✗

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM, TrP 🟡 abductor hallucis, Ashi 🟢
medial arch, Marma 🟡 Kurchashira, AT 🟡 SBL+DFL). Higher than KI1
(3/8) due to medial arch clinical relevance (Ashi), trigger point
presence, and dual fascial line access — the transition from sole
(KI1) to medial arch (KI2) brings KI2 into musculoskeletal territory
where cross-tradition correspondences are more frequent.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA)

**Nguồn cổ:**
- Nan Jing Ch.68: Spring points for "heat in the body" (身热) —
  category-level indication.
- Jiayi Jing: irregular menstruation, pruritus vulvae, seminal
  emission, difficult urination, infantile convulsions, throat pain.
- Qian Jin Yao Fang: hemoptysis (咯血), night sweats (盗汗).

**Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):**
Irregular menstruation, uterine prolapse, pruritus vulvae; seminal
emission, impotence; difficult urination, dark urine; throat pain,
hemoptysis; infantile convulsions; diabetes (消渴 Xiao Ke); night
sweats, bone-steaming heat.

**Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:**

*Deficiency heat specialist:* KI2's primary clinical identity is
clearing Kidney Yin deficiency heat. The classical combination
KI2 + KI6 (Zhaohai) + HT6 (Yinxi) = standard protocol for night
sweats and bone-steaming fever. This reflects the Spring/Fire
designation: Fire point used to address pathological Fire, not
generate it.

*Menstrual/reproductive:* Frequent protocol inclusion for irregular
menstruation and reproductive disorders alongside KI3 (Yuan-Source)
and SP6 (Three Yin Crossing).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Deficiency heat / night sweats — 🔴 Unvalidated**

No systematic review or RCT for KI2-specific treatment of night sweats
or deficiency heat. KI2 appears in traditional protocols but has never
been isolated as a study variable. The condition category "deficiency
heat" itself lacks standardized diagnostic criteria in modern medicine.

**2. Menstrual disorders — 🔴 Unvalidated**

KI2 is included in acupuncture protocols for menstrual irregularity
but no study has isolated KI2's contribution. Protocol-level evidence
for acupuncture and menstrual disorders = 🟡 (multiple SRs exist for
acupuncture generally), but KI2-specific = 🔴.

Confidence: 🔴 across all indications. KI2 has strong traditional
identity but zero point-specific modern evidence.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No disease-state sensitization data specific to KI2. The medial arch
area shows increased tenderness in plantar fasciitis (an Ashi finding)
but this represents musculoskeletal pathology, not visceral-acupoint
sensitization in the Kim 2017 model. The red-white skin junction may
have variable receptor density between individuals, affecting De Qi
characteristics.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4, C05 S3

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at KI2:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): Medial plantar nerve (L4–L5).
  Moderate Aδ/C fiber density at red-white skin junction (less than
  KI1 pure sole, more than dorsal foot).
- A03 (Fascia-Connective Tissue): SBL medial extension (plantar fascia
  medial band) + DFL connection (tibialis posterior insertion at
  navicular). Dual fascial line access.
- A07 (Dermatome): L4–L5 territory → Kidney viscerotome T10–L1 =
  5-segment gap (minimum). Same non-segmental gap category as KI1.

**Non-segmental gap:** L4–L5 → T10–L1 = 5 segments for Kidney,
identical to the gap at many Phase 1 points (ST36, LR3). The
traditional "deficiency heat" indication targets a systemic condition,
not a specific organ — suggesting the mechanism (if real) operates
through supraspinal regulation rather than segmental reflex.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S2, C04 S3.5

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular.
- De Qi: distention and soreness radiating along medial arch. Less
  painful than KI1 (medial arch vs sole center).
- Clinical note: may needle obliquely toward the sole to access deeper
  plantar structures.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02):
- Thumb pressure on medial arch at red-white skin junction. Moderate
  pressure tolerable (less sensitive than sole). Self-applicable.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Cứu (B08):
- Moxa applicable. Indirect moxa (moxa stick) at medial arch. Used in
  deficiency heat protocols, though theoretically paradoxical (adding
  Fire to address excess Fire — the traditional explanation is that
  moxa "draws out" pathological heat).
→ Cross-ref: B08

### An toàn

Vascular risk: Medial plantar artery is present but small caliber.
Low risk at standard depth.

Neural risk: Medial plantar nerve branches are superficial in this
area. Risk of nerve irritation exists but is LOW at standard depth.

Pneumothorax: None. Foot location.

Pregnancy: NOT listed as contraindicated.

Pain: Moderate. Less painful than KI1 (sole) but more sensitive than
dorsal foot points due to red-white skin junction.

## KI3 — Thái Khê (太溪, Tàixī)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Great Ravine" — Yuan-Source point AND Shu-Stream (Earth) point of the
Kidney channel. The most prescribed Kidney channel point in clinical
practice. KI3's dual designation (Yuan + Stream) confirms the Yin
channel pattern at 6/6: Yuan-Source always coincides with the Stream
point on Yin channels (previously confirmed at LU9, HT7, PC7, SP3,
LR3). As Stream/Earth, KI3 is the Ke Controller: Earth controls Water
(土克水). Posterior tibial artery palpable here — one of three
Yuan-Source points at major pulse sites (with LU9 radial, ST42
dorsalis pedis).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO 2008: In the depression between the tip of the medial malleolus
and the Achilles tendon, level with the tip of the medial malleolus.

Anatomical landmarks: the groove posterior to the medial malleolus and
anterior to the calcaneal (Achilles) tendon. Posterior tibial artery
pulse PALPABLE here — standard clinical pulse-check site. The tarsal
tunnel (formed by flexor retinaculum) lies just deep to this point,
containing the tibial nerve trunk, posterior tibial artery and veins,
and tendons of tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus (FDL), and
flexor hallucis longus (FHL).

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular. The posterior tibial artery runs
through this zone — palpate pulse BEFORE insertion and needle ANTERIOR
or POSTERIOR to the artery, never directly into it.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

**Nerve supply:** Tibial nerve trunk (L4–S3). At KI3's level, the
tibial nerve has NOT YET divided into medial and lateral plantar
branches (division occurs inferior to medial malleolus, at or below
KI3 level). This means needling at KI3 accesses the UNDIVIDED tibial
nerve trunk — all fiber types (motor, sensory, autonomic) in one
bundle. This pre-bifurcation access is analogous to LI11 (radial
nerve pre-split, AP-02 Obs. 15) and ST41 (deep peroneal pre-split).

Spinal segments: L4–S3

**Fascial plane:** Flexor retinaculum (laciniate ligament) = roof of
the tarsal tunnel. Dense connective tissue band bridging medial
malleolus to calcaneus. Deep fascia continuous with crural fascia of
the leg. The tarsal tunnel is a fascial COMPARTMENT — needling into it
accesses a confined neurovascular bundle.

**Vascular:** Posterior tibial artery — MAJOR ARTERY. Palpable pulse at
KI3 = standard clinical assessment for peripheral arterial disease.
Accompanied by posterior tibial veins (venae comitantes). Artery runs
between tibial nerve (lateral) and tendons (medial).

**Muscle layers (superficial → deep):**
1. Skin + subcutaneous tissue
2. Flexor retinaculum (no muscle fibers — fascial bridge)
3. Tarsal tunnel contents: tibialis posterior tendon (anterior),
   FDL tendon, posterior tibial artery + veins, tibial nerve, FHL
   tendon (posterior)

**Skin type:** Hairy skin (medial ankle). CT afferents PRESENT —
unlike KI1 (glabrous sole) and the transitional zone at KI2, KI3 is
fully within hairy skin territory. This means the affective touch
pathway (CT → posterior insula → oxytocin via paraventricular nucleus)
is accessible at KI3 but not at KI1.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.5.7, A01 S4.3

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Shu-Stream (Earth)** — 3rd of Five Shu Transport points.
Ke Controller: Earth controls Water (土克水). Stream points indicated
for "heaviness of the body and joint pain" (体重节痛, Nan Jing Ch.68).

**Yuan-Source: YES — Kidney (Taixi)**
The Yuan-Source point of the Kidney channel. Classical: Yuan points
access the original Qi (原气) of the organ. Diagnostic: tenderness at
KI3 = Kidney deficiency (Nanjing Ch.66).

**Yuan = Stream overlap confirmed:** KI3 = 6th/6th Yin channel where
Yuan-Source coincides with the Stream point. Complete Yin pattern:

| Channel | Yuan-Source | Stream | Same point? |
|---------|-----------|--------|-------------|
| LU | LU9 | LU9 | ✓ |
| HT | HT7 | HT7 | ✓ |
| PC | PC7 | PC7 | ✓ |
| SP | SP3 | SP3 | ✓ |
| LR | LR3 | LR3 | ✓ |
| KI | KI3 | KI3 | ✓ |

6/6 confirmed. Yang pattern (Yuan ≠ Five Shu) also 6/6. Total: 12/12
channels consistent.

Luo-Connecting: no (KI4)
Xi-Cleft: no (KI5)
Back-Shu: no (BL23)
Front-Mu: no (GB25)
Eight Confluent: no
Hui-Meeting: no

**Host-Guest pairing (KI↔BL):**
- Regular: BL64(Yuan) + KI4(Luo) — treats BL via KI Luo connection
- Reverse: KI3(Yuan) + BL58(Luo) — treats KI via BL Luo connection
BL58 and BL64 not yet documented (AP-07 Phase 2 pending).

### Cross-tradition mapping

**TCM acupoint:** KI3 Taixi — Yuan-Source of Kidney. THE primary
Kidney tonification point. Functions: tonify Kidney Yin/Yang/Essence,
nourish Kidney Water, strengthen lumbar and knees, benefit ears and
eyes, anchor Qi (纳气). Used in virtually every Kidney-pattern
prescription.

**Trigger point:** Posterior tibialis muscle trigger points refer pain
to the Achilles tendon and medial ankle region — overlapping KI3
territory. FDL trigger points also project to this area. Dorsher
mapping identifies correspondence. 🟡

**Ashi correspondence:** Tenderness in the groove between medial
malleolus and Achilles tendon = clinically meaningful. Indicates
posterior tibialis tendinopathy, tarsal tunnel syndrome, medial ankle
sprain, or Achilles tendinopathy. Common clinical finding. 🟢

**Marma point: Gulpha (गुल्फ, "Ankle")** — one of 107 Marma points.
Located at the ankle joint. Asthi (bone) Marma. Two Gulpha points
(medial and lateral ankle). Associated with foot/ankle mobility,
lower limb circulation, and Apana Vayu (downward energy flow).
Injury classification: Rujakara (pain-causing). The medial Gulpha
aligns closely with KI3's position behind the medial malleolus. 🟡

**Head zone:** L4–S3 dermatome. No visceral projection to Kidney
(T10–L1). Minimum 4-segment gap. This gap is SMALLER than KI1's
(L4–S2 → T10–L1 = 6 segments) because KI3's nerve supply extends
to S3, bringing it closer to the spinal segments that innervate
pelvic structures (S2–S4 = parasympathetic outflow to bladder,
reproductive organs). ✗ for Kidney organ, but ⚠ for pelvic/
urogenital targets.

**Anatomy Trains: Deep Front Line (DFL)** — tibialis posterior muscle
is a core component of the DFL (Myers 2001). The DFL connects foot
sole → tibialis posterior → popliteal fascia → adductors → pelvic
floor → psoas → diaphragm → mediastinum. KI3 overlies the tibialis
posterior tendon at the ankle, providing access to the DFL at a key
transition point (leg → foot). 🟡

**Neurogenic spot:** L4–S3 territory. Kim 2017 identified neurogenic
inflammatory spots in lower limb dermatomes but did not specifically
map the retro-malleolar groove. No specific data. ✗

**Cupping point:** Not practical. Narrow groove between malleolus and
Achilles tendon — insufficient flat surface for cup suction. ✗

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM, TrP 🟡 posterior tibialis/FDL,
Ashi 🟢 ankle, Marma 🟡 Gulpha, AT 🟡 DFL). Highest of the KI ankle
cluster. The retro-malleolar groove is a genuine multi-tradition
convergence zone — independently identified by TCM, Ayurveda, Western
trigger point mapping, and fascial anatomy.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA)

**Nguồn cổ:**
- Nan Jing Ch.66: Yuan-Source points access original Qi (原气).
  Diagnostic: "When the five Zang are diseased, select the twelve
  Yuan points" (五脏有疾，当取之十二原).
- Ling Shu Ch.1: Stream points for "heaviness of the body and joint
  pain" (体重节痛).
- Jiayi Jing: tinnitus, deafness, toothache, sore throat, hemoptysis,
  seminal emission, impotence, irregular menstruation, lumbar pain,
  insomnia, consumptive disease (虚劳).

**Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):**
Tinnitus, deafness; sore throat, toothache; insomnia; impotence,
seminal emission, frequent urination; irregular menstruation,
infertility; lumbar pain, heel pain; cough, asthma (Kidney failing
to grasp Lung Qi); dizziness; diabetes (消渴).

**Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:**

*Broadest-scope Kidney point:* KI3 is included in virtually every
Kidney-pattern protocol in clinical acupuncture. The standard Kidney
tonification combination is BL23 (Back-Shu) + KI3 (Yuan-Source) ±
GV4 (Mingmen) ± KI7 (Tonification). This four-point protocol appears
in AP-07 (BL23 entry) and is the most referenced Kidney prescription
across all completed Atlas entries.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL23 L1484

*Diagnostic pulse at Yuan-Source:* The posterior tibial pulse at KI3
is a clinical diagnostic tool in BOTH traditions — TCM pulse diagnosis
(太溪脉) and Western peripheral vascular assessment. Absence of the
pulse = "Kidney Qi exhaustion" (TCM) or peripheral arterial disease
(Western). This is the 3rd Yuan-Source point at a major pulse site
(after LU9 radial, ST42 dorsalis pedis), completing a three-pulse
diagnostic system: wrist (LU9) + foot dorsum (ST42) + ankle (KI3).
→ Cross-ref: AP-03 ST42

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Chronic kidney disease (CKD) — 🔴 Unvalidated (KI3-specific)**

Tong et al. 2014, Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine —
acupuncture at KI3 + ST36 + SP6 + other points in CKD patients showed
improved estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Multi-point
protocol — KI3's individual contribution undetermined. Additional
small trials include KI3 in CKD protocols but none isolate it.

Systematic reviews of acupuncture for CKD (Lu et al. 2021, etc.)
include KI3 in analyzed protocols but do not assess point-specific
effects.

Confidence: 🔴 — included in CKD protocols but never isolated.
Traditional prominence vastly exceeds evidence base.

**2. Low back pain (LBP) — 🟡 Plausible (protocol component)**

BL23 + KI3 combination is included in multiple LBP acupuncture
protocols. Systematic reviews for acupuncture and LBP show moderate
overall evidence (Chou et al. 2017, Annals of Internal Medicine —
acupuncture recommended for chronic LBP). KI3's specific contribution
is not isolated, but the BL23+KI3 combination has stronger protocol-
level support than most individual points.

Confidence: 🟡 — protocol-level support for the BL23+KI3 combination
in LBP. Not point-specific but consistently included.

**3. Tinnitus — 🔴 Unvalidated**

KI3 is the primary acupuncture point for tinnitus in TCM (Kidney
opens into ears). Small RCTs exist but with high risk of bias, no sham
control, and always multi-point protocols.

Confidence: 🔴 — traditional indication with insufficient modern
evidence.

**Evidence-importance paradox:** KI3 is among the 10 most prescribed
acupoints in clinical practice, yet has ZERO point-specific controlled
evidence. This parallels LU9 (triple designation, zero RCTs — AP-01
Obs. 12) and represents a systematic gap in acupuncture research:
the most theoretically important points are studied only as protocol
components, never in isolation.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

The posterior tibial pulse amplitude at KI3 varies with cardiovascular
conditions — peripheral arterial disease, heart failure, and autonomic
dysfunction all alter pulse characteristics. In TCM, a "thin and weak"
pulse at KI3 = Kidney Qi deficiency; "absent" = critical. This makes
KI3 a natural site for dynamic assessment, though the dynamic
phenomenon is VASCULAR (pulse change) rather than the neural
sensitization model described at Kim 2017 neurogenic spots.

The tarsal tunnel area may show increased sensitivity in tarsal
tunnel syndrome (tibial nerve compression) — a neural dynamic change
relevant to the Ashi model but representing peripheral nerve pathology,
not visceral-acupoint sensitization.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at KI3:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): Tibial nerve trunk (L4–S3),
  PRE-BIFURCATION. Access to undivided nerve = all fiber types
  (motor + sensory + autonomic). Dense Aβ fibers from hairy skin
  (CT afferents PRESENT). Aδ/C fibers for nociception.
- A03 (Fascia-Connective Tissue): Deep Front Line via tibialis
  posterior tendon. Tarsal tunnel = fascial compartment providing
  contained neurovascular access.
- A07 (Dermatome): L4–S3 territory → Kidney T10–L1 = 4-segment gap
  (minimum). S2–S4 parasympathetic outflow = pelvic floor, bladder,
  reproductive organs — closer to traditional reproductive/urinary
  indications than the Kidney organ itself.

**Mechanism note:** KI3's strongest mechanistic rationale is NOT for
Kidney-organ effects but for pelvic/urogenital effects via S2–S4
parasympathetic proximity. The tibial nerve (L4–S3) shares spinal
segments with the pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2–S4), providing a
plausible segmental pathway for urinary and reproductive effects.
This is the PTNS (Posterior Tibial Nerve Stimulation) principle — an
established neuromodulation technique for overactive bladder (FDA-
cleared) that stimulates the posterior tibial nerve at approximately
the KI3 location.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S2, C04 S3.5

**PTNS connection:** Percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation (PTNS,
brand name Urgent PC) inserts a needle at the posterior tibial nerve
near the medial malleolus — anatomically equivalent to acupuncture at
KI3. PTNS has FDA clearance for overactive bladder (Peters et al.
2013, SUmiT trial). This represents the STRONGEST modern validation
of a specific acupoint for a specific indication in the Atlas,
though PTNS literature does not reference acupuncture and acupuncture
literature rarely references PTNS.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular. PALPATE posterior tibial
  artery pulse FIRST — insert anterior or posterior to the pulse,
  never directly into it.
- De Qi: deep distention, numbness, or electric sensation radiating
  to the sole (medial plantar nerve territory) or up the medial leg.
- EA: applicable. Low-frequency (2–4 Hz) at KI3 = standard protocol
  for Kidney tonification. PTNS uses similar parameters (20 Hz,
  200 μs, 30 min sessions) with demonstrated efficacy.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02):
- Thumb pressure in the retro-malleolar groove. Moderate pressure.
  Patient can self-apply. Commonly taught as a home-care technique
  for Kidney tonification.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Cứu (B08):
- Moxa applicable and commonly used. Indirect moxa at KI3 = standard
  Kidney Yang tonification technique. Often combined with BL23 + GV4
  moxa (the "three-fire" protocol for Kidney Yang deficiency).
→ Cross-ref: B08

### An toàn

Vascular risk: **MODERATE.** Posterior tibial artery runs through KI3's
needle zone. Palpate pulse before insertion. Hematoma possible if
artery is punctured. Avoid in patients on anticoagulants without
careful technique. Comparable to ST42 (dorsalis pedis) risk level.

Neural risk: Tibial nerve trunk in the tarsal tunnel. Excessive depth
or medial angulation could contact the nerve trunk. Risk is LOW at
standard depth with proper technique but HIGHER than most acupoints
due to the confined neurovascular bundle.

Pneumothorax: None. Ankle location.

Pregnancy: NOT listed as contraindicated.

## KI4 — Đại Chung (大钟, Dàzhōng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Great Bell" (also "Great Goblet") — Luo-Connecting point of the
Kidney channel, linking KI to its paired channel BL (Bladder). The
Luo vessel from KI4 encircles the heel and connects to the BL
channel. 11th Luo-Connecting point documented in the Atlas (10
previously: LU7, LI6, HT5, SI7, ST40, SP4, PC6, TE5, GB37, LR5).
Only BL58 remains to complete all 12 regular Luo points.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO 2008: On the medial side of the foot, posterior and inferior to
the medial malleolus, in the depression medial to the attachment of
the Achilles tendon to the calcaneus.

Anatomical landmarks: medial aspect of the calcaneus, at the junction
where the Achilles tendon inserts into the calcaneal tuberosity. The
point is in a depression palpable between the Achilles tendon and the
medial surface of the calcaneus, inferior and posterior to KI3.

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular. The calcaneus provides a bony
backstop, limiting depth.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

**Nerve supply:** Medial calcaneal nerve (branch of tibial nerve,
L4–S2). Sensory to the medial heel and posterior ankle. At KI4, the
tibial nerve has begun dividing — the medial calcaneal branches are
among the first to separate from the trunk, proximal to the
plantar bifurcation.

Spinal segments: L4–S2

**Fascial plane:** Achilles tendon insertion zone — the calcaneal
(Achilles) tendon is the THICKEST and STRONGEST tendon in the body.
Part of the Superficial Back Line (SBL): gastrocnemius/soleus →
Achilles tendon → calcaneus → plantar fascia. KI4 sits at a key SBL
node — the Achilles-calcaneus junction where muscle force converts to
fascial tension.

**Vascular:** Posterior tibial artery branches (medial calcaneal
arteries). Smaller than the trunk at KI3.

**Muscle layers:**

## KI7 — Phục Lưu (复溜, Fùliū)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Recover Flow" (also "Return Current") — Jing-River (Metal) point
and Tonification point of the Kidney channel. Metal generates Water
(金生水): the Mother element nourishes the Child (Kidney = Water
organ), making KI7 the most important Five Element supplementation
point for Kidney deficiency. Part of the standard Kidney Yang
Deficiency protocol: BL23 + GV4 + KI3 + KI7 (AP-07 BL23 L1484).
Distinctive clinical identity: THE point for sweating disorders
(止汗/发汗).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO 2008: On the medial aspect of the leg, 2 cun directly above
KI3 (Taixi), anterior to the Achilles tendon (calcaneal tendon).

Anatomical landmarks: 2 cun proximal to the midpoint between the
medial malleolus and the Achilles tendon, on the anterior border of
the Achilles tendon. In the groove between the Achilles tendon
(posterior) and the flexor digitorum longus (FDL) tendon / tibialis
posterior (anterior).

Needle depth: 15–25 mm perpendicular. Deeper tissue access than
the ankle points (KI2–KI6) due to greater soft tissue depth at
the lower leg.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

**Nerve supply:** Tibial nerve (L4–S3). At 2 cun above the medial
malleolus, the tibial nerve trunk is UNDIVIDED — same pre-
bifurcation access as KI3 but more proximal, with the nerve trunk
deeper in the posterior compartment. Additionally: saphenous nerve
(L3–L4, branch of femoral nerve) runs superficially along the
medial leg and may be accessed by superficial insertion.

Spinal segments: L4–S3 (tibial, primary) + L3–L4 (saphenous,
superficial)

**Fascial plane:** Deep crural fascia. KI7 sits between the
Achilles tendon and the deep posterior compartment muscles (FDL,
tibialis posterior). The deep posterior compartment fascia is
continuous with the tarsal tunnel fascia below. Posterior
intermuscular septum of the leg is nearby.

**Vascular:** Posterior tibial artery — runs in the deep posterior
compartment. At 2 cun above the malleolus, the artery is deeper
than at KI3 (where it is at the surface), lying between FDL and
soleus. Accompanied by posterior tibial veins.

**Muscle layers (superficial → deep):**
1. Skin + subcutaneous tissue
2. Deep crural fascia
3. Soleus muscle (medial portion)
4. FDL and tibialis posterior (deep posterior compartment)
5. Posterior tibial nerve and artery (deepest relevant structures)

**Skin type:** Hairy skin. CT afferents present.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.5.7, A01 S4.3

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Jing-River (Metal)** — 4th of Five Shu Transport points.
Nan Jing Ch.68: River points treat "cough and dyspnea, cold and
heat" (喘咳寒热). Metal on the Water channel creates the Mother-
Child relationship: Metal generates Water (金生水).

**Tonification point (补穴):** The Five Element point whose element
is the MOTHER of the organ element. For Kidney (Water): Metal is
the mother → KI7 = River/Metal = Tonification point. Used to
supplement Kidney Qi/Yang/Essence when the organ is deficient.

| LR | Wood | Water | LR8 (Ququan) | He-Sea/Water |

## KI9 — Trúc Tân (筑宾, Zhùbīn)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Guest House" — 筑 (zhù, build/construct) + 宾 (bīn, guest). The
point where the Yin Wei Mai vessel is "hosted" on the Kidney channel.
Alternative interpretation: KI9 treats conditions where the body
harbors unwanted "guests" (toxins, pathogenic Qi), hence the
classical detoxification (解毒) function — unique among documented
acupoints. KI9 is the Xi-Cleft (郄穴) of Yin Wei Mai (阴维脉) —
the SECOND extraordinary vessel Xi-Cleft on the KI channel (after
KI8 for Yin Qiao Mai). No other meridian hosts two extraordinary
vessel Xi-Clefts.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO 2008: On the medial aspect of the leg, on the line drawn from
KI3 (Taixi) to KI10 (Yingu), 5 cun above KI3, at the lower end of
the belly of the gastrocnemius muscle.

Anatomical landmarks: 5 cun above the tip of the medial malleolus,
in the medial gastrocnemius belly — the most substantial muscular
tissue in the entire KI ankle-to-knee trajectory. Palpable as the
medial calf muscle prominence.

Needle depth: 15–25 mm perpendicular. The gastrocnemius belly
provides substantial soft tissue depth. Deeper insertion accesses
the soleus muscle beneath.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2

trunk is large — deeper than at KI7/KI8 but with more muscle to

Saphenous nerve (L3–L4) runs superficially along the medial leg.

Spinal segments: L4–S3 (tibial, primary) + L3–L4 (saphenous,
superficial)

**Fascial plane:** Gastrocnemius-soleus intermuscular fascia — a
significant fascial plane where loose connective tissue, nerve
branches, and small vessels concentrate. Analogous to the
intermuscular septum points at GB35 and other fascial junctions.

**Vascular:** Posterior tibial artery — deep in the posterior
compartment, well-covered by the gastrocnemius-soleus complex at
this level. Small muscular branches and sural veins nearby.

**Muscle layers (superficial → deep):**
1. Skin + subcutaneous tissue
2. Deep crural fascia
3. Medial gastrocnemius (bulk of tissue)
4. Gastrocnemius-soleus intermuscular fascia
5. Soleus muscle

the inter-tendon groove and the joint capsule.

genicular artery branches nearby. Great saphenous vein superficially.

1. Skin + subcutaneous tissue
2. Popliteal fascia
3. Semitendinosus tendon (superficial-medial)

### Cross-tradition mapping

**TCM acupoint:** KI10 Yingu — He-Sea/Water, Horary point. "Yin
Valley." Functions: tonifies Kidney Yin, clears Damp-Heat from
lower jiao, benefits the knee. Classical: urinary disorders (dysuria,
retention, incontinence), genital disorders (impotence, genital
pruritus, hernia), knee pain, abdominal distension, diarrhea.

*He-Sea clinical pattern:* He-Sea points on Yin channels treat
"fullness" of the Zang organ — for Kidney: edema, urinary retention,
lower abdominal fullness. KI10's He-Sea function overlaps with KI3's

indicated for fullness/excess patterns while KI3 addresses deficiency.

**Trigger point:** Semitendinosus and semimembranosus trigger points.
Medial hamstring TrPs at tendinous insertion refer pain to posterior-
medial knee and lower medial thigh (Travell & Simons). Good local

**Ashi correspondence:** Popliteal fossa tenderness at the medial
aspect. Clinically significant in Baker's cyst, posterior knee pain,
hamstring tendinopathy at medial insertion, and medial meniscus

→ Cross-ref: AP-12 LR8 L747

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Urogenital disorders — 🔴 Unvalidated**

No RCT or systematic review for KI10-specific treatment. KI3 (with
PTNS evidence) dominates Kidney-point urological research. KI10
appears in multi-point protocols but has never been isolated.

**2. Knee osteoarthritis — 🔴 Unvalidated**

KI10 may appear in medial knee protocols alongside LR8, SP9, ST35,
and EX-LE5 (Xiyan). No study has isolated KI10. Acupuncture for
knee OA overall has moderate evidence (Vickers et al. 2018, JAMA),
but individual point contributions are unresolved.

**3. Diarrhea (He-Sea function) — 🔴 Unvalidated**

The He-Sea "rebellious Qi and diarrhea" indication (Nan Jing Ch.68)
is a category-level prescription. No study tests KI10 for diarrhea.

Confidence: 🔴 across all indications. Horary points are the least
studied and least prescribed of the Five Shu designations.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

The medial popliteal fossa may show tenderness in Baker's cyst,

sensitization data specific to KI10. The posterior tibial pulse at
the popliteal fossa (more proximal than KI3) may vary with

→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at KI10:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): Tibial nerve trunk (L4–S3) —
  MOST PROXIMAL tibial nerve access on KI channel. Maximum diameter
  before distal branching. Mixed motor-sensory. Different nerve from
  LR8's saphenous (L3–L4): 1 cun separation, completely different
  pathways.
- A03 (Fascia-Connective Tissue): SBL at the hamstring-to-calf
  transition (hamstring tendons → gastrocnemius via popliteal fascia).
  Inter-tendon fascial junction.
- A07 (Dermatome): L4–S3 → Kidney T10–L1 = 4-segment gap. Same
  PTNS-based rationale as all KI leg points for urogenital effects
  via S2–S4 parasympathetic proximity.

**Mechanism — urogenital effects:** Same PTNS-derived rationale as
KI3, KI7, KI8. At the popliteal fossa, the tibial nerve trunk is
undivided and at maximum caliber — potentially the strongest tibial
nerve stimulation point on KI channel. KI10 should be at least as
effective as KI3 for urogenital neuromodulation, yet KI3 is vastly
more prescribed. The clinical preference for KI3 may reflect Yuan-
Source prestige, accessibility (ankle easier than posterior knee), or
the PTNS research tradition anchored at the ankle.

**Mechanism — knee pain:** Direct local mechanism. Periarticular
structures (hamstring tendons, joint capsule, periosteum) + tibial
nerve branches → segmental spinal gate at L4–S1 → modulation of
knee nociception. Same mechanism as LR8 but via different nerve
(tibial vs saphenous) and different periarticular structures
(posterior vs anterior medial).
→ Cross-ref: A09 S2, C04 S3.4

## KI11 — Hoành Cốt (横骨, Hénggu)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**

"Pubic Bone" — 横 (héng, transverse) + 骨 (gǔ, bone). Named after
the pubic bone (横骨 = "transverse bone," a classical anatomical
term). KI11 is the LOWEST KI abdominal point and the first of 11
consecutive Chong Mai (冲脉) meeting points on the KI channel
(KI11–KI21). Meeting of Foot Shaoyin (KI) and Chong Mai.

**Chong Mai (冲脉, Penetrating Vessel)** — "Sea of Blood" (血海)
and "Sea of the 12 Channels" (十二经之海). Eight Confluent: SP4
(Opening) + PC6 (Coupled). Originates in the uterus/lower abdomen
→ ascends at 0.5 cun lateral to midline alongside KI → disperses
in the chest. Functions: regulates Qi and Blood, governs
reproductive blood. Nan Jing Ch.29: "Chong Mai disease → Qi rushes
upward, tension in the abdomen" (冲脉为病，气逆而里急). KI11–KI21
trace the Chong Mai's abdominal course — the vessel's trajectory
and KI's abdominal trajectory are identical at 0.5 cun lateral to
midline.
→ Cross-ref: AP-04 SP4, AP-09 PC6

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO 2008: On the lower abdomen, at the superior border of the
pubic symphysis, 0.5 cun lateral to CV2 (Qugu).

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the superior border of the pubic
bone. KI11 is 0.5 cun (~1 cm) lateral to the midline, at the
medial border of the rectus abdominis insertion on the pubic crest.

Spatial relationships:
- CV2 (Qugu): 0.5 cun medial (midline)
- KI12 (Dahe): 1 cun superior
- ST30 (Qichong): 2 cun lateral — also a Chong Mai meeting point

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular. Empty bladder before needling.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply: iliohypogastric nerve (L1) anterior cutaneous branch;
ilioinguinal nerve (L1) nearby.
Spinal segments: L1 — most caudal segmental level in the KI
abdominal series.

Fascial plane: anterior rectus sheath → rectus abdominis (medial
fibers at pubic insertion) → transversalis fascia → peritoneum.
Below the arcuate line: no posterior rectus sheath — only
transversalis fascia separates muscle from peritoneal cavity.

Vascular: inferior epigastric artery (deep to rectus). Superficial
epigastric and external pudendal branches nearby.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No Five Shu, Yuan-Source, Luo, Xi-Cleft, Eight Confluent, or
Hui-Meeting designation.

Meeting point (交会穴): KI + Chong Mai. First of 11 consecutive
Chong Mai meetings (KI11–KI21).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**TCM acupoint:** KI11 Henggu. Benefits Kidney, regulates lower
jiao, benefits urination. Urinary retention, enuresis, genital
pain, hernia, impotence.

— referred pain to lower abdomen and suprapubic region. 🟡

**Ashi:** suprapubic tenderness in cystitis, urinary retention,

**Marma:** Basti (बस्ति) — lower abdominal Marma (Sushruta). KI11

**Head zone:** L1 dermatome — inguinal/genital region. Marginal
overlap with Kidney viscerotome upper limit (T10–L1). ✗

**Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Front Line — rectus abdominis at
pubic insertion. 🟢

**Neurogenic spot / Cupping:** no data / not standard. ✗ ✗

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, TrP 🟡 rectus inferior, Ashi 🟡
suprapubic, AT 🟢 SFL).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

> **Observation 11 — CHONG MAI CORRIDOR ARCHITECTURE + KI-CV PAIRING
> SYSTEM:**
> KI11 marks the start of 11 consecutive Chong Mai (冲脉) meeting
> points (KI11–KI21). The Chong Mai's abdominal trajectory and KI's

> between the integer-cun grid. Three of these CV partners are
> Front-Mu points of specific organs (CV3=BL, CV4=SI, CV5=Triple Burner),
> suggesting the 0.5-cun paramedian strip accesses similar visceral
> domains through a different channel system (KI+Chong Mai vs CV).
> This creates a four-line abdominal tiling: CV at 0, KI at 0.5,
> ST at 2, SP at 4 cun — each line reaching the same visceral
> targets via different segmental angles and meridian affiliations.
> → Cross-ref: ST plan Obs. 17

> **Observation 12 — CATEGORY DENSITY CLIFF (KI10 → KI11) — SHARPEST
> TRANSITION IN THE ATLAS:**
> KI1–KI10: 11 major designations in 10 consecutive points (Five Shu
> ×5, Yuan-Source, Luo-Connecting, Xi-Cleft, Eight Confluent,
> extraordinary vessel Xi-Cleft ×2). KI11–KI16: ONE designation each
> (Chong Mai meeting). This is the sharpest category-density drop in

> correlates with structural diversity, not necessarily with clinical
> importance — the Chong Mai corridor points serve major reproductive
> and GI functions despite their minimal category designations.
