## TE5 — Ngoại Quan (外关, Wài Guān)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**
"Outer Gate" (外关) — 外 (Wài, outer/external) identifies the
dorsal (Yang) forearm surface, and 关 (Guān, gate/pass) denotes a
critical junction point. The name directly mirrors PC6 "Inner Gate"
(内关, Nèiguān) on the palmar (Yin) forearm surface. Together they
form the most architecturally complete Yin-Yang point pairing in
the entire meridian system — same forearm level, opposite sides,
both Luo-Connecting points of their respective channels, both
Eight Confluent points accessing Wei Mai vessels (TE5 = Yang Wei
Mai, PC6 = Yin Wei Mai).

TE5 holds **DUAL DESIGNATION** — the densest category membership
on the TE channel:

1. **Luo-Connecting (络穴):** Branches from the TE channel to
   connect with the Pericardium (PC) channel. Creates the Interior-
   Exterior connection between Hand Shaoyang (TE) and Hand Jueyin
   (PC). Host-Guest pairing: PC7 (Yuan of PC) + TE5 (Luo of TE)
   for Pericardium-origin conditions.
2. **Eight Confluent Point (Yang Wei Mai 阳维脉):** One of 8
   points on regular meridians that access Extraordinary Vessels.
   TE5 opens Yang Wei Mai (Yang Linking Vessel). Paired with GB41
   (Zulinqi) on Foot Shaoyang. Both are Shaoyang family points —
   same-family Yang pairing (matching SI3+BL62 Taiyang pattern).

TE5 is the **MOST-STUDIED TE POINT** — 8th most frequent point
across 911 migraine clinical trials (He et al. 2025), included
in Cochrane migraine reviews, and the TE point with the richest
neuroimaging dataset (3 fMRI/PET-CT studies with point-specific
controls). Clinically, TE5 is the default distal point for
lateral headache, temporal pain, ear disorders, and upper limb
conditions — the "workhorse" of the TE channel, analogous to
LI4 on LI or PC6 on PC.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the dorsal aspect of the
forearm, 2 cun proximal to the dorsal wrist crease (TE4
Yangchi), in the depression between the radius and the ulna.

Anatomical landmarks: with forearm pronated, measure 2 cun (~4 cm)
proximal to the midpoint of the dorsal wrist crease, between the
tendons of the extensor digitorum communis and extensor digiti
minimi. The point lies in the interosseous space between radius
and ulna, directly overlying the interosseous membrane at depth.

Spatial relationships:
- TE4 (Yangchi, Yuan-Source) is 2 cun distal, at the dorsal
  wrist crease
- TE6 (Zhigou, Jing-River/Fire) is 1 cun proximal — 3 cun from
  the wrist crease
- **PC6 (Neiguan)** lies DIRECTLY OPPOSITE on the palmar forearm
  surface at the same 2-cun-from-wrist level, separated by the
  interosseous membrane (~2–3 cm tissue depth). This is the
  anatomical basis for the through-needling technique (透刺, tòu
  cì) described below.

Needle depth: 15–25 mm (0.5–1 cun) perpendicular. The needle
traverses: skin → subcutaneous tissue → extensor digitorum →
interosseous space. For through-needling to PC6: 25–35 mm,
penetrating the interosseous membrane to reach the palmar
compartment.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Posterior interosseous nerve** (deep branch of radial nerve,
  C7-C8) — the primary motor and sensory nerve of the dorsal
  forearm deep compartment. Runs between the two heads of
  supinator, then descends between radius and ulna along the
  interosseous membrane. At TE5 level, it lies deep in the
  interosseous space, providing motor innervation to extensor
  muscles and proprioceptive afferents from the forearm extensors.
  This is the deep needle target.
- **Posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve** (radial nerve, C5-C8)
  — sensory branch providing cutaneous innervation to the dorsal
  forearm surface. The superficial needle target.
- **Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve** (medial cord, C8-T1) —
  may contribute to the ulnar-side skin of the dorsal forearm at
  this level in some individuals.
- Spinal segments: C5-C8 (radial nerve primary, broader than PC6's
  C8-T1 median nerve — TE5 accesses MORE spinal segments from the
  dorsal side)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**TE5 vs PC6 neural architecture — the Inner/Outer Gate divergence:**
PC6 = median nerve (C8-T1) → NTS → GABA inhibition → vagal
pathway. TE5 = posterior interosseous nerve (C5-C8) → sensorimotor
cortex + insular cortex (BA 13). Same forearm level, DIFFERENT
nerves, DIFFERENT central targets. PC6 accesses the vagal-autonomic
system (nausea, cardiac). TE5 accesses sensorimotor integration and
pain modulation (headache, upper limb motor). The Yin-Yang naming
(Inner Gate / Outer Gate) maps precisely onto the Yin-Yang neural
anatomy: palmar/autonomic vs dorsal/somatomotor.
→ Cross-ref: AP-09 PC6

Fascial plane: the interosseous membrane of the forearm is a dense
fibrous sheet connecting the medial radius to the lateral ulna.
TE5 sits directly above this membrane, which is a key fascial
boundary dividing the forearm into anterior (flexor/Yin) and
posterior (extensor/Yang) compartments. Needling at TE5 accesses
this fascial plane — the same membrane that the through-needling
technique penetrates to reach PC6. The interosseous membrane is
continuous with the wrist joint capsule distally and the elbow
joint capsule proximally, creating a longitudinal fascial pathway
along the TE channel trajectory.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: posterior interosseous artery and vein accompany the
posterior interosseous nerve in the interosseous space. Small
caliber at this level (~1–2 mm). The anterior interosseous artery
lies on the opposite side of the interosseous membrane (PC6 side).
Low-moderate vascular risk with standard depth; through-needling
technique risks both posterior and anterior interosseous vessels.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (moderate thickness, dorsal forearm)
- Extensor digitorum communis (EDC) — main finger extensor
- Extensor digiti minimi (EDM) — 5th finger extensor
- Interosseous space: loose connective tissue between EDC/EDM
  tendons and the deep extensor layer
- Extensor pollicis longus (deep, radial side) — TE5 is identified
  as the **motor point of EPL** (Callison, Sports Medicine
  Acupuncture), meaning deep needling directly stimulates the
  motor nerve entry to this muscle
- Interosseous membrane (dense fibrous sheet — the fascial floor,
  ~1.86 ± 0.25 mm thick at this level)
- [Through the membrane: flexor digitorum profundus → median nerve
  territory → PC6]

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE5 Waiguan (外关). Luo-Connecting + Eight
Confluent (Yang Wei Mai). Primary functions: opens Yang Wei Mai
(temporal headache, lateral pain, alternating chills/fever),
releases exterior (疏表散风, common cold/flu), activates channel
(upper limb pain, wrist/hand disorders), benefits the ear (tinnitus,
deafness via lateral channel trajectory), clears heat. TE5 is the
PRIMARY DISTAL POINT for temporal/lateral headache and ear
conditions in the TE channel — the Shaoyang equivalent of LI4's
role for facial conditions and SI3's role for spinal conditions.

**2. Trigger point:** TE5 overlies the **extensor digitorum communis
(EDC)** and **extensor digiti minimi (EDM)** muscle bellies on the
dorsal forearm. EDC trigger points (Travell & Simons): located in
the proximal muscle belly, 3–5 cm distal to the lateral epicondyle.
Referred pain pattern: dorsal forearm, wrist, dorsal hand, and
middle/ring fingers. TE5 at 2 cun from the wrist is slightly MORE
DISTAL than the classic EDC trigger point (which is more proximal,
closer to TE8–TE9 level). However, the EDM muscle — more specific
to the ring/little finger — has trigger points that extend more
distally, closer to TE5. Overall: MODERATE correspondence. The
trigger point literature maps the common extensor mass as a
continuous zone rather than a single point, and TE5 lies within
this zone.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.3

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in lateral epicondylitis (tennis
elbow — pain radiates distally from the lateral epicondyle along
the extensor mass, and TE5 becomes spontaneously tender), de
Quervain's tenosynovitis (dorsal wrist), extensor overuse
(keyboard/mouse strain — "repetitive strain injury"), and carpal
tunnel syndrome (dorsal wrist stiffness). More broadly: the entire
TE5–TE8 dorsal forearm zone is reactive in upper limb repetitive
strain. Palpation at TE5 often reveals localized tenderness in
patients with lateral elbow/wrist conditions even when they report
pain at the elbow. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** **Manibandha Marma** (wrist Marma, 107-point
Ayurvedic system) — located at the wrist joint area. TE5 at 2 cun
proximal is at the proximal boundary of this Marma's territory.
Same Marma assigned to PC6 in AP-09 — Inner and Outer Gate both
fall within the Manibandha zone, reinforcing the paired-point
concept. No precise Marma match at the exact 2-cun level; the
correspondence is regional rather than point-specific.

**5. Head zone:** C5-C8 dermatome (radial nerve territory on the
dorsal forearm). The C5-C8 viscerotome corresponds broadly to the
upper respiratory tract and cardiac territory. However, TE5's
primary clinical indications (headache, ear, lateral pain) are NOT
within the C5-C8 viscerotome — they are cranial territory (CN V,
CN VII). This reinforces the NON-SEGMENTAL mechanism: TE5's effects
on headache and ear conditions require SUPRASPINAL pathways (C5-C8
entry → brainstem → cranial targets), not dermatomal reflex.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** **Superficial Back Arm Line (SBAL)** — the
dorsal forearm extensors are key stations on this myofascial line.
SBAL pathway: dorsal hand → forearm extensors → lateral epicondyle
→ lateral intermuscular septum → deltoid → trapezius. TE5 is a
meaningful station on the SBAL. This is DISTINCT from PC6's
position on the Deep Front Arm Line (DFAL) — dorsal vs palmar,
extensor vs flexor, SBAL vs DFAL. The SBAL trajectory from forearm
to shoulder/neck parallels the TE channel trajectory and extends
into Yang Wei Mai territory (shoulder → neck → head).
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No specific data for TE5. Kim
2017 studied trunk primarily. Dorsal forearm neurogenic spots are
plausible given the rich innervation but unstudied.

**8. Cupping point:** Not a standard cupping site. The dorsal
forearm is occasionally cupped for forearm/wrist pain (local
treatment), but TE5 is not a named cupping point in either TCM or
Sunnah traditions.

**Supplementary — Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture:** Not one of
the 8 standard cross-tradition systems, but notable: Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture Upper 5
point is located at EXACTLY the same position as TE5 (2 cun
proximal to dorsal wrist crease, center of dorsal forearm between
radius and ulna). Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture was developed independently in the 1970s —
this represents a modern independent rediscovery of the TE5
location within a separate microsystem framework.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, Trigger Point moderate, Ashi,
Anatomy Trains). Higher than SI3 (3/8) — the extensor muscle mass
at TE5 provides better trigger point and myofascial line
correspondence than the hypothenar hand surface. Below high-
convergence muscular points like LI4 (5/8) or LI11 (6/8), which
benefit from larger muscle mass and stronger trigger point
matching. (Would be 5/8 if Wrist-Ankle Acupuncture were counted as a standard system.)

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Migraine — 🟡 Plausible (strong indirect evidence)**

He et al. 2025 (Journal of Pain Research): data mining of 911
clinical trials and 1,610 acupuncture prescriptions for migraine
across 181 distinct acupoints. **TE5 ranked 8th overall** with
381 occurrences (3.52% of total point usage). Core Shaoyang
prescription: **GB20–GB8–TE5** with support 15.65% and confidence
90.48% — the strongest association rule in the dataset.

Finding: "Eight of the top 11 most frequently used points (≥5
times) were from the Shaoyang meridian." The Shaoyang (GB+TE)
dominance in migraine protocols is the strongest meridian-condition
correlation in the migraine evidence base. TE5's position as the
primary TE channel contributor to this pattern is architecturally
coherent with Yang Wei Mai's temporal trajectory.

Zhao et al. 2017 (JAMA Internal Medicine): 249 patients, 3-arm
RCT (true acupuncture vs sham vs usual care), 24 weeks. For
Shaoyang-type headache (temporal): **TE5 and GB34 were the
specified additional points** alongside obligatory GB20 + GB8.
This is the most rigorous migraine acupuncture trial published
(JAMA-level), and TE5 is a designated component.

Linde et al. 2016 (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews): 22
RCTs, 4,985 participants. Acupuncture at least as effective as
prophylactic drugs (topiramate, propranolol, valproate) for
reducing migraine frequency. TE5 listed among commonly used distal
arm points across trial protocols.

Tong et al. 2025 (Frontiers in Neurology): review of 40 migraine
neuroimaging (fMRI) studies. **TE5 appeared in 21/40 studies,
tied with GB20 as the most frequently used acupoint** in migraine
neuroimaging research. 8/11 top points were Shaoyang.

2023 SR (15 studies): acupuncture reduced migraine attack frequency,
migraine days, and pain scores more than sham at 3-month follow-up.

Rating rationale: 🟡 Plausible (not 🟢) because — (a) TE5's
individual contribution is NOT isolated from multi-point protocols,
(b) the data mining evidence shows strong ASSOCIATION but not
causation, (c) Cochrane review evidence is for acupuncture-as-
protocol, not TE5 specifically. However, the convergence of
evidence is stronger than typical 🟡: data mining (8th/181 points),
association rules (90.48% confidence), Cochrane-level protocol
evidence, and architectural coherence with Yang Wei Mai trajectory.

**2. Neuroimaging — Point-specific brain activation 🟡 Plausible**

Three studies with TE5-specific neuroimaging data:

**(a) Qi et al. 2014 (Neural Regeneration Research):** 16 ischemic
stroke patients. fMRI during TE5 stimulation:
- Activation: right precuneus (BA 7, 19), right temporal (BA 39),
  right posterior cingulate (BA 23), bilateral occipital (BA 18)
- Inhibition: right inferior frontal (BA 45), right pre/
  postcentral gyrus (BA 1, 4, 5, 6)
- **Real vs sham: TE5 significantly inhibited BA5 (somatosensory
  association cortex)** compared to sham — point-specific effect.
  BA5 integrates proprioceptive and somatosensory information for
  motor planning. Inhibition of BA5 in stroke patients may
  represent modulation of maladaptive sensorimotor processing.

**(b) Chen et al. 2014 (PLoS One):** 24 left basal ganglia stroke
patients (18 completed). Functional connectivity MRI:
- TE5 enhanced correlation between left precuneus and left motor/
  somatosensory areas during verum acupuncture
- Enhanced INTERHEMISPHERIC sensorimotor connectivity
- Mechanism: modulation of synchronizations between brain networks

**(c) Huang et al. 2012 (BMC Neuroscience):** 43 ischemic stroke
patients, 5-group design (TE5 needling, TE5 sham needling, sham
point needling, sham point sham needling, non-needling). PET-CT:
- TE5 needling activated BA 30 (retrosplenial cortex)
- Compared with sham at TE5: activated **BA 13 (insular cortex),
  BA 19 (visual association), BA 47 (orbital frontal)**
- **Point-specific effect CONFIRMED**: real TE5 vs sham TE5 showed
  differential glucose metabolism patterns. The BEST-CONTROLLED
  study — 5 groups allow isolation of point-specificity, needle-
  specificity, and acupuncture-specificity independently.

**Key neuroimaging synthesis:**

TE5 activates the **insular cortex (BA 13)** — a key integration
hub for interoception, pain processing, autonomic regulation, and
salience detection. The insula is implicated in migraine
pathophysiology (interictal structural and functional changes in
migraine patients). TE5's insular activation provides a plausible
neural basis for its migraine efficacy: modulating insula function
→ altered pain processing and autonomic regulation in the
trigeminovascular system.

Additionally, TE5 modulates sensorimotor cortex (BA 1, 4, 5, 6)
— consistent with its clinical use in post-stroke motor
rehabilitation. And the retrosplenial cortex (BA 30) and posterior
cingulate (BA 23) are components of the default mode network
(DMN), involved in self-referential processing and pain
chronification.

**Compare with PC6 neuroimaging:** PC6 activates the NTS
(nucleus tractus solitarius) → vagal pathway → antiemetic effect
(AP-09 PC6). TE5 activates insular cortex → pain processing +
sensorimotor integration. DIFFERENT central targets, confirming
the neural architecture divergence at the Inner/Outer Gate pair.
→ Cross-ref: AP-09 PC6

**3. Post-stroke upper limb — 🟡 Plausible (protocol evidence)**

TE5 is a standard protocol point in stroke upper limb rehabilitation:
- Warm acupuncture at TE5 for post-stroke shoulder-hand syndrome
  (PMID 25069201): significant decreases in pain and edema severity,
  increased Fugl-Meyer motor scores
- Nazari 2025 (Case Reports in Neurological Medicine): dry needling
  of LI4 + TE5 for post-stroke wrist flexor spasticity — case
  report showing benefit
- Network meta-analysis 2025 (PMC12931285): different acupuncture
  methods for post-stroke upper-limb spasticity

TE5's inclusion in EVERY standard stroke upper-limb protocol
(LI4, LI10, LI11, LI15, TE5) is notable — it is the ONLY
non-LI channel point consistently included. This may reflect its
position as the Yang counterpart to PC6 (which accesses median
nerve/autonomic pathways) — TE5 provides radial nerve/sensorimotor
access to complement LI's radial nerve motor territory.

Rating: 🟡 Plausible — protocol-level evidence only. TE5's
individual contribution not isolated. But consistent inclusion
across protocols suggests clinical utility beyond placebo.
→ Cross-ref: AP-02 LI11, LI15

**4. Lateral epicondylitis — 🟡 Plausible (protocol evidence)**

TE5 included in lateral epicondylitis protocols alongside Ashi,
LI4, LI10, LI11 (documented in AP-02 LI11). Described as one of
"the two most commonly used acupoints for lateral epicondylitis"
alongside LI11.

Rating: 🟡 Plausible — protocol inclusion without individual
isolation. Same methodological limitation as post-stroke evidence.

**5. Neuroprotection — 🔴 Unvalidated (animal model, TE5 as
sole point)**

Lin et al. 2016 (Neural Regeneration Research): 39 Sprague-Dawley
rats. TE5 was the PRIMARY AND ONLY intervention point. 20 minutes
of manual acupuncture at TE5. Result: Bcl-2 mRNA significantly
elevated at 20 min post-treatment (p<0.001), remained elevated at
2 hours (p<0.01). Bcl-2 protein expression in cerebellum remained
significantly higher up to 2 hours. Also upregulated Birc1b
(anti-apoptotic factor).

This is the ONLY study using TE5 as a SOLE intervention point
with molecular-level outcome measures. The anti-apoptotic pathway
(Bcl-2/Birc1b) provides a plausible mechanism for TE5's inclusion
in stroke rehabilitation protocols — neuroprotection via anti-
apoptotic signaling, independent of multi-point protocol effects.

Rating: 🔴 because animal model only, no human replication.

**6. All other traditional indications — 🔴 Unvalidated**

No study isolates TE5 for common cold/flu, tinnitus/deafness,
alternating chills and fever, wrist pain, or finger contracture.

**Evidence-importance profile (vs SI3):** Unlike SI3 (zero
completed RCTs despite maximum clinical importance), TE5 has
INDIRECT evidence from multiple SRs and protocol-level data.
The gap between evidence and clinical use is narrower at TE5
than at SI3, LU9, or HT7 — but the fundamental limitation
persists: TE5 has never been tested as a SOLE intervention.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No specific sensitization data at TE5. However, the dorsal forearm
extensor mass at TE5 becomes tender in:
- Lateral epicondylitis (extensor tendinopathy at lateral
  epicondyle radiates distally to TE5 zone)
- Repetitive strain injury (keyboard/mouse overuse → forearm
  extensor fatigue → TE5 area tenderness)
- De Quervain's tenosynovitis (radial wrist extensors → referred
  tenderness in dorsal forearm)

Whether TE5 specifically shows pathology-dependent threshold
changes (as predicted by the Dynamic Acupoint model, C04 S4) in
migraine patients is unstudied. The SI3→Du Mai thermal prediction
(AP-06) generates a parallel question: does TE5 show altered
pressure-pain threshold or temperature in patients with temporal
headache vs controls?
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

### Cơ chế giải thích

**The central question: HOW does a dorsal forearm point (C5-C8)
affect the temporal head and ear (CN V, CN VII territory)?**

TE5's primary clinical functions — temporal headache, ear
conditions, lateral pain — require traversing from the radial
nerve spinal territory (C5-C8) to cranial nerve territory (CN V
trigeminal for temporal pain, CN VII facial for ear). This is a
NON-SEGMENTAL gap of ~5+ segments plus cranial nerve territory,
similar in magnitude to LI4→trigeminal (AP-02 LI4 analysis) but
accessing DIFFERENT cranial targets (LI4→CN V2/V3 maxillary/
mandibular; TE5→CN V1/V2 frontal/temporal + CN VII auricular).

**Proposed mechanism — supraspinal relay via insular cortex:**

```
Step 1: Needling TE5 activates A-beta/A-delta fibers of posterior
        interosseous nerve (C5-C8) + posterior antebrachial
        cutaneous nerve
Step 2: Afferents enter the dorsal horn at C5-C8, ascend via
        spinothalamic and spinoreticular tracts to brainstem
Step 3: Brainstem relay: PAG + RVM (descending pain modulation)
        + INSULA (BA 13, confirmed by PET-CT Huang 2012)
Step 4: Insular cortex modulation: the anterior insula is a hub
        for trigeminovascular processing in migraine — modulation
        here may alter the threshold for cortical spreading
        depression (CSD) or trigeminal nociceptive transmission
Step 5: Descending modulation via trigeminal nucleus caudalis
        (TNC) → altered pain processing in temporal and
        periauricular territory
```

This mechanism differs from PC6's NTS-vagal pathway in both
the relay station (insula vs NTS) and the effector system (trigeminal
vs vagal). The Inner/Outer Gate pair thus accesses TWO DISTINCT
supraspinal modulation systems from adjacent forearm locations.

**The Yang Wei Mai architectural coherence:**

The vessel's trajectory (ST8-GB13-GB20, see §3 above) maps the
temporal-occipital migraine distribution. The NEURAL correlate of
this trajectory is the trigeminal territory (CN V1 supraorbital
→ CN V2 temporal → C2-C3 occipital via trigemino-cervical complex).
TE5's access to insular cortex (the central hub of trigeminal
pain processing) provides a plausible mechanism for the classical
claim that TE5 "opens" the Yang Wei Mai territory: not literally
opening a vessel, but activating a supraspinal pathway that
modulates pain processing across the entire temporal-occipital
distribution.

**Compare with SI3→Du Mai mechanism (AP-06):**
- SI3 (C8-T1) → PAG → descending inhibition → spine (Du Mai
  territory) — thermal imaging confirmed (Trinh 2024)
- TE5 (C5-C8) → insula (BA 13) → trigeminal modulation → temporal/
  periauricular (Yang Wei Mai territory) — PET-CT confirmed
  (Huang 2012)

Both Eight Confluent points show point-specific central activation
at supraspinal structures relevant to their vessel's territory.
This strengthens the testable prediction from AP-06: if SI3→Du
Mai and TE5→Yang Wei Mai both show measurable physiological
signatures at their vessel territories, the Eight Confluent system
may be empirically verifiable point-by-point.

**Gap analysis:**

1. TE5's insular cortex activation (Huang 2012) was demonstrated
   in STROKE patients, not migraine patients. The insula's role
   differs between stroke recovery (motor planning, interoception)
   and migraine (pain threshold, CSD). A TE5 fMRI study specifically
   in migraine patients is needed.

2. The Yang Wei Mai trajectory is described classically, but its
   modern neural/fascial correlate has not been mapped systematically.
   Unlike Du Mai (midline spine, clear neural correlate), Yang Wei
   Mai's lateral trajectory may correspond to the lateral fascial
   compartment (Anatomy Trains Lateral Line) or the trigeminal-
   cervical complex neural territory — or both.

3. TE5's Luo-Connecting function (treating Interior-Exterior pair
   conditions) has no modern evidence. Does stimulating TE5 produce
   any measurable effect on median nerve or vagal function
   (PC territory)?

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

**Acupuncture (châm):** 15–25 mm perpendicular insertion between
radius and ulna. The needle enters the extensor digitorum/digiti
minimi muscle mass, descending toward the interosseous membrane.
De Qi: distension, heaviness, or aching sensation in the dorsal
forearm, often radiating distally toward the wrist or proximally
toward the elbow. Stronger De Qi than superficial points —
the extensor muscle mass provides robust proprioceptive feedback.

**Through-needling to PC6 (透刺, tòu cì):** Advanced classical
technique. A 40–50 mm needle is inserted at TE5 perpendicular to
the skin, advanced through the interosseous membrane, and continued
into the palmar compartment to reach the tissue depth of PC6. This
simultaneously stimulates:
- Posterior interosseous nerve (TE5/radial territory)
- Interosseous membrane (dense fascial structure)
- Median nerve territory (PC6 side)
Two afferent pathways to two brainstem targets in a single needle
insertion. The technique is specifically used for conditions
requiring both Interior and Exterior regulation (e.g., cardiac
palpitation with fever/chills — PC domain + TE domain).

Precaution: through-needling requires anatomical precision and
experience. Risk of interosseous artery/vein injury and nerve
damage (both posterior interosseous and median) if angle or depth
is incorrect.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

**EA (electroacupuncture):** TE5 is commonly paired with TE3,
TE4, or LI4 for EA. For lateral epicondylitis: TE5–LI11 pair at
2–4 Hz (low frequency, enkephalin-mediated analgesia). For
post-stroke motor: TE5–LI4 pair at 50–100 Hz (high frequency,
dynorphin-mediated + motor cortex stimulation).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.4

**Acupressure (bấm huyệt):** Self-applicable. Thumb pressure on
the dorsal forearm between radius and ulna, 2 cun above the wrist
crease. Moderate to firm pressure for 1–2 minutes. Commonly used
for tension headache and wrist stiffness self-care. Accessible
point for patient self-management.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S2.1

**Moxibustion (cứu):** Applicable. Indirect moxa or moxa stick
for Wind-Cold exterior invasion (common cold with chills > fever,
body aches). The warming technique enhances the exterior-releasing
(解表) function. Not a primary moxa point.

### An toàn

LOW-MODERATE risk.

Precautions:
- **Posterior interosseous nerve and artery:** deep in the
  interosseous space. Standard-depth perpendicular needling
  (15–25 mm) may reach the nerve — paresthesia radiating to the
  dorsal hand/fingers = nerve contact, withdraw slightly
- **Through-needling risk:** penetrating the interosseous membrane
  risks both posterior and anterior interosseous vessels, and
  potentially the median nerve on the palmar side. Advanced
  technique only.
- **Vascular:** small-caliber posterior interosseous artery. Minor
  bruising possible. No major vessel risk.
- **Bent needle risk** (Deadman): the point lies between radius
  and ulna — forearm rotation after needle insertion can bend or
  trap the needle. Instruct patient not to pronate/supinate.
- One case report of posterior interosseous nerve palsy after
  forearm acupuncture (Sreedharan et al. 2012, J Hand Surg Eur
  Vol). Location consistent with TE channel area. Patient
  recovered. No other TE5-specific adverse events in systematic
  reviews of acupuncture safety (Xu 2013, Wu 2015, Chan 2017).
- No pneumothorax risk (forearm)
- No pregnancy contraindication at TE5
- No classical needling or moxibustion prohibitions

> **Observation 1 (Batch 1):** The TE5-PC6 Inner/Outer Gate pair

With TE5, the Atlas now has THREE Eight Confluent points documented

**3. SHAOYANG DOMINANCE IN MIGRAINE — MERIDIAN-CONDITION SPECIFICITY**

The He et al. 2025 finding — 8 of top 11 migraine points are

- Based on traditional theory without empirical quantification

> the insular cortex activation by TE5 (a forearm point, not a
> temporal point) provides a non-local, neuroimaging-confirmed
> mechanism beyond simple geographic proximity.

## TE1 — Quan Xung (关冲, Guān Chōng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Rushing the Pass" (关冲) — 关 (Guān, pass/gate) + 冲 (Chōng,
rush/surge). Jing-Well (**Metal**) point of the Triple Burner
channel. Metal on a **Fire** channel = **Ke-cycle subject** (Fire
controls Metal, 火克金) — the point element is dominated by the
channel's own element. Third Yang Jing-Well documented in the
Atlas (after LI1 and SI1). All Yang Well points are Metal.

TE1 is the STARTING POINT of the TE channel on the ring finger
(4th digit). Its Interior-Exterior mirror is PC9 (Zhongchong) on
the middle finger (3rd digit) — but unlike the SI1-HT9 pair
(same finger, opposite sides, ~3–5 mm apart), TE1 and PC9 are on
ADJACENT FINGERS. This is the widest anatomical separation between
any Interior-Exterior Jing-Well pair on the hand.

Classical identity: emergency bloodletting point (febrile disease,
coma, sore throat). Part of the 12 Jing-Well emergency set.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the ring finger, at the
junction of lines drawn along the ulnar border of the nail and
the base of the nail, approximately 0.1 cun from the ulnar corner
of the nail.

Needle depth: 1–2 mm shallow puncture or prick to bleed.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Proper palmar digital nerve of the ring finger, ulnar side
  (from common digital branch of median nerve in most individuals,
  or ulnar nerve variant — the 4th digit has VARIABLE innervation,
  with ~60% median nerve dominant, ~20% ulnar, ~20% shared)
- Dorsal digital nerve from superficial branch of radial nerve
  (C6-C7) on the dorsal side
- Spinal segments: C6-C8 (variable — broader range than SI1's
  C8-T1 ulnar nerve exclusive)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Variable innervation at TE1:** The ring finger is the classic
"watershed" digit between median and ulnar nerve territories.
TE1's innervation depends on individual anatomical variation.
This contrasts with SI1 (always ulnar, C8-T1) and LI1 (always
radial + median, C6-C7). The neuroanatomical variability at TE1
may partly explain why the TE channel is traditionally considered
more "diffuse" than LI or SI channels.

Vascular: proper palmar digital artery (ulnar side, 4th digit).
Rich subungual capillary plexus — basis for bloodletting.

**Five Shu: Jing-Well (Metal)** — 1st of Five Shu on the TE
channel.

**Metal on Fire channel = Ke-cycle subject** (Fire controls Metal,
火克金). Fire melts Metal in the Ke (controlling) cycle. The
channel element dominates the point element — this is the position
of least resistance/most controlled element.

**Ke Subject across documented Yang channels (Fire element):**

- **TE1: Well/Metal on Fire. Ke Subject (Fire克Metal).** ✓

SI1 and TE1 are the paired-element Ke Subject points: SAME element
(Metal), SAME Shu position (Well), SAME Five Element role. This
confirms the algebraic prediction: all Fire-element Yang channels
assign Ke Subject to Well/Metal.
→ Cross-ref: AP-06 SI1, AP-01 LU9 (algebraic orthogonality)

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE1 Guanchong (关冲). Primary function:
clears heat, benefits the throat, resuscitates consciousness.
Emergency point for febrile delirium, coma, heatstroke. Combined
with other Jing-Well points for emergency bloodletting.

**2. Trigger point:** No trigger point at digit tip (no muscle
mass). Same limitation as SI1, LI1, HT9, PC9, LU11.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender with 4th DIP arthritis, ring

**7. Neurogenic spot:** No data (digit tip).

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable (digit).

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi). Same as most digit-tip

🔴 Unvalidated for all indications. No RCT or SR isolating TE1.
Standard Jing-Well evidence gap — consistent with all digit-tip

### An toàn

LOW risk. Digit tip — no significant neurovascular structures.

## TE3 — Trung Chữ (中渚, Zhōng Zhǔ)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Middle Islet" (中渚) — 中 (Zhōng, middle/center) + 渚 (Zhǔ,
islet/sandbar). The image evokes a small island in a stream —
the Shu-Stream designation where "Qi pours" (所注为输). Shu-Stream
(**Wood**) point of the TE channel. Wood on a **Fire** channel =
**TONIFICATION** (mother of Fire in the Sheng cycle, 木生火) —
stimulating Wood nourishes Fire. This is the most important
functional role for treating deficiency of the channel's own
element.

TE3 holds clinical significance as the primary distal TE point
for tinnitus and deafness — the ear is the signature treatment
domain of the TE channel, and TE3 is the most commonly selected
distal point for ear conditions after TE5. Combined with TE5
(same channel) or with SI3 (paired Taiyang tonification point)
for reinforcement protocols.

TE3 parallels SI3 structurally: both are Shu-Stream/Wood points
on Fire channels, both are Tonification points, both lie on the
dorsal hand proximal to the MCP joint. SI3 has the Eight Confluent
designation that TE3 lacks, but TE3 has greater clinical evidence
for tinnitus.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the dorsum of the hand,
between the 4th and 5th metacarpal bones, in the depression
proximal to the 4th metacarpophalangeal joint.

Anatomical landmarks: with the hand in a fist, the depression
between the heads of the 4th and 5th metacarpal bones on the
dorsal surface. TE3 lies in the 4th intermetacarpal space, on
the dorsal interosseous muscle.

Spatial relationships:
- TE2 is distal (at the web space margin)
- TE4 is proximal (at the dorsal wrist crease)
- SI3 is on the ULNAR side of the 5th metacarpal (different
  intermetacarpal space — SI3 is ulnar to the 5th MC, TE3 is
  between 4th and 5th)

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular or oblique (0.3–0.5 cun).

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Dorsal digital nerve from the superficial branch of the ulnar
  nerve (C8-T1) — provides sensory innervation to the 4th web
  space dorsal surface
- Fourth dorsal interosseous muscle innervated by the deep branch

  enters this muscle.
- Possible contribution from dorsal branch of radial nerve
  (variable at the 4th intermetacarpal space)
- Spinal segments: C8-T1 (primarily ulnar nerve)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Tissue transition at TE3:** Like SI3, TE3 is the FIRST TE point
with significant muscle tissue (4th dorsal interosseous muscle).

## TE4 — Dương Trì (阳池, Yáng Chí)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Yang Pool" (阳池) — 阳 (Yáng, Yang) + 池 (Chí, pool/pond). The
"pool" image evokes Qi collecting at a joint depression — the
dorsal wrist crease where TE4 sits. Yuan-Source (原穴) point of
the Triple Burner channel. Yuan-Source points are WHERE Yuan Qi
(原气, Source Qi) surfaces from the deep interior to the channel
level. In TCM theory, San Jiao distributes Yuan Qi from Kidney/
Ming Men to all organs — making TE4 the SOURCE POINT of the
organ responsible for source Qi distribution. A recursive
function: the Yuan-Source of the Yuan Qi distributor.

TE4 participates in the Host-Guest system with PC:
- TE-origin conditions: **TE4 (Yuan) + PC6 (Luo of PC)** — the
  recommended pairing for Triple Burner pathology
- PC-origin conditions: PC7 (Yuan of PC) + TE5 (Luo of TE) — the
  reverse pairing
→ Cross-ref: AP-09 PC6, PC7

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the dorsum of the wrist,
in the depression on the ulnar side of the tendon of extensor
digitorum communis, at the level of the dorsal wrist crease.

Anatomical landmarks: at the dorsal wrist crease, in the
depression between the tendons of the extensor digitorum communis
(radially) and the extensor digiti minimi (ulnarly). Directly
overlies the radiocarpal joint capsule.

Spatial relationships:
- TE3 is distal (dorsal hand, between 4th-5th metacarpals)
- TE5 is 2 cun proximal (dorsal forearm between radius and ulna)
- LI5 (Yangxi, anatomical snuffbox) is on the radial side of the
  same dorsal wrist crease — both are Yang wrist points at the
  same level, but on opposite sides (radial vs ulnar)
- SI4 (Wangu) is also on the dorsal wrist crease but at the ulnar
  border of the 5th metacarpal — more ulnar than TE4

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular (0.3–0.5 cun).

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Posterior interosseous nerve (terminal branch)** (radial
  nerve, C7-C8) — at the dorsal wrist crease, the posterior
  interosseous nerve terminates in a small pseudoganglion on the
  dorsal surface of the radiocarpal joint capsule. TE4 lies
  directly over this termination point. The pseudoganglion
  contains proprioceptive fibers from the wrist joint — TE4
  needling directly stimulates wrist joint proprioceptors.
- Posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve (radial nerve, C5-C8) —
  cutaneous sensation at the dorsal wrist
- Dorsal branch of ulnar nerve (C8-T1) — passes nearby on the
  ulnar side
- Spinal segments: C6-T1 (broad — convergence of radial and ulnar
  nerve territories at the wrist)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Posterior interosseous nerve pseudoganglion at TE4:** The
terminal pseudoganglion of the posterior interosseous nerve is a
unique anatomical feature — a concentration of proprioceptive
nerve fibers on the dorsal wrist capsule. Its proximity to TE4
means needling this point directly engages wrist joint
proprioception. This may explain TE4's traditional indication for
wrist disorders more precisely than general "activates the
channel" explanations.

Fascial plane: the dorsal wrist retinaculum (extensor retinaculum)
passes over this area, binding the extensor tendons to the dorsal
wrist. TE4 lies deep to the extensor tendons but superficial to
the retinaculum at this level.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: dorsal carpal arch (from radial and ulnar arteries).
Multiple small dorsal carpal branches. Low risk — no major vessel.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (thin at dorsal wrist)
- Extensor digitorum communis tendon (radial) + extensor digiti
  minimi tendon (ulnar)
- Dorsal wrist retinaculum
- Dorsal radiocarpal joint capsule + posterior interosseous nerve
  termination (pseudoganglion)

clinical effect.

## TE6 — Chi Câu (支沟, Zhī Gōu)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Branch Ditch" (支沟) — 支 (Zhī, branch/diverge) + 沟 (Gōu,
ditch/channel). The name evokes a branching waterway — consistent
with the San Jiao's role as controller of water passages (决渎之官).
Jing-River (**Fire**) point of the Triple Burner channel — 4th of
the Five Shu. Fire on a **Fire** channel = **HORARY** (时穴) — the
position where point element and channel element coincide. TE6 is
the point that MOST PURELY represents the TE channel's Fire nature.

TE6 is the **MOST CONDITION-SPECIFIC POINT** on the TE channel and
arguably in the entire tradition: its constipation indication has
been the headline function for over 2000 years. The classical maxim
"胁痛取支沟" ("for rib pain, take Zhigou") and the constipation
specificity make TE6 a rare example of a point whose clinical
identity is defined by a SINGLE condition rather than a broad range.

Located 3 cun proximal to the dorsal wrist crease (1 cun proximal
to TE5), TE6 sits in the extensor muscle belly — the first TE
forearm point with substantial muscle mass rather than the tendon-
dominated wrist area.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the dorsal aspect of the
forearm, 3 cun proximal to the dorsal wrist crease, in the
depression between the radius and ulna.

Anatomical landmarks: with forearm pronated, measure 3 cun (~6 cm)
proximal to the midpoint of the dorsal wrist crease. The point lies
between the extensor digitorum communis (radial) and extensor digiti
minimi (ulnar), in the interosseous space. 1 cun proximal to TE5.

Spatial relationships:
- TE5 (Waiguan, Luo-Connecting) is 1 cun distal (2 cun from wrist)
- TE7 (Huizong, Xi-Cleft) is at the SAME proximal-distal level but
  1 cun ULNAR — deviated to the radial border of the ulna
- TE8 (Sanyangluo) is 1 cun proximal (4 cun from wrist)

Needle depth: 15–25 mm perpendicular. Greater muscle mass than TE5
allows deeper insertion into the extensor muscle belly.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Posterior interosseous nerve (C7-C8)** — motor and sensory. At
  3 cun from the wrist, the PIN runs on the interosseous membrane
  deep to the extensor muscles. TE6 needling at standard depth
  reaches the extensor muscle belly; deeper insertion approaches
  the nerve trunk.
- Posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve (radial nerve, C5-C8) —
  cutaneous sensation of the dorsal forearm
- Spinal segments: C5-C8 (Neural Zone 2, same as TE5)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Tendon-to-muscle transition:** TE6 is the first TE point proximal
to the wrist where needle insertion enters substantial muscle tissue
(extensor digitorum communis belly) rather than tendons. The muscle
belly provides richer proprioceptive afferent density than the
tendinous wrist area — a potential factor in TE6's stronger clinical
identity compared to TE4/TE5.

Fascial plane: antebrachial fascia between extensor compartments.
Interosseous membrane at depth.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: posterior interosseous artery (deep, on the interosseous
membrane). Small caliber at this level. Low vascular risk.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Extensor digitorum communis (primary muscle at this level)
- Extensor digiti minimi (ulnar)
- Extensor pollicis longus and extensor indicis (deeper)
- Interosseous membrane (floor)

specificity — TE6's defining function — has NO cross-tradition
support. None of the 7 non-TCM systems predict a bowel function at
a dorsal forearm point. This makes TE6 the most **TCM-EXCLUSIVE**
high-impact point in the Atlas: its headline indication is supported
only by Chinese medicine tradition and modern clinical evidence,
with zero convergence from independent discovery.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Ling Shu Ch.1 (Ben Shu): Jing-River of the Triple Burner.
- Jiayi Jing: constipation (大便难), rib pain (胁痛), fever with
  alternating chills, shoulder/arm pain, vomiting, sore throat.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: constipation (大便秘结) — PRIMARY, rib fullness/
  pain (胁肋胀满), sudden loss of voice (暴喑), febrile disease.
- Classical maxim: "胁痛取支沟" ("for rib pain, take Zhigou") —

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Promotes bowel movement (PRIMARY):** constipation — the most
  consistent and distinctive indication across all sources. TE6 for
  constipation is as condition-specific as LI4 for facial pain or
  ST36 for gastric disorders.
- **Benefits the hypochondrium:** rib pain, intercostal neuralgia,
  herpes zoster (lateral trunk distribution)
- Clears San Jiao heat: fever, alternating chills/fever (Shaoyang)
- Activates the channel: forearm pain, shoulder pain

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**Constipation — 🟡 Plausible (one multi-center RCT)**

Yang et al. (2007, Zhongguo Zhen Jiu): multi-center RCT of
electroacupuncture at TE6 vs sham non-acupoint. 4-week treatment.
Effective rate: **94.4% (TE6) vs 61.3% (sham), P < 0.0001.** EA
at TE6 significantly improved clinical symptoms and colon transit
time (radiopaque marker test), and decreased laxative use. No
adverse events reported.

Context: Liu et al. (2016, Annals of Internal Medicine) — large
multi-center RCT (1,075 patients, 15 hospitals) demonstrated EA for
chronic constipation with significant improvement, but used ST25/
SP14/ST36/ST37, NOT TE6. Zheng et al. (2020, EBCAM): SR/MA of 28
RCTs (3,525 patients) found acupuncture superior to sham for
constipation overall. Cochrane protocol (Cao et al. 2023) rates
TE6 as "weakly recommended," ST25 as "strongly recommended."

Rating: 🟡 because TE6 has one dedicated sham-controlled RCT with
positive results, but the study is single (replication needed) and
published in a Chinese-language journal.

**Herpes zoster — 🟡 Plausible (protocol evidence)**

TE6 is included in acupuncture protocols for herpes zoster,
particularly lateral trunk distribution (Shaoyang territory).
Combined acupuncture + drug therapy reduced postherpetic neuralgia
incidence. Umbrella review of SRs exists (2022). TE6's individual
contribution not isolated.

**Intercostal neuralgia / rib pain — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Classical tradition ("胁痛取支沟") with no modern controlled
evidence.

**Post-surgical ileus — 🔴 Unvalidated**

One trial of EA at TE6+ST36 for adhesive ileus (2012, Zhongguo
Zhen Jiu). TE6 not isolated.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No specific sensitization data at TE6. The segmental mismatch
(C5-C8 point → T10-L2 colon) makes classical viscerosomatic
sensitization unlikely — any Dynamic property would need to be
mediated via supraspinal pathways rather than segmental reflexes.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**The constipation mechanism puzzle — heterosegmental vagal:**

TE6 (C5-C8 posterior interosseous nerve) treats constipation
(colon = T10-L2 innervation). The segmental gap spans 4+ spinal
segments — no direct spinal reflex can connect them. The mechanism
must be supraspinal.

**Proposed pathway (Liu et al. 2022, Current Opinion in
Neurobiology):** C5-C8 somatic afferents at TE6 → spinal cord →
ascending to brainstem → nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) and
dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus (DMV) → vagal efferents →
cholinergic stimulation of colonic motility.

This is the HETEROSEGMENTAL mechanism: distant somatic input
modulates visceral function via brainstem relay. The same pathway
has been demonstrated for ST36 (L4-S1 → vagal → gastric motility)
in animal models (Liu et al. 2021, Nature).

**Compare with ST25 for constipation (SEGMENTAL mechanism):**
ST25 (T10-T12 intercostal nerves) → local spinal sympatho-
inhibitory reflex → reduced sympathetic tone to colon → increased
colonic motility. ST25's mechanism is SEGMENTAL — the point's
spinal level matches the organ's innervation. The Liu 2016 Ann
Intern Med RCT (n=1,075, strongest constipation evidence) used
ST25, not TE6.

**Two pathways to one organ:**
| | TE6 (heterosegmental) | ST25 (segmental) |
|---|---|---|
| Spinal level | C5-C8 | T10-T12 |
| Relay | Brainstem (NTS/DMV) | Spinal cord (local) |
| Effector | Vagal (parasympathetic ↑) | Sympathetic ↓ |
| Evidence | 🟡 one RCT | 🟢 multiple RCTs |
| Cochrane rating | "weakly recommended" | "strongly recommended" |

**Rib pain mechanism:** clearer segmental basis. TE6 (C5-C8)
overlaps with upper intercostal nerve territory via brachial plexus
(C5-T1). The Shaoyang syndrome (alternating fever/chills) also
involves lateral trunk structures accessible via Shaoyang channel
territory.

**fMRI data:** At least 2 fMRI studies of TE6 exist (Chinese-
language journals) confirming distinct central brain activation vs
sham. Specific activated regions not fully reported in available
abstracts.

**Gap:** TE6's vagal pathway to colon is inferred from analogy with
ST36 (experimentally demonstrated), not directly traced for TE6.
An animal study blocking vagal input during TE6 stimulation would
confirm the mechanism.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.4

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 15–25 mm perpendicular insertion into the extensor
digitorum communis muscle belly. De Qi: distension, heaviness in
the dorsal forearm, sometimes radiating to the hand or elbow. EA:
TE6 alone or TE6+ST25 pair for constipation (2 Hz continuous wave).

**Constipation-specific technique:** traditionally, TE6 is needled
with strong reducing manipulation (提插泻法, lift-thrust reducing)
for excess-type constipation. For deficiency-type (elderly, post-
partum), tonifying manipulation or moxa is used instead.

Acupressure: self-applicable. Thumb or knuckle pressure on dorsal
forearm, 3 cun above wrist crease. Firm pressure for 1–2 minutes.
Widely recommended for constipation self-care.

Moxibustion: applicable, particularly for cold-type/deficiency
constipation.

### An toàn

LOW risk. Dorsal forearm — standard risk profile.

Precautions:
- Posterior interosseous nerve and artery at depth (interosseous
  membrane). Standard-depth perpendicular needling is safe; deep
  insertion may contact the nerve.
- Same bent needle risk as TE5 — instruct patient not to rotate
  forearm during needling
- No pregnancy contraindication
- No classical needling or moxibustion prohibitions

> **Observation 7 (Batch 3):** TE6 for constipation operates via
> a HETEROSEGMENTAL mechanism: C5-C8 somatic input → brainstem
> NTS/DMV → vagal efferents → colonic motility. ST25 for the same
> condition operates via a SEGMENTAL mechanism: T10-T12 input →
> local spinal sympatho-inhibition → colonic motility. Two points
> for the same condition using completely different neural circuits
> — one supraspinal/vagal (parasympathetic), one spinal/sympathetic.
> The meridian system provides DUAL ACCESS to visceral function:
> segmental points (on-territory) work through local spinal
> reflexes, heterosegmental points (off-territory) work through
> brainstem autonomic relay. The Cochrane protocol's stronger
> recommendation for ST25 over TE6 may reflect the mechanistic
> advantage of direct segmental access.
> → Cross-ref: C04 S3.4, AP-02 LI4 (also heterosegmental via PAG)

## TE7 — Hội Tông (会宗, Huì Zōng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Ancestral Meeting" (会宗) — 会 (Huì, meeting/convergence) +
宗 (Zōng, ancestor/origin). The name suggests convergence at the
channel's ancestral source — where channel Qi gathers deeply.
**Xi-Cleft point** (郄穴) of the Triple Burner channel — the
point where channel Qi accumulates for acute conditions.

TE7 is the **3rd Yang Xi-Cleft** documented in the Atlas (after
LI7 and SI6) and the **8th Xi-Cleft overall**. Classical theory:
Yang Xi-Cleft → acute pain/inflammatory conditions. TE7's primary
indication is **sudden deafness** — a channel-domain-specific
acute emergency rather than generic pain.

**UNIQUE ANATOMICAL DEVIATION:** TE7 sits 1 cun ULNAR to TE6, at
the **radial border of the ulna** — OFF the interosseous space
where all other forearm TE points (TE5, TE6, TE8, TE9) align.
This is the only forearm TE point that deviates from the radius-
ulna interosseous line, moving onto the ulnar bone itself.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the dorsal aspect of the
forearm, 3 cun proximal to the dorsal wrist crease, on the radial
side of the ulna, 1 cun ulnar to TE6.

Anatomical landmarks: TE7 is at the SAME proximal-distal level as
TE6 (both 3 cun from the wrist crease) but deviated 1 cun (~2 cm)
toward the ulna. Palpate the radial border of the ulna — TE7 sits
in the depression between the ulna and the extensor digitorum
communis muscle. The extensor carpi ulnaris originates from the
posterior border of the ulna at this level.

Spatial relationships:
- TE6 (Zhigou, Horary) is 1 cun radial — in the interosseous
  space between radius and ulna
- TE6 and TE7 form a **HORIZONTAL PAIR** at the same level (3 cun
  from wrist) — unique among TE forearm points

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular or oblique toward the ulna.
Shallower than TE6 due to proximity to bone.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Posterior interosseous nerve terminal branches (C7-C8)** — at
  this level, the PIN sends articular branches to the ulnar
  periosteum and the distal radioulnar joint. TE7's position on
  the radial border of the ulna targets these periosteal/articular
  nerve endings specifically.
- Posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve (C5-C8) — cutaneous
- Spinal segments: C5-C8 (same neural zone as TE6)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Why the ulnar deviation?** TE7 targets the posterior interosseous
nerve's periosteal branches where they adhere to the ulna, rather
than the nerve trunk in the interosseous space (TE5, TE6). The
extensor carpi ulnaris has its ulnar head origin along the
posterior ulnar border — TE7 sits at the muscle-bone junction
where the ECU meets the ulna periosteum. The periosteal afferents
(primarily Aδ nociceptive fibers) differ from the muscular
afferents (Aδ + group III/IV proprioceptive) stimulated at TE6 —
different fiber types may engage different brainstem targets.

Fascial plane: periosteum of ulna (radial surface). Less soft
tissue than the interosseous space — primarily bone + muscle
attachment site.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: no major vessel at the radial ulnar border. Posterior
interosseous artery is in the interosseous space (at TE6, not
TE7). Very low vascular risk.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Extensor digitorum communis (radial/superficial)
- Extensor carpi ulnaris (ulnar head origin at ulna)
- Periosteum of ulna (floor)

**Xi-Cleft: YES** — Xi-Cleft point (郄穴) of the Triple Burner.

| LI | LI7 | Yang | Acute toothache, facial pain |

## TE9 — Tứ Độc (四渎, Sì Dú)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Four Rivers" (四渎) — 四 (Sì, four) + 渎 (Dú, large river/
waterway). The name references the Four Great Rivers (四渎) of
ancient China (Jiang 江, He 河, Huai 淮, Ji 济) — a metaphor for
the San Jiao's function as controller of water passages (决渎之官,
气化出焉). Located in the mid-forearm, the last TE point before
the elbow.

TE9 has no special category designation. It is the most proximal
point in Neural Zone 2 (TE5-TE9: all posterior interosseous nerve
territory) — a 5-point same-nerve sequence that parallels the SP
saphenous corridor (SP5-SP9).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the dorsal aspect of the
forearm, 5 cun distal to the olecranon, between the radius and
ulna.

Anatomical landmarks: at mid-forearm. The standard forearm
measurement = 12 cun (dorsal wrist crease to olecranon tip),
placing TE9 at 7 cun from the wrist (12 − 5 = 7). This creates a
3-cun gap between TE8 (4 cun from wrist) and TE9 — the largest
inter-point gap on the TE forearm.

Spatial relationships:
- TE8 is 3 cun distal (4 cun from wrist) — large gap
- TE10 (Tianjing, He-Sea) is 5 cun proximal, at the olecranon

Needle depth: 15–25 mm perpendicular. Maximum forearm muscle mass.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Posterior interosseous nerve (C7-C8)** — at mid-forearm, the
  PIN is larger and more accessible than distally. Motor branches
  supply extensor muscles actively at this level.
- Posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve (C5-C8) — cutaneous
- Spinal segments: C5-C8 (Neural Zone 2 — TE9 is the 5th and
  final point in this zone)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural Zone 2 spans 5 consecutive points (TE5-TE9):** all in
the posterior interosseous nerve + posterior antebrachial cutaneous
nerve territory, C5-C8. This is the longest same-zone sequence on
the TE channel, paralleling the SP saphenous corridor (SP5-SP9,
also 5 consecutive points in one nerve territory). Five points with
identical gross innervation but different traditional functions —
the same functional subdivision paradox seen throughout the Atlas.

Fascial plane: antebrachial fascia. Maximum muscle thickness at
the mid-forearm.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

- Skin → subcutaneous tissue → antebrachial fascia

- Skin → subcutaneous tissue → brachial fascia
- Triceps brachii (lateral head overlying the radial groove)
- Radial groove (humerus) — radial nerve + profunda brachii
  at depth

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No special category designation. Regular channel point.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE12 Xiaoluo (消泺). Functions: activates
the channel, dispels Wind. Indications: headache, neckache,
toothache, arm pain.

**2. Trigger point:** Triceps lateral head TrP — referral to
lateral epicondyle and posterior arm. Moderate anatomical
overlap at this mid-belly level. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in triceps overuse, posterior
arm strain. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No specific correspondence.

**5. Head zone:** C5-T1 dermatome.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SBAL — triceps mid-belly, maximum
cross-sectional area.

**7. Neurogenic spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Posterior upper arm — applicable. 🟡

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: headache, neckache (项痛), toothache, arm pain.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: headache, neck stiffness, shoulder/arm pain,
  toothache.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Dispels Wind:** headache, neck pain/stiffness

- **Toothache**

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

🔴 Unvalidated for all indications. No studies isolating TE12.

### An toàn

LOW-MODERATE risk. Posterior upper arm — generally safe, but the

provides adequate protection.

**Clinical note:** if de qi produces tingling/numbness radiating
to the dorsal hand or thumb (radial nerve territory), withdraw

spiral groove.

## TE13 — Nhu Hội (臑会, Nào Huì)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Upper Arm Meeting" (臑会) — 臑 (Nào, upper arm — specifically
the posterior/lateral surface) + 会 (Huì, meeting/convergence).
The name indicates a point where channels or vessels meet at the
upper arm. Some classical sources (notably Jiayi Jing) classify
TE13 as a **Meeting Point (交会穴)** of the Triple Burner
channel with the **Yang Wei Mai** (Yang Linking Vessel). This
designation appears in multiple classical references but is not
universally endorsed in modern texts.

Located on the posterior-lateral upper arm, at the posterior
border of the deltoid muscle, approximately 3 cun distal to
TE14 (Jianliao). This is the most proximal upper arm point
before the channel reaches the shoulder.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterior-lateral
surface of the upper arm, on the line connecting TE10 and TE14,
at the posterior border of the deltoid muscle.

Anatomical landmarks: the junction between the deltoid
(posterolateral shoulder) and the triceps (posterior arm). The
V-shaped groove where the deltoid's posterior fibers meet the
triceps long head is palpable. Approximately 3 cun distal to
TE14 and 2 cun proximal to TE12.

Spatial relationships:
- TE12 is approximately 2 cun distal (mid-arm)
- TE14 is 3 cun proximal (at the shoulder joint)
- TE13 marks the transition from upper arm to shoulder region

Needle depth: 15–25 mm perpendicular. Adequate muscle mass at
the deltoid-triceps junction.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Posterior brachial cutaneous nerve** (C5-T1) — cutaneous
  supply of the posterior arm, extending to the deltoid border.
- **Axillary nerve** (C5-C6) — motor to deltoid. Terminal
  cutaneous branch (superior lateral brachial cutaneous nerve)
  supplies skin over the deltoid and proximal lateral arm. At
  TE13, the axillary nerve territory is transitioning from motor
  (deltoid) to cutaneous terminal.
- Muscular branches from radial nerve to triceps long head.
- Spinal segments: C5-T1 (overlapping Zones 4 and 5)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural zone transition:** TE13 sits at the boundary between
Zone 4 (posterior brachial cutaneous, upper arm) and Zone 5
(suprascapular + axillary, shoulder). The deltoid-triceps
junction is the anatomical landmark for this transition.

Fascial plane: deltoid fascia posterolaterally, brachial fascia
posteriorly. The two fascial planes converge at the deltoid
border — a fascial intersection.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: posterior circumflex humeral artery branches (from
axillary artery, via quadrilateral space) supply the deltoid
posteriorly. At TE13, these are small terminal branches.

Muscle layers:
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Deltoid (posterior fibers) — laterally
- Triceps (long head) — medially/posteriorly
- Humerus at depth

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Meeting Point (交会穴) — disputed status:**

Some classical sources (Jiayi Jing, certain Qing dynasty texts)
list TE13 as a Meeting Point of TE and Yang Wei Mai. Modern
references are divided — Deadman does not list TE13 as Yang Wei
Mai meeting; some Chinese acupuncture textbooks do.

If the designation is valid, TE13 is one of MULTIPLE Yang Wei
Mai touchpoints on the TE channel:
- TE5: Eight Confluent (OPENS Yang Wei Mai)
- TE13: Meeting (Yang Wei Mai CROSSES TE channel) — disputed
- TE15: Meeting (TE + GB + Yang Wei Mai) — more widely accepted

This would give TE the densest Yang Wei Mai intersection of any
single channel — appropriate since TE5 is the vessel's confluent
point. But the disputed status of TE13's designation means this
density may be overestimated.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE13 Naohui (臑会). Functions: transforms
phlegm, dissipates nodules (散结), activates the channel.
Indications: goiter (瘿气), scrofula (瘰疬), arm/shoulder pain.

**2. Trigger point:** Deltoid posterior TrP / triceps long head
proximal TrP area. Moderate anatomical overlap at the muscle
junction. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender at deltoid-triceps junction
in shoulder impingement, posterior shoulder strain. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No specific correspondence at this junction.

**5. Head zone:** C5-C6 dermatome (axillary nerve territory).

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SBAL-to-SBL transition — where the arm
line meets the shoulder/trunk line.

**7. Neurogenic spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Curved surface at deltoid-triceps
junction — marginally applicable. 🔴

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: goiter (瘿气), scrofula (瘰疬), arm/shoulder
  pain.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: goiter, arm pain, shoulder inability to
  raise, scrofula.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Transforms phlegm/dissipates nodules:** goiter, scrofula —
  shares this function with TE10 (He-Sea/Sedation). The
  Shaoyang channel's phlegm-nodule affinity concentrates at
  TE10 and TE13.
- **Activates channel:** arm pain, shoulder pain

**Goiter note:** TE13 is a distal goiter point — the thyroid
gland is in the anterior neck, not on the TE channel trajectory
at this level. The therapeutic connection is via the channel's
cervical passage, not local proximity. TE13 + TE10 form a
two-point Shaoyang anti-nodular pair on the arm.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

🔴 Unvalidated for all indications. No studies isolating TE13.

### An toàn

LOW risk. Deltoid-triceps junction — adequate muscle mass, no
major neurovascular structures at standard depth. The axillary
nerve's motor branches to the deltoid are deeper and more
medial. Standard posterior arm precautions.

## TE14 — Kiên Liêu (肩髎, Jiān Liáo)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Shoulder Bone Hole" (肩髎) — 肩 (Jiān, shoulder) + 髎 (Liáo,
bone cavity/foramen/depression). 髎 denotes a depression in a
bony landmark — here, the depression posteroinferior to the
lateral acromion when the arm is abducted. Located on the
posterolateral aspect of the shoulder, in the depression
posterior and inferior to the acromion.

TE14 is the PRIMARY shoulder point on the TE channel — the
Triple Burner's contribution to the shoulder joint coverage.
Three arm Yang channels each provide a major shoulder point
from their designated spatial territory:
- **LI15 (Jianyu):** anterolateral shoulder, anterior deltoid
  insertion, acromioclavicular region
- **TE14 (Jianliao):** posterolateral shoulder, posterior to
  acromion, infraspinatus/supraspinatus region
- **SI9 (Jianzhen):** posterior-inferior shoulder, posterior
  axillary fold, teres minor/subscapularis region

Together, these three points ring the shoulder joint from
anterior, posterolateral, and posterior positions — the same
spatial distribution pattern documented at the elbow (Obs. 10)
now repeated at the shoulder.

TE14 has no special category designation (not Five Shu, Luo,
Xi-Cleft, Yuan, Meeting, or Window of Heaven). It is a regular channel point
whose clinical importance derives entirely from its anatomical
position at the shoulder joint.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): with the arm abducted, in
the depression posterior and inferior to the lateral end of the
acromion.

Anatomical landmarks: abduct the arm to ~90°. Two depressions
appear on the shoulder:
- **Anterior depression:** LI15 (Jianyu) — between the anterior
  acromion and the greater tubercle, at the anterior deltoid
  origin
- **Posterior depression:** TE14 (Jianliao) — posterior and
  inferior to the lateral acromion

TE14 is directly over the junction between the supraspinatus
(superior) and infraspinatus (posterior) muscles, at the
posterolateral shoulder joint capsule. The subacromial bursa is
immediately superior.

Spatial relationships:
- LI15 is anterior at the same joint — on opposite sides of the
  acromion
- TE13 is 3 cun distal (deltoid-triceps junction, upper arm)
- TE15 is medial-superior (suprascapular fossa, upper trapezius)
- SI9 is posteroinferior (posterior axillary fold)

Needle depth: 15–25 mm perpendicular or oblique (inferiorly
directed). With arm abducted, the subacromial space opens
and allows access to the periarticular structures.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Suprascapular nerve (C5-C6):** motor to supraspinatus and
  infraspinatus. After passing through the suprascapular notch,
  it crosses the spine of the scapula to reach the infraspinous
  fossa. Articular branches supply the posterior shoulder joint
  capsule. At TE14, the suprascapular nerve's articular branches
  are immediately deep.
- **Axillary nerve (C5-C6):** motor to deltoid (posterior fibers
  at this level) and teres minor. The axillary nerve emerges
  from the quadrilateral space inferior to the joint capsule.
- Superior lateral brachial cutaneous nerve (terminal branch of
  axillary nerve) — cutaneous supply over the lateral deltoid.
- Spinal segments: C5-C6 (Neural Zone 5: shoulder)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural Zone 5 (shoulder):** TE14 is squarely in Zone 5
(suprascapular + axillary nerves, C5-C6) — the narrowest
segmental range of any TE neural zone. Both primary nerves share
the same spinal segments, creating a focused segmental input
that contrasts with the broad C5-T1 range of Zone 4 (upper arm).

Fascial plane: deltoid fascia → subacromial bursa → rotator
cuff tendons (supraspinatus/infraspinatus). TE14 sits at the
fascial boundary between the deltoid envelope and the rotator
cuff layer.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: posterior circumflex humeral artery (from axillary
artery, via quadrilateral space) supplies the posterior shoulder.
Suprascapular artery (from thyrocervical trunk) accompanies the
suprascapular nerve. Both are moderate-caliber vessels.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Deltoid (posterior fibers)
- Subacromial bursa
- Supraspinatus / infraspinatus tendons (rotator cuff)
- Shoulder joint capsule (deepest)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No special category designation. Regular channel point. TE14's
clinical significance derives from its anatomical position
rather than any traditional category membership.

**Three arm Yang shoulder points — spatial distribution:**
| Channel | Shoulder Point | Position | Rotator Cuff Access |
|---------|---------------|----------|-------------------|
| LI (Yangming) | LI15 | Anterolateral | Supraspinatus tendon (anterior) |
| **TE (Shaoyang)** | **TE14** | **Posterolateral** | **Supraspinatus-infraspinatus junction** |
| SI (Taiyang) | SI9 | Posterior-inferior | Teres minor, subscapularis (posterior) |

This parallels the Yang Elbow Trifecta (Obs. 10): at BOTH
major arm joints (elbow and shoulder), three arm Yang channels
distribute around the joint from their designated spatial
territories. Yangming = anterolateral, Shaoyang = posterolateral,
Taiyang = posterior.

The rotator cuff mapping is clinically relevant:
- Supraspinatus tears (most common) → LI15 access
- Infraspinatus pathology → TE14 access
- Teres minor / posterior capsule → SI9 access
The traditional shoulder point selection provides direct
rotator-cuff-component-specific access.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE14 Jianliao (肩髎). Functions: dispels
Wind-Damp from the shoulder joint, activates the channel,
benefits the shoulder joint. Indications: shoulder pain
(PRIMARY), inability to raise the arm, heaviness of the
shoulder, arm pain.

**2. Trigger point:** **Infraspinatus TrP** — the
infraspinatus muscle has its primary TrP directly beneath TE14.
Infraspinatus TrP referral: deep anterior shoulder pain,
anterolateral arm. This is one of the best TrP-acupoint
correspondences in the shoulder region. 🟢
→ Cross-ref: B11

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Strongly reactive in rotator cuff
tendinopathy, subacromial impingement, and frozen shoulder. The
posterolateral shoulder depression is one of the most common Ashi
sites in clinical practice. 🟢

**4. Marma point:** **Amsa Marma** (अंस, shoulder junction) —
one of 107 Marma points. Classified as Snayu Marma (ligament/
tendon type). The Amsa Marma encompasses the shoulder top region.
TE14 corresponds to the posterolateral aspect. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: C04

**5. Head zone:** C5-C6 dermatome — matches the suprascapular
nerve territory (C5-C6). Segmental overlap with shoulder joint
innervation.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Spiral Line (SPL) — the infraspinatus/
posterior rotator cuff connects into the Spiral Line's posterior
shoulder component. Also: Deep Back Arm Line (DBAL) at the
shoulder junction.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No specific data for
posterior shoulder.

**8. Cupping point:** Shoulder is convex — cup placement is
difficult but possible with smaller cups. Used in clinical
practice for shoulder Wind-Damp conditions. 🟡

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM, TrP strong, Ashi strong,
Marma, Cupping). HIGHEST convergence on the TE channel — the
posterolateral shoulder has strong cross-tradition recognition as
a treatment site for shoulder pain.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: shoulder pain (肩痛), inability to raise the arm
  (肩不举), heaviness of the shoulder, arm pain.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: shoulder pain, shoulder joint stiffness,
  arm pain, lateral shoulder pain radiating down.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Benefits shoulder (PRIMARY):** shoulder pain, frozen
  shoulder, inability to raise the arm, shoulder heaviness
- **Activates channel:** arm pain, lateral arm pain
- **Dispels Wind-Damp:** shoulder joint conditions with damp-
  heavy quality

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- TE14 is a primary LOCAL point for posterolateral shoulder
  pain. It is almost always combined with other shoulder points
  — most commonly LI15 (anterolateral) and SI9 or SI11
  (posterior) for comprehensive shoulder joint coverage.
- The three-point combination LI15+TE14+SI9 provides access
  from three angles, covering all major rotator cuff
  components — a protocol basis for rotator cuff pathology.
- Also combined with TE5 (Luo-Connecting, distal TE point)
  for channel-specific distal-proximal pairing.
- In frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis), TE14 is one of
  the "four shoulder needles" (肩四针) — typically LI15+TE14+
  SI9+SI10 or variations.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No RCT for TE14 as an individual point. TE14 appears in
virtually ALL acupuncture shoulder pain protocols — included
alongside LI15 and SI9/SI10 in systematic reviews of frozen
shoulder (Ben-Arie et al. 2020) and rotator cuff tendinopathy.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated (as individual point).

**Protocol inclusion pattern:** Like LI15 (AP-02 Obs. 27),
TE14 demonstrates the "protocol inclusion without isolation"
paradox: frequently included in positive shoulder pain trials,
never tested alone. The combination evidence suggests that
multi-point shoulder protocols are effective, but the
individual contribution of TE14 is unknown.

**Shoulder evidence comparison:**
| Point | Inclusion Frequency | Individual Evidence |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|
| LI15 | Highest (most included) | 🔴 never isolated |
| TE14 | High (2nd most included) | 🔴 never isolated |
| SI9 | Moderate | 🔴 never isolated |

The three primary shoulder points share the SAME evidence gap:
universally included, never individually tested.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No disease-state sensitization data for TE14. The
posterolateral shoulder depression becomes tender in rotator
cuff tendinopathy and subacromial impingement — strong clinical
Ashi reactivity. Whether TE14 demonstrates point-specific
sensitization (vs general periarticular tenderness) in shoulder
pathology is unstudied.

The subacromial bursa is a potential sensitization substrate:
bursitis produces localized tenderness at exactly the TE14
location. This clinical observation is consistent with the
dynamic acupoint hypothesis but has not been confirmed using
the formal methodology (Kim 2017, Chen Rixin).
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Local effects (shoulder pain):** directly explicable. TE14
needling stimulates the suprascapular nerve's articular
branches (C5-C6) → segmental gate control at C5-C6 dorsal
horn. The infraspinatus TrP deactivation mechanism is also
relevant: needling into a hypertonic infraspinatus muscle can
produce a local twitch response, reducing myofascial tension
and referred pain.

**Rotator cuff component-specific mechanism:** TE14's posterior
position accesses the infraspinatus and posterior supraspinatus
— muscles involved in external rotation and shoulder
stabilization. Pathology of these muscles (tendinopathy,
impingement, tear) produces pain at exactly the TE14 location.
The traditional point placement maps to the anatomical site of
maximal pathological tenderness in posterior rotator cuff
disease.

**Comparison with LI15:** LI15 accesses the anterior
supraspinatus tendon at its insertion on the greater tubercle
(the most common site of rotator cuff tears). TE14 accesses the
supraspinatus-infraspinatus junction. Different aspects of the
same pathology → different traditional channel assignments
(Yangming vs Shaoyang) for what is often the same clinical
condition (shoulder pain).

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 15–25 mm perpendicular or oblique (inferiorly).
**Arm must be abducted to ~90°** to open the posterolateral
subacromial depression. With the arm at the side, the deltoid
covers the depression and the space narrows. De qi: deep ache
in the shoulder joint, may radiate to the lateral arm. If the
infraspinatus TrP is engaged, a local twitch response may
occur.

Moxa: applicable. Indirect moxa for chronic shoulder conditions
with Wind-Cold-Damp pattern (heavy, stiff, weather-sensitive
shoulder). Commonly used in clinical practice.

Acupressure: palpate the posterolateral shoulder depression with
the arm slightly abducted. Deep pressure with thumb. Self-
accessible with contralateral hand.

### An toàn

**MODERATE risk.**

**Shoulder joint capsule:** deep needling (>25 mm, directed
medially) can penetrate the posterior shoulder joint capsule.
Risks: septic arthritis (sterility-critical), intra-articular
hemorrhage, cartilage damage. At standard depth and angle, the
rotator cuff tendons provide a buffer.

**Subacromial bursa:** TE14 overlies the subacromial bursa.
Repeated needling can cause bursitis or irritate an existing
bursitis. Risk mitigated by avoiding the superior subacromial
space and using moderate depth.

**Suprascapular nerve:** the articular branches of the
suprascapular nerve are in the vicinity. Direct nerve
stimulation causes deep shoulder ache (often interpreted as de
qi). Sustained stimulation or EA at high intensity may cause
nerve irritation.

**Vascular:** posterior circumflex humeral artery branches —
moderate caliber. Hematoma risk is higher at the shoulder than
on the arm due to looser connective tissue and greater arterial
supply.

**NOT pneumothorax risk:** the lung apex is medial and
anterior (behind the clavicle). TE14 is lateral and posterior
— no lung tissue accessible at standard depth and angle.

## TE15 — Thiên Liêu (天髎, Tiān Liáo)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Celestial Bone Hole" (天髎) — 天 (Tiān, celestial/heaven) +
髎 (Liáo, bone cavity/depression — same character as TE14's
second component). The 天-prefix places this point in the
"celestial" (head/neck/upper body) zone, at a bony depression
on the suprascapular region. Located midway between GB21
(Jianjing) and SI13 (Quyuan), on the superior angle of the
scapula.

TE15 is a **Meeting Point (交会穴)** of three pathways: the
**Triple Burner channel**, **Gallbladder channel (GB)**, and
**Yang Wei Mai** (Yang Linking Vessel). This triple meeting is
more widely accepted in classical and modern references than
the disputed TE13–Yang Wei Mai meeting, making TE15 the primary
Shaoyang junction point on the upper trunk.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): in the suprascapular fossa,
midway between GB21 (Jianjing) and SI13 (Quyuan), at the
superior angle of the scapula.

Anatomical landmarks: on the upper trapezius, at the superior
angle of the scapula. GB21 is at the midpoint between C7 and
the acromion (center of the upper trapezius). SI13 is at the
medial end of the supraspinous fossa. TE15 lies between them.

Spatial relationships:
- TE14 is lateral-inferior (posterolateral shoulder)
- TE16 is superior-anterior (posterolateral neck)
- GB21 is lateral (upper trapezius, same general region)
- SI13 is medial (medial supraspinous fossa)

Needle depth: 10–20 mm perpendicular or oblique (posteriorly).
AVOID deep medial-anterior-directed needling (pneumothorax
risk).

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Accessory nerve (CN XI):** motor to trapezius. At TE15's
  level, CN XI is within the trapezius or at its deep surface.
- Suprascapular nerve (C5-C6): deeper, beneath the trapezius,
  entering the supraspinous fossa via the suprascapular notch.
- Dorsal scapular nerve (C5): to rhomboids, passes medially.
- Cutaneous: supraclavicular nerves (C3-C4).
- Spinal segments: C3-C6 (Zone 5-6 transition)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: trapezius fascia (superficial) → supraspinous
fascia (deep) → supraspinatus muscle.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: transverse cervical artery (superficial branch) and
suprascapular artery — both in the vicinity.

Muscle layers:
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Trapezius (upper fibers)
- Supraspinatus (deep to trapezius)
- Scapula (bony floor) at the superior angle

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Meeting Point (交会穴): TE + GB + Yang Wei Mai**

This is the most widely accepted Yang Wei Mai Meeting Point on
the TE channel (vs disputed TE13). The triple meeting creates a
**Shaoyang junction** — both Shaoyang channels (Hand = TE,
Foot = GB) converge here along with Yang Wei Mai:
- TE5: Eight Confluent (OPENS Yang Wei Mai)
- TE15: Meeting (TE + GB + Yang Wei Mai cross together)
- GB21: adjacent but NOT a Yang Wei Mai point — yet functionally
  connected due to proximity

The TE-GB-Yang Wei Mai convergence at the suprascapular region
reinforces the Shaoyang system's role in the shoulder/lateral
trunk area.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE15 Tianliao (天髎). Functions: dispels
Wind-Damp, activates the channel, relaxes sinews. Indications:
shoulder/neck pain, stiffness, heaviness, arm inability to
raise.

**2. Trigger point:** Upper trapezius TrP zone — the upper
trapezius has its primary TrP near this region (GB21-TE15
area). Referral: temple, posterior neck, shoulder. Strong
anatomical overlap. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in neck/shoulder tension,
trapezius strain, cervicogenic headache. Common Ashi site. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** **Amsa Marma** (shoulder junction) —
encompasses the superior scapular region. Weaker correspondence
than at TE14. 🔴

**5. Head zone:** C3-C6 dermatome.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line → trapezius component.

**7. Neurogenic spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Upper trapezius/suprascapular area — good
cupping surface, commonly cupped for shoulder tension. 🟡

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: shoulder/neck pain, stiffness, arm pain, arm
  inability to raise.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: shoulder heaviness, neck stiffness, arm pain.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Dispels Wind-Damp:** shoulder/neck pain, heaviness, stiffness
- **Activates channel:** arm pain, inability to raise arm
- **Neck stiffness**

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

🔴 Unvalidated for all indications. No studies isolating TE15.

### An toàn

**MODERATE risk.**

**Pneumothorax:** the lung apex (pleural dome) rises 2–3 cm
above the first rib behind the medial clavicle. At TE15's
location (superior scapular angle), deep needling directed
medially and anteriorly can potentially reach the pleural dome.
The scapula provides a partial bony barrier posteriorly, but the
superior angle is where the scapula is narrowest, offering less
protection than the scapular body.

**Safety protocol:** needle perpendicular to the skin surface
or oblique-posteriorly (tangential to the chest wall). NEVER
angle the needle anteriorly or medially toward the lung apex.

**Accessory nerve (CN XI):** at or near the deep surface of the
trapezius. Deep needling may stimulate CN XI (transient shoulder
shrug), but injury risk is low with standard technique.

## TE16 — Thiên Dũ (天牖, Tiān Yǒu)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Celestial Window" (天牖) — 天 (Tiān, celestial/heaven) + 牖
(Yǒu, window — specifically a window in a wall, an opening for
light and air). TE16 is the **EPONYMOUS Window of Heaven
point** — the point whose name IS the category name. The
category 天牖穴 (Window of Heaven points) takes its name from
this very point, making TE16 the prototype or "name-giver" of
the entire ~10-point Window of Heaven group. Of all Window of Heaven points, only TE16
bears the EXACT category name (天牖); the others use variants
like 天窗 (SI16, celestial window-pane), 天府 (LU3, celestial
storehouse), 天突 (CV22, celestial prominence), 天柱 (BL10,
celestial pillar), 天容 (SI17, celestial appearance).

TE16 is listed in **Ling Shu Chapter 2** as one of the ORIGINAL
Window of Heaven points. It is the THIRD Window of Heaven point documented
in the Atlas (after LI18 and SI16) and the only Window of Heaven point on
the TE channel. Located on the posterolateral neck, posterior
and inferior to the mastoid process, at the posterior border of
the SCM, at the level of the mandibular angle.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the posterolateral
aspect of the neck, at the posterior border of the SCM, at the
level of the angle of the mandible.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the angle of the mandible. At
this level, find the posterior border of the SCM. TE16 is in
the depression behind the SCM, posterior and inferior to the
mastoid process. The SCM's insertion on the mastoid is
approximately 1–2 cun superior.

Spatial relationships:
- TE15 is inferior-posterior (suprascapular region)
- TE17 (Yifeng) is superior (directly behind the earlobe, at
  the stylomastoid foramen) — 1 cun above
- SI17 (Tianrong) is anterior-inferior (below the mandibular
  angle, anterior to the SCM)
- SI16 (Tianchuang) is inferior on the same SCM border
  (at laryngeal prominence level)
- LI18 (Futu) is anterior (between SCM heads, also at
  laryngeal level)

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular, shallow. Deep cervical
structures at risk.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Lesser occipital nerve (C2):** ascending along the posterior
  border of the SCM. Sensory to the skin behind the ear and
  lateral scalp. At TE16's level, the lesser occipital nerve is
  superficially palpable.
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3):** ascending from Erb's point
  (at the posterior border of the SCM, mid-neck) toward the ear.
  Sensory to the external ear, earlobe, and periauricular skin.
  At TE16, the great auricular nerve's ascending trunk is in
  the immediate vicinity.
- **Accessory nerve (CN XI):** motor to SCM and trapezius. At
  this level, CN XI is within or deep to the SCM. Injury risk
  is lower here than at more inferior points (SI16 level) where
  CN XI crosses the posterior triangle superficially.
- Deep: **internal jugular vein** (behind the SCM at this
  level), **internal carotid artery** (deep and medial, at
  C1-C2 level it is anterior to the transverse processes).
  **Vagus nerve (CN X)** within the carotid sheath.
- Spinal segments: C2-C3 (Neural Zone 6: neck)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural Zone 6 (neck):** TE16 transitions from the shoulder
(Zone 5: suprascapular/axillary) to the cervical plexus (Zone
6: lesser occipital/great auricular, C2-C3). This is the same
cervical plexus territory as SI16 and LI18, but accessed from
a HIGHER position on the neck. The cervical plexus provides
cutaneous supply to the neck, ear, and lateral scalp — matching
TE16's traditional ear/throat indications.

Fascial plane: investing layer of deep cervical fascia →
posterior SCM border → prevertebral fascia (deep).
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular:
- **External jugular vein:** crosses the SCM superficially,
  visible in many patients. At TE16's level, the EJV may be
  immediately superficial. ALWAYS identify and avoid.
- **Internal jugular vein:** deep to SCM, within the carotid
  sheath. At the mandibular angle level (C1-C2), the IJV is
  large-caliber (near the jugular bulb superiorly).
- **Internal carotid artery:** deep and medial — deeper than at
  LI18 level due to the higher position. Less risk than LI18
  but still present.
- **Occipital artery:** branch of external carotid, runs
  posteriorly behind the SCM. May be encountered at depth.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → platysma → investing fascia
- SCM (posterior border — needling behind the muscle)
- Splenius capitis and levator scapulae (deep to SCM at this
  level)
- Prevertebral muscles (longus capitis) and transverse
  processes of C1-C2 at depth

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Window of Heaven (天牖穴) — Eponymous Point**

Classical source: **Ling Shu Chapter 2 (本输 Běn Shū)** — one
of the ORIGINAL Window of Heaven points. TE16 is listed
alongside ST9, LI18, BL10, and LU3 in the Ling Shu Ch.21
compilation.

TE16 is unique among Window of Heaven points: its name (天牖) IS the
category name. The Window of Heaven category in classical literature is
called 天牖五部 (Five Regions of the Celestial Window) in some
Ling Shu interpretations, taking its name from THIS point. This
makes TE16 the conceptual prototype of the entire group.

**TE16 in the cervical ring (AP-02 LI18 Obs. 26):**
**POSTEROLATERAL** position — behind the SCM, at the mandibular
angle level. This completes a three-point arm-Yang arc around
the lateral neck:

| Channel | Window of Heaven Point | Ring Position | SCM Relationship | Level |
|---------|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| LI (Yangming) | LI18 | Anterolateral | BETWEEN SCM heads | Laryngeal prominence (C3-C4) |
| SI (Taiyang) | SI16 | Lateral | POSTERIOR border | Laryngeal prominence (C3-C4) |
| **TE (Shaoyang)** | **TE16** | **Posterolateral** | **BEHIND SCM** | **Mandibular angle (C1-C2)** |

Three arm Yang channels provide graduated access around the
SCM:
- LI18 passes THROUGH the muscle (between its two heads)
- SI16 sits AT the muscle's posterior edge
- TE16 is entirely BEHIND the muscle, at a higher level

**TE16's vertical offset:** TE16 is at the mandibular angle
(C1-C2), approximately 2 vertebral levels ABOVE LI18 and SI16
(both at laryngeal prominence, C3-C4). This vertical offset
matches the TE channel's ascending trajectory toward the ear
and temporal region. The three Window of Heaven points are NOT at the same
horizontal level — they form a RISING ARC from anterior-
inferior (LI18) to posterolateral-superior (TE16).

**Window of Heaven points documented in Atlas (3/~10):**
- ✅ LI18 (Futu) — anterolateral (AP-02)
- ✅ SI16 (Tianchuang) — lateral (AP-06)
- ✅ **TE16 (Tianyou) — posterolateral** (this entry)
- ⬜ 7 remaining: ST9, SI17, BL10, LU3, CV22, GV16, PC1

**Channel distribution of Window of Heaven points:**
- SI: 2 points (SI16 + SI17) — MOST from any channel
- All others: 1 each (LU3, LI18, ST9, BL10, TE16, CV22, GV16)
- PC1 included in some traditions only

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE16 Tianyou (天牖). Functions: benefits
the ears (PRIMARY), brightens the eyes, clears the head,
activates the channel. Window of Heaven function: treats
conditions where Qi fails to ascend — sudden deafness, sudden
loss of vision, disorientation. Indications: deafness, tinnitus,
blurred vision, facial edema, sudden loss of vision, neck
stiffness, headache, dizziness.

**2. Trigger point:** SCM posterior border/splenius capitis TrP
area. At the mandibular angle level, the splenius capitis
underlies the SCM; its TrP refers pain to the vertex of the
head. Moderate anatomical overlap. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Reactive in cervicogenic headache,
neck rotation restriction, posterior auricular pain. Tender
when the splenius capitis or SCM is taut. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No strong specific correspondence. Possible
weak relation to **Krikatika Marma** (कृकाटिक, neck junction/
atlanto-occipital) — but Krikatika is more posterior/midline. 🔴

**5. Head zone:** C2-C3 dermatome. Cervical viscerotome overlap
with ear/throat structures — same segmental justification as
SI16.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line → SCM component (posterior
border). At TE16's level, the SCM is transitioning toward its
mastoid insertion — the superior anchor of the Lateral Line's
cervical segment.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — cervical region with
major vascular structures. Cupping contraindicated on the
lateral neck.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi). LOWER than LI18
and SI16 (both 4/8 — with Marma correspondence). The
posterolateral neck behind the SCM has fewer cross-tradition
reference points than the anterolateral (LI18) or lateral
(SI16) positions.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Ling Shu Ch.2 (Ben Shu): listed as Window of Heaven point for
  conditions of ascending Qi failure, sensory disturbances.
- Ling Shu Ch.21: included in Window of Heaven compilation.
- Jiayi Jing: sudden deafness (暴聋), blurred vision (目不明),
  facial edema (面肿), sudden headache, dizziness, neck
  stiffness.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: deafness, tinnitus, blurred/sudden vision
  loss, headache, dizziness, night blindness, nasal congestion,
  facial swelling.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Benefits ears (PRIMARY):** sudden deafness, tinnitus — same
  ear emphasis as SI16, reflecting the great auricular nerve
  (C2-C3) shared territory
- **Brightens eyes:** blurred vision, sudden vision loss, night
  blindness — unique among the 3 documented Window of Heaven points (LI18
  is throat-dominant, SI16 is ear-dominant, TE16 adds VISION)
- **Window of Heaven function:** disorientation, dizziness,
  sudden onset sensory changes
- **Activates channel:** neck stiffness, headache

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- TE16 is selected for EAR + VISION conditions — its combined
  auditory-visual indication profile distinguishes it from SI16
  (ear-dominant) and LI18 (throat-dominant) among the documented
  Window of Heaven points.
- Combined with TE17 (Yifeng, behind earlobe) and TE3
  (Zhongzhu, hand) for tinnitus/deafness — a proximal-distal
  TE channel combination.
- Combined with GB20 (Fengchi, suboccipital) for dizziness and
  cervicogenic headache.
- The vision indications (blurred vision, night blindness) are
  UNIQUE to TE16 among the 3 documented Window of Heaven points. This may
  reflect the TE channel's trajectory toward the lateral orbit
  (TE23 at the lateral eyebrow) — the channel "opens" both ear
  and eye portals on its way to the temporal/orbital terminus.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for TE16 as an individual point.
The Window of Heaven group as a whole has not been
systematically evaluated in controlled studies.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

**Window of Heaven evidence desert (3/3 documented):** TE16 joins LI18 and
SI16 with zero individual evidence for Window of Heaven-specific functions
(ascending Qi regulation, consciousness/sensory restoration).
The Window of Heaven concept remains the largest evidence gap between
traditional importance and modern evidence in the Atlas — a
coherent classical system with a plausible anatomical rationale
(cervical ring at the body-head bottleneck) but ZERO controlled
validation.

**Observation 11 — Three Arm Yang Window of Heaven Arc:**

With TE16 documented, three of ~10 Window of Heaven points are
in the Atlas — one from each arm Yang channel:

| Channel | Window of Heaven | Position | Level | SCM Access |
|---------|-----|----------|-------|------------|
| LI (Yangming) | LI18 | Anterolateral | C3-C4 | Between heads |
| SI (Taiyang) | SI16 | Lateral | C3-C4 | Posterior border |
| TE (Shaoyang) | TE16 | Posterolateral | C1-C2 | Behind SCM |

The three arm Yang channels provide a graduated arc around the
SCM: LI18 accesses the carotid sheath from WITHIN the muscle,
SI16 from the muscle's EDGE, and TE16 from BEHIND the muscle.
This mirrors the Yang Elbow Trifecta (Obs. 10) — at every
major anatomical landmark (elbow, shoulder, neck), the three
arm Yang channels maintain spatial distribution:
- Yangming (LI): anterolateral at all levels
- Taiyang (SI): lateral/medial-posterior at all levels
- Shaoyang (TE): posterolateral at all levels

TE16's VERTICAL OFFSET is unique: it sits 2 vertebral levels
higher (C1-C2 vs C3-C4) than LI18 and SI16. This matches the
TE channel's ascending trajectory toward the ear/temporal
region, while LI and SI remain at the laryngeal level before
branching. The three Window of Heaven points form a RISING ARC, not a
horizontal ring — anterior-inferior (LI18) → lateral (SI16) →
posterolateral-superior (TE16).

**Window of Heaven SPECIALIZATION by channel:**
- LI18: THROAT dominant (sore throat, voice loss, goiter —
  laryngeal proximity, superior laryngeal nerve access)
- SI16: EAR dominant (sudden deafness, tinnitus — great
  auricular nerve, ear referral)
- TE16: EAR + VISION (deafness AND blurred vision, night
  blindness — combined auditory-visual)

Each Window of Heaven point's dominant indication matches its channel's
traditional domain: LI (face/throat), SI (ear/sensory), TE
(ear + eye — the channel reaches BOTH sensory organs on its
path to TE23 at the lateral eyebrow). The Window of Heaven group is not
functionally homogeneous — each point specializes within the
broader Window of Heaven theme, and the specialization follows the
channel's trajectory.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No sensitization data for TE16. The posterolateral neck behind
the SCM becomes tender in cervicogenic headache, posterior
auricular pain, and acute torticollis — clinical Ashi
reactivity. Whether TE16 sensitizes during EAR or VISION
pathology (as its traditional indications suggest) is
unstudied.

The Window of Heaven cervical ring as a whole may demonstrate disease-state
sensitization: neck points becoming reactive during
consciousness disturbances, vertigo, or ascending Qi conditions.
This has never been systematically investigated.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Ear indications (segmentally explicable):** The great
auricular nerve (C2-C3) provides sensory supply to the external
ear. The lesser occipital nerve (C2) supplies the posterior
auricular region. Needling at TE16 stimulates C2-C3 afferents →
dorsal horn → gate control for ear-referred pain. Same segmental
mechanism as SI16 (also C2-C3, also ear-dominant).

**Vision indications (non-segmental, speculative):** C2-C3
afferents have no direct connection to the visual system. The
vision effects claimed for TE16 require supraspinal pathways —
either via trigeminal-cervical convergence at the trigeminocervical
nucleus (C2-C3 afferents converge with CN V afferents, and CN V
supplies the orbit) or through vagal afferent modulation (the
vagus nerve in the nearby carotid sheath projects to the NTS →
widespread brainstem/cortical effects). Both pathways are
theoretical for TE16.

**Window of Heaven — cervical ring mechanism:** TE16's position
behind the SCM provides access to the carotid sheath from the
posterolateral angle. The Window of Heaven cervical ring hypothesis (AP-02
Obs. 26) proposes that the ~10 Window of Heaven points modulate ascending
flow at the body-head bottleneck. TE16's contribution:
- Vagal access (CN X in carotid sheath) from the posterolateral
  angle — complementing LI18's anterolateral access
- Great auricular nerve (C2-C3) → cervical-brainstem relay
- Vertebral artery at depth (C1-C2 level) — potential
  (undemonstrated) influence on posterior cerebral circulation

**Gap:** no mechanism study specific to TE16. The Window of Heaven group as
a whole lacks mechanism investigation. TE16's combined ear +
vision indication profile is the most multi-sensory of the
three documented Window of Heaven points but has the least anatomical
justification for the vision component.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 10–15 mm perpendicular, shallow. Insert at the
posterior border of the SCM at the mandibular angle level,
directing the needle into the space behind the muscle. De qi:
local distension, may radiate to the ear or posterior scalp
(lesser occipital/great auricular nerve engagement). AVOID
deep insertion toward the midline.

Moxa: indirect moxa applicable for chronic neck/ear conditions
(Wind-Cold pattern). Direct moxa inappropriate (thin neck skin,
vascular proximity).

Acupressure: palpate behind the SCM at the mandibular angle
level. Moderate pressure with thumb. Self-accessible by turning
the head to expose the posterior SCM border.

### An toàn

**HIGH risk** — same risk category as LI18 and SI16. The FIRST
HIGH-risk point on the TE channel. All three documented Window of Heaven
points are HIGH risk — inherent to cervical ring architecture.

**Vascular structures:**
- **External jugular vein:** superficial, visible in many
  patients. At the mandibular angle level, the EJV may cross
  directly over or near TE16. ALWAYS identify and avoid before
  needling.
- **Internal jugular vein:** deep to SCM, large-caliber at this
  level (near the jugular bulb). Deep insertion risk: venous
  puncture, hematoma, potential air embolism.
- **Internal carotid artery:** deep and medial, within the
  carotid sheath. Less accessible from the posterolateral
  approach (TE16) than from the anterolateral approach (LI18),
  but still present at depth.
- **Occipital artery:** branch of the external carotid, runs
  posteriorly behind the SCM. May be encountered.

**Neural structures:**
- **Vagus nerve (CN X):** within the carotid sheath, deep.
  Direct stimulation → bradycardia, syncope.
- **Lesser occipital nerve (C2):** superficial at the posterior
  SCM border. Brief paresthesia to the posterior ear/scalp is
  common and generally harmless.
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3):** ascending along SCM.
  Paresthesia to the ear may occur.

**Safety protocol:**
- Identify the external jugular vein BEFORE needling — avoid
- Shallow insertion (10–15 mm maximum)
- NEVER direct the needle anteriorly toward the carotid sheath
- Palpate for the carotid pulse — needle POSTERIOR to it
- Same contraindications as LI18: carotid artery disease,
  carotid sinus hypersensitivity, anticoagulant therapy

**Comparison with other documented Window of Heaven points:**
| Window of Heaven Point | Risk Level | Primary Risk |
|-----------|------------|-------------|
| LI18 | HIGH | Carotid sheath (between SCM heads, thinnest coverage) |
| SI16 | HIGH | EJV superficial, carotid deep, CN XI in posterior triangle |
| TE16 | HIGH | EJV superficial, IJV/ICA deep, behind SCM at high level |

All three documented Window of Heaven points are HIGH risk — the cervical
ring is the body's most vascular/neural-dense region. HIGH-risk
is inherent to Window of Heaven architecture, not incidental.

## TE17 — Ế Phong (翳风, Yì Fēng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Wind Screen" (翳风) — 翳 (Yì, screen/shield/hidden behind) +
风 (Fēng, wind). The earlobe "screens" from wind — TE17 lies
in the sheltered depression directly behind the earlobe, at
the junction between the lobule and the mastoid process.
Classical interpretation: a point that both treats Wind
conditions (facial paralysis = "Wind strikes the channels"
风中经络) and is physically shielded by the auricle. The Wind
association is clinically dominant — TE17 is THE primary point
for facial paralysis (Bell's palsy), rivaling only LI4 and
ST36 for inclusion frequency in facial nerve protocols.

TE17 is positioned at the **stylomastoid foramen** — the exit
point of the facial nerve (CN VII) main trunk from the temporal
bone. This is the most anatomically precise cranial nerve motor
access in the Atlas: no other documented point sits at a cranial
motor nerve's skull exit with such precision. The facial nerve
emerges from this foramen as an undivided trunk (~10,000 motor
fibers, 2–3 mm diameter), immediately before branching into its
five terminal divisions (temporal, zygomatic, buccal, marginal
mandibular, cervical) within the parotid gland. TE17 therefore
accesses CN VII at its most proximal extracranial point — before
any branching — making it the single point with the broadest
potential influence on facial motor function.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): behind the earlobe, in the
depression between the angle of the mandible and the mastoid
process.

Anatomical landmarks: with the patient's head slightly turned
to the opposite side, palpate behind the earlobe. The depression
is bounded by the ramus of the mandible anteriorly, the mastoid
process posteriorly, and the ear lobule superiorly. The
stylomastoid foramen lies deep within this depression, at the
base of the mastoid process.

Spatial relationships:
- TE16 (Tianyou, Window of Heaven) is inferior (posterolateral neck, behind
  SCM at mandibular angle level)
- TE18 (Chimai) is posterosuperior (on the mastoid surface)
- SI17 (Tianrong) is anteroinferior (anterior SCM border at
  mandibular angle)
- GB12 (Wangu) is posteroinferior (behind and below the mastoid
  tip)

Needle depth: 10–20 mm perpendicular or slightly anteriorly
directed. The stylomastoid foramen is approximately 15–25 mm
deep from the skin surface. Standard acupuncture depth (10–15
mm) stays ABOVE the foramen level in most patients; deeper
insertion approaches the CN VII trunk directly.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Facial nerve (CN VII) — main trunk:** exits the stylomastoid
  foramen at the depth of TE17. The trunk is 2–3 mm in diameter
  at this point — the thickest concentration of facial motor
  fibers at any accessible location. After emerging, CN VII
  enters the parotid gland and branches into 5 terminal
  divisions: temporal (forehead), zygomatic (eyes), buccal
  (cheeks), marginal mandibular (lower lip), cervical
  (platysma). All five divisions are accessible at TE17 via
  the undivided trunk.
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3):** ascending toward the ear
  from Erb's point. Sensory to the earlobe and periauricular
  skin. Same nerve accessed at TE16, SI16, and SI17 —
  continuous cervical plexus territory.
- **Lesser occipital nerve (C2):** cutaneous to the posterior
  auricular region.
- **Posterior auricular nerve:** branch of CN VII (exits
  immediately after the main trunk emerges). Motor to the
  posterior auricular muscle and occipitalis.
- Spinal segments: C2-C3 (cervical plexus sensory) + CN VII
  (cranial motor)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural Zone 7 (mastoid/periauricular):** TE17 marks the
transition from Zone 6 (neck: cervical plexus C2-C3, lesser
occipital + great auricular) to Zone 7 (mastoid: CN VII + great
auricular). The defining feature of Zone 7 is the addition of
CN VII motor fibers to the cervical plexus sensory territory —
the first zone on the TE channel where a CRANIAL MOTOR NERVE is
the primary clinical target.

Fascial plane: parotid fascia (extension of investing cervical
fascia) → parotid gland parenchyma (deep). The stylomastoid
foramen and CN VII trunk are deep to the parotid gland's
posterosuperior surface.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular:
- **Posterior auricular artery:** branch of the external carotid,
  ascending behind the ear along the mastoid. Palpable in some
  patients.
- **Stylomastoid artery:** small branch supplying the facial
  nerve itself — accompanies CN VII at the foramen.
- Retromandibular vein: within the parotid gland, deep to TE17.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Posterior auricular muscle fibers
- Parotid gland (posterosuperior surface)
- Stylomastoid foramen + CN VII trunk (deepest)
- Mastoid process periosteum (lateral bony limit)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No special category designation (not Five Shu, Yuan-Source,
Luo, Xi-Cleft, Eight Confluent, Hui-Meeting, or Window of
Heaven). TE17's clinical significance derives entirely from
its ANATOMICAL POSITION at the CN VII exit — the most
functionally important non-category point on the TE channel.

**Facial paralysis protocol role:**
TE17 is the anchor point in the standard acupuncture facial
paralysis protocol: ST4 + ST6 + SI18 + **TE17** + LI4 + ST36.
Within this protocol, TE17 occupies a unique structural
position:
- ST4, ST6, SI18: local facial points over CN VII TERMINAL
  BRANCHES (buccal, marginal mandibular, zygomatic)
- LI4, ST36: distal points (analgesic, immunomodulatory)
- **TE17: the only point targeting the CN VII MAIN TRUNK** —
  accessing the entire motor nerve at its origin rather than
  individual branches

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE17 Yifeng (翳风). Functions: expels
Wind (PRIMARY), benefits the ears, resolves swelling, activates
the channel. Indications: facial paralysis (口眼歪斜, mouth-eye
deviation), tinnitus, deafness, parotitis (mumps), lockjaw,
cheek swelling, toothache.

**2. Trigger point:** SCM insertion zone — the SCM's mastoid
insertion is adjacent to TE17. The SCM TrP at the mastoid
refers pain to the forehead, temple, and ear. Additionally,
the posterior belly of the digastric muscle (deep to TE17) has
a TrP that refers to the mastoid tip. Moderate correspondence.
🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Strongly reactive in acute Bell's
palsy — tender behind the ear at onset. Retro-auricular pain
is a recognized prodromal symptom of Bell's palsy, reflecting
CN VII inflammation within the temporal bone before the nerve
exits the stylomastoid foramen. Also tender in parotitis
(mumps), mastoiditis, and TMJ dysfunction with posterior
referral. 🟢

**4. Marma point:** **Vidhura Marma (विधुर)** — one of 107
Marma points. Located below/behind the ear, near the mastoid
process. Classified as Snayu Marma (ligament/tendon type).
TE17 is a STRONGER Vidhura correspondence than SI19 (noted as
PARTIAL in AP-06 because SI19 is anterior to the ear). TE17's
position behind the earlobe at the mastoid matches Vidhura's
described location more precisely. Vidhura is classified as a
vital point where injury causes deafness — matching TE17's
auditory and CN VII indications. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: C04

**5. Head zone:** C2-C3 dermatome (great auricular nerve
territory). Cranial nerve motor territory (CN VII) overlaps —
the surface is cervical plexus, the depth is cranial motor.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line → SCM mastoid insertion.
The posterosuperior SCM attachment is the superior anchor of
the Lateral Line's cervical segment.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No specific data for the
retro-auricular depression.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — bony depression with
major nerve at depth. Surface area insufficient for cup
placement.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi strong, Marma).
Second-highest convergence on the TE channel after TE14 (5/8).
The retro-auricular depression is recognized across traditions
as a clinically significant site, with Vidhura Marma providing
the strongest non-TCM correspondence.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: mouth-eye deviation (口眼歪斜, facial paralysis),
  tinnitus (耳鸣), deafness (耳聋), lockjaw (牙关紧闭), cheek
  swelling.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: facial paralysis (PRIMARY — listed first),
  parotitis (腮肿, mumps), deafness, tinnitus, toothache,
  facial Wind conditions.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Expels Wind / facial paralysis (PRIMARY):** Bell's palsy,
  post-stroke facial deviation. THE most important acupoint
  for facial nerve conditions — universally included in every
  acupuncture facial paralysis protocol.
- **Benefits ears:** tinnitus, deafness (sudden or chronic).
  Shares auditory emphasis with TE3 (distal), TE16 (Window of Heaven), and
  the pre-auricular triad (SI19 + TE21 + GB2).
- **Resolves swelling:** parotitis (mumps — parotid gland
  directly anterior to TE17), submandibular swelling, scrofula
  (cervical lymphadenopathy).
- **Activates channel:** lockjaw, toothache, TMJ dysfunction.

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- TE17 is **THE anchor point** for facial paralysis treatment.
  In the standard protocol (ST4 + ST6 + SI18 + TE17 + LI4 +
  ST36), TE17 is the only point accessing the CN VII main trunk
  — all other facial points target terminal branches.
- Bell's palsy staging: TE17 is used throughout all stages —
  (1) acute stage: gentle stimulation, NO EA at TE17 to avoid
  further CN VII irritation; (2) recovery stage: moderate
  stimulation, EA typically between ST4 and ST6 (not across
  TE17); (3) sequelae stage: stronger stimulation for residual
  weakness.
- Combined with TE3 (Zhongzhu, distal TE point) as channel-
  specific proximal-distal pair for ear conditions.
- Combined with GB20 (Fengchi) and GB12 (Wangu) for Wind-Cold
  invasion behind the ear (acute retro-auricular pain).
- In parotitis (mumps), TE17 is the primary local point — the
  parotid gland lies directly anterior, making TE17 both the
  nearest acupoint and the most anatomically relevant.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**Facial paralysis (Bell's palsy):**
- Systematic review and meta-analysis (Li et al. 2015,
  Medicine): acupuncture for Bell's palsy. TE17 identified as
  one of the most frequently included points across protocols.
  Combined treatment showed favorable outcomes vs medication
  alone. Evidence quality: LOW-MODERATE (heterogeneity). TE17
  not individually isolated. 🟡
- Cochrane review (Chen et al. 2010): acupuncture for Bell's
  palsy. Insufficient evidence to conclude for or against
  acupuncture. TE17 included in most protocols but study
  quality was low. 🟡

**Tinnitus:**
- TE17 appears in some tinnitus protocols alongside the pre-
  auricular triad (SI19 + TE21 + GB2) but less frequently than
  SI19. When included, typically combined with TE3 and GB2. No
  study isolating TE17 for tinnitus. 🔴

**Parotitis:**
- Case series in Chinese-language journals report favorable
  outcomes for parotitis protocols including TE17. No English-
  language RCT. 🔴

Overall evidence: **🟡 Plausible** for facial paralysis (as
part of multi-point protocols). TE17 is the MOST CONSISTENTLY
INCLUDED point in Bell's palsy acupuncture trials — even more
consistently included than LI4, reflecting its anatomical logic
(CN VII access). However, the same "protocol inclusion without
isolation" pattern persists: no study has evaluated TE17 alone
despite it being the most anatomically justified point for
CN VII modulation.

**Observation 12 — Cranial Motor Nerve Exit Access at TE17:**

TE17 accesses the facial nerve (CN VII) at the stylomastoid
foramen — the nerve's skull exit. This represents a unique
level of nerve access in the Atlas. Three levels of point-nerve
relationship are documented:

| Level | Description | Examples |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 1. Course | Point along the nerve's peripheral course | LI4 (digital), TE5 (PIN), SI8 (ulnar tunnel) |
| 2. Pre-branch | Point at a trunk before it divides | LI11 (radial before PIN/superficial split) |
| **3. Skull exit** | **Point at cranial nerve's osseous foramen** | **TE17 (CN VII at stylomastoid)** |

Level 3 is anatomically distinct: the stylomastoid foramen is a
bony channel from which CN VII has no escape. The nerve is
maximally vulnerable here — compressed between bone (foramen
walls) and any external force (including needle). This creates
a dual nature at TE17:

**Therapeutic maximum:** accessing the entire motor supply to
ALL facial muscles via a single undivided trunk. No other point
modulates a complete cranial motor nerve at its origin.

**Risk maximum:** any nerve damage at trunk level affects ALL
five divisions (forehead, eyes, cheeks, lips, platysma).
Compare with facial points (ST4, ST6, SI18) that access
individual CN VII terminal branches — damage to one branch
affects one facial region.

In the standard Bell's palsy protocol:
- ST4 + ST6 + SI18 → BRANCH-level access (selective, lower risk)
- **TE17 → TRUNK-level access (comprehensive, higher risk)**
- LI4 + ST36 → distal systemic points

TE17 is the "master switch" — the protocol's trunk-level point
complemented by branch-level facial points for selective
stimulation. This trunk-vs-branch architecture is unique among
documented point protocols.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No formal sensitization data using the dynamic acupoint
methodology (Chen Rixin, Kim 2017). However, the retro-
auricular depression at TE17 becomes spontaneously tender during
acute Bell's palsy — retro-auricular pain is a recognized
prodromal symptom, reflecting CN VII inflammation within the
temporal bone before the nerve exits the stylomastoid foramen.

This clinical observation may represent the most explicit
example of disease-state point sensitization in the tradition:
the disease (CN VII inflammation) announces itself by making
the anatomically corresponding acupoint (TE17) tender — often
hours to days before the motor deficit appears. The temporal
sequence (pain at TE17 → facial weakness) suggests that the
nerve inflammation propagates from the intratemporal segment
to the extracranial exit point at TE17's depth. Whether TE17
specifically (vs the general retro-auricular area) shows
enhanced sensitivity has not been formally tested.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Facial paralysis (LOCAL — directly explicable):**
TE17 needling at the stylomastoid foramen stimulates the CN VII
main trunk at its exit. Proposed mechanisms:
1. **Anti-inflammatory:** needle-induced local anti-inflammatory
   effects (axon reflex, CGRP release) at the site of CN VII
   edema. Bell's palsy pathology is CN VII swelling within the
   fallopian canal — stimulation at the nerve exit may reduce
   perineural edema in the immediately extracranial segment.
2. **Neurotrophic:** repeated stimulation may promote nerve
   regeneration (BDNF, NGF release). Animal studies of EA at
   peripheral nerve injury sites show enhanced regeneration,
   though not specifically at TE17.
3. **Blood flow:** local vasodilation (posterior auricular artery,
   stylomastoid artery) improving vascular supply to CN VII.

This is the most mechanistically straightforward indication on
the TE channel: point AT motor nerve exit → treats motor nerve
dysfunction. No segmental gap, no supraspinal pathway required.

**Tinnitus/deafness (LOCAL-SEGMENTAL):**
Great auricular nerve (C2-C3) provides sensory supply to the
external ear. C2-C3 afferent stimulation → same segmental
auditory modulation as at SI16 and TE16. Additionally, CN VII
carries sensory fibers (nervus intermedius) to part of the
external ear — CN VII stimulation at TE17 may activate this
secondary auditory-relevant pathway.

**Parotitis (LOCAL):**
The parotid gland lies directly anterior to TE17. Needling
stimulates local anti-inflammatory and vascular responses in
the immediate vicinity of the inflamed gland.

**Gap:** no mechanism study specific to TE17. The key
unanswered question: does trunk-level access (TE17) provide a
therapeutic advantage over branch-level access (ST4, ST6,
SI18) for facial paralysis recovery? The anatomical logic
strongly predicts yes, but no comparative study exists.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 10–20 mm perpendicular or slightly anteriorly
directed. Insert into the depression behind the earlobe,
between the mastoid process and the mandibular ramus. De qi:
distension behind the ear, may radiate to the face or ear
canal. A sensation radiating to specific facial regions
(forehead, cheek, lip) suggests CN VII trunk stimulation.

**Acute Bell's palsy caution:** in the acute inflammatory
stage (first 1–2 weeks), aggressive stimulation at TE17 is
controversial. Some practitioners avoid deep needling and EA at
TE17 during the acute phase to prevent exacerbating CN VII
irritation. Others use gentle superficial needling. The staging
approach (gentle → moderate → stronger) reflects concern about
nerve inflammation at the compression site.

EA: when used, bipolar EA is typically placed between facial
points (ST4–ST6, ST6–SI18) rather than across TE17 — avoiding
electrical stimulation at the CN VII trunk level.

Moxa: indirect moxa (stick) for chronic facial paralysis with
Wind-Cold pattern. Used in the sequelae stage. Avoid direct
moxa (thin skin, nerve proximity).

Acupressure: palpate the depression behind the earlobe.
Self-accessible by reaching behind the ear. Moderate pressure.
Used in self-care for tinnitus and post-Bell's palsy
rehabilitation.

### An toàn

**HIGH risk** — the second HIGH-risk point on the TE channel
(after TE16). Fourth consecutive elevated-risk point in the
ascending cervical-periauricular sequence (TE14 MODERATE →
TE15 MODERATE → TE16 HIGH → TE17 HIGH).

**Facial nerve (CN VII) — PRIMARY RISK:**
The CN VII main trunk exits the stylomastoid foramen at TE17's
depth. Risks:
- **Motor nerve injury:** deep needling directly into the CN VII
  trunk could cause neuropraxia (temporary conduction block)
  or axonotmesis (prolonged injury). Symptom: ipsilateral facial
  weakness — exactly the condition TE17 treats. The therapeutic
  target IS the safety hazard.
- **Facial nerve stimulation:** needle contact with CN VII
  produces involuntary facial twitching — a sign of trunk-level
  needle placement. If controlled, this is therapeutic; if
  uncontrolled or aggressive, it risks nerve damage.
- **EA risk:** EA at TE17 with high intensity may cause
  sustained CN VII stimulation → muscle fatigue, aberrant
  regeneration, or synkinesis (unwanted motor coupling during
  recovery).

**Parotid gland:** the posterosuperior surface lies anterior to
TE17. Needle passes through or near glandular tissue —
generally well tolerated. Infection risk if sterile technique
is compromised.

**Posterior auricular artery:** branch of the external carotid,
ascending behind the ear. Moderate caliber. Hematoma possible
but generally self-limiting.

**Deep structures:** beyond the stylomastoid foramen, deep
medial insertion could approach the styloid process area (CN IX,
X, XI near the jugular foramen) — but standard acupuncture
depths (10–20 mm) do not reach these structures.

**Safety protocol:**
- MODERATE depth only (10–15 mm for safety)
- If involuntary facial twitching occurs: note it (trunk
  contact), avoid aggressive manipulation
- In acute Bell's palsy: gentle stimulation, NO EA at TE17
- Palpate for posterior auricular artery pulsation — avoid
- Monitor for facial asymmetry after treatment

## TE18 — Khiết Mạch (瘈脉, Chì Mài)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Spasm Vessel" (瘈脉) — 瘈 (Chì, spasm/convulsion/twitching) +
脉 (Mài, vessel/pulse). The name references the point's
traditional indication for infantile convulsions (瘈疭, twitching
and spasms). Located behind the ear, at the center of the
mastoid process, directly posterior to the mid-auricle. TE18 is
on the mastoid surface — the bony prominence behind the ear —
one position above TE17 in the ascending periauricular sequence.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): behind the ear, at the
center of the mastoid process, at the junction of the upper 2/3
and lower 1/3 of the curve formed by TE17 below and TE20 above.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the mastoid process behind the
auricle. TE18 is at the center of the mastoid surface. TE17 is
inferior (retro-auricular depression at stylomastoid foramen).
TE19 is superior (upper mastoid border). TE20 is anterosuperior
(above ear apex).

Needle depth: 3–5 mm oblique (subcutaneous along the mastoid
surface) or transverse. The mastoid process is solid bone
beneath a thin layer of periosteum — perpendicular deep
insertion is impossible. Some sources recommend bleeding the
superficial vein behind the ear at this location for febrile
conditions.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Posterior auricular nerve:** motor branch of CN VII (already
  separated from the main trunk at TE17). Motor to the posterior
  auricular muscle and occipitalis.
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3):** sensory to the posterior
  auricular skin and mastoid region.
- **Lesser occipital nerve (C2):** sensory to the posterior
  auricular/lateral scalp.
- Spinal segments: C2-C3 + CN VII motor branch
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural Zone 7 (continued):** second point in Zone 7. Same
neural composition as TE17 (CN VII + great auricular C2-C3) but
at the BRANCH level — the posterior auricular nerve is already a
separated branch, making TE18 a less concentrated neural target
than TE17's trunk-level access.

Vascular: posterior auricular artery (ascending along mastoid).
Superficial veins behind the ear (traditional bloodletting
site).

Tissue layers: skin → thin subcutaneous tissue → posterior
auricular muscle → mastoid periosteum → mastoid bone (NO deep
soft tissue — solid bone immediately beneath).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No special category designation. Regular channel point on the
mastoid surface.

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE18 Chimai (瘈脉). Functions: calms Wind
and stops convulsions, benefits the ears. Indications: infantile
convulsions (惊风), tinnitus, deafness, headache (temporal/
posterior), vomiting in children, diarrhea.

**2. Trigger point:** no significant TrP at the mid-mastoid
surface. The posterior auricular area lacks a well-defined TrP
referral pattern. 🔴

**3. Ashi correspondence:** tender in mastoiditis, posterior
auricular lymphadenopathy, and tension headache with posterior
referral. Moderate clinical reactivity. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** regional overlap with Vidhura Marma but
Vidhura is better matched to TE17 (retro-auricular depression).
🔴

**5. Head zone:** C2-C3 dermatome.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** no significant fascial line at the
mastoid surface.

**7. Neurogenic spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — bony mastoid surface.

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi). The mastoid surface
has minimal cross-tradition significance — primarily a TCM-
specific treatment site.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: infantile convulsions (惊风), vomiting, headache,
  deafness.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: infantile convulsions, tinnitus, headache
  behind the ear, diarrhea in children, night crying.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Calms Wind / convulsions (PRIMARY):** infantile convulsions
  (acute febrile seizures), twitching. The point name directly
  references this function.
- **Benefits ears:** tinnitus, deafness — shared auditory
  emphasis across all periauricular TE points (TE16–TE21)
- **Headache:** temporal and posterior headache

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- TE18 is clinically overshadowed by TE17 (CN VII trunk access)
  and the pre-auricular triad (SI19 + TE21 + GB2). Its
  pediatric convulsion indication is rarely used in modern
  practice. The traditional bloodletting technique (pricking
  the superficial vein behind the ear) for febrile conditions
  has historical significance in Chinese pediatric acupuncture.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

🔴 Unvalidated for all indications. No studies isolating TE18.

### An toàn

**LOW risk.** The mastoid bone provides a solid bony floor.

Precautions:
- **Posterior auricular artery:** runs along the mastoid — minor
  bleeding risk with superficial needling. Self-limiting.
- **Mastoid air cells:** the mastoid contains pneumatized air
  cells. In patients with thin mastoid cortex (children,
  post-mastoidectomy), theoretically deep insertion could breach
  the bone, but standard subcutaneous technique is safe.
- **Ear canal proximity:** the external auditory meatus is
  immediately anterior. Needle direction should follow the
  mastoid surface, not anteriorly.

## TE19 — Lư Tức (颅息, Lú Xī)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Skull Rest" (颅息) — 颅 (Lú, skull/cranium) + 息 (Xī, rest/
cease/breath). Interpretations vary: "place where the skull
rests" — implying calming of cranial agitation (headache,
tinnitus, convulsions); or "breathing of the skull" — referencing
the fontanelle area in infants where the skull "breathes"
(fontanelle pulsation, relevant to the infantile convulsion
indication). Located behind the ear, at the upper portion of
the mastoid process, posterior to the upper auricle. TE19
continues the ascending mastoid sequence (TE17 → TE18 → TE19).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): behind the ear, at the
junction of the upper 1/3 and middle 1/3 of the curve from
TE17 to TE20, at the upper border of the mastoid process.

Anatomical landmarks: posterior to the upper portion of the
auricle, at the upper margin of the mastoid where it transitions
to the squamous temporal bone. TE18 is inferior (mid-mastoid).
TE20 is anterosuperior (above ear apex).

Needle depth: 3–5 mm oblique (subcutaneous along the mastoid/
temporal bone surface) or transverse. Same bony constraint as
TE18.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Posterior auricular nerve:** CN VII motor branch — ascending
  from the mastoid toward the occipitalis. Same nerve as TE18.
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3):** upper terminal distribution
  at the posterior auricular region.
- **Lesser occipital nerve (C2):** more prominent at TE19 than
  at TE18 (ascending course toward the scalp).
- Spinal segments: C2-C3 + CN VII branch
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**Neural Zone 7 (continued):** third point in Zone 7, same
neural composition as TE18. The three Zone 7 points (TE17,
TE18, TE19) form the neurally most homogeneous cluster on the
TE channel — same two primary nerves (CN VII branch + great
auricular C2-C3) across three consecutive points on the mastoid
surface.

Vascular: posterior auricular artery terminal branches.
Superficial temporal artery branches at the upper mastoid level.

Tissue layers: skin → thin subcutaneous tissue → auricularis
superior muscle fibers → periosteum → temporal bone
(squamomastoid transition).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No special category designation. Regular channel point.

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE19 Luxi (颅息). Functions: benefits the
ears, calms Wind, stops convulsions. Indications: tinnitus,
deafness, headache, infantile convulsions, ear pain.

**2. Trigger point:** no significant TrP at the upper mastoid.
🔴

**3. Ashi correspondence:** tender in tension headache with
temporal-posterior distribution. Some tenderness in chronic
otitis media (referred to mastoid region). 🟡

**4. Marma point:** no specific correspondence. 🔴

**5. Head zone:** C2-C3 dermatome.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** no significant fascial line.

**7. Neurogenic spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable.

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi). Same minimal
convergence as TE18 — the mastoid surface is not a prominent
site in non-TCM traditions.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: tinnitus, deafness, headache, infantile
  convulsions, ear pain.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: headache, tinnitus, deafness, infantile
  convulsions, fright epilepsy.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Benefits ears:** tinnitus, deafness, ear pain — dominant
  auditory emphasis
- **Calms Wind / convulsions:** infantile convulsions — same
  as TE18
- **Headache:** temporal and posterior headache

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- TE19 is one of the least-used periauricular points in modern
  practice. Indications overlap with TE17 (stronger CN VII
  access), TE18 (same mastoid territory), and the pre-auricular
  triad (stronger auditory evidence). Selected mainly as part
  of a local periauricular cluster rather than individually.

🔴 Unvalidated for all indications. No studies isolating TE19.

bone beneath.

Precautions:

  individuals, particularly near the temporal fossa superiorly
- Subcutaneous technique only — bony floor prevents deep
  complications

## TE20 — Giác Tôn (角孙, Jiǎo Sūn)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Ear Apex Angle" (角孙) — 角 (Jiǎo, angle/horn/corner) + 孙
(Sūn, grandchild/offspring — in meridian theory, 孙脉 means
fine collateral vessel). The 角 refers to the angular projection
of the ear apex (耳尖), where the auricle reaches its highest
point. Located directly above the ear apex — when the ear is
folded forward, TE20 is where the tip touches the scalp.

TE20 is a **Meeting Point (交会穴)** of **Hand Shaoyang (TE)**,
**Hand Taiyang (SI)**, and **Foot Shaoyang (GB)** — the SAME
three channels that meet at SI19 (Tinggong, pre-auricular). The
ear is thus bracketed by two triple-channel meetings of
identical composition: SI19 anteriorly (tragus level) and TE20
superiorly (ear apex), spanning the full height of the auricle.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): directly above the ear apex,
on the hairline, at the point where the ear is folded anteriorly
and the tip touches the scalp.

Anatomical landmarks: fold the ear forward. Where the ear apex
touches the temporal scalp = TE20. On the hairline. TE19 is
posteroinferior (upper mastoid). TE21 is anteroinferior (pre-
auricular, supratragic notch). GB8 (Shuaigu) is in the same
general temporal region above the ear.

Needle depth: 5–10 mm transverse (subcutaneous along the
temporal scalp) or oblique. Temporal bone immediately beneath.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Auriculotemporal nerve (CN V3):** sensory to the temporal
  region and anterior ear. At TE20, this nerve transitions from
  the pre-auricular area to the temporal scalp — the same
  primary nerve as at SI19, now ascending above the ear.
- **Posterior auricular nerve (CN VII branch):** ascending from
  the mastoid — at TE20, this represents the superior limit of
  the posterior auricular motor territory.
- Spinal segments: CN V3 (trigeminal) + C2-C3 (great auricular,
  terminal distribution) + CN VII branch
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

**Neural Zone 7→8 transition:** TE20 marks the shift from
Zone 7 (mastoid: CN VII + great auricular C2-C3) to Zone 8
(pre-auricular/temporal: CN V3 auriculotemporal + CN V1 toward
the orbit). Both CN VII territory (ascending from behind the
ear) and CN V3 territory (ascending from in front of the ear)
overlap at the ear apex — making TE20 a neural transition point.
The dominance shifts from CN VII (motor, posterior) to CN V
(sensory, anterior) as the channel continues to TE21.

Vascular: **superficial temporal artery** (parietal branch) —
the dominant vessel of the temporal scalp. Palpable in many
patients. This artery accompanies the auriculotemporal nerve.

Tissue layers: skin → temporoparietal fascia → temporal fascia →
temporalis muscle → temporal bone.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Meeting Point (交会穴): TE + SI + GB**

The same three channels that meet at SI19:

| Meeting Point | Position | Channel Meeting |
|--------------|----------|-----------------|
| SI19 (Tinggong) | Pre-auricular (tragus level) | SI + TE + GB |
| **TE20 (Jiaosun)** | **Supra-auricular (ear apex)** | **SI + TE + GB** |

Two meetings of the SAME three channels both dedicated to the
ear — unique in the meridian system. The vertical distance
between them (~4 cm) spans the full auricle height. Together,
SI19 and TE20 define the "ear territory" as shared ground of
SI, TE, and GB — reinforcing SI19's Obs. 34 (three-channel
pre-auricular convergence). The pattern: two Shaoyang channels
(TE + GB) converge with one Taiyang channel (SI) at both the
anterior and superior boundaries of the ear.

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE20 Jiaosun (角孙). Functions: clears
Heat, reduces swelling, benefits the ears, brightens the eyes.
Indications: parotitis (腮肿, mumps), toothache (upper and
lower), eye redness (目赤), neck stiffness, ear swelling.

**2. Trigger point:** temporalis muscle TrP — the anterior
temporalis TrP refers pain to the temple and upper teeth.
Partial spatial overlap at the supra-auricular level. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** tender in temporal headache, TMJ-
related temporal pain. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** no specific correspondence at the ear apex.
🔴

**5. Head zone:** CN V3 (mandibular) dermatome —
auriculotemporal territory.

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line → temporalis attachment
zone.

**7. Neurogenic spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — scalp.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi). Moderate
convergence — the temporal-auricular junction has some cross-
tradition recognition through the temporalis TrP.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: parotitis (mumps), toothache, eye redness, neck
  stiffness, gum swelling.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: parotitis, toothache (upper teeth), swollen
  gums, eye redness and pain, neck stiffness.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Clears Heat / resolves swelling (PRIMARY):** parotitis
  (mumps), toothache, gum swelling. The parotid gland extends
  superiorly toward the ear — TE20 accesses its superior pole
  region indirectly.
- **Brightens eyes:** eye redness, blurred vision — likely
  related to CN V3 auriculotemporal → trigeminal nucleus
  connections
- **Benefits ears:** ear swelling, ear pain — consistent with
  the periauricular cluster's shared auditory emphasis

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- TE20 is selected primarily for parotitis (mumps) — combined
  with TE17 (behind earlobe) and ST6 (Jiache, masseter) for
  comprehensive parotid gland access from three angles.
- Toothache: alternative local point for upper dental pain in
  the temporal region. Less commonly used than the primary
  dental points (ST6, ST7, LI4).
- Modern usage is limited — one of the less frequently
  selected periauricular points.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

🔴 Unvalidated for all indications. No studies isolating TE20.

**LOW risk.** Temporal bone beneath provides bony protection.

Precautions:
- **Superficial temporal artery:** palpable in the temporal
  region. Parietal branch ascends nearby. Palpate before
  needling — avoid the pulse. Hematoma risk is the main concern.
- **Scalp vascularity:** the temporal scalp is highly vascular —
  bleeding is common but self-limiting with pressure.
- Subcutaneous technique — bony floor prevents deep
  complications.

## TE21 — Nhĩ Môn (耳门, Ěr Mén)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Ear Gate" (耳门) — 耳 (Ěr, ear) + 门 (Mén, gate/door). The
only TE point with 耳 in its name — directly declaring its
primary function of treating ear conditions. Located anterior to
the ear, at the supratragic notch (above the tragus), in the
depression formed when the mouth is opened. TE21 is the
superior point in the **Three Pre-auricular Points** (耳前三穴):
GB2 (inferior, at intertragic notch), SI19 (middle, at tragus),
TE21 (superior, at supratragic notch) — the standard
acupuncture grouping for auditory conditions, documented in
detail at SI19 (AP-06 Obs. 34).

The three pre-auricular points represent a unique anatomical-
functional convergence: three separate channels (SI, TE, GB),
three separate points within ~2 cm vertical distance, all
accessing the SAME nerve (auriculotemporal, CN V3) and
targeting the SAME organ (ear). TE21 is the TE channel's
dedicated entry in this convergence.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): anterior to the supratragic
notch, in the depression at the posterior border of the
condyloid process of the mandible, formed when the mouth is
opened.

Anatomical landmarks: with the mouth OPEN, palpate above the
tragus. The supratragic notch becomes more apparent. TE21 is
in the depression anterior to this notch, at the posterior
border of the mandibular condyle. SI19 is inferior (~0.5 cun,
at tragus level). GB2 is inferior to SI19 (at intertragic
notch). TE22 is superior (above TE21, at the root of the
zygomatic arch).

Needle depth: 10–15 mm perpendicular, with mouth OPEN (same
requirement as SI19 — condylar translation creates the
treatment space).

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Auriculotemporal nerve (CN V3):** PRIMARY nerve — same nerve
  as SI19. Sensory to the tragus, anterior ear, external
  auditory canal, tympanic membrane (outer surface), TMJ
  capsule, temporal region. TE21 accesses this nerve at a
  slightly more superior position than SI19, above the ear
  canal opening.
- **Temporal branch of facial nerve (CN VII):** motor to
  frontalis and upper orbicularis oculi. Crosses superficially
  over the zygomatic arch. At TE21's level, temporal branches
  are in the vicinity.
- **Great auricular nerve (C2-C3):** terminal distribution to
  the upper ear lobule area. Less prominent here than at
  TE17-TE19 (posterior auricular territory).
- Spinal segments: primarily CN V3 (trigeminal), with C2-C3
  contribution
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

**Neural Zone 8 (pre-auricular/temporal):** TE21 is fully
within Zone 8 — the auriculotemporal nerve (CN V3) dominates.
The transition from Zone 7 (mastoid: CN VII + great auricular)
completed at TE20. Zone 8 extends to TE23 via the orbital
branch of the trigeminal (CN V1).

Fascial plane: parotid fascia (tail of parotid gland) → TMJ
capsule (at depth). Similar tissue plane as SI19.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular:
- **Superficial temporal artery:** the main vessel of the
  temporal region, ascending anterior to the ear. Palpable at
  the root of the zygomatic arch. At TE21's level, the artery
  passes approximately 1 cm anterior.
- Superficial temporal vein: accompanies the artery.
- Deep: maxillary artery in the infratemporal fossa.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Parotid gland (accessory lobe or tail, variable)
- TMJ capsule + condyloid process (mouth closed) or articular
  disc space (mouth open)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No special category designation (not Five Shu, Yuan-Source,
Luo, Xi-Cleft, Eight Confluent, Hui-Meeting).

**Three Pre-auricular Points (耳前三穴):**
TE21 is the superior point in the standard auditory grouping:

| Point | Channel | Position | Landmark |
|-------|---------|----------|----------|
| GB2 (Tinghui) | Foot Shaoyang | Inferior | Intertragic notch |
| SI19 (Tinggong) | Hand Taiyang | Middle | Tragus level |
| **TE21 (Ermen)** | **Hand Shaoyang** | **Superior** | **Supratragic notch** |

All three access the auriculotemporal nerve (CN V3) at slightly
different vertical positions around the tragus. Their functional
differentiation in TCM theory:
- SI19: "Palace of Hearing" — central, most comprehensive
- TE21: "Ear Gate" — opens the ear orifice, removes obstruction
- GB2: "Auditory Convergence" — gathers and converges auditory qi

Whether these functional distinctions correspond to any
anatomical difference is unstudied — the ~2 cm vertical
separation likely accesses the same nerve trunk.

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** TE21 Ermen (耳门). Functions: opens the
ear orifices (通耳窍), benefits hearing, clears Heat.
Indications: tinnitus, deafness (all types), otitis media, ear
discharge (耳中脓), toothache, TMJ dysfunction (lockjaw).

**2. Trigger point:** lateral pterygoid TrP — referred pain to
the TMJ and pre-auricular area. Same TrP correspondence as
SI19. Masseter TrP1 refers to the ear region. Moderate
correspondence. 🟡
→ Cross-ref: B11

**3. Ashi correspondence:** tender in TMJ dysfunction, tinnitus
(acute), pre-auricular lymphadenitis. Same Ashi profile as
SI19. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** no specific pre-auricular Marma. Vidhura is
behind the ear (TE17). 🔴

**5. Head zone:** CN V3 (mandibular) dermatome —
auriculotemporal territory. Direct dermatomal correspondence:
V3 supplies the ear canal and tympanic membrane, and TE21 is
in V3 territory treating ear conditions.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** no significant fascial line involvement.

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** NOT applicable — pre-auricular face.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP, Ashi). The pre-auricular
area is a universal ear treatment site but lacks distinctive
non-TCM correspondence beyond TrP and Ashi.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: tinnitus (耳鸣), deafness (耳聋), ear discharge
  (耳中脓), toothache, TMJ dysfunction.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: tinnitus, deafness (PRIMARY), ear pain,
  lockjaw (牙关紧闭), toothache.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- **Opens the ear / benefits hearing (PRIMARY):** tinnitus,
  deafness (sudden and chronic), otitis media, ear discharge.
  TE21 is the TE channel's primary auditory treatment point —
  paired with SI19 and GB2 in the Three Pre-auricular Points.
- **Benefits TMJ:** lockjaw, TMJ dysfunction — same TMJ benefit
  as SI19 (both access the TMJ capsule via V3).
- **Toothache:** upper dental pain — auriculotemporal nerve
  territory overlap.

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- TE21 is ALWAYS used in combination with SI19 and/or GB2 —
  virtually never as a sole auditory point. The Three Pre-
  auricular Points protocol is the most standard grouping in
  clinical acupuncture for ear conditions.
- Point differentiation in practice: when only ONE ear point is
  used, SI19 is typically preferred (meeting of three channels).
  TE21 is added when the goal is to "open the ear gate" —
  particularly for conditions with ear fullness or blockage.
- Threading technique (透刺): a single needle inserted at TE21
  directed inferiorly through SI19 to GB2 — treating all three
  points with one insertion (Ekofitranto 2026).
- Combined with TE3 (Zhongzhu, distal) and TE17 (Yifeng,
  posterior auricular) for comprehensive TE channel ear
  treatment: proximal (TE17) + local (TE21) + distal (TE3).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**Tinnitus:**
- Systematic review (Kim et al. 2020, 12 RCTs): TE21
  identified as a frequently used acupoint in tinnitus
  protocols alongside SI19 and GB2. TE21 never isolated from
  multi-point combinations. 🟡

**Sudden sensorineural hearing loss (SSNHL):**
- Systematic review (2024, PMC, 12 RCTs): TE21 among the top
  frequently used acupoints. Combined treatment showed higher
  effective rate. TE21 not isolated. 🟡

**TMJ dysfunction:**
- Included in TMJ acupuncture protocols alongside SI19, ST7,
  ST6. No study isolating TE21 for TMJ. 🔴

Overall evidence: **🟡 Plausible** for tinnitus and SSNHL (as
part of the Three Pre-auricular Points). TE21 shares SI19's
evidence profile: always included, never isolated. The
"protocol inclusion without isolation" pattern is especially
acute here because the Three Pre-auricular Points are treated
as an inseparable unit — virtually no trial uses only one.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No formal sensitization data. Clinical Ashi reactivity overlaps
with SI19: the pre-auricular area shows increased tenderness in
TMJ dysfunction and acute ear infections. The specific question
— whether TE21 sensitizes independently from SI19 and GB2, or
whether the entire ~2 cm pre-auricular strip sensitizes as a
unit — has not been studied. Given that all three points access
the same nerve (auriculotemporal, V3), unit sensitization of
the V3 field is more parsimonious than three independent point-
specific responses.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Auditory effects (tinnitus, hearing loss):**
Same mechanism as SI19 — LOCAL trigeminal pathway. The
auriculotemporal nerve (V3) at TE21 directly innervates the
ear canal and tympanic membrane. Needle stimulation activates
V3 afferents → trigeminal nucleus (brainstem) → potential
modulation of auditory processing.

**Functional distinction from SI19:** TE21 accesses the
auriculotemporal nerve ~0.5 cun superior to SI19. Whether this
vertical offset produces functionally distinct neural
activation (different fiber recruitment, different dermatomal
segment) is unknown. The three pre-auricular points may be
THREE NAMES FOR ONE NEURAL TARGET — the auriculotemporal nerve's
pre-auricular trunk — rather than three functionally distinct
points. This is the most challenging test of point specificity
within the acupoint system.

**TMJ effects:**
Same direct mechanical mechanism as SI19. TE21's slightly
superior position may access the superior TMJ capsule and
temporal attachment of the articular disc, compared to SI19's
lateral capsule access at condylar level.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 10–15 mm perpendicular, with MOUTH OPEN (same
requirement as SI19). De qi: fullness or aching radiating into
the ear canal — auriculotemporal nerve stimulation.

EA: bipolar EA between TE21 and SI19 or TE21 and GB2. Used in
tinnitus and SSNHL protocols. Frequency: typically 2 Hz.
Intensity: below comfort threshold.

Threading technique (透刺): single needle from TE21 directed
inferiorly through SI19 toward GB2. Covers all three pre-
auricular points with one insertion. Requires precise
angulation along the vertical pre-auricular line.

Moxa: indirect moxa (stick) for chronic ear conditions with
Cold pattern. Caution: proximity to hair and ear canal.

Acupressure: self-applicable with mouth open — press in the
depression above the tragus. Used in self-care tinnitus
protocols.

### An toàn

**MODERATE risk.** Same risk profile as SI19.

**Superficial temporal artery:** palpable anterior to the ear.
At TE21's level (supratragic), the artery is slightly closer
than at SI19 (tragus level). PALPATE before needling — avoid
the arterial pulse. Hematoma is the most common adverse event.

**Facial nerve (CN VII) temporal branch:** crosses superficially
over the zygomatic arch area. Motor injury → frontalis weakness
(inability to raise the eyebrow). Risk LOW with standard depth.

**External auditory canal:** medial to TE21. Needling directed
medially could enter the ear canal. Direction should be
perpendicular to the skin surface.

**Parotid gland:** tail of the parotid may overlie the pre-
auricular area. Generally well tolerated.

**Safety protocol:**
- MOUTH OPEN during needling
- Palpate superficial temporal artery BEFORE insertion
- Perpendicular insertion — avoid medial angulation
- Moderate depth (10–15 mm)

## TE22 — Hòa Liêu (耳和髎, Ěr Hé Liáo)

marks it as the second TE point with 耳 in its name (after
TE21). TE22 is the highest pre-auricular point — transitioning
from the auricular to the temporal region.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

posterior to the superficial temporal artery.

consecutive TE head points).

Needle depth: 5–10 mm oblique or transverse (subcutaneous).

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

  overlap.
- **Temporal branch of facial nerve (CN VII):** motor branch
  crossing the zygomatic arch. More prominent here than at
  TE21.
- Spinal segments: CN V2/V3 (trigeminal)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

**Neural Zone 8 (continued):** TE22 is in the trigeminal
transition zone where V3 (auriculotemporal, ascending from

zygomatic bone).

Vascular: **superficial temporal artery** — palpable
immediately anterior to TE22. The most prominent arterial
landmark at any pre-auricular TE point.

Tissue layers: skin → temporal fascia → temporalis muscle →
zygomatic arch periosteum.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No special category designation. Regular channel point.

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

the ears, dispels Wind. Indications: tinnitus, lockjaw, heavy
sensation in the head, blurred vision, TMJ dysfunction.

**2. Trigger point:** temporalis TrP — the anterior temporalis
refers pain to the temple and upper teeth. At zygomatic arch
level, the TrP zone overlaps with TE22. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** tender in temporal headache, TMJ-

**4. Marma point:** no specific correspondence. 🔴

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):

## TE23 — Ty Trúc Không (丝竹空, Sī Zhú Kōng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Silk Bamboo Hollow" (丝竹空) — 丝竹 (Sī Zhú, silk bamboo —
metaphor for the fine, thread-like eyebrow hairs resembling
slender bamboo or silk threads) + 空 (Kōng, hollow/empty/
depression). The point is in the bony depression at the lateral
tip of the eyebrow, where the supraorbital ridge forms a
palpable hollow. The name is purely anatomical-descriptive —
naming the physical appearance of the site rather than a
sensory or therapeutic function. This contrasts with the other
two documented Yang channel endpoints: SI19 "Palace of Hearing"
(names auditory function) and LI20 "Welcome Fragrance" (names
olfactory function).

TE23 is the **TERMINAL POINT (止穴)** of the Triple Burner
channel — the final destination of the Hand Shaoyang trajectory
from the ring fingertip (TE1) to the lateral eyebrow. From
TE23, qi flow transitions to **GB1 (Tongziliao 瞳子髎)** at the
lateral canthus of the eye — establishing the **Hand Shaoyang
(TE) → Foot Shaoyang (GB)** same-name continuity.

TE23 has a **CLASSICAL MOXA PROHIBITION (禁灸)** — the fourth
documented moxa-prohibited point in the Atlas (after LU8, LR10,
LR12). The prohibition reflects the eye's proximity: the
orbital rim is immediately inferior, and thermal injury to the
cornea or periorbital tissues has disproportionate consequences.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): in the depression at the
lateral end of the eyebrow.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the lateral tip of the eyebrow.
Follow it to its termination — TE23 is in the bony depression
at the lateral end, on the lateral orbital rim (frontal process
of the zygomatic bone). The orbital cavity is immediately
inferior and medial.

Spatial relationships:
- TE22 is posteroinferior (root of zygomatic arch, ~4–5 cm
  away — the largest gap between consecutive TE points on the
  head)
- GB1 (Tongziliao) is inferior (lateral canthus of the eye —
  the NEXT channel's first point)
- BL2 (Zanzhu 攒竹) is at the MEDIAL end of the eyebrow —
  TE23 and BL2 bracket the eyebrow from opposite ends
- Yuyao (EX-HN4) is at the mid-eyebrow — an extra point
  between TE23 and BL2

Needle depth: 5–10 mm transverse (subcutaneous along the
orbital rim) or oblique. NEVER directed medially or inferiorly
into the orbit.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Zygomaticotemporal nerve (CN V2):** branch of the zygomatic
  nerve (from maxillary division of CN V), emerges through the
  temporal surface of the zygomatic bone. Sensory to the lateral
  temple and lateral orbital region.
- **Supraorbital nerve lateral branch (CN V1):** the frontal
  nerve (from ophthalmic division of CN V) branches into
  supraorbital and supratrochlear nerves. The lateral branch
  extends along the eyebrow toward TE23.
- **Temporal branch of facial nerve (CN VII):** motor to
  frontalis and orbicularis oculi. Superficial to the orbital
  rim at TE23's level.
- **Lacrimal nerve (CN V1):** sensory to the lacrimal gland and
  lateral upper eyelid. Deep, within the orbit.
- Spinal segments: CN V1/V2 (trigeminal ophthalmic and
  maxillary divisions) + CN VII motor
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

**Neural Zone 8 (orbital terminus):** TE23 is the final point
in Zone 8 and the terminal point of the entire TE neural map.
The zone transitions from CN V3 (auriculotemporal at TE21-TE22)
to CN V1/V2 (supraorbital + zygomaticotemporal at TE23). The
complete TE neural zone map:

| Zone | Points | Primary Nerve | Spinal/Cranial |
|------|--------|---------------|----------------|
| 1 | TE1-TE4 | Dorsal digital + superficial radial | C6-C8 |
| 2 | TE5-TE9 | Post. interosseous + post. antebrachial cutaneous | C5-C8 |
| 3 | TE10 | Post. antebrachial cutaneous + ulnar | C7-C8 |
| 4 | TE11-TE13 | Posterior brachial cutaneous | C5-T1 |
| 5 | TE14-TE15 | Suprascapular + axillary | C5-C6 |
| 6 | TE16 | Lesser occipital + great auricular | C2-C3 |
| 7 | TE17-TE19 | Facial (CN VII) + great auricular | C2-C3 + CN VII |
| 8 | TE20-TE23 | Auriculotemporal (V3) → frontal (V1) | CN V |

**8 neural zones across 23 points** — MOST of any channel
(SP and SI tied at 6, LI at 5). The TE channel spans C5-T1
spinal segments + CN V (3 divisions: V1, V2, V3) + CN VII +
CN XI = the broadest neural diversity of any documented channel.

Fascial plane: orbicularis oculi (lateral fibers) → periosteum
of the lateral orbital rim.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: lateral palpebral artery (branch of lacrimal artery
from ophthalmic artery). Small-caliber. Superficial temporal
artery terminal branches in the lateral temple.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (thin)
- Orbicularis oculi (lateral fibers)
- Periosteum of the lateral orbital rim (frontal process of
  zygomatic bone)
- Orbit (deep — NO ENTRY)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Channel terminal point (止穴):** TE23 is the final point on
the Triple Burner channel. From TE23, qi flow transitions to
**GB1 (Tongziliao 瞳子髎)** at the lateral canthus —
establishing the **Hand Shaoyang (TE) → Foot Shaoyang (GB)**
same-name continuity. This completes the documentation of ALL
THREE Yang same-name channel transitions at the face:

| Same-name Pair | Transition | Facial Location |
|---------------|------------|-----------------|
| Yangming | LI20 → ST1 | Nose (nasolabial → infraorbital) |
| Taiyang | SI19 → BL1 | Medial eye (pre-auricular → inner canthus) |
| **Shaoyang** | **TE23 → GB1** | **Lateral eye (eyebrow → lateral canthus)** |

**Naming pattern divergence:**
SI19 "Palace of Hearing" names auditory function. LI20
"Welcome Fragrance" names olfactory function. TE23 "Silk
Bamboo Hollow" names physical appearance (eyebrow). The
divergence may reflect that the EAR — not the eye — is the TE
channel's primary sensory domain. TE23 arrives at the orbital
region geographically (trajectory terminus) but does not claim
the eye as its functional territory.

**Moxa prohibition (禁灸):**

Classical texts prohibit moxibustion at TE23. The 4th documented
moxa prohibition in the Atlas:

| Point | Structure at Risk | Risk Type |
|-------|------------------|-----------|
| LU8 (Jingqu) | Radial artery | Thermal-vascular |
| LR10 (Wuli) | Femoral artery | Thermal-vascular |
| LR12 (Jimai) | External genitalia | Thermal-sensitive tissue |
| **TE23 (Sizhukong)** | **Eye / orbital rim** | **Thermal-ocular** |

Pattern: moxa prohibition = proximity to structures where
THERMAL injury produces disproportionate or irreversible
consequences. Each site involves a different vulnerable
structure type, but all share the principle of thermal risk
exceeding benefit.

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No formal sensitization data. The lateral eyebrow depression
is clinically tender during migraine attacks with temporal-
periorbital distribution — consistent with C-fiber
sensitization in the V1/V2 dermatome during trigeminal
activation. Whether TE23 specifically (vs the general
periorbital area) shows point-specific sensitization is
unstudied.

Eyelid twitching (benign eyelid myokymia) produces palpable
fasciculations at TE23's location — the orbicularis oculi
lateral fibers are directly beneath. This represents motor-
system "sensitization" (increased motor unit firing) rather
than the sensory sensitization typically studied in the dynamic
acupoint paradigm.

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Headache (periorbital/temporal):**
LOCAL trigeminal mechanism — the supraorbital nerve lateral
branch (CN V1) and zygomaticotemporal nerve (CN V2) are both
trigeminal afferents. Needling stimulates V1/V2 afferents →
trigeminal nucleus → gate control for periorbital pain. Same
segmental mechanism as TE5 for migraine, but at the LOCAL level
(TE23 = local periorbital, TE5 = distal forearm accessing the
same trigeminal system via non-segmental pathways).

**Eye conditions:**
CN V1 supplies the eye and periorbital structures. TE23
stimulates V1 afferents at the orbital rim → trigeminal nucleus
→ potential modulation of ocular pain, lacrimation, and
periorbital inflammation via the trigeminovascular reflex.

**Eyelid twitching:**
Direct access to the orbicularis oculi (lateral fibers) and
its motor supply (temporal branch of CN VII). Needling may
reduce motor unit hyperexcitability through mechanical
disruption or local neuroinhibitory effects.

**Gap:** no mechanism study specific to TE23. Whether lateral
(TE23, CN V1 lateral) vs medial (BL2, CN V1 medial) eyebrow
access produces different clinical outcomes is unknown — both
access CN V1 but at different positions along the same nerve
territory.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích

Acupuncture: 5–10 mm transverse (subcutaneous along the
orbital rim, directed medially toward BL2 or laterally toward
the temple). Perpendicular insertion is shallow (5 mm) due to
thin subcutaneous tissue over bone.

**MOXA PROHIBITED (禁灸):** classical consensus. The orbital
rim is immediately below — thermal application risks corneal
injury, periorbital burns, or damage to delicate eyelid skin.
The most directly eye-threatening moxa prohibition in the Atlas
(LU8, LR10, LR12 involve vascular or genital structures, not
sensory organs).

EA: used for eyelid twitching (bipolar with BL2 or Yuyao). Low
intensity only (orbicularis oculi is small and thin). Avoid
high-intensity EA near the globe.

Acupressure: palpate the depression at the lateral eyebrow tip.
Self-accessible. Gentle pressure — the orbital rim is tender.
Used in self-care for temporal headache and eyelid twitching.

### An toàn

**MODERATE risk** — orbital proximity creates a specific risk
profile distinct from other facial points.

**Orbital entry:** the orbit is immediately inferior and medial
to TE23. Needling directed inferiorly or medially at depth
(>10 mm) could enter the orbital cavity. Within the orbit:
the globe, extraocular muscles, optic nerve (CN II), ophthalmic
artery. Orbital entry is the most serious potential complication
— retrobulbar hemorrhage, globe perforation (extremely rare
with proper technique).

**Safety protocol:**
- NEVER direct the needle inferiorly into the orbit
- Transverse or subcutaneous technique ALONG the orbital rim
  (tangential to the bone)
- Shallow depth only (5–10 mm)
- Support the needle with the non-inserting hand to prevent
  depth changes during manipulation

**Moxa prohibition:** NO moxibustion (classical consensus). This
includes direct moxa, indirect moxa, and moxa stick in close
proximity. Thermal risk to the cornea and periorbital tissues
is unacceptable.

**CN VII temporal branch:** crosses superficially. Transient
weakness of the frontalis is possible. Generally self-limiting.

**Periorbital hematoma:** the orbital rim area is prone to
bruising (thin skin, rich vasculature). "Black eye" can occur
from superficial vessel puncture. Cosmetically concerning but
not dangerous. Apply pressure immediately if bleeding occurs.
