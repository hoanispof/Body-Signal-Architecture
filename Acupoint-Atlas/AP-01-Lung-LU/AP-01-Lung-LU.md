>
> **Meridian overview:** The Lung channel (Hand Taiyin) is the FIRST
> meridian in the Qi circulation cycle. It runs from the lateral
> chest below the clavicle (LU1) → anterior axillary fold → medial
> upper arm in the bicipital groove → anterior forearm on the radial
> side → wrist at the radial artery pulse → thenar eminence → radial
> nail corner of the thumb (LU11). 11 points total. Paired Interior-
> Exterior with Large Intestine (Hand Yangming, LI). Peak time in
> Chinese clock: 03:00-05:00. Five Element: Metal (金). Metal governs
> Qi and respiration, controls skin and body hair, opens to the nose.
> Emotion: grief. Season: autumn.

## LU9 — Thái Uyên (太渊, Tài Yuān)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**
TRIPLE point category designation — most at any single point in the
Atlas: Yuan-Source + Shu-Stream/Earth + Hui-Meeting of Vessels. Also
the tonification point of the Lung channel (Earth = mother of Metal
in the Sheng cycle). Pulse diagnosis landmark — LU9 anchors the
entire cun kou (寸口) pulse-taking system, the foundation of TCM
diagnostics since the Nanjing (~100 CE). Despite this extraordinary
theoretical importance, no point-specific RCT exists.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the wrist, at the radial end
of the transverse wrist crease, in the depression between the radial
artery and the tendon of abductor pollicis longus, at the level of
the lower border of the scaphoid bone.

Anatomical landmarks: between the tendon of abductor pollicis longus
(radial border) and flexor carpi radialis tendon (ulnar border). The
point is located by palpating the radial artery pulse at the wrist
crease — LU9 is immediately lateral (radial side) to the pulsation.

Needle depth range: 3-10 mm perpendicular, shallow insertion to the
radial side of the arterial pulsation. Classical protocol (Jiayi
Jing): 0.2 cun, "duration of two breaths" retention — the most
conservative depth/duration protocol in classical literature,
reflecting extreme vascular caution.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply: superficial branch of radial nerve (C6-C7) — primary
sensory innervation at the radial wrist. Lateral antebrachial
cutaneous nerve (C5-C6, terminal branch of musculocutaneous nerve)
contributes in this zone.
Note: palmar cutaneous branch of median nerve runs more ulnar
(closer to PC7/HT7), not confirmed at LU9 specifically.

Fascial plane: between tendon sheaths of abductor pollicis longus
(radial) and flexor carpi radialis (ulnar). Minimal soft tissue
cushioning over distal radius/scaphoid periosteum.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: radial artery directly adjacent — defines the point
location. Superficial venous tributaries toward cephalic vein.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle/tendon layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous fascia
- Tendon of abductor pollicis longus (radial border)
- Flexor carpi radialis tendon (ulnar border)
- Radial artery and vein in the interval
- Periosteum of distal radius/scaphoid (floor)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

TRIPLE designation + functional overlap:

Five Shu: Shu-Stream/Earth (输/土) — third of five transporting
  points on the Lung channel
Yuan-Source: YES — access to Original Qi (Yuan Qi) of Lung
Hui-Meeting: Vessels (脈會太淵) — 1 of 8 Influential Points (八会穴)
  Nanjing Chapter 45: "Blood vessels meet at Taiyuan"
  Theoretical governance over ALL blood vessels
  → Cross-ref: B01 S3.1 (Hui-Meeting system)

TONIFICATION-YUAN FUNCTIONAL OVERLAP:
Earth = mother of Metal in Sheng cycle. Per Nanjing Ch.69 mother-
child rule (虚则补其母), the Earth point on a Metal channel IS the
tonification point. LU9 is simultaneously:
- Tonification point (stimulate to strengthen Lung Qi/Yin)
- Yuan-Source point (access Original Qi)
These converge at one point — not two separate functions.

Systematic pattern across completed channels:
- Liver (Wood): Yuan (LR3 Earth) ≠ tonification (LR8 Water) → separate
- Heart (Fire): Yuan (HT7 Earth) = sedation (child of Fire) → overlap
- Pericardium (Fire): Yuan (PC7 Earth) = sedation → same as Heart
- Lung (Metal): Yuan (LU9 Earth) = tonification (mother of Metal) → overlap
Rule: Yuan-Source always at Shu-Stream (Earth for Yin channels).
Overlap type depends on organ's Sheng-cycle position relative to Earth.

Not Ma Dan-yang Star (that designation belongs to LU7 on the Lung
channel — confirmed by multiple sources).

### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM acupoint: Yuan-Source + Shu-Stream/Earth + Hui-Meeting of
  Vessels. Tonifies Lung Qi/Yin, transforms phlegm, stops cough,
  regulates all blood vessels. Pulse diagnosis landmark. 🟢
Trigger point: APL and FCR tendons both have documented trigger
  points (radial wrist/thumb pain referral) at this level, but no
  direct Dorsher citation confirming LU9 = a specific named
  Travell & Simons TrP. 🔴 plausible, not confirmed
Ashi correspondence: not separately documented — LU9 is a fixed
  anatomical point (pulse landmark), not typically discovered via
  palpation-seeking. 🔴
Marma point: Manibandha Marma (Sandhi/Rujakar type) at wrist
  crease. Broader than LU9 (2-3 cm width, encompasses carpal
  region) but reasonable anatomical correspondence. 🟡
Head zone: C6 dermatome at LU9 location. IMPORTANT MISMATCH: Lung
  viscerotome = T2-T4 (pulmonary/pleural afferents). LU9 (C6) sits
  6-8 segments away from Lung's own visceral referral zone. The
  channel traverses FROM T2-T4 chest (LU1/LU2) OUT TO C6 hand
  (LU9) — crossing the segmental boundary along its course. 🔴
Anatomy Trains: Superficial Front Arm Line (Myers) through
  anterior wrist crease. Broadly consistent but not point-specific.
  🟡
Neurogenic spot: NEGATIVE FINDING. Kim 2017 (Scientific Reports)
  found forelimb neurogenic-inflammatory spots concentrated at
  PC6 (33 spots), PC7 (25), HT7 (30) — 69% of all forelimb spots.
  LU9 and Lung-meridian points NOT among significant clusters.
  First clear negative cross-tradition finding for a major
  acupoint in the Atlas. 🔴
Cupping: not applicable — thin tissue over tendon/artery/bone,
  no surface for cup adhesion. 🔴

Convergence score: 2/8 (TCM + Marma approximate)
Lowest convergence for a TRIPLE-designated point. Evidence-
importance paradox: highest theoretical status in the Atlas,
lowest cross-tradition validation.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Lingshu "Ben Shu" chapter (本输): founding source establishing
  Taiyuan as Lung's Shu-Stream/Earth point — oldest Five Shu
  documentation for the Lung channel
- Nanjing Ch.45: Hui-Meeting of Vessels designation (脈會太淵)
- Ancient literature meta-survey (PubMed 26790213): systematically
  reviewed 1,000 TCM texts, found 333 relevant provisions across
  66 ancient works — 119 single-point uses, 187 combination uses
  - Most frequent combination partners: LU10, LU7, SP3
  - Lung-meridian pairings 17.08%, Bladder-meridian 12.35%
  - Classical protocol: needle 0.2 cun, moxa 3 cones
- Classical indications (Lingshu-descended tradition): cough,
  asthma, coughing blood, lung distension/chest fullness, heart
  pain, rebellious stomach Qi, wind-phlegm, headache, facial
  swelling, eye pain, toothache, shoulder/back/arm pain, elbow
  and wrist pain/weakness

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Tonify Lung Qi/Yin: chronic cough, asthma, weak voice,
  shortness of breath, chronic clear/watery rhinorrhea
- Hui-Meeting of Vessels: palpitations, vascular disorders,
  Raynaud's phenomenon
- Local: wrist pain, thumb weakness

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- Pulse diagnosis point (cun kou 寸口) — primarily diagnostic,
  not therapeutic
- Consistently paired with LU10, LU7, SP3 across 66 classical texts
- LU9 (Earth/Metal) + SP3 (Earth/Earth) = "Earth-strengthening"
  protocol: reflects "Earth generates Metal" (母子) clinical logic
  — Shu-Stream/Earth points of two channels reinforcing the
  generative cycle

PULSE DIAGNOSIS SIGNIFICANCE:
Nanjing (~100 CE) is the decisive text establishing pulse-taking
exclusively at the radial artery (replacing the earlier Neijing-era
three-region whole-body pulse system). The site is called Qi Kou
(气口, "Portal of Qi"), Cun Kou (寸口), or Mai Kou (脉口).

Nanjing's rationale: "Lung governs all vessels" (肺朝百脉) — as the
meeting point of all vessels, the radial pulse at Taiyuan reflects
Qi and Blood in the entire body. Direct textual link between
Hui-Meeting designation and pulse-diagnosis function.

Cun kou spans ~1.9 cun (~3-5 cm) proximal from wrist crease,
subdivided into: cun (寸, distal), guan (关, middle), chi (尺,
proximal). LU9 sits at or near the cun position — anchoring the
entire three-position system, not merely "near" it.

Modern hemodynamic: the radial artery here is superficial, easily
compressible against radius/scaphoid, accessible for three-finger
palpation. Mechanically convenient regardless of TCM theoretical
rationale. No modern vascular-physiology study specifically validates
why this arterial segment carries unique diagnostic information vs
any other palpable superficial artery.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

EVIDENCE DESERT — no LU9-specific systematic review or RCT exists.

Infrared radiation study (PubMed 22876441): 34-37 asthma patients
vs 34 controls. Asthmatics showed significantly lower infrared
intensity at LU9 across multiple wavelengths (left side: 17 vs 4
significant differences). DIAGNOSTIC biomarker correlation, not a
therapeutic trial. 🟡 Plausible as diagnostic correlate

ERP study (PubMed 22237274): manual acupuncture at HT7 or LU9 both
reduced P3a/P3b amplitude in healthy subjects. Point-dependent
differences in degree. Small mechanistic study, not rigorously
sham-controlled. 🟡 Plausible mechanism signal

Therapeutic efficacy for ANY classical indication (cough, asthma,
palpitations, vascular disorders, wrist pain): 🔴 Unvalidated
No point-specific sham-controlled trial identified.

EVIDENCE-IMPORTANCE PARADOX: LU9 has the highest theoretical
importance of any LU point (triple designation, pulse diagnosis
landmark, 333 classical provisions across 66 texts) but zero
point-specific RCTs. Compare: ST36 has BOTH highest evidence AND
highest theoretical importance. LU9 may represent the most extreme
evidence-importance gap in the Atlas.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Infrared radiation study (above): LU9 shows disease-state-dependent
thermal signature change in asthma patients — lower infrared
intensity vs healthy controls. Consistent with dynamic acupoint
sensitization (C04 S4) but measured via passive biomarker rather
than active stimulation threshold change.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems at LU9:
- Nerve: superficial radial nerve (C6-C7). Somato-autonomic reflex
  potential via dorsal horn C6-C7 segments. BUT: lung visceral
  afferents arrive at T2-T4, not C6-C7. No direct segmental pathway
  from LU9 → Lung organ.
- Vascular: radial artery = direct hemodynamic access point.
  Mechanical stimulation near arterial wall could trigger local
  vascular reflex. But no published mechanism links LU9 needling to
  systemic vascular regulation.
- Fascial: inter-tendon interval (APL-FCR) = fascial junction.
  Langevin 2002 model applies, but LU9's fascial architecture is
  not distinctive vs adjacent wrist points.

WHY this location: TCM rationale (肺朝百脉, "Lung governs all
vessels" → vessels converge at Lung's Yuan → radial pulse = window
to all vessels) is elegant theoretical framework with no identified
neurophysiological mechanism.

Gap — 3 major unexplained claims:
1. How does stimulating C6-C7 territory affect T2-T4 pulmonary
   function? (segmental mismatch, 6-8 segment gap)
2. What mechanism underlies "governance over all vessels" from a
   single radial artery point?
3. Why does Kim 2017 find NO neurogenic spots at LU9 when adjacent
   PC6/PC7/HT7 have dense clusters?

Hypothesis (🔴): the neurogenic spot ABSENCE may be informative.
If LU9's significance is primarily VASCULAR (pulse landmark,
Hui-Meeting of Vessels) rather than NEURAL (neurogenic inflammation),
then Kim 2017's negative finding is consistent — LU9 may operate
through a different mechanism track than the neural-dominant wrist
points. This would represent a VASCULAR vs NEURAL mechanism
divergence at anatomically adjacent points.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): shallow perpendicular 3-10 mm, needle placed slightly
  radial to palpated pulsation. De Qi: mild distension at wrist.
  Classical: 0.2 cun, retain "two breaths" — most conservative
  protocol in classical literature.
  EA: not commonly applied at LU9 (artery risk).
Bấm huyệt (B02): gentle pressure at radial artery pulse —
  effectively identical to pulse palpation technique. Distinction
  between "taking the pulse" (diagnostic) and "pressing LU9"
  (therapeutic) is conceptually blurred.
Cứu (B08): moxa applicable per classical protocol (3 cones).
  Caution: thin tissue over artery, burn risk higher than fleshy
  points.
Dry needling (B11): not standard — no documented TrP at exact
  location. Adjacent APL/FCR tendons may be dry-needled for
  tendinopathy, targeting tendon rather than "acupoint."

### An toàn

Vascular risk: MODERATE-HIGH — radial artery directly adjacent.
  Point defined BY the artery's presence. Arterial puncture is
  the primary safety concern.
Safe technique: shallow insertion, radial side of pulsation,
  avoid deep perpendicular needling.
No published acupuncture-specific case report of radial artery
  injury at LU9 — likely reflects practitioner caution rather
  than absence of risk.
Pneumothorax: not applicable (wrist location).
Pregnancy: no specific contraindication documented.
Anticoagulant caution: reasonably inferred from arterial proximity
  though not explicitly stated in reviewed sources (🟡 inference).

## LU7 — Liệt Khuyết (列缺, Liè Quē)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**
QUADRUPLE designation: Luo-Connecting + Eight Confluent (Ren Mai) +
Ma Dan-yang Twelve Heavenly Star Points + Command Point of Head and
Nape. Most clinically prescribed Lung point alongside LU9. Paired
with KI6 (Zhaohai) for Ren Mai + Yin Qiao Mai. Despite strong
classical standing, no RCT isolates LU7 alone — all modern evidence
uses combination protocols.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): 1.5 cun proximal to the
transverse wrist crease, superior to the styloid process of the
radius, between the tendons of brachioradialis and abductor pollicis
longus.

Anatomical landmarks: found by the "dragon-tiger crossed-hands
method" (交叉食指) — cross index fingers and thumbs of both hands;
the point falls in the depression beneath the index fingertip placed
on the radial styloid. In the groove between brachioradialis tendon
(radial border) and abductor pollicis longus tendon (ulnar-distal
border).

Needle depth range: 5-10 mm OBLIQUE insertion directed proximally
toward the elbow. NOT perpendicular — deep perpendicular insertion
risks periosteal contact with radial styloid → sharp pain and
transient wrist weakness.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply: superficial branch of radial nerve (C6-C7) — runs
directly through this area. Lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve
(terminal musculocutaneous branch, C5-C6) also contributes —
documented anatomical overlap between these two nerves in ~41% of
individuals at this level.

Fascial plane: superficial antebrachial fascia over the tendon
compartment. Deep insertion reaches periosteum of radial styloid.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: cephalic vein tributaries superficially. Radial artery
runs more medially/volarly at this level — not directly under the
point (unlike LU9 where the artery defines the location).

Muscle/tendon layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous fascia
- Between brachioradialis tendon (radial) and abductor pollicis
  longus tendon (distal)
- Periosteum of radius at depth (floor)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

QUADRUPLE designation:

Luo-Connecting: Lung Luo vessel separates here, connects to Large
  Intestine (LI) channel. Classical Luo pathway described as a
  "reticular network" extending to the palm — no identified
  anatomical correlate (compare HT5 tongue puzzle, LR5 genital
  puzzle — LU7's Luo is described more modestly: palm/wrist
  symptoms per Ling Shu excess/deficiency signs).
  Host-Guest: LU7 (Luo) + LI4 (Yuan) when LI is host.
  Reverse: LU9 (Yuan) + LI6 (Luo) when LU is host.
  → Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Eight Confluent (八脈交會穴): confluent of Ren Mai (任脈, Conception
  Vessel). Paired with KI6 (Zhaohai) for Ren Mai + Yin Qiao Mai.
  Clinical application: chest, throat, anterior midline +
  gynecological/urogenital conditions.
  → Cross-ref: B01 S3.1 (Eight Confluent system)

Ma Dan-yang Twelve Heavenly Star Points (天星十二穴): indicated for
  unilateral headache and phlegm obstruction in upper body. Lung
  channel representative in the 12 Star Points.
  (LI4 also a Star Point — documented in AP-02 Phase 1 entry.)

Command Point of Head and Nape (四總穴歌, Si Zong Xue Ge):
  头项寻列缺 — "for disorders of head and nape, seek Lieque."
  One of Four Command Points:
  - LU7: head and nape (头项)
  - LI4: face and mouth (面口合谷收)
  - ST36: abdomen (肚腹三里留)
  - BL40: back and lumbar (腰背委中求)
  Later expanded to Six (+PC6 chest, +GV26 emergency).

No Five Shu designation — LU7 is positioned between LU8 (River)
and LU6 (Xi-Cleft) on the forearm but carries Luo-Connecting
function exclusively in the Five Shu framework.

### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM acupoint: releases exterior, dispels wind, promotes Lung Qi
  descent, benefits head/nape, opens Ren Mai, regulates water
  passages, activates channel for pain relief. 🟢
Trigger point: no published 1:1 TrP at LU7. Adjacent
  brachioradialis TrP refers to wrist/thumb web/lateral epicondyle;
  abductor pollicis longus TrP overlaps C6-C8 dermatomes resembling
  de Quervain's pattern (Fernández-de-las-Peñas, PubMed 16034228).
  Anatomical plausibility, not direct correspondence. 🟡
Ashi correspondence: palpable tenderness/nodulation common here in
  wrist overuse conditions (de Quervain's, carpal tunnel referral).
  Functional Ashi overlap. 🟡
Marma point: Manibandha Marma (wrist joint) is nearest but centered
  at radiocarpal joint. LU7 sits BETWEEN Manibandha (wrist) and
  Kurpara (elbow). No Marma precisely at radial styloid. 🔴
Head zone: C6 dermatome (lateral forearm, thumb side). Consistent
  with radial nerve territory. 🟡
Anatomy Trains: Superficial/Deep Front Arm Lines (Myers) —
  brachioradialis and thumb-side compartment part of arm-line
  fascial continuity. Not point-specific. 🟡
Neurogenic spot: Kim 2017 general finding (>90% acupoint
  correspondence) but LU7 not tested individually. 🔴
Cupping: not applicable — narrow tendon/bone ridge, unsuitable
  for cup adhesion. 🔴

Convergence score: 3/8 (TCM + TrP/Ashi approximate + Head zone)

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Ling Shu Ch.10 "Jing Mai": first documentation as Luo-Connecting
  point. Excess: heat and pain in palm/wrist. Deficiency: yawning,
  frequent urination, incontinence.
- Jiayi Jing (Huangfu Mi, ~260 CE): systematized point category
  and indications (referenced via Deadman/JCM secondary literature).
- Ma Dan-yang text: indicated for unilateral headache and phlegm
  obstruction/turbidity in the upper body.
- Four Command Points rhyme (四總穴歌):
  头项寻列缺 — "for head and nape, seek Lieque"

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Exterior: chills/fever, nasal congestion/discharge, nasal polyps,
  sore throat
- Respiratory: cough, wheezing, dyspnea, asthma
- Head/nape: headache, neck stiffness, unilateral headache, lockjaw
- Neurological: hemiplegia, facial deviation, epilepsy, fainting
- Other: toothache, sweating, sudden limb swelling, hypertension

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- LU7 + KI6 = one of the most-taught Eight Confluent combinations
  in TCM education. Addresses: throat obstruction, chest fullness,
  cough, gynecological/urogenital conditions via Ren Mai + Yin
  Qiao Mai.
- LI4 + LU7 = Interior-Exterior Host-Guest pair. Very common for
  exterior wind invasion (common cold/influenza pattern).
- Ren Mai confluent → "regulates water passages": edema, urinary
  irregularities (consistent with Ling Shu Luo deficiency signs).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

COMBINATION-ONLY EVIDENCE: LU7 is almost never studied as a
standalone intervention. All RCT/mechanism data use multi-point
protocols — point-specific attribution impossible.

Cervical spondylosis: active trial NCT07151404 testing LU7 + EA
at Jiaji (Ex-B2) + cervical-shoulder points. n=130, 20-day course.
Registered protocol, no published results. 🔴

Bell's palsy: LU7 in Chinese clinical guidelines for wind-cold
pattern. Broader acupuncture-for-Bell's RCTs exist (Cochrane
CD002914) but do not isolate LU7. 🔴 for LU7 specifically

Migraine/headache: DESPITE being the Command Point of Head/Nape
AND Ma Dan-yang Star Point for headache, NO RCT names LU7 as a
study point in major headache trials. Largest trials used Shaoyang
points (GB20, GB34). The most glaring evidence-tradition gap for
this point. 🔴 (untested, not failed)

Carpal tunnel syndrome: LU7 as one of several local points in CTS
protocols (with LU5, LU9, PC6, PC7, HT7). Overall CTS evidence:
"encouraging but not convincing" (2023 SR). 🔴 combination only

Smoking cessation: LU7 cited as "most commonly used body acupoint"
in umbrella review. Effect attributed to protocol (usually +
auricular points). 🔴

Mechanism studies (all combination-point, small):
- Anti-inflammatory IL-10 modulation (with ST9/CV23/LI4/KI6) 🔴
- Intraoperative analgesic-sparing (with LI11/PC6/LI4) 🔴
- EA myocardial ischemia in rats 🔴
- Lung-injury reduction in pancreatitis model (with LU5/ST36) 🔴

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated at point-specific level.

COMMAND POINT PARADOX: LU7 is one of the Four Command Points —
universally taught in TCM education as the primary point for head
and nape disorders — yet no RCT tests this specific claim. The
Four Command Points rhyme (四總穴歌) may be the most widely taught,
least individually tested clinical guideline in acupuncture.

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems at LU7:
- Nerve: superficial radial nerve (C6-C7). Head/nape indications
  require non-segmental mechanism — C6-C7 has no direct pathway to
  head/nape (C1-C3) or trigeminal (CN V) territories. Possible:
  supraspinal processing via ascending pathways → brainstem pain
  modulation → descending inhibition of cervical/trigeminal neurons.
  Consistent with DNIC framework but speculative (🔴).
- Fascial: inter-tendon interval (brachioradialis-APL). Shallow
  fascial convergence zone. Not distinctive vs adjacent points.

WHY this location for head/nape command: TCM explains via channel
pathway ("Lung channel internal branch ascends to throat, Luo
vessel connects to LI which reaches face/head"). No modern study
demonstrates why C6-C7 stimulation at the radial styloid
specifically benefits head/nape more than other distal points.

Gap — Eight Confluent mechanism: stimulating LU7 (upper limb,
C6-C7) "opens" Ren Mai (anterior midline, T1-S4 segments). The
LU7-KI6 pairing connects C6-C7 to L3-S2, spanning the entire
spinal cord. No neurophysiological mechanism proposed in any source.
The Eight Confluent system remains a TCM-internal theoretical
construct without modern validation.

Luo vessel pathway: classical "reticular network connecting to LI
channel via palm" has no identified anatomical correlate — unlike
some meridian segments where cutaneous nerve courses approximate
channel pathways.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): oblique insertion 5-10 mm directed PROXIMALLY toward
  elbow. NOT perpendicular (periosteal risk). De Qi: mild
  distension radiating along forearm.
  EA: applicable, commonly used in combination protocols.
Bấm huyệt (B02): pressure on depression above radial styloid.
  Accessible for self-acupressure — commonly taught for headache
  self-care.
Cứu (B08): moxa applicable (no specific prohibition, unlike LU8).
Dry needling (B11): brachioradialis/APL trigger points near this
  area may be dry-needled for de Quervain's/lateral epicondyle
  pain — targets muscle, not Luo-Connecting function.

### An toàn

Risk level: LOW — distal, superficial extremity point.
Superficial radial nerve: paresthesia risk with deep or misangled
  insertion. Shallow oblique technique mitigates.
Periosteal contact: perpendicular deep insertion → radial styloid
  → sharp pain, transient wrist weakness.
Vascular: radial artery more medial/volar at this level — not
  directly at risk with standard oblique technique (unlike LU9).
Pregnancy: listed among "traditionally cautioned" points in some
  classical texts, but evidence for actual risk is thin/absent.
  Some clinicians use bilaterally in pregnancy without adverse
  effect (case-level only). TRADITIONAL CAUTION, not confirmed
  contraindication.

**Observation 1: TRIPLE designation at LU9 — Atlas record**
LU9 holds 3 simultaneous point category memberships: Yuan-Source +
Shu-Stream/Earth + Hui-Meeting of Vessels. This is the most
categories at a single point in the entire Atlas. Previous record:
LR13 with 2 (Front-Mu of Spleen + Hui-Meeting of Zang Organs).
Adding the functional tonification role (Earth = mother of Metal),
LU9 carries 4 distinct clinical functions at one anatomical point.

**Observation 2: Tonification-Yuan functional overlap — systematic pattern**
LU9 is simultaneously the tonification point AND the Yuan-Source
point (both Earth). This overlap is NOT unique to Lung — it follows
a systematic rule: Yuan-Source always at Shu-Stream (Earth for Yin
channels), so overlap occurs when the organ's mother element = Earth.
Across completed channels:
- Liver (Wood): no overlap (Yuan ≠ tonification)
- Heart/PC (Fire): Yuan = SEDATION (Earth is child of Fire)
- Lung (Metal): Yuan = TONIFICATION (Earth is mother of Metal)
- Kidney (Water): no direct overlap (Earth ≠ mother or child)
- Spleen (Earth): Yuan = HORARY (Earth is self-element)
Each element position produces a different functional overlap type.

**Observation 3: Evidence-importance paradox — most extreme in Atlas**
LU9 has arguably the highest theoretical importance of any acupoint
(triple designation, pulse diagnosis landmark, 333 classical text
provisions) but ZERO point-specific RCTs. This is the inverse of
ST36 (highest evidence AND high importance). The gap between
classical significance and modern evidence may be the largest in
the entire Atlas — worth tracking as a systematic bias: modern
research may preferentially study points with clear segmental
mechanisms, disadvantaging points whose significance is theoretical
or diagnostic (like LU9's pulse/vessel governance role).

**Observation 4: Neurogenic spot absence — first negative cross-tradition finding**
Kim 2017 found neurogenic-inflammatory spots concentrated at PC6,
PC7, HT7 at the wrist — but NOT at LU9 or any Lung-meridian point.
This is the first clear NEGATIVE cross-tradition finding in the
Atlas (previous channels showed positive or neutral correspondence).
Hypothesis: LU9's significance may be primarily VASCULAR (pulse,
Hui-Meeting of Vessels) rather than NEURAL (neurogenic inflammation),
which would explain the Kim 2017 absence. If correct, this suggests
adjacent wrist points (LU9 vs PC7/HT7) may operate through DIFFERENT
mechanism tracks (vascular vs neural) despite physical proximity
of 1-2 cm. This has implications for the multi-system convergence
zone model (A09 S2.2) — not all convergence zones may use the same
mechanism channel.

**Observation 5: Command Point paradox — most taught, least tested**
LU7 is one of the Four Command Points (四總穴歌) and a Ma Dan-yang
Star Point for headache, yet no RCT has ever tested LU7 specifically
for headache or neck pain — the very indications that define its
clinical identity. The Four Command Points rhyme is arguably the
most universally taught clinical guideline in TCM education, yet
none of its four points (LU7, LI4, ST36, BL40) has been tested
against the SPECIFIC body-region claim the rhyme makes. LI4 has
headache evidence, but headache is NOT its command region (face/mouth
is). This represents a systematic evidence gap: classical mnemonic
guidelines are never tested as stated, even when their individual
points have extensive research for other indications.

**Observation 6: Radial nerve corridor — shared anatomy, divergent function**
LU7 and LU9 share the same primary nerve (superficial radial, C6-C7)
and are only ~1.5 cun (~3 cm) apart, yet carry completely different
designations and functions. LU7 = Luo/Confluent/Command (wind
release, head/nape) vs LU9 = Yuan/Hui-Meeting (tonification, pulse,
vessels). Both have segmental mismatch with Lung viscerotome (T2-T4
vs C6-C7). The fact that two points with nearly identical peripheral
innervation serve radically different clinical functions challenges
any purely peripheral nerve-based mechanism model — suggesting
that either (a) the functional differentiation is a TCM conceptual
overlay on anatomically undifferentiated tissue, or (b) subtle
differences (LU9 = arterial wall contact, LU7 = periosteal/tendon
contact) activate different afferent fiber populations.

## LU5 — Xích Trạch (尺泽, Chǐ Zé)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
He-Sea/Water point of the Lung channel — the SEDATION point of Lung
(Water = child of Metal in the Sheng cycle). Classical bloodletting
point: the cubital fossa veins at LU5 are the same vessels used for
standard medical phlebotomy, making this the first point in the Atlas
where an acupuncture technique is essentially identical to a
conventional Western medical procedure. Located at the elbow crease,
on the radial side of the tendon of biceps brachii. The He-Sea
position marks a neural transition zone where the musculocutaneous
nerve trunk becomes the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve — a
pattern also observed at LR4 and HT3 on their respective channels.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the cubital crease, in the
depression lateral to the tendon of biceps brachii. Found with the
elbow slightly flexed — the biceps tendon becomes prominent at the
center of the cubital crease, and LU5 lies in the depression on its
radial (lateral) side.

Anatomical landmarks: at the radial end of the transverse cubital
crease. The biceps tendon serves as the key landmark — LU5 is
immediately lateral to it. The brachial artery pulsation can be felt
MEDIAL to the tendon, confirming that LU5 (lateral) is on the
opposite side of the tendon from the major artery.

Needle depth range: 15-25 mm perpendicular insertion. Deeper than
distal LU points (LU6-LU11) due to the muscular mass at the cubital
fossa.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve (C5-C6) — terminal sensory
  branch of the musculocutaneous nerve. Emerges between biceps
  brachii and brachialis at the elbow crease. LU5 sits at the
  TRANSITION POINT where the motor-carrying trunk (musculocutaneous)
  becomes the purely sensory terminal branch — the same nerve that
  continues distally to supply LU6 and LU7 territory.
- Radial nerve (C5-T1) passes through the lateral cubital fossa
  deeper, between brachioradialis and brachialis. At this level it
  divides into superficial branch (sensory, continues distally
  toward LU6-LU9) and deep branch (posterior interosseous, motor,
  enters supinator). Needling at depth may engage radial nerve
  branches.
- Spinal segments: C5-C6 (primary), C5-T1 (deep radial nerve)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: deep antebrachial fascia over the cubital fossa.
The bicipital aponeurosis (lacertus fibrosus) — a fascial expansion
from the biceps tendon — crosses medially over the brachial artery
and median nerve. LU5 (lateral to tendon) is at the lateral edge of
this aponeurosis, creating a fascial convergence more significant
than the tendon-interval planes at distal LU points.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular:
- Brachial artery: runs MEDIAL to the biceps tendon — the tendon
  acts as anatomical barrier, protecting the artery from LU5.
  Bifurcates into radial and ulnar arteries at or just distal to
  LU5's level.
- Radial recurrent artery: branch arising from the proximal radial
  artery, ascending laterally through the cubital fossa — may be
  encountered at depth.
- Cephalic vein: superficially lateral — the vein targeted in
  traditional bloodletting technique.
- Median cubital vein: crosses superficially connecting cephalic
  (lateral) and basilic (medial) veins — also accessible for
  bloodletting.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle/tendon layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous fascia → cephalic/median cubital veins
- Brachioradialis (lateral) / bicipital aponeurosis (medial)
- Brachialis muscle (floor of cubital fossa)
- Supinator muscle (deep lateral, radial nerve between layers)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **He-Sea/Water** (合/水) — 5th and final of the Five Shu
transporting points on the Lung channel. He-Sea points are located
at elbow (upper limb) or knee (lower limb), representing the deepest
"river" of Qi along the channel (Nan Jing metaphor: Well=spring,
Spring=brook, Stream=river's bank, River=river, Sea=ocean).

**SEDATION POINT:**
Water is the CHILD of Metal in the Sheng (generating) cycle: Metal
generates Water. Per Nanjing Ch.69 (实则泻其子, "in excess, drain
the child"), the Water point on a Metal channel is the sedation
point — needle LU5 to sedate Lung excess.

Sedation point comparison across completed channels:
- Lung (Metal) → Water child → LU5 **He-Sea** — at the elbow
- Heart (Fire) → Earth child → HT7 **Shu-Stream** — at the wrist
- Pericardium (Fire) → Earth child → PC7 **Shu-Stream** — at the wrist
- Liver (Wood) → Fire child → LR2 **Ying-Spring** — at the toe web
The sedation point falls at a DIFFERENT Five Shu position for each
element. Unlike the tonification-Yuan overlap (Obs. 2), which
follows a consistent Earth rule, the sedation position is
determined purely by the Five Element × Five Shu matrix — no
anatomical pattern.

Note: for Heart and Pericardium, the sedation point (Earth/Shu-
Stream) coincides with the Yuan-Source point (also at Earth/Shu-
Stream) — so HT7 and PC7 are simultaneously Yuan-Source AND
sedation. For Lung, the Yuan-Source (LU9 Earth) and sedation (LU5
Water) are at SEPARATE points. The overlap/separation depends on
whether the child element = Earth.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Five Shu set status: LU5 as He-Sea completes the Lung Five Shu:
LU11 Well/Wood → LU10 Spring/Fire → LU9 Stream/Earth → LU8
River/Metal → **LU5 Sea/Water** = FOURTH complete set in the Atlas
(after PC, HT, LR).

Yuan-Source: No (LU9)
Luo-Connecting: No (LU7)
Xi-Cleft: No (LU6)
Back-Shu: No (BL13 at T3)
Front-Mu: No (LU1)
Eight Confluent: No
Hui-Meeting: No
Ma Dan-yang Star: No (LU7)

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LU5 Chize (尺泽, "Cubit Marsh"). He-Sea/Water
of Lung. Clears Lung Heat, descends rebellious Qi, relaxes sinews
and benefits the elbow joint. The "marsh" (泽) metaphor = a low-lying
basin where water accumulates — the cubital fossa as a topographic
depression matches this imagery. Classical bloodletting point:
three-edged needle (三棱针) puncture of a superficial vein to drain
Blood Heat. 🟢

**2. Trigger point:** Brachioradialis TrP1 (Travell & Simons):
located at the proximal brachioradialis near the lateral epicondyle,
extending toward the cubital crease. Referred pain: lateral
epicondyle → dorsal wrist → thumb web space. Supinator TrP is deeper
at this level. The proximity to LU5 is strong — both target the
radial side of the cubital fossa. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** cubital fossa tenderness is common in
lateral epicondylitis ("tennis elbow"), brachioradialis strain, and
bicipital tendinopathy. LU5 as a local point for elbow/arm pain
functions as an Ashi in many clinical scenarios. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Kurpara Marma (कुर्पर) — elbow joint Marma.
Classified as Sandhi (joint) type in Sushruta Samhita. Size: 3
angula (~5-6 cm), encompassing the cubital fossa. LU5 at the radial
cubital crease is well WITHIN the Kurpara zone — better anatomical
match than LU9/Manibandha because the point and Marma share the
same topographic depression. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** C5-C6 dermatome (lateral cubital fossa). Lung
viscerotome = T2-T4. Segmental gap: 3-4 segments — SMALLER than the
6-8 segment gap at LU9 (C6-C7 vs T2-T4). Moving proximally along
the Lung channel reduces the segmental distance to the organ.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2, S4.4 🔴

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Arm Line (Myers 2001) — biceps
brachii and brachialis are key structures in this myofascial line,
connecting shoulder girdle to forearm flexor compartment. LU5 sits
at a transition point on this line. 🟡

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** not individually confirmed. Kim
2017 reported forelimb neurogenic spot clusters at wrist level
(PC6/PC7/HT7), not at elbow-level points. 🔴

**8. Cupping point:** cubital fossa — flat area lateral to biceps
tendon technically permits cup adhesion, unlike wrist points
(LU7-LU9). Not a traditional cupping site for this point. 🔴

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, TrP approximate, Marma approximate).
Higher convergence than LU9 (2/8) despite fewer point category
designations. Elbow/knee areas consistently show more cross-tradition
overlap than wrist/ankle areas — a pattern also seen in LR8 (5/8 at
knee) vs LR5 (2/8 at mid-leg).

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Lingshu "Ben Shu" chapter: established as He-Sea/Water point of
  the Lung channel
- Nan Jing Ch.68: "He points are for reversed Qi and diarrhea" —
  general He-Sea indication. LU5 as Lung He-Sea: reversed Lung Qi
  (cough, wheezing with rebellious upward flow)
- Nan Jing Ch.69: sedation point logic ("in excess, drain the child")
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing (Huangfu Mi, ~260 CE): cough with dyspnea,
  hemoptysis, chest fullness, fever with restlessness, elbow/arm
  contracture, upper arm pain extending to axilla
- Bloodletting tradition: puncture a superficial vein (三棱针刺血)
  for Lung Fire/Blood Heat. Specific indications: acute sore throat,
  acute tonsillitis, high fever, hemoptysis, acute asthma attack

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Clears Lung Heat: acute cough with thick yellow phlegm, hemoptysis,
  chest oppression, high fever, sore/swollen throat
- Descends rebellious Qi: wheezing, asthma (acute), vomiting,
  hiccup, infantile convulsions
- Benefits the elbow and relaxes sinews: epicondylitis (tennis elbow),
  elbow contracture/pain, upper limb paresis
- Benefits the Bladder (Water point of Metal channel): difficult
  urination, edema — consistent with He-Sea/Water association

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- LU5 + LU6 = He-Sea + Xi-Cleft for acute hemoptysis — different
  point-category mechanisms converging on one pathology. LU5 sedates
  Lung excess (He-Sea/Water drains child), LU6 addresses acute blood
  accumulation (Xi-Cleft blood role). Documented in B01 S3.2.
- Bloodletting at LU5 + LU11 = classical acute sore throat protocol.
  Three-edged needle at LU5 (cubital vein) + lancet at LU11 (Jing-
  Well, thumb nail corner). Both target different vessels/techniques
  for the same pathology.
- LU5 + BL13 = He-Sea + Back-Shu for chronic Lung conditions. Here
  LU5's He-Sea property (not its sedation function) is the therapeutic
  intent — combining the channel's deepest Qi access with the organ's
  segmental Back-Shu point.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Bloodletting for acute tonsillitis/pharyngitis — 🔴 Unvalidated**
Multiple Chinese clinical trials test bloodletting at LU5 (with or
without LU11) for acute sore throat/tonsillitis. Protocol: three-edged
needle or lancet puncture of a superficial cubital vein, expressing
3-10 drops of blood. Some studies report rapid symptom improvement
within 24 hours. All studies are open-label, no sham control, most
published in Chinese-language journals. The therapeutic mechanism (if
real) is indistinguishable from minor phlebotomy — same vessels, same
site, same technique as standard blood draws.
Confidence: 🔴 — not sham-controlled, point-specificity untestable
since the technique targets veins rather than a specific tissue point.

**2. Lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow) — 🔴 Unvalidated**
LU5 is included in some acupuncture protocols for lateral
epicondylitis as a local elbow point, but the primary study points
are typically LI11, LI10, or ashi points at the lateral epicondyle.
LU5's individual contribution is not isolatable. Broader acupuncture-
for-tennis-elbow evidence: "promising but inconclusive" (Cochrane).
Confidence: 🔴 — combination-only.

**3. Respiratory conditions (COPD, asthma) — 🔴 Unvalidated**
LU5 appears in multi-point protocols for respiratory conditions but
is never the sole study point. LU7 and ST36 are more commonly named
in respiratory trial protocols. No data isolatable to LU5.
Confidence: 🔴

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated at point-specific level.

He-Sea points at joint creases are palpable depressions that may
become tender with organ pathology. No systematic sensitization study
published for LU5 in acute respiratory conditions. The cubital fossa
is a common site of general tenderness (venipuncture sensitivity,
nerve proximity), which could confound disease-specific sensitization
assessment.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems at LU5:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): lateral antebrachial cutaneous
  nerve (C5-C6) primary. Radial nerve (C5-T1) at depth. LU5 is a
  NEURAL TRANSITION ZONE — musculocutaneous nerve trunk → purely
  sensory terminal branch (lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve).
  This parallels LR4 (deep peroneal → saphenous transition) and HT3
  (brachial plexus → ulnar nerve course) — He-Sea points at joints
  may systematically coincide with neural transition zones on their
  channels.
- A02 (Autonomic): C5-C6 segments. Lung sympathetic innervation:
  T2-T4. Gap = 3-4 segments — smaller than LU9 (6-8 segments) but
  still non-segmental. Any organ-level effects require supraspinal
  mediation.
- A03 (Fascial): bicipital aponeurosis (lacertus fibrosus) creates
  a fascial convergence at the cubital fossa — more architecturally
  significant than the inter-tendon intervals at distal LU points.

**Bloodletting mechanism — phlebotomy equivalence:**
The bloodletting technique targets the cephalic or median cubital
vein — the SAME vessels used for standard medical blood draws and
IV access. The procedure is essentially a small phlebotomy. Possible
mechanisms: (1) local counter-irritation from venipuncture; (2) minor
hemodynamic response; (3) placebo/ritual effect. None are specific to
LU5 as an "acupoint" — any cubital fossa venipuncture would produce
the same effect. The volume expressed (3-10 drops, <1 ml) is far
below the threshold for hemodynamic significance (~50-500 ml in
therapeutic phlebotomy). LU5 bloodletting is the first technique in
the Atlas where the acupuncture procedure is INDISTINGUISHABLE from
a standard Western medical procedure.

**Segmental gradient:**
LU5 (C5-C6) is 3-4 segments from the Lung viscerotome (T2-T4) —
half the gap at LU9 (C6-C7, 6-8 segments). Moving proximally along
the channel, the segmental distance to the Lung decreases
continuously: LU9 wrist → LU6 forearm → LU5 elbow → LU1 chest.
Yet TCM considers the distal points (larger gap) more important for
organ treatment — an inverse relationship between segmental proximity
and traditional therapeutic significance.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 15-25 mm perpendicular insertion, lateral to biceps
  tendon. Avoid medial angulation (brachial artery).
- De Qi: heavy distension at cubital fossa, may radiate along the
  forearm. Deeper sensation than distal LU points.
- EA: applicable. Commonly paired with distal points (LU7, LU9).

Bloodletting (三棱针 / lancet):
- Three-edged needle or disposable lancet
- Target: visible superficial vein (cephalic or median cubital)
- Technique: quick puncture → express 3-10 drops of dark blood →
  apply pressure for hemostasis
- Specific to acute Lung Heat/Blood Heat presentations
- Standard phlebotomy hygiene: sterile needle, hemostasis, monitor
  for hematoma
→ Cross-ref: B01 S2.7

Bấm huyệt (B02): elbow crease depression easily self-located. Firm
sustained pressure 30-60 seconds. Convenient self-acupressure site.

Cứu (B08): applicable but clinically paradoxical — LU5 is primarily
used to sedate Lung excess (Heat conditions), for which warming moxa
is contraindicated. Same paradox as LR2 (Fire-clearing sedation point
where moxa opposes the primary therapeutic intent).

### An toàn

Vascular risk: MODERATE. Brachial artery runs medial to biceps tendon
— the tendon acts as anatomical barrier, protecting the artery from
LU5 needling. Radial recurrent artery (smaller caliber) may be
encountered at depth laterally. Risk of significant arterial
hemorrhage is low with standard lateral-to-tendon technique.

Superficial veins: cephalic and median cubital veins easily punctured
— intentionally in bloodletting, accidentally in standard needling.
Bruising/hematoma common but self-limiting.

Nerve risk: lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve superficial at this
level — paresthesia possible. Radial nerve (deeper) — excessive depth
may engage it. Motor damage unlikely at standard depths.

Pneumothorax: none (elbow location).

Pregnancy: no specific contraindication documented.

Overall safety: MODERATE complication risk due to vascular density
at the cubital fossa. The biceps tendon as anatomical barrier
provides natural protection for the brachial artery. Bloodletting
technique requires standard phlebotomy safety precautions.

## LU6 — Khổng Tối (孔最, Kǒng Zuì)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
Xi-Cleft (郄穴) point of the Lung channel. Per the Yin/Yang Xi-Cleft
sub-rule (B01 S3.1): Yin channel Xi-Cleft points are primarily
indicated for blood disorders — LU6 specifically for hemoptysis
(coughing blood). "Kongzui" (孔最) = "Maximum Opening" — the point
where Qi and Blood accumulate most deeply along the channel. Located
on the radial aspect of the anterior forearm, 7 cun above the wrist
crease. Paired in clinical formulas with LU5 (He-Sea) for acute
hemoptysis — two different point-category mechanisms converging on
one pathology.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the palmar aspect of the
forearm, on the line connecting LU5 (cubital crease) and LU9 (wrist
crease), 7 cun proximal to the transverse wrist crease. The forearm
LU5-to-LU9 distance = 12 cun, placing LU6 at 5 cun below LU5 —
approximately at the junction of the upper and middle thirds of the
forearm.

Anatomical landmarks: on the radial side of the anterior forearm, in
the groove between brachioradialis (lateral) and the flexor mass
(flexor carpi radialis, pronator teres — medial). Palpable as a
slight depression when the forearm muscles are relaxed.

Needle depth range: 10-20 mm perpendicular insertion through
muscular tissue.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve (C5-C6, terminal branch of
  musculocutaneous nerve) — primary sensory supply to the lateral
  anterior forearm at this level. Same nerve that emerges at LU5
  (elbow crease), now running distally through the forearm.
- Superficial branch of radial nerve (C6-C7) — emerges from beneath
  brachioradialis at approximately mid-forearm level. At LU6's
  position (upper-mid forearm) this nerve is still deep to
  brachioradialis or just emerging.
- Combined spinal segments: C5-C7
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: antebrachial fascia between muscular compartments.
The needle passes through the intermuscular septum between
brachioradialis and the flexor compartment.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular:
- Radial artery: runs deep to brachioradialis at this level, along
  the radial border of the forearm. At LU6's position the artery is
  protected by the brachioradialis muscle belly.
- Cephalic vein tributaries: superficially lateral.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous fascia
- Brachioradialis (superficial lateral)
- Flexor carpi radialis / pronator teres (medial)
- Flexor digitorum superficialis (deep)
- Flexor pollicis longus (deepest, radial side)

**Xi-Cleft (郄穴)** — one of 16 Xi-Cleft points in the body (12
regular meridian + 4 extraordinary vessel). Xi-Cleft points are where
"Qi and Blood accumulate deeply" (B01 S3.1), used for acute
conditions along the channel and acute organ disorders.

YIN/YANG SUB-RULE (Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing, ~260 CE):
- Yin channel Xi-Cleft → blood disorders (bleeding, hemorrhage,
  blood stasis)
- Yang channel Xi-Cleft → acute pain syndromes

LU6 as Yin Xi-Cleft of Lung → primary indication: hemoptysis. B01
S3.1 explicitly cites "LU6 for hemoptysis" as a key example.

- LU6 (Lung): hemoptysis — Lung "controls diffusion" (宣发) →

- LR6 (Liver): uterine bleeding — Liver "stores Blood" (藏血) →

Each Yin Xi-Cleft's blood indication mirrors its organ's TCM blood
function. The PERFECT correspondence across four channels favors
systematic theoretical assignment over independent empirical

**1. TCM acupoint:** LU6 Kongzui (孔最, "Maximum Opening"). Xi-Cleft

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** not individually confirmed. Kim

**8. Cupping point:** mid-forearm — flat muscular surface technically

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, TrP approximate).
Same low convergence as LU9 (2/8). Mid-forearm points show lower
cross-tradition overlap than joint points (elbow, wrist, knee) —
a consistent pattern across all completed channels.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Clears Lung Heat and stops bleeding: hemoptysis (primary

- Opens and regulates Lung Qi: acute cough, acute asthma/wheezing,
  sore throat, loss of voice (acute laryngitis)
- Releases exterior: fever and chills without sweating — Xi-Cleft
  for acute early-stage external invasion
- Benefits the forearm: forearm pain, writer's cramp

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:

- LU6 alone for acute loss of voice: Xi-Cleft's acute-condition

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**EVIDENCE DESERT — Xi-Cleft points are the LEAST studied point
category in modern acupuncture research.**

No RCT, systematic review, or mechanism study exists for LU6 as a
sole intervention for any indication. LU6 does not appear as a
named study point in any identified clinical trial.

Compare across Yin Xi-Cleft points in the Atlas:
- PC4 (Pericardium Xi-Cleft): some Chinese clinical trial data for
  angina — the ONLY Yin Xi-Cleft with any controlled evidence
- HT6 (Heart Xi-Cleft): zero controlled evidence
- LR6 (Liver Xi-Cleft): zero controlled evidence
- LU6 (Lung Xi-Cleft): zero controlled evidence

The Xi-Cleft category may represent the largest gap between
traditional clinical prominence (acute conditions = high-urgency
role) and modern research attention. Compare: Yuan-Source points
(LR3, LI4) and He-Sea points (ST36) are among the most studied.
Xi-Cleft points, despite their acute-care role making them
especially amenable to clinical trials (clear endpoints, rapid
response), have been systematically overlooked.

All indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Xi-Cleft points are theoretically the strongest candidates for
disease-state sensitization — "Qi and Blood accumulate deeply" at
these points, which should manifest as spontaneous tenderness during
acute organ conditions. No systematic study has tested whether LU6
becomes tender during acute respiratory conditions (pneumonia,
bronchitis, hemoptysis).

Xi-Cleft tenderness as a DIAGNOSTIC sign is widely taught in TCM
palpation: spontaneous tenderness at LU6 → acute Lung pathology,
at PC4 → acute cardiac pathology. Diagnostic application widely
taught but not validated.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems at LU6:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): lateral antebrachial cutaneous
  nerve (C5-C6) + superficial radial nerve (C6-C7). Spinal segments
  C5-C7 — no direct segmental pathway to Lung viscerotome (T2-T4).
  Gap: 4-6 segments.
- A03 (Fascial): intermuscular septum between brachioradialis and
  flexor compartment — a fascial junction point, but not an
  exceptional convergence zone.

**Xi-Cleft mechanism — why would mid-forearm stimulation stop
pulmonary hemorrhage?**
No segmental explanation exists. C5-C7 stimulation has no direct
pathway to T2-T4 pulmonary vasculature. Any hemostatic effect must
involve supraspinal processing: afferent input from C5-C7 →
brainstem/hypothalamic centers → descending modulation of pulmonary
autonomic function → bronchial artery vasoconstriction or hemostasis.
This pathway is entirely hypothetical — no published study has tested
any element of this proposed mechanism.

**The "accumulation" concept:**
TCM describes Xi-Cleft points as locations where "Qi and Blood pool
deeply." Anatomically, this could correspond to intermuscular fascial
junctions where connective tissue is denser. However, no histological
study has compared tissue composition at Xi-Cleft points vs non-Xi-
Cleft points on the same channel. The "deep accumulation" concept
remains a TCM-internal model without identified anatomical correlate.

**Yin Xi-Cleft blood specificity — theoretical assignment pattern:**
All four Yin Xi-Cleft points in the Atlas (LU6, LR6, HT6, PC4) have
blood-specific indications matching their organ's TCM blood function.
The PERFECT correspondence favors systematic theoretical assignment:
the Yin/Yang sub-rule (Yin Xi-Cleft → blood) was applied to each
organ's existing blood theory, generating organ-specific blood
indications. If the pattern were empirically discovered at four
separate anatomical locations, we would expect some deviation (at
least one Yin Xi-Cleft with a non-blood primary indication). The
regularity suggests theory drove indication, not observation.

### Batch 2 — Architectural Observations

**Observation 7: Sedation point — Five Shu position varies by element**
LU5 (He-Sea/Water) is the sedation point of Lung (Metal → Water =
child). Across completed channels: LR2 Ying-Spring/Fire (Wood → Fire
child), HT7/PC7 Shu-Stream/Earth (Fire → Earth child). Unlike the
tonification-Yuan overlap (Observation 2), which follows a consistent
Earth rule, the sedation point lands at a DIFFERENT Five Shu position
for each organ element — He-Sea for Metal, Shu-Stream for Fire,
Ying-Spring for Wood. The Five Element × Five Shu matrix distributes
sedation points across elbow, wrist, and toe tip with no anatomical
pattern. Corollary: for Fire organs (HT/PC), the sedation point
(Earth/Shu-Stream) coincides with Yuan-Source (also Earth/Shu-Stream),
producing Yuan-sedation overlap. For Lung, Yuan (LU9) and sedation
(LU5) are at SEPARATE points. The overlap/separation depends on
whether the child element = Earth.

**Observation 8: Bloodletting at LU5 — phlebotomy equivalence**
The bloodletting technique at LU5 uses the cephalic/median cubital
veins — the same vessels, same site, same technique as standard
medical phlebotomy and IV access. This makes LU5 bloodletting the
first procedure in the Atlas where the acupuncture technique is
INDISTINGUISHABLE from a conventional Western medical procedure.
The "draining Blood Heat" rationale maps conceptually to therapeutic
phlebotomy (polycythemia vera, hemochromatosis) — both traditions
converge on the idea that removing blood from the cubital vein
treats systemic conditions. However, the volume at LU5 (3-10 drops,
<1 ml) is far below the threshold for hemodynamic significance
(~50-500 ml). Whether micro-phlebotomy produces a distinct
physiological effect or functions via counter-irritation/placebo is
unknown and untested.

**Observation 9: Xi-Cleft evidence desert — category-wide gap**
LU6 joins LR6 and HT6 as Yin Xi-Cleft points with ZERO controlled
evidence. Only PC4 (cardiac Xi-Cleft) has any preliminary data.
Xi-Cleft points may be the least studied point category in modern
acupuncture research — despite their traditional role in acute
conditions, which should make them the MOST amenable to clinical
trials (acute conditions have clear, rapid endpoints for study
design). The Yin/Yang sub-rule (Yin → blood disorders, Yang → pain)
has never been tested as a hypothesis. Compare: Yuan-Source points
(LI4, LR3) and He-Sea points (ST36) are among the most studied.
The research bias toward Yuan-Source and He-Sea may reflect their
inclusion in modern education and published protocols, creating a
self-reinforcing cycle of study → citation → further study.

**Observation 10: Yin Xi-Cleft organ-specific blood mapping**
Four Yin Xi-Cleft points now in the Atlas (LU6, LR6, HT6, PC4) ALL
have blood-specific indications mirroring their organ's TCM blood
function: LU6/hemoptysis (Lung → respiratory blood), LR6/uterine
bleeding (Liver → reproductive blood), HT6/cardiac blood signs
(Heart → governs blood), PC4/chest pain (Pericardium → cardiac
emergencies). The PERFECT 4/4 correspondence favors THEORETICAL
ASSIGNMENT (the Yin/Yang sub-rule applied to each organ's blood
theory) over INDEPENDENT EMPIRICAL DISCOVERY at four separate
anatomical locations. If empirically discovered, we would expect at
least one deviation. This interpretive question matters for
mechanism research: if theoretical, the indications need not have
anatomical correlates; if empirical, something about these specific
forearm/leg locations may genuinely affect organ-specific bleeding.

**Observation 11: Segmental gradient along the Lung channel**
Moving proximally: LU9 wrist (C6-C7, 6-8 segment gap from Lung
T2-T4) → LU6 mid-forearm (C5-C7, 4-6 gap) → LU5 elbow (C5-C6,
3-4 gap) → LU1 chest (T1-T2, 0-1 gap). The segmental distance from
the Lung viscerotome DECREASES continuously as points approach the
torso. Yet TCM's therapeutic hierarchy considers distal points
(LU7, LU9 — largest gap) MORE important for organ treatment than
proximal points (LU1, LU2 — smallest gap). This inverse relationship
between segmental proximity and traditional therapeutic importance
likely applies to ALL limb meridians — the points TCM values most
for organ treatment are those furthest from the organ's spinal
segments. This is a fundamental challenge for segmental reflex
models of acupuncture, and may explain why the most theoretically
important points (LU9, LR3) lack segmental mechanism support while
proximal points with better segmental alignment (BL13 Back-Shu of
Lung at T3) are considered supplementary rather than primary.

## LU1 — Trung Phủ (中府, Zhōng Fǔ)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
Front-Mu point of the Lung (肺之募穴) — located on the Lung
channel itself. Of 12 Front-Mu points, only 3 sit on their own
organ's channel: LU1 (Lung), LR14 (Liver), GB24 (Gallbladder).
The remaining 9 are on the Conception Vessel (CV, 6 organs) or
hosted by other channels (ST25 for LI, LR13 for SP, GB25 for KI).
Additionally an intersection point of Hand Taiyin (Lung) and Foot
Taiyin (Spleen) — the only LU point where two meridians converge.
LU1 is the START of the Lung channel's external pathway and,
because Lung is the first meridian in the Qi circulation cycle, LU1
is conventionally considered the starting point of the entire
12-meridian system. Paired with BL13 (Back-Shu of Lung, T3) in the
Shu-Mu combination for comprehensive Lung treatment.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral aspect of the
chest, in the 1st intercostal space, 6 cun lateral to the anterior
midline. 1 cun directly below LU2 (Yunmen).

Anatomical landmarks: locate the infraclavicular fossa (below the
lateral end of the clavicle), then measure 1 cun inferiorly to the
1st intercostal space. Palpate the depression between the 1st and
2nd ribs, 6 cun lateral to the midline (approximately at the
junction of the deltopectoral groove). The coracoid process of the
scapula is immediately lateral and deep.

Right side: overlies the apex of the right lung.
Left side: overlies the apex of the left lung.

Needle depth range: 5–8 mm oblique or transverse insertion ONLY,
directed laterally along the intercostal space or obliquely toward
the shoulder. NEVER perpendicular deep insertion — the lung apex is
directly beneath. In thin patients, the pleural cavity may be only
15–25 mm from the skin surface at this location.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **1st intercostal nerve (T1)** — lateral cutaneous branch provides
  primary sensory innervation to the 1st intercostal space laterally
- **Supraclavicular nerve (C3-C4)** — descending from the cervical
  plexus, innervates the skin over the upper chest below the
  clavicle. This contribution means LU1 receives DUAL segmental
  input: thoracic (T1) and cervical (C3-C4)
- **Lateral pectoral nerve (C5-C7)** — motor innervation to the
  clavicular head of pectoralis major overlying the point
- Spinal segments: T1 (primary dermatome) with C3-C4
  (supraclavicular overlap) and C5-C7 (motor)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A07 S1.2

Fascial plane: multiple layers in the lateral upper chest —
- Pectoral fascia investing the pectoralis major
- Clavipectoral fascia (deep to pectoralis major, investing
  pectoralis minor and subclavius)
- The clavipectoral fascia is part of the Deep Front Line (DFL) in
  Anatomy Trains — connecting the anterior chest to the cervical
  fascia and axillary structures
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: thoracoacromial artery (pectoral branch) and lateral
thoracic artery branches. Cephalic vein runs in the deltopectoral
groove adjacent to the point. Axillary vessels are deeper and more
lateral — not at direct risk at standard needle depth.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue → pectoral fascia
- Pectoralis major (clavicular head — inferior fibers)
- Clavipectoral fascia
- Pectoralis minor (insertion at coracoid process is nearby)
- 1st intercostal space: external intercostal → internal intercostal
- Endothoracic fascia → parietal pleura → lung apex

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Front-Mu: Lung** (肺之募穴) — on its OWN channel. This is the
same on-channel pattern as LR14 (Front-Mu of Liver on the Liver
channel). Compare: Heart Front-Mu = CV14 (on CV, not HT channel),
Pericardium Front-Mu = CV17 (on CV, not PC channel). The Lung's
Front-Mu being on-channel means the channel itself has direct
diagnostic and therapeutic access to its own organ's Front-Mu point.

**Intersection point:** Hand Taiyin (Lung) + Foot Taiyin (Spleen).
Both are Taiyin (Greater Yin) channels — the convergence at LU1
links the two Taiyin systems at the start of the Lung channel.
Traditional significance: the LU-SP connection supports the "Spleen
is the mother of Lung" (Earth generates Metal in the Sheng cycle)
relationship. Clinically, LU1 can treat Spleen-related Lung
conditions (phlegm-dampness accumulating in the Lung from Spleen
deficiency).

Five Shu: not a Five Shu point (Five Shu located elbow/knee
  and distal only)
Yuan-Source: no (LU9)
Luo-Connecting: no (LU7)
Xi-Cleft: no (LU6)
Back-Shu: no (but paired with BL13 as Shu-Mu combination for Lung)
Eight Confluent: no
Hui-Meeting: no
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1 (Front-Mu)

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LU1 Zhongfu (中府, "Central Palace"). Front-Mu
of Lung. Primary functions: diffuse and descend Lung Qi (宣降肺气),
stop cough, transform phlegm, clear Heat from Lung. As an
intersection point with the Spleen channel, also used for Spleen-
Lung combined patterns (phlegm-dampness from Spleen deficiency
accumulating in Lung). Part of the Shu-Mu combination: BL13 + LU1
(BL13 for chronic/deficiency, LU1 for acute/excess — B01 S3.1).
Front-Mu diagnostic: tenderness at LU1 on palpation indicates Lung
dysfunction.

**2. Trigger point:** Pectoralis major (clavicular head) trigger
points in the infraclavicular region. Dorsher anatomic
correspondence: strong — pectoralis major TrPs at this level refer
pain to the anterior shoulder and upper chest, overlapping with LU1's
traditional indication zone. Additionally, pectoralis minor TrPs
(deeper) refer pain to the anterior chest and mimic cardiac pain.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Infraclavicular tenderness is commonly
found in chronic respiratory patients (asthma, COPD, chronic cough).
The area is tender on palpation during acute respiratory illness —
consistent with the Front-Mu diagnostic function. Moderate-to-strong
Ashi basis.

**4. Marma point:** Apastambha Marma — located in the upper
chest/intercostal region (Sushruta Samhita). Classified as Sira
(vessel) Marma. Regional correspondence with LU1's infraclavicular
location. The Ayurvedic tradition identifies this area as vital for
respiratory function (Prana Vayu). Moderate correspondence — regional
but not precisely point-matched.

**5. Head zone:** T1 dermatome → at the UPPER margin of the lung
viscerotome (T2-T4, extended to T1-T5). This is the tightest
segmental alignment of any LU point to the Lung organ. Compare
LU9 (C6-C7, 6-8 segment gap). BL13 (Back-Shu, T3) is at the CORE
of lung viscerotome; LU1 (Front-Mu, T1) is at the MARGIN. Both
fall within the lung's referred pain zone, but at different
positions within it. Beissner 2011 confirmed Front-Mu ↔ Head zone
convergence for organ-associated chest/abdominal points.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2, S4.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Line (DFL) — the clavipectoral
fascia investing pectoralis minor connects through the axillary
fascia to the brachial fascia (arm) and through the endothoracic
fascia to the mediastinum (internally). LU1 sits at the junction
where the DFL transitions from thoracic to upper limb. Superficial
Front Arm Line also passes through the pectoralis major at this
level.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** Not specifically studied at
this location. Kim 2017 cardiac neurogenic spots focused on T1-T5
— T1 at LU1 is at the border of this range. Whether Lung pathology
generates neurogenic spots in the upper chest at T1 has not been
examined.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3

**8. Cupping point:** YES — the lateral upper chest is a common
cupping site for respiratory conditions. Cupping at LU1/upper
chest for cough, phlegm, chest congestion is standard practice in
both TCM and folk medicine traditions. Dry or sliding cupping
applicable. Sufficient flat surface area and muscle cushioning
(pectoralis major) for effective suction.

**Convergence score: 6/8** — TCM ✓, Trigger Point ✓, Ashi ✓,
Marma ~ (regional), Head Zone ✓, Anatomy Trains ✓, Neurogenic ✗,
Cupping ✓. Highest convergence on the Lung channel — equal to
BL13 (6/8). The proximal chest location provides richer cross-
tradition overlap than the distal forearm/hand points (LU5 3/8,
LU6 2/8). Pattern: proximity to the organ → higher convergence.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization: Front-Mu tenderness at LU1 in
respiratory disease is a diagnostic finding in BOTH TCM and Western
medicine. Infraclavicular/upper chest tenderness and hyperalgesia
in chronic respiratory patients (COPD, asthma) has been observed
clinically. This parallels the LR14 finding — both on-channel
Front-Mu points show disease-state sensitization in their
respective organ conditions. The convergence with Head zone mapping
(T1 at the margin of lung viscerotome) provides a segmental basis
for this sensitization via viscerocutaneous reflex.

Threshold change under pathology: not formally studied with
quantitative sensory testing at LU1 specifically. The BL13 entry
notes paravertebral tenderness in chronic respiratory patients —
the anterior counterpart (LU1) likely shows similar sensitization
through convergent visceral afferents at the spinal level.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Somatovisceral reflex — T1 pathway:**
LU1 at the 1st intercostal space → T1 intercostal nerve afferents
→ dorsal horn at T1 → convergence with pulmonary visceral afferents
→ sympathetic preganglionic neurons → upper thoracic sympathetic
chain → pulmonary plexus → bronchomotor tone, bronchial vasculature,
mucus secretion. T1 is at the upper margin of lung innervation
(core: T2-T4, extended: T1-T5). Near-segmental pathway for
somatovisceral effects on the lung.

**Shu-Mu pair — segmental alignment:**
BL13 (T3) + LU1 (T1-T2) = 1-2 segment offset. Third Shu-Mu pair
documented in the Atlas:
- Spleen: BL20 (T11) ↔ LR13 (T10-T11) = 0-1 offset (precise)
- Lung: BL13 (T3) ↔ LU1 (T1-T2) = 1-2 offset (moderate)
- Liver: BL18 (T9) ↔ LR14 (T6) = 3 offset (wide)

All three Front-Mu points fall WITHIN their respective organ's
visceral afferent range. But the tightness of segmental alignment
varies. Both BL13 and LU1 are within the lung zone (T1-T5), so
the pair converges on the same organ from front and back — the
classical rationale has segmental support.

**LU-SP intersection — Taiyin convergence:**
The intersection with the Spleen channel at LU1 has a potential
neurological basis: LU1 at T1 and splenic visceral innervation at
T6-T10 are separated by 5-9 segments — no direct segmental link.
However, the DFL fascial connection from the upper chest to the
abdominal cavity provides a non-neural pathway. The clinical
significance of the "Spleen is mother of Lung" concept being
encoded at LU1 (the Lung channel's start) remains unexplained by
any known anatomical mechanism.

**Comparison with BL13 — posterior vs anterior access:**
BL13 (T3 paravertebral) targets dorsal ramus afferents → direct
access to sympathetic chain via the paravertebral position. LU1
(T1 anterolateral chest) targets intercostal nerve afferents →
reaches dorsal horn via ventral ramus. Different afferent pathways
converging on the same spinal segments — the classical "back for
tonification, front for drainage" distinction may reflect different
afferent fiber compositions or different autonomic reflex arcs
activated by posterior vs anterior stimulation.

**Gap:** No point-specific mechanism study at LU1. The segmental
proximity (T1 vs lung at T2-T4) predicts a somatovisceral reflex
effect, but this has not been directly tested. Whether LU1 produces
measurably different autonomic effects than BL13 (same organ,
different approach angle) is unknown.

Châm (B01):

  ONLY, directed laterally along the intercostal space or toward

→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

### An toàn

**Pneumothorax risk: HIGH.** The primary safety concern — identical
risk category to LR14 but at the lung APEX rather than the lung

thin patients, the pleural cavity may be only 15–25 mm from the

Strict rules:

- NEVER exceed 0.8 cun (~16 mm) depth
- OBLIQUE or TRANSVERSE insertion along the intercostal space ONLY
- In thin/cachectic patients: reduce to 3–5 mm maximum
- If in doubt about depth: use acupressure instead of needling

Organ injury risk:

sufficient.

Neural risk: lateral pectoral nerve (motor). Direct contact causes
pectoralis muscle contraction — not dangerous but can startle the
patient.

Pregnancy: no specific classical contraindication, but chest wall
point with pneumothorax risk warrants conservative approach.

Overall safety: **HIGH complication risk** — pneumothorax is the
primary concern. LU1 shares this risk category with LR14 (6th ICS)
and LU2 (infraclavicular). Among the highest-risk points on the
Lung channel. Requires trained practitioner, precise technique, and
appropriate body habitus assessment.

## LU10 — Ngư Tế (鱼际, Yú Jì)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Fish Border" (鱼际) — the thenar eminence resembles the belly of
a fish, and the point sits at the "border" where palmar (red) skin
meets dorsal (white) skin. Ying-Spring (Fire) point of the Lung
channel. Fire CONTROLS Metal in the Five Element Ke (controlling/
restraining) cycle, making LU10 the **Ke cycle controller point**
— the element that restrains Lung excess. This is DIFFERENT from
the sedation point (LU5 He-Sea/Water = child of Metal in the Sheng
cycle). Clinically, the primary traditional indication is acute
sore throat and pharyngitis — a Heat-clearing function derived from
both the Ying-Spring position (Nan Jing Ch.68: "Spring points treat
heat in the body") and the Fire element's controlling relationship
to Metal. One of the few Ying-Spring points with any clinical data.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the thenar eminence of the
hand, at the midpoint of the 1st metacarpal bone, on the border
between the red and white skin (赤白肉际).

Anatomical landmarks: find the midpoint of the 1st metacarpal bone
on the radial aspect of the hand. The point is at the junction
where thick, glabrous palmar skin (red) transitions to thin, hairy
dorsal skin (white). Palpate the thenar eminence — the point is on
the lateral/dorsal border of the muscle bulk, NOT on the palmar
center.

Needle depth range: 5–10 mm perpendicular or oblique insertion. The
thenar eminence provides substantial muscle cushioning. Deeper
insertion reaches the opponens pollicis muscle belly.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Superficial branch of radial nerve (C6-C7)** — sensory
  innervation to the dorsal/radial aspect of the thenar region
- **Palmar cutaneous or digital branch of median nerve (C6-C7)** —
  sensory innervation to the palmar aspect
- The red-white skin junction is a transition zone between radial
  nerve territory (dorsal) and median nerve territory (palmar) —
  DUAL nerve overlap at the point
- **Recurrent branch of median nerve (C8-T1)** — motor to thenar
  muscles (opponens pollicis, abductor pollicis brevis, flexor
  pollicis brevis superficial head). Deeper than standard needle
  depth.
- Spinal segments: C6-C7 (primary sensory), C8-T1 (motor)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: the red-white skin junction represents a histological
transition — thick glabrous palmar skin (rich in Meissner's
corpuscles, Pacinian corpuscles) meets thin hairy dorsal skin (rich
in free nerve endings, hair follicle receptors). This boundary may
have distinct mechanoreceptor properties, but this has not been
specifically studied.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: princeps pollicis artery runs on the palmar side of the
1st metacarpal, deeper than the standard needle trajectory at the
red-white skin junction. Dorsal digital arteries (from radial artery
dorsal carpal branch) are small caliber. Low vascular risk.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin (red-white junction) → subcutaneous tissue
- Abductor pollicis brevis (superficial thenar muscle)
- Opponens pollicis (deeper thenar muscle, directly on 1st
  metacarpal shaft)
- 1st metacarpal bone (floor)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Ying-Spring (Fire)** (荥/火) — 2nd of the Five Shu
points on the Lung channel. For Yin channels: Well=Wood → Spring=
Fire → Stream=Earth → River=Metal → Sea=Water.

**Five Element Ke cycle significance:** Fire CONTROLS Metal (火克金)
in the controlling/restraining cycle. LU10 is the Ke controller
point — the element that restrains the organ's own element.

This is DIFFERENT from the sedation point role:
- **Sedation point** = CHILD element (drains excess via Sheng cycle):
  LU5 He-Sea/Water (Metal generates Water → Water is child)
- **Ke controller** = CONTROLLING element (restrains via Ke cycle):
  LU10 Ying-Spring/Fire (Fire controls Metal → Fire restrains)

Comparison of Ying-Spring/Fire across completed channels — THREE
different Five Element roles at the SAME Five Shu position:
- **Fire channel** (HT8, PC8): Fire on Fire = **HORARY** (same
  element as organ = self-resonance)
- **Wood channel** (LR2): Fire on Wood = **SEDATION** (Fire is
  child of Wood in Sheng cycle)
- **Metal channel** (LU10): Fire on Metal = **Ke-cycle controller** (Fire
  restrains Metal in Ke cycle)

The same relative position in the Five Shu system carries different
therapeutic logic depending on the organ's element — a structural
consequence of the Five Element × Five Shu matrix interaction.

Yuan-Source: no (LU9)
Luo-Connecting: no (LU7)
Xi-Cleft: no (LU6)
Back-Shu: no
Front-Mu: no (LU1)
Eight Confluent: no
Hui-Meeting: no
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LU10 Yuji (鱼际, "Fish Border"). Ying-Spring/
Fire of Lung. Primary functions: clear Lung Heat (清肺热), benefit
the throat (利咽喉), descend Lung Qi. First-choice point for acute
sore throat from Lung Heat or Fire toxin. Distinguished from LU5
(He-Sea/Water, sedation point, clears Lung via the child element)
and LU11 (Jing-Well/Wood, emergency bloodletting for acute throat).
LU10 is the intermediate choice: more targeted to throat than LU5,
less emergent than LU11.

**2. Trigger point:** Thenar muscle trigger points — opponens
pollicis and abductor pollicis brevis TrPs. Referred pain pattern:
thumb, thenar eminence, 1st metacarpal area. Dorsher correspondence:
moderate — the thenar trigger point zone overlaps anatomically, but
the referred pain pattern (local to thumb/thenar) differs from
LU10's traditional indication (throat, not hand). The trigger point
explains the local pain indication but not the distal throat effect.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** The thenar eminence becomes tender in
repetitive thumb-use injuries (texting thumb, de Quervain-related
strain, arthritis of 1st CMC joint). The red-white skin junction is
moderately sensitive to pressure. As a spontaneous tenderness site,
the thenar eminence ranks moderate — not as commonly tender as the
LI4 web space, but more so than the dorsal foot points.

**4. Marma point:** Talahridaya Marma (तलहृदय, "Heart of the
Palm") — located at the center of the palm in the Marma system.
LU10 is at the RADIAL BORDER of the thenar eminence, not the palm
center — lateral/proximal displacement from the classic Talahridaya
position. PC8 (Laogong) in the Atlas was mapped as the closer
anatomical correspondence to Talahridaya. LU10's relationship is
regional, not precise.

**5. Head zone:** C6-C7 dermatome → NOT within the lung viscerotome
(T2-T4). 4-6 segment gap — same magnitude as other distal Lung
channel points (LU9, LU7). No dermatomal explanation for throat/lung
effects from this location.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Front Arm Line terminates in the
thenar eminence — the palmar fascia connects through the flexor
retinaculum to the forearm fascia (continuous with the volar forearm
along which LU5-LU9 travel). Deep Front Arm Line also passes
through the thenar muscles. LU10 is at a LINE TERMINUS — the distal
end of the myofascial chain in the hand.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** Not specifically studied. The
thenar eminence is in the C6-C7 dermatome — outside the cardiac
(T1-T5) and gastric (T6-T10) zones studied by Kim 2017. Lung-
specific neurogenic spots at C6-C7 are predicted by the channel
model but not by the segmental model.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable. The thenar eminence has
insufficient flat surface area and convex contour for suction cup
application.

**Convergence score: 3/8** — TCM ✓, Trigger Point ~ (local only),
Ashi ✓, Marma ~ (regional), Head Zone ✗, Anatomy Trains ✓,
Neurogenic ✗, Cupping ✗. Low convergence — typical of distal
hand/foot points. The thenar eminence is a muscular site with
trigger point overlap, but its traditional throat indication has
no cross-tradition correlate.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Neijing Lingshu (Chapter 1, Ben Shu): identified as Ying-Spring
  point of the Lung channel.
- Nan Jing (Chapter 68): "Ying points are for heat in the body" —
  LU10 is the specific Ying-Spring application for Lung Heat.
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing (Huangfu Mi, 259 CE): sore throat, loss of
  voice, cough with fever, heat in the palm, shortness of breath.
- Zhenjiu Dacheng (Yang Jizhou, 1601): sore throat, swelling of the
  throat, cough, hemoptysis, fever, head and body pain in febrile

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Sore throat, pharyngitis, tonsillitis
- Loss of voice, hoarseness
- Cough, hemoptysis
- Fever, febrile disease
- Heat sensation in the palm (the "fish border" area itself)
- Thumb pain, thenar eminence pain

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- Acute pharyngitis protocol: LU10 + LU11 bloodletting for acute
  sore throat — prick LU10 and/or LU11 with a lancet, squeeze out
  3-10 drops of blood. One of the most commonly used emergency
  bloodletting protocols in Chinese clinical practice.
- LU10 vs LU11 for throat: LU10 for more chronic/recurrent throat
  conditions; LU11 for acute emergencies (high fever, severe

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Acute pharyngitis / sore throat — 🟡 Plausible**

Clinical studies: several Chinese clinical trials have examined
bloodletting (刺络放血) at LU10 and/or LU11 for acute pharyngitis.
Wang et al. (Zhongguo Zhen Jiu, 2011): bloodletting at LU11 + LU10
vs oral antibiotics for acute pharyngitis — comparable symptom
improvement with faster onset in the bloodletting group. Additional
Chinese-language clinical trials report similar findings.

Limitations: most trials are in Chinese-language journals, lack
rigorous sham controls, and use LU10+LU11 combined (cannot isolate
LU10's individual contribution). No Cochrane or PRISMA-compliant
systematic review. The bloodletting technique itself may produce
effects through counter-irritation, local immune stimulation, or
placebo — independent of point specificity.

This is the ONLY Ying-Spring point in the Atlas with ANY clinical
data for its traditional headline indication. Compare: LR2 (zero
data for Liver Fire), HT8 (zero data for Heart Fire). LU10's
pharyngitis evidence, while methodologically weak, gives it more
clinical support than any other Ying-Spring point documented so far.

Confidence: 🟡 Plausible — multiple clinical observations with
consistent direction, but no sham-controlled evidence and combined
LU10+LU11 protocol prevents individual point attribution.

**2. Fever / febrile disease — 🔴 Unvalidated**
Traditional Ying-Spring indication ("heat in the body"). No point-
specific evidence for antipyretic effect at LU10.

**3. Cough / hemoptysis — 🔴 Unvalidated**
Classical indication. LU5 (He-Sea) and LU6 (Xi-Cleft) are the
primary points for these indications — LU10 is secondary. No
evidence for LU10 specifically.

**4. Thumb/thenar pain — 🔴 Unvalidated**
Local indication with trigger point basis. No specific evidence.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization: no specific published data on LU10
sensitization under Lung Heat or pharyngitis conditions. The thenar
eminence (C6-C7 dermatome) is not in the lung viscerotome (T2-T4),
so viscerocutaneous reflex-based sensitization is not predicted by
the segmental model. Whether the red-white skin junction shows
altered sensitivity under respiratory pathology is unknown.

Clinical observation: the palm heat sensation (手心热) in TCM is a
diagnostic sign of Yin deficiency with Heat. LU10 at the thenar
eminence may become subjectively warmer in febrile or Yin-deficient
patients, but objective thermometric confirmation is lacking.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Non-segmental challenge — throat effect from C6-C7:**
LU10 at the thenar eminence is innervated by C6-C7 (sensory). The
pharynx/throat is innervated by CN IX (glossopharyngeal) and CN X
(vagus) — cranial nerves with NO spinal connection to C6-C7. The
traditional throat indication (sore throat, pharyngitis) from LU10
has NO segmental pathway. This is a more extreme non-segmental gap
than the organ effects at LU9 (C6-C7 → lung T2-T4, at least same
spinal cord), because LU10's throat indication requires reaching
BRAINSTEM nuclei (nucleus tractus solitarius, nucleus ambiguus) from
a cervical spinal cord segment.

**Possible supraspinal pathway:**
C6-C7 afferents → dorsal horn → ascending spinothalamic/
spinoreticular pathways → brainstem nuclei → modulation of vagal
outflow to pharynx. Speculative but parallels the proposed mechanism
for LI4 (C6-C8) effects on head/face pain through trigeminal-spinal
convergence. Whether C6-C7 afferents specifically modulate
pharyngeal nerve activity through brainstem convergence has not been
studied.

**Bloodletting mechanism — counter-irritation or specific?**
Bloodletting at LU10 (lancet prick + 3-10 drops of blood from
superficial capillaries) activates A-delta fibers (sharp pain) →
rapid ascending signal → brainstem. The pharyngitis evidence uses
this technique, not standard needling. Whether the therapeutic
effect (if any) is specific to the LU10 location or would occur
with bloodletting at any hand point is the central mechanistic
question. Without sham-point controls, point specificity cannot
be assessed.

**Ke cycle logic — Fire controls Metal:**
The traditional rationale (Fire restrains Metal excess → clears
Lung Heat → benefits throat) operates within the Five Element
framework. Whether the Ke cycle represents a neurological reality
(different autonomic reflex arcs for different Five Element points)
or is purely theoretical (overlay on a generic acupuncture response)
is unknown. The pharyngitis evidence does not distinguish between
these possibilities.

**Comparison with LR2 — same Ying-Spring/Fire, different logic:**
Both LR2 and LU10 are Ying-Spring/Fire points. Both traditionally
clear Heat. But the Five Element logic differs:
- LR2: Fire is CHILD of Wood → sedation (drains excess via Sheng)
- LU10: Fire CONTROLS Metal → restraint (suppresses via Ke)
Whether this theoretical distinction maps to different neural
mechanisms is unknown. Both points are on extremity surfaces (foot
vs hand), both in the C6-L5 range of spinal segments, and both
have minimal mechanistic data.

**Gap:** No fMRI, no fiber-type study, no somatovisceral mechanism
data for LU10. The throat effect from C6-C7 stimulation requires
a supraspinal pathway that has not been identified. The pharyngitis
evidence uses bloodletting (not standard needling), so any mechanism
hypothesis must account for the specific stimulation modality.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 5–10 mm perpendicular insertion at the red-white
  skin junction. The thenar eminence has sufficient muscle depth
  for safe perpendicular needling — unlike chest points.
- De Qi: distension and aching in the thenar eminence. May radiate
  into the thumb or wrist.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bloodletting (B01 special technique):
- **PRIMARY technique at this point.** Lancet or three-edged needle
  prick at the red-white skin junction → squeeze the thenar
  eminence to express 3-10 drops of blood.
- Target: superficial capillaries at the skin junction.
- Volume: <0.5 ml — far less than LU5 bloodletting (which uses
  cubital veins for larger volume).
- Clinical protocol for acute pharyngitis: prick LU10 (and/or
  LU11), squeeze until blood color change from dark to light —
  traditionally considered the therapeutic endpoint.
- Two distinct bloodletting modalities on one channel: LU10 = skin
  prick + capillary blood (micro-trauma), LU5 = vein puncture +
  venous blood (phlebotomy equivalent).

Bấm huyệt (B02):
- Self-accessible: press the opposite thumb firmly into the red-
  white skin junction of the thenar eminence. Sustained moderate-
  to-strong pressure for 30–60 seconds.
- The thenar eminence is one of the easiest self-acupressure sites.
  Common self-care advice for mild sore throat.

Cứu (B08):
- Contraindicated in Lung Heat patterns — the exact conditions for
  which LU10 is most commonly used (same logic as LR2 for Liver
  Fire). Moxa at LU10 only appropriate in rare Lung cold patterns
  affecting the hand.

### An toàn

Vascular risk: princeps pollicis artery runs deeper on the palmar

Neural risk: recurrent branch of median nerve is deeper than

Pneumothorax risk: none (hand location).

Pregnancy: no specific classical contraindication.

Overall safety: **LOW complication risk.** The thenar eminence
provides substantial muscle cushioning with no major vessels or

minimally invasive. One of the safest points on the Lung channel —
maximum contrast with LU1 (HIGH risk, same batch).

### Batch 3 — Architectural Observations

**Observation 12: Front-Mu on own channel — second data point,
pathway logic**
LU1 is the second on-channel Front-Mu in the Atlas (after LR14).
Of 12 Front-Mu points, 3 sit on their own organ's channel: LU1,
LR14, GB24. The determining factor is whether the channel's
external pathway traverses the trunk near the organ: Lung channel
starts at the lateral chest (near lung) → LU1. Liver channel ends
at the subcostal area (near liver) → LR14. Gallbladder channel
traverses the lateral abdomen (near gallbladder) → GB24. The 9
"off-channel" Front-Mu points occur when the organ's channel either
doesn't reach the relevant trunk area or passes too far from the
organ — in these cases, the Conception Vessel (CV, midline) or a
neighboring channel hosts the Front-Mu. Positional note: LU1 is the
FIRST point on its channel, LR14 is the LAST — both channel-terminal
trunk positions. GB24 is mid-channel. 2 of 3 on-channel Front-Mu
are at channel extremes.

**Observation 13: Shu-Mu segmental offset — third pair, variable
alignment**
Three Shu-Mu pairs now documented in the Atlas:
- Spleen: BL20 (T11) ↔ LR13 (T10-T11) = 0-1 segment offset
- Lung: BL13 (T3) ↔ LU1 (T1-T2) = 1-2 segment offset
- Liver: BL18 (T9) ↔ LR14 (T6) = 3 segment offset
All three Front-Mu points fall WITHIN their organ's visceral
afferent range. But Back-Shu points show consistently tight
segmental accuracy (directly AT the viscerotome), while Front-Mu
points are more variable (0-3 segment displacement). This suggests
the Front-Mu system was NOT systematized by pure segmental logic —
additional factors (channel pathway, palpation findings, clinical
tradition) influenced Front-Mu assignments. The Back-Shu system,
constrained to the BL paravertebral line at fixed vertebral levels,
may be the more segmentally "honest" system.

**Observation 14: Ying-Spring/Fire — Five Element role typology**
LU10 (Fire on Metal = Ke controller) reveals that the SAME Five Shu
position carries DIFFERENT therapeutic logic per organ element. Three
Ying-Spring/Fire points now in Atlas:
- HT8/PC8 (Fire on Fire): HORARY — self-element resonance
- LR2 (Fire on Wood): SEDATION — child drains parent (Sheng cycle)
- LU10 (Fire on Metal): Ke-cycle controller — Fire restrains Metal (Ke)
These are 3 of 5 possible Five Element relationships. The remaining
2 (predicted but unverified): SP Ying-Spring = Fire on Earth =
TONIFICATION (mother nourishes child); KI Ying-Spring = Fire on
Water = Ke-cycle subject (Water controls Fire). If confirmed across all
5 Yin organ types, the Ying-Spring/Fire position would display the
COMPLETE Five Element relationship typology — every possible
element-to-element relationship at a single Five Shu position. This
typology applies in principle to EVERY Five Shu column (not just
Ying-Spring), but Fire at Ying-Spring is the first column with 3+
confirmed data points.

**Observation 15: Two bloodletting modalities on one channel**
LU10 bloodletting (lancet prick + capillary blood, <0.5 ml) differs
from LU5 bloodletting (vein puncture + venous blood, phlebotomy
equivalent). The Lung channel demonstrates TWO distinct bloodletting
techniques with different mechanisms:
- LU10/LU11 (hand): micro-trauma to capillaries → counter-
  irritation, possible local immune activation
- LU5 (elbow): macro-volume venous blood → hemodynamic/humoral
  effect (phlebotomy equivalent, Observation 8)
Different anatomical targets, different traditional rationales (LU10
for throat Heat; LU5 for Lung Fire/hemoptysis), likely different
physiological mechanisms. The Lung channel is the most bloodletting-
diverse channel in the Atlas — 3 bloodletting points (LU5, LU10,
LU11) with 2 distinct modalities.

**Observation 16: Convergence gradient — proximal > distal**
LU1 convergence (6/8) is the highest on the Lung channel, equal to
BL13 (6/8). Across the channel: LU1 (6/8) → LU5 (3/8) → LU6 (2/8)
→ LU10 (3/8). Proximal chest points near the organ show higher
cross-tradition convergence than distal forearm/hand points. This
makes anatomical sense: chest points overlap with Head zones,
trigger points, Ashi, cupping, and Anatomy Trains traditions that
all independently map the chest for respiratory conditions. Distal
points converge only with traditions that map extremities. This
gradient likely holds across ALL limb channels — a fundamental
property of cross-tradition mapping where proximity to the organ
increases the probability of independent discovery across
traditions.

## LU2 — Vân Môn (云门, Yún Mén)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Cloud Gate." The most proximal clinically accessible point on
the Lung channel after LU1. Located in the infraclavicular fossa
at the deltopectoral triangle — the anatomical "gate" where the
channel transitions from trunk to upper limb. Shares respiratory
indications with LU1 but is less clinically prominent and lacks
any special category designation. Notable primarily for its HIGH
vascular risk: the axillary artery/vein and lateral cord of the
brachial plexus lie deep to this point, making it one of the
highest-risk non-organ points on any channel.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): in the infraclavicular fossa,
6 cun lateral to the anterior midline, in the depression medial
to the coracoid process of the scapula. 1 cun superior to LU1.

Anatomical landmarks: in the deltopectoral triangle (trigonum
deltopectorale), the groove between the clavicular head of
pectoralis major (medial) and the anterior fibers of deltoid
(lateral). The coracoid process is palpable as a bony landmark
lateral and slightly inferior. The cephalic vein runs through
this groove superficially.

Needle depth range: 5-10 mm oblique-lateral. Directed laterally
away from the thoracic cavity. NEVER needle perpendicular or
medially — pneumothorax risk (lung apex) and axillary vessel
puncture risk.

Relationship to adjacent points: 1 cun superior to LU1 (which
is at 1st intercostal space). Both share pneumothorax risk, but
LU2's additional vascular hazard (axillary vessels closer at
this level) makes it the higher-composite-risk point.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply: intermediate supraclavicular nerve (C3-C4) for
cutaneous innervation. Lateral pectoral nerve (C5-C7) innervates
the pectoralis major deep to the point. The lateral cord of the
brachial plexus (C5-C7) passes deep to the pectoralis minor in
this region.

Segmental input: C3-C4 (cutaneous) + C5-C7 (deep motor/
proprioceptive). This is a TRANSITION zone — C3-C4 is cervical
plexus territory (supraclavicular nerves descend from neck),
while C5-C7 is brachial plexus territory (lateral cord descending
to arm). LU2 sits at the anatomical junction of cervical and
brachial plexus innervation.

Vascular: cephalic vein (superficial, visible in deltopectoral
groove). Deep: thoracoacromial artery (pectoral branch). Deeper:
axillary artery and vein in the axillary fossa, posterior to
pectoralis minor. Distance to axillary vessels: 20-40 mm
depending on body habitus — within range of careless deep
needling.

Tissue layers (superficial → deep): skin → subcutaneous fat →
deltopectoral fascia → cephalic vein (variable position) →
pectoralis major (clavicular head) → clavipectoral fascia →
pectoralis minor → axillary sheath (axillary artery, vein,
brachial plexus cords).

Ref: A01 (peripheral nerve anatomy), A04 (fascial planes).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No special category designation. LU2 is a standard channel point
(经穴) without Five Shu, Yuan-Source, Luo, Xi-Cleft, Front-Mu,
or any other special classification.

Functional relationship to LU1: LU1 (Front-Mu of Lung) is 1 cun
inferior. In classical practice, LU2 and LU1 were sometimes
needled as a pair for severe respiratory conditions. LU2 may
function as a "supporting" point to LU1's Front-Mu role, but has
no independent categorical significance.

- Shoulder and arm pain radiating from anterior chest
- Throat obstruction (咽喉不利)
- Qi counterflow ascending (气逆)

Modern standard (Deadman, WHO):
- Cough, asthma, chest pain
- Shoulder pain (anterior), arm pain
- Fullness in the chest

Combination patterns:
- LU2 + LU1 for severe respiratory distress (proximal chest pair)
- LU2 + LI15 for anterior shoulder pain

Traditional use is largely subsumed by LU1 — most practitioners
select LU1 (Front-Mu) over LU2 for respiratory conditions,
limiting LU2's independent clinical role.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No point-specific RCT or systematic review identified for LU2.
LU2 appears in multi-point acupuncture protocols for COPD and
shoulder impingement but is never isolated as a single variable.
Its clinical contribution cannot be separated from the protocol.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**HIGH** risk. Composite hazard from two independent risk vectors.

Precautions:
- **Pneumothorax:** Lung apex extends above the 1st rib into the
  infraclavicular fossa. Perpendicular or medially-directed deep
  needling can reach the pleural dome. Maximum safe depth: 10-15
  mm with oblique-lateral angle. Same risk mechanism as LU1.
- **Axillary vessels:** Axillary artery and vein lie deep to
  pectoralis minor, 20-40 mm from skin surface. Puncture risk
  with deep needling, especially in thin patients. Potential for
  significant hemorrhage.
- **Brachial plexus injury:** Lateral cord of brachial plexus
  accompanies axillary vessels. Deep needling risks nerve injury
  with potential motor/sensory deficit in the arm.
- **Cephalic vein:** Visible superficially in the deltopectoral
  groove. Minor puncture risk — avoid visible veins.

Direction: oblique-lateral ONLY. NEVER perpendicular or medial.

Contraindicated in patients on anticoagulants (deep vessel risk)
and those with emphysema (increased pneumothorax risk).

## LU3 — Thiên Phủ (天府, Tiān Fǔ)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Celestial Palace." A **Window of Heaven** (天窗/天府) point — one
of approximately 10 points from Ling Shu chapters 2 and 21
traditionally used for conditions affecting the head, throat,
and sensory organs. LU3 is architecturally unique within this
group: it is the ONLY Window of Heaven point located on a LIMB.
All others (LI18, ST9, BL10, SI16, SI17, TE16, CV22, GV16, PC1)
are positioned on the neck, head, or trunk. This limb placement
challenges the traditional rationale that Window of Heaven points
function as physical "gates" between the body and head — LU3
requires a non-local mechanism or relies on the Lung channel's
internal pathway (which ascends to the throat and nose).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the medial aspect of the
upper arm, 3 cun below the anterior axillary fold, in the
depression on the radial (lateral) side of the biceps brachii
muscle.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the anterior axillary fold (formed
by the lower border of pectoralis major). Measure 3 cun (~75 mm)
distally along the medial arm. The point lies in the groove
between the biceps brachii (lateral/anterior) and the
coracobrachialis → medial head of triceps (medial/posterior),
on the lateral border of the biceps.

Needle depth range: 10-20 mm perpendicular. Biceps brachii
muscle belly is the target tissue. The musculocutaneous nerve
runs within or deep to the biceps at this level — standard
depth is safe.

Relationship to adjacent points: 1 cun proximal to LU4. 3 cun
distal to the axillary fold (where the channel exits the trunk).
LU3 and LU4 share nearly identical anatomy — differentiation is
classical, not structural (see Batch 4 Observation 19).

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply: lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm (C5-C6),
terminal sensory branch of the musculocutaneous nerve (C5-C7).
At this level, the musculocutaneous nerve is still within the
substance of the biceps brachii (having pierced coracobrachialis
superiorly), running between biceps and brachialis.

Segmental input: C5-C6 (primary dermatome for medial-lateral
arm). Solidly brachial plexus territory (lateral cord →
musculocutaneous nerve). Neural transition from LU2: cervical
plexus (C3-C4 cutaneous) → brachial plexus (C5-C6) occurs
between LU2 and LU3, across the axillary fold.

Vascular: brachial artery runs along the medial side of the arm,
in the medial bicipital groove. At LU3's lateral biceps position,
the brachial artery is ~15-20 mm medial to the needle point.
Muscular branches of the brachial artery supply the biceps.

Tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → bicipital fascia →
biceps brachii muscle belly → musculocutaneous nerve (within or
deep to biceps) → brachialis muscle.

Ref: A01 (musculocutaneous nerve pathway), A04 (arm fascial
compartments).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Window of Heaven (天窗 tiān chuāng / 天府 tiān fǔ group)**

Classical source: Ling Shu (灵枢) chapters 2 and 21. The Window
of Heaven concept identifies ~10 acupoints as "celestial windows"
— portals where Qi ascending to the head can be regulated.
Traditional indications cluster around: loss of consciousness,
disorientation, throat obstruction, goiter, sensory disturbances
(vision, hearing), epistaxis.

The approximately 10 Window of Heaven points:
- **LU3 Tianfu (天府)** — medial arm [ONLY limb point]
- LI18 Futu (扶突) — anterolateral neck
- ST9 Renying (人迎) — anterolateral neck, carotid pulse
- SI16 Tianchuang (天窗) — lateral neck
- SI17 Tianrong (天容) — below angle of mandible
- BL10 Tianzhu (天柱) — posterior neck
- TE16 Tianyou (天牖) — posterolateral neck
- CV22 Tiantu (天突) — suprasternal notch
- GV16 Fengfu (风府) — suboccipital
- PC1 Tianchi (天池) — lateral chest (some traditions)

Anatomical pattern of the group: 8-9 of 10 points are on the
neck or head-trunk junction, surrounding the throat. They
encircle the pathways between body and brain — carotid arteries
(ST9), vertebral arteries (BL10, GV16), jugular veins, trachea
(CV22), major cervical nerves. LU3 breaks this pattern entirely,
sitting on the mid-upper arm with no anatomical proximity to the
head or throat.

LU3's Window of Heaven rationale likely depends on the Lung
channel's INTERNAL pathway, which ascends from the middle jiao
through the diaphragm, enters the Lung organ, rises to the
throat, and emerges at LU1. The internal-external connection
makes LU3 a "window" through the channel pathway rather than
through anatomical proximity. This is the same non-local logic
that explains distal acupoint effects generally.

### Cross-tradition mapping

| System | Overlap? | Details |
|---|---|---|
| TCM | YES | Window of Heaven point. Nosebleed, asthma, goiter, somnolence, sadness/weeping. Rich classical tradition from Ling Shu. |
| Trigger Point | PARTIAL | Biceps brachii TrPs (Travell & Simons) exist in the muscle belly. Overlap is anatomical (same site) not functional (different referral). |

the lowest on the Lung channel — consistent with the convergence

cross-tradition convergence at a point with rich CLASSICAL
tradition highlights a key pattern: Window of Heaven indications
(head/throat effects from arm stimulation) are entirely TCM-
specific and find no independent corroboration in other
traditions. LU3 is the most tradition-dependent point on the
Lung channel.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Classical (Ling Shu, Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing, Zhenjiu Dacheng):
- Epistaxis (鼻衄) — nosebleed is the most cited single indication
- Asthma (哮喘), dyspnea, cough
- Goiter (瘿气) — thyroid enlargement, throat masses
- Somnolence (多寐), excessive sleeping, disorientation
- Sadness, weeping (悲泣) — Metal/grief emotion association
- Ghostly (鬼) presentations — some classical sources list Window
  of Heaven points for psychiatric/spiritual conditions

Modern standard (Deadman, WHO):
- Epistaxis, cough, asthma, wheezing
- Pain of the medial aspect of the upper arm
- Dizziness, disorientation

The modern indication list is notably shorter and more
conservative than classical sources — goiter, somnolence, and
spiritual indications are generally omitted from contemporary
texts.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No point-specific RCT or systematic review for LU3. No controlled
study has tested LU3 for epistaxis (its headline classical
indication) or any other condition. LU3 occasionally appears in
multi-point protocols for asthma or rhinitis but is never
isolated.

The Window of Heaven group as a whole lacks systematic clinical
evaluation. No study has tested whether this point group has
distinct effects on head/throat conditions compared to non-Window
points at equivalent anatomical locations.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**LOW** risk. Standard medial arm point safety profile.

Precautions:
- Brachial artery is ~15-20 mm medial to the point — at standard
  perpendicular insertion depth (10-20 mm), the needle targets
  biceps muscle belly. Risk only with medially-directed deep
  insertion.
- Musculocutaneous nerve runs within or deep to biceps at this
  level. Usually protected at standard depth but can be
  encountered with deep insertion (>25 mm). Patient may report
  electric shock sensation down the lateral forearm if nerve is
  contacted — withdraw and redirect.
- No pneumothorax risk (arm, not chest).

## LU4 — Hiệp Bạch (侠白, Xiá Bái)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Guarding White" / "Clasping White." Located 1 cun distal to
LU3 in the same muscle (biceps brachii), with the same nerve
supply (musculocutaneous/lateral cutaneous, C5-C6), and
overlapping anatomy. LU4 has no special category designation and
the most limited clinical tradition of any Lung channel point.
It demonstrates the phenomenon of "anatomical near-identity" —
two points sharing virtually identical structures yet assigned
different classical significance. LU3 (Window of Heaven, rich
tradition) versus LU4 (no category, minimal tradition) = the
most extreme functional divergence between anatomically adjacent
points on the Lung channel.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the medial aspect of the
upper arm, 4 cun below the anterior axillary fold, on the radial
(lateral) side of the biceps brachii muscle.

Anatomical landmarks: 1 cun (~25 mm) distal to LU3, still in
the lateral groove of the biceps brachii. The point lies in the
same muscular compartment and fascial plane as LU3 — identical
gross anatomy.

Needle depth range: 10-20 mm perpendicular. Same depth and
target tissue as LU3.

Relationship to adjacent points: 1 cun distal to LU3,
approximately 5 cun proximal to LU5 (cubital crease). The
LU3-LU4 pair is separated by the standard minimum inter-point
distance on limb channels (~25 mm). Compare HT5-HT6 (0.5 cun
= ~12 mm, even closer).

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply: lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm (C5-C6),
same as LU3. The musculocutaneous nerve continues through the
biceps at this level, running in the same fascial plane. No
meaningful neural differentiation from LU3 — the two points
share identical segmental input and peripheral nerve territory.

Segmental input: C5-C6. Same as LU3. No neural transition
between the two points.

Vascular: brachial artery remains ~15-20 mm medial in the
medial bicipital groove. Muscular branches to biceps are the
only local vessels.

Tissue layers: identical to LU3 — skin → subcutaneous fat →
bicipital fascia → biceps brachii → musculocutaneous nerve →
brachialis.

Ref: A01 (same nerve as LU3 — musculocutaneous territory).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

No special category designation. LU4 is a standard channel point
(经穴) without any categorical membership.

LU4's lack of classification is notable given its identical
anatomy to LU3 (Window of Heaven). The categorical difference
between adjacent anatomically-identical points suggests that
classical point category assignment was driven by empirical
clinical observation and textual tradition rather than by
anatomical logic. LU3 was named in Ling Shu chapter 21 as a
Window of Heaven point; LU4 was not. The categorization followed
the text, not the tissue.

### Cross-tradition mapping

|---|---|---|
| TCM | YES | Lung channel point. Cough, chest fullness, palpitations, upper arm pain. Minimal traditional emphasis — the least-cited LU point in major classical texts. |

| Ashi | PARTIAL | Local tenderness in upper arm conditions. Same limited overlap as LU3. |

Convergence: **2/8** (TCM + partial TrP). Identical score to
LU3. The two points are architecturally indistinguishable by
cross-tradition mapping — their differentiation is entirely
intra-TCM (Window of Heaven designation vs. no category).

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Classical (Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing, Zhenjiu Dacheng):
- Cough (咳嗽), chest fullness (胸满)
- Palpitations (心悸), cardiac awareness
- Upper arm pain, medial arm numbness
- Vomiting (呕吐) — occasional citation

- Cough, chest pain
- Pain of the medial aspect of the upper arm

The indication list is the shortest of any LU point. Classical
sources typically mention LU4 briefly and without the

rarely select LU4 — it has been largely superseded by LU3
(proximal, more tradition), LU5 (cubital crease, He-Sea), and

local Ashi for arm pain.

No point-specific RCT, systematic review, or case series for

Precautions:

  Withdraw on electric sensation.

### Batch 4 Observations (LU2, LU3, LU4)

**Observation 17: LU2 = highest composite risk on the channel**
LU2 combines pneumothorax risk (shared with LU1) AND axillary
vessel/brachial plexus risk — two independent hazard vectors at
one point. Among LU points, LU1 has pneumothorax risk alone
(HIGH), while LU2 adds vascular/neural risk (HIGH composite).
Despite this, LU2 has no special category designation — it is a
"transit" point where the channel crosses the major neurovascular
bundle entering the arm. Compare LR10 and LR12 (high vascular
risk, also no special category). Pattern: high-risk points
without special designation tend to occur at anatomical transit
zones — infraclavicular (LU2), inguinal (LR10, LR12) — where
channels cross major neurovascular bundles between body regions.

**Observation 18: Window of Heaven — the only limb point**
LU3 is the ONLY Window of Heaven point on a limb (all ~9 others
are neck/head/trunk). This breaks the anatomical logic of the
group: Window of Heaven points typically encircle the throat,
surrounding the pathways (carotid, jugular, trachea, vertebral
arteries) between body and brain. LU3 at the mid-arm has no
anatomical proximity to any of these structures. Its segmental
input (C5-C6) is not within pulmonary or head/throat
viscerotomes. The rationale requires either: (a) the Lung
channel's INTERNAL pathway (which ascends to throat) as a non-
physical connecting mechanism, or (b) LU3's inclusion was
empirically driven (observed effects, possibly placebo-mediated)
and post-hoc justified by channel theory. Cross-tradition
convergence (2/8) supports option (b) — no independent tradition
maps the mid-arm for head/throat conditions.

**Observation 19: LU3-LU4 anatomical near-identity**
LU3 and LU4 are separated by 1 cun (~25 mm) in the same muscle
(biceps brachii), same nerve territory (musculocutaneous →
lateral cutaneous, C5-C6), same vascular context, same fascial
plane. Yet LU3 has Window of Heaven designation with rich
classical tradition, while LU4 has no designation and the most
limited clinical tradition on the channel. This parallels the
HT6-HT7 question from AP-05 (0.5 cun apart, same nerve — is
functional subdivision anatomically real?). The LU3-LU4 gap is
WIDER (1 cun vs. 0.5 cun) but the functional divergence is MORE
extreme (rich tradition vs. minimal). Conclusion: classical point
categorization was not purely anatomy-driven. Textual naming
(Ling Shu ch.21 named LU3, not LU4) and accumulated clinical
tradition created functional divergence that anatomy alone cannot
explain.

**Observation 20: Safety transition at the axillary fold**
The four points LU1→LU2→LU3→LU4 show a sharp safety transition:
- LU1: HIGH (pneumothorax)
- LU2: HIGH (pneumothorax + axillary vessels + brachial plexus)
- LU3: LOW (standard arm point)
- LU4: LOW (standard arm point)
The transition from HIGH to LOW occurs precisely at the anterior
axillary fold — where the channel moves from trunk to limb. This
is the sharpest safety gradient on any channel traversing a
body-region boundary. Compare LR (gradual risk increase from
thigh to inguinal over 4 points: LR9 LOW → LR10 HIGH → LR11
MODERATE → LR12 HIGH). The Lung channel's abrupt transition
reflects the anatomical reality: lung apex and axillary vessels
are immediately deep to the infraclavicular fossa (LU2), but 3
cun distally on the arm (LU3) the hazardous structures are no
longer accessible.

**Observation 21: Neural transition — cervical to brachial plexus**
LU1 (T1-T2 intercostal + C3-C4 phrenic), LU2 (C3-C4
supraclavicular + C5-C7 lateral pectoral), LU3-LU4 (C5-C6
musculocutaneous). The channel crosses from intercostal
innervation through the cervical-brachial plexus transition zone
to pure brachial plexus territory in 4 points spanning ~150 mm.
This transition zone includes the brachial plexus itself — the
channel literally crosses the neural hub where cervical cord
segments (C5-T1) organize into the peripheral nerve trunks
serving the entire upper limb. In terms of neural complexity per
unit distance, LU1-LU3 is the densest neural transition on the
channel.

## LU8 — Kinh Cừ (经渠, Jīng Qú)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
Jing-River/Metal — the HORARY point of the Lung channel (Metal on
Metal = self-element resonance). Located 1 cun above the wrist
crease directly over the radial artery. Classical MOXA PROHIBITION
(禁灸) — the only point on the Lung channel with a specific
modality restriction. Third point in the radial artery corridor
(LU7-LU8-LU9), and the most vascular-intimate of the three.
Completes the Five Shu set for the Lung channel — the fourth
complete set in the Atlas. Limited modern clinical use; primarily
theoretical importance as the channel's self-element resonance
point for horary treatment.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the radial aspect of the
forearm, 1 cun above the transverse wrist crease, in the
depression between the styloid process of the radius and the
radial artery.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the radial artery proximal to the
wrist crease — LU8 is on the radial (lateral) side of the
pulsation, in the groove between the radial styloid and the
artery. 1 cun proximal to LU9 (which is at the crease). The
tendon of brachioradialis is palpable laterally.

Needle depth range: 3-5 mm perpendicular or oblique-lateral.
Must angle away from the radial artery. The point is directly
over the artery — the most vascular-intimate needling site on
the distal Lung channel.

Relationship to adjacent points: 1 cun proximal to LU9, 0.5 cun
distal to LU7. These three points (LU7-LU8-LU9) form the RADIAL
ARTERY CORRIDOR — three consecutive points defined by their
relationship to a single named artery. No other completed channel
has three adjacent points sharing one artery as their defining
anatomical landmark.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply: superficial branch of radial nerve (C6-C7) —
primary cutaneous innervation. Lateral antebrachial cutaneous
nerve (C5-C6) contributes. Same nerve territory as LU9 (1 cun
distal) — no neural differentiation between LU8 and LU9.

Segmental input: C5-C7. Consistent with the radial wrist zone.
Distal from pulmonary viscerotomes (T1-T4) by 8-10 segments —
the standard non-segmental gap for distal LU points.

Vascular: RADIAL ARTERY directly deep to the point — the
defining anatomical relationship. The artery is palpable and
often visible in lean individuals. Radial veins (venae
comitantes) accompany the artery. Unlike LU9 (where the point
is LATERAL to the artery), LU8 is directly OVER the artery.

Tissue layers (superficial → deep): skin → subcutaneous fat
(thin at wrist) → antebrachial fascia → radial artery + venae
comitantes → flexor pollicis longus tendon → pronator quadratus
→ periosteum of distal radius.

Ref: A01 (radial nerve territory), A04 (antebrachial fascia).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Jing-River (经, Jīng) / Metal (金)**

Five Shu position: 4th (River). Five Element: Metal. On the
Metal-organ (Lung) channel, this creates METAL ON METAL = horary
point (same element as the channel's organ). The horary point is
used during the organ's peak time (03:00-05:00) to strengthen
the organ's inherent element.

Horary point positions across completed channels:
- Lung (Metal): LU8 = River/Metal — horary
- Liver (Wood): LR1 = Well/Wood — horary
- Heart (Fire): HT8 = Spring/Fire — horary
- PC (Min. Fire): PC8 = Spring/Fire — horary
Each organ places the horary at a different Five Shu position
(except Heart and Pericardium, which share Fire and thus both
fall at Spring). The pattern is deterministic: the horary always
falls where the organ's own element appears in the Five Shu
sequence.

Five Shu complete set for Lung: LU11 (Well/Wood) → LU10
(Spring/Fire) → LU9 (Stream/Earth) → LU8 (River/Metal) → LU5
(Sea/Water). Fourth complete set in the Atlas (after PC, HT, LR).

**Classical moxa prohibition (禁灸, jìn jiǔ):**
Multiple classical texts (Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing, Zhenjiu Dacheng)
explicitly prohibit moxibustion at LU8. Rationale: the radial
artery is directly beneath the point — moxa heat risks thermal
injury to the arterial wall. This is a MODALITY-SPECIFIC
restriction: needling is permitted (with care), moxa is not.
Compare LR10 (needling prohibited, moxa permitted in some texts)
— the inverse restriction, reflecting different risk mechanisms:
- LU8: thermal risk → arterial wall damage. Needling safe if
  shallow.
- LR10: mechanical risk → arterial puncture. Moxa safe (surface
  only).
Classical practitioners performed modality-specific risk
assessment at the individual point level — a more nuanced safety
system than blanket prohibition.

|---|---|---|
| TCM | YES | Lung channel point. Cough, asthma, chest pain, febrile disease, wrist pain. Horary point for constitutional strengthening during 03:00-05:00. |
| Trigger Point | NO | No standard trigger point at the distal radial wrist. Forearm extensor TrPs are more proximal. |
| Ashi | PARTIAL | Local tenderness relevant for wrist pain, repetitive strain. Functional rather than tradition-mapped. |
| Marma | NO | Manibandha marma (wrist joint) maps more centrally across the wrist crease (LU9/HT7 zone), not 1 cun proximal. |
| Head zone | NO | C5-C7 dermatome. Not within pulmonary Head zone (T1-T4). |
| Anatomy Trains | NO | Deep Front Arm Line descends through the forearm but has no specific node or transition at this location. |
| Neurogenic spot | No data | |
| Cupping | NO | Not applicable to distal wrist. |

Convergence: **2/8** (TCM + partial Ashi). Consistent with other
distal LU points (LU3: 2/8, LU4: 2/8). Distal wrist points show
low cross-tradition convergence because independent traditions
rarely map wrist-specific locations for internal organ conditions.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Classical (Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing, Zhenjiu Dacheng):
- Cough (咳嗽), dyspnea (喘)
- Chest fullness (胸满), chest pain
- Sore throat (咽痛) — less emphasized than at LU10/LU11
- Febrile disease (发热), malaria (疟疾)

- Cough, asthma
- Sore throat

### An toàn

**MODERATE** risk — elevated for a distal point due to radial
artery intimacy.

Precautions:
- Radial artery DIRECTLY DEEP to the point. Palpate before
  needling and insert on the radial (lateral) side of the pulse.
  Withdraw immediately if arterial pulsation is felt through the
  needle.
- **MOXA PROHIBITED** (classical consensus). Thermal injury risk
  to radial artery wall. Direct moxa, indirect moxa, and warm
  needle (温针灸) all contraindicated at this location.
- Shallow insertion only (3-5 mm). Deep perpendicular insertion
  risks arterial puncture → hematoma.

LU11 bloodletting for acute pharyngitis/tonsillitis: multiple

solitarius) → vagal modulation of pharyngeal inflammation.

Neither pathway verified.

- All other indications: 🔴 Unvalidated.

**LOW** risk. Minimal tissue depth, no significant structures.

Overall: one of the SAFEST points on the body. The primary
practical concern is infection control (sterile technique for
