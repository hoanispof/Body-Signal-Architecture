# AP-14 — Conception Vessel (CV/REN) — Ren Mai (任脉)

> **Status:** Phase 2 COMPLETE. CV1–CV24 documented (all 24). Review DONE.
>
> **Vessel overview:** The Conception Vessel (Ren Mai, 任脉) is one of
> the 8 Extraordinary Vessels and one of only 2 (with Du Mai) that
> possess their own acupoints. Runs along the anterior midline:
> perineum (CV1 Huiyin) → pubic symphysis → ascending through lower
> abdomen → umbilicus (CV8 Shenque) → upper abdomen → sternum → throat
> (CV22 Tiantu) → chin (CV24 Chengjiang). Called "Sea of all Yin
> Meridians" (阴脉之海) — all Yin channels connect with Ren Mai.
> Governs reproduction, conception, and Yin Qi. The Yin counterpart
> to Du Mai (posterior midline, "Sea of all Yang"). Together they form
> the "Small Heavenly Circuit" (小周天): GV ascending posterior, CV
> ascending anterior, meeting at perineum (CV1↔GV1) and oral cavity
> (CV24↔GV28). Accessed via Eight Confluent pair: LU7 (Lieque) + KI6
> (Zhaohai). 24 points total. No paired channel. No Chinese clock.
>
> **Key features:** 6/12 Front-Mu points (most of any channel).
> 2/8 Hui-Meeting points (CV12 Fu, CV17 Qi). 2 Ghost Points (CV1 #11,
> CV24 #8). Window of Heaven CV22. Luo-Connecting CV15. CV8 = ONLY
> moxa-only point in the 361-point system. KI channel runs parallel
> 0.5–2 cun lateral (KI11–KI27).
>
> **Total points:** 24 standard | Phase 2: CV1–CV24

## Perineal–Lower Abdomen Region (CV1–CV4)

Four points from the perineum (CV1) to 3 cun below the umbilicus
(CV4). This region spans the pelvic floor to the lower hypogastric
zone of the Ren Mai, from the lowest point on the anterior midline
to the "Gate of Origin" at the level of the bladder and uterus.
Nerve supply transitions from the pudendal nerve territory (S2–S4)
at CV1 through the ilioinguinal and iliohypogastric nerves (L1) at
CV2–CV3 to the subcostal and iliohypogastric territory (T12–L1) at
CV4. The KI channel corridor begins at this level: KI11 sits 0.5
cun lateral to CV2, KI12 to CV3, KI13 to CV4. Three of four points
carry special designations: CV1 (Ghost Point #11 + triple Extraordinary Vessel
meeting), CV3 (Front-Mu of Bladder + Three Yin meeting), CV4
(Front-Mu of Small Intestine + Three Yin meeting + Gate of Origin).

### CV1 — Hội Âm (会阴, Huìyīn)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Meeting of Yin" (会 = meeting, 阴 = Yin). On the perineum, at the
midpoint between the anus and the posterior border of the external
genitalia (scrotum in males, posterior labial commissure in females).
The LOWEST point on the anterior midline and the meeting point of
three extraordinary vessels: Ren Mai (CV), Du Mai (GV), and Chong
Mai. Also **Ghost Point #11 (鬼藏 Gui Cang, "Ghost Store/Hide")** in
Sun Si-miao's 13 Ghost Points — part of the 4th trinity (GV23 #10 +
CV1 #11 + LI11 #12 + EX-HN11 #13). The Ghost name 鬼藏 (hidden/stored)
suggests pathology concealed in the most private, Yin location.
Drowning rescue, prolapse, urogenital disorders.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the perineum, at the center
of the line connecting the anus to the root of the scrotum (males)
or to the posterior labial commissure (females).

Anatomical landmarks: patient in supine with hips flexed, or in
lithotomy position. The point lies on the perineal body (central
tendon of the perineum) — the fibromuscular node between the
urogenital and anal triangles.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.0 cun (~1.2–2.5 cm) perpendicular. The
perineal body is ~3–4 cm thick in males, ~2–3 cm in females.
Superficial needling into the perineal body is safe; deeper
insertion risks urethral or rectal puncture.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Perineal nerve** — terminal branch of the pudendal nerve
  (S2–S4), providing sensory innervation to the perineal skin and
  motor innervation to the bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus
  muscles.
- **Inferior rectal nerve** — branch of pudendal nerve (S2–S4),
  overlapping from the posterior perineum.
- **Posterior labial/scrotal nerves** — perineal branches of the
  pudendal nerve providing cutaneous innervation.
- Spinal segments: S2–S4 (pudendal nerve territory).
- The pudendal nerve is the primary somatic nerve of the perineum,
  passing through Alcock's canal (pudendal canal) along the lateral
  wall of the ischioanal fossa.

Fascial plane: perineal body (central perineal tendon) — the
fibromuscular junction where the bulbospongiosus, external anal
sphincter, superficial and deep transverse perineal muscles, and the
anterior fibers of the levator ani all converge. This is the most
complex muscular intersection in the pelvic floor.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1.3, A03

Vascular: perineal artery and vein (branches of internal pudendal
artery/vein). Rich perineal venous plexus.

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous perineal fat → perineal body
(fibromuscular node). The perineal body is the central anchor of
the entire pelvic floor — its integrity is essential for pelvic
organ support.

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No (Ren Mai = extraordinary vessel). Yuan-Source: No.
Xi-Cleft: No. Back-Shu: No. Front-Mu: No. Eight Confluent: No.
Hui-Meeting: No. Luo-Connecting: No.

**Ghost Point #11 — 鬼藏 (Gui Cang, "Ghost Store/Hide").** The
11th point in Sun Si-miao's 13 Ghost Points (鬼穴十三针). Part of
the 4th trinity: GV23 (#10 鬼堂) + CV1 (#11 鬼藏) + LI11 (#12
鬼腿/鬼臣) + EX-HN11 (#13 鬼封). The 4th trinity addresses the
deepest/most refractory level of "possession" or mental illness.
CV1's placement as #11 — late in the sequence — suggests it was
reserved for resistant cases requiring access to the most hidden
(藏) bodily territory. The perineum is the most Yin location on
the body, consistent with the Ghost Point tradition's progressive
deepening: from surface (#1 GV26 philtrum) toward hidden interior
(#11 CV1 perineum).
→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV23 (#10), AP-13 GV26 (#1), AP-02 LI11 (#12),
AP-09 PC7 (#4), AP-07 BL62 (#5)

**Meeting point (交会穴):** CV1 is the meeting point of three
extraordinary vessels: Ren Mai + Du Mai + Chong Mai. This triple
convergence at the perineum represents the lowest junction of the
body's three major midline/axial vessels:
- Ren Mai: ascends the anterior midline from CV1 to CV24
- Du Mai: ascends the posterior midline from GV1 to GV28
- Chong Mai: ascends through the interior, emerging as KI11–KI21

The perineum is where the two midline extraordinary vessels (CV
ascending anterior, GV ascending posterior) share their origin
point, before diverging along opposite surfaces of the body. In
classical theory, this is the root of both Yin (CV) and Yang (GV).
→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV1 (GV starting point, meeting GV+CV)

#### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** CV1 (Huiyin 会阴). Functions: resuscitates
from drowning, regulates Ren Mai and Du Mai, clears Damp-Heat,
benefits the Yin orifices (urethra, vagina, anus). The triple Extraordinary Vessel
meeting makes CV1 the theoretical root of all three midline
extraordinary vessels. ✓

**2. Trigger point:** the perineal body and surrounding muscles
(bulbospongiosus, transverse perineal, levator ani) develop trigger
points in chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CPPS), vulvodynia, and
proctalgia fugax. Perineal trigger points refer pain to the
perineum, genitals, and lower abdomen. CV1 overlies the central
convergence of these muscles. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** perineal tenderness is a diagnostic
feature of chronic pelvic pain, pudendal neuralgia, and
vulvodynia/prostatitis. The perineal body (CV1 location) is the
standard palpation point in pelvic floor physiotherapy assessment.
🟡

**4. Marma point:** Vitapa Marma (विटप, "Groin/Perineum") — located
at the junction between the lower abdomen and the thigh, in the
perineal region. Classified as Snayu (ligament/tendon) Marma. Size:
1 anguli. Injury consequence: infertility/impotence (Vaikalyakara).
Some authors map the Yoni Marma directly to the perineum. The
Ayurvedic tradition recognizes this region as governing reproductive
and urinary function, paralleling CV1's indications. ~2–3 cm. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** S2–S4 dermatome covers the perineal skin
("saddle area"). Viscerotome: S2–S4 parasympathetic outflow (pelvic
splanchnic nerves) innervates bladder, rectum, and reproductive
organs. CV1's perineal location sits at the center of the sacral
viscerotome for ALL pelvic organs. 🟡

**6. Anatomy Trains:** the Deep Front Line (DFL) includes the
pelvic floor as a transverse diaphragm. The perineal body is the
central node where DFL forces converge from all four directions
(anterior urogenital, posterior anal, bilateral levator ani). CV1
sits at the most central point of this fascial intersection. 🟡

**7. Neurogenic spot:** no data. Kim 2017 did not map the perineal
region. ✗

**8. Cupping point:** not applicable. Perineal location prevents
cupping. ✗

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Marma 🟡, Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡). High convergence for a
perineal point, matching GV1 (6/8) at the opposite end of the
pelvic floor. The perineum is a multi-tradition recognized zone.

#### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Neijing (Ling Shu Ch.10 Jing Mai): Ren Mai pathway begins at
  the perineum, ascends to the throat
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: drowning rescue (primary emergency), genital
  disorders, hemorrhoids, urinary retention, enuresis, perineal pain,
  mania/epilepsy
- Qianjin Yaofang (Sun Si-miao): Ghost Point #11 for refractory
  psychiatric conditions — "Ghost Store" at the most hidden location

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Drowning, loss of consciousness (emergency resuscitation)
- Genital pain, impotence, vaginal discharge, genital eczema
- Hemorrhoids, rectal prolapse
- Urinary retention, enuresis
- Epilepsy, mania (via Ren Mai spirit function)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- RARELY needled in clinical practice due to anatomical sensitivity
  and patient modesty — often substituted by nearby points (CV2,
  CV3) or distal Ren Mai points
- Moxa at CV1 for prolapse (supporting upward Qi movement)
- Kegel-style perineal contraction exercises combined with CV1
  acupressure for pelvic floor rehabilitation
- Classical drowning rescue protocol: moxa at CV1 + GV26 fingernail
  pressure + KI1 stimulation

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Chronic pelvic pain — 🔴 Unvalidated**

No SR/MA specific to CV1 for chronic pelvic pain, despite the
anatomical rationale. The perineal body is a standard physiotherapy
target, but acupuncture evidence at CV1 specifically is absent.

**2. Drowning / resuscitation — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Classical primary emergency indication. No modern evidence supports
acupuncture at CV1 for resuscitation. Modern emergency medicine
uses CPR and AED, not perineal stimulation. The classical protocol
likely reflects historical practice before standardized
resuscitation methods.

**3. Pelvic floor dysfunction — 🟡 Plausible**

Acupuncture is studied for urinary incontinence and pelvic floor
disorders, but protocols typically use CV3, CV4, SP6, BL32–34
rather than CV1 specifically. The pudendal nerve (S2–S4) accessed
at CV1 is the primary motor nerve of the pelvic floor, providing
a plausible mechanism. Electroacupuncture at sacral points (BL33)
for stress urinary incontinence has Cochrane support, but CV1 is
not typically included in these protocols.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The perineal region (CV1 area) becomes sensitized in:
- Chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CPPS): allodynia and hyperalgesia
  at the perineal body — lowered pressure pain threshold
- Pudendal neuralgia: neuropathic sensitization of S2–S4 territory
- Vulvodynia: vestibular and perineal hypersensitivity
- Prostatitis (NIH category III): perineal tenderness is a cardinal
  diagnostic sign

No specific published data on CV1 threshold changes comparable to
trunk/limb sensitization studies (Kim 2017).
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: sacral segmental mechanism.**
Pudendal nerve / perineal nerve (S2–S4) → sacral dorsal horn →
bidirectional output:
- Somatic motor: external urethral sphincter, external anal
  sphincter, pelvic floor muscles (via pudendal motor fibers)
- Parasympathetic: S2–S4 pelvic splanchnic nerves → pelvic
  ganglia → bladder detrusor, rectal smooth muscle, reproductive
  organs

The S2–S4 segments are the spinal hub for ALL pelvic organ
function. CV1 accesses this hub via the perineal branch of the
pudendal nerve, which carries both sensory (afferent) and motor
(efferent) fibers. This provides a segmental mechanism for the
traditional indications: urogenital, anorectal, and reproductive
effects are mediated via the same S2–S4 spinal segments.

**Ghost Point mechanism:** psychiatric indications (mania,
epilepsy) attributed to Ren Mai "spirit-calming" function have no
direct segmental explanation from the S2–S4 territory. Unlike
GV26 (V2 → ARAS) or GV20 (vertex cranial nerve convergence),
CV1's perineal access does not project to consciousness-related
brainstem nuclei. The Ghost Point tradition may reflect:
(a) strong nocifensive arousal from perineal stimulation
(noxious stimulus → general alerting via spinothalamic tract), or
(b) symbolic/psychological significance of the perineum in
historical conceptions of mental illness.

Gap: no mechanism identified for the drowning/resuscitation
indication. The classical protocol (moxa at CV1 for drowning)
predates understanding of cardiopulmonary physiology.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1.3, A07 S4.2

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–1.0 cun perpendicular. De Qi: distending sensation
in perineum, may radiate to genitals or anus. Patient positioning:
supine with hips flexed, lithotomy, or lateral decubitus. Due to
anatomical sensitivity, many practitioners prefer indirect
stimulation (acupressure, moxa) over needling.
EA: rarely documented at CV1 specifically.

Bấm huyệt (B02): pressure at perineal body — standard pelvic
floor rehabilitation technique. Often combined with Kegel exercises.
Moderate sustained pressure 30–60 seconds.

Cứu (B08): indirect moxa preferred (moxa stick 2–3 cm above, not
direct cone). Classical drowning protocol used direct moxa at CV1.

Dry needling (B11): not applicable — pelvic floor dry needling
typically targets levator ani via transvaginal/transrectal approach,
not the midline perineal body.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1, B02, B08

#### An toàn

**Anatomical sensitivity:** the perineum is highly innervated
(pudendal nerve) — needling is often painful. Patient consent and
comfort are essential.

**Depth risk:** perpendicular needling deeper than ~2.5 cm risks
puncturing the membranous urethra (males) or vaginal wall (females).
The rectum lies posterior. Safe depth is limited to the perineal
body thickness.

**Pregnancy:** CV1 is traditionally CONTRAINDICATED during
pregnancy — perineal stimulation may affect pelvic floor tone and
is avoided as a precautionary measure.

**Hygiene:** perineal location requires strict antiseptic technique.

### CV2 — Khúc Cốt (曲骨, Qūgǔ)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Curved Bone" (曲 = curved, 骨 = bone) — named for the pubic
symphysis (the curved bony landmark). On the anterior midline, at
the superior border of the pubic symphysis. Meeting point of CV
+ Liver channel. The pubic symphysis is the anterior bony landmark
of the pelvis and the inferior boundary of the anterior abdominal
wall. Urinary and reproductive disorders.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anterior midline, at
the midpoint of the upper border of the symphysis pubis.
Anatomical landmarks: palpate the upper border of the pubic
symphysis; CV2 is at the midline of this border, in the depression
immediately above the bone.
Needle depth: 0.5–1.0 cun perpendicular. The needle passes through
skin, subcutaneous fat, linea alba, and transversalis fascia. The
bladder lies directly posterior (deeper) when distended.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: anterior cutaneous branch of the iliohypogastric nerve
(L1) and the ilioinguinal nerve (L1). Spinal segment: L1. The
pubic region marks the inferior extent of the anterior abdominal
wall innervation.
Vascular: superficial epigastric artery (branch of femoral artery)
at the skin level. Deep: external iliac artery/vein lie
posterolateral to the pubic symphysis.
Fascial: linea alba attachment to pubic symphysis — the inferior
anchor of the midline anterior abdominal fascia.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03

#### Phân loại huyệt

No standard Five Shu, Yuan-Source, Xi-Cleft, Back-Shu, Front-Mu,
Eight Confluent, or Hui-Meeting designations.

**Meeting point (交会穴):** CV2 is the meeting of CV + Liver
channel (足厥阴). The Liver channel's internal pathway reaches
the genitalia and lower abdomen, meeting CV at the pubic region.
→ Cross-ref: AP-12 (LR internal pathway)

**KI corridor:** KI11 (Henggu 横骨, "Pubic Bone") sits 0.5 cun
lateral to CV2, on the superior border of the pubic symphysis.
Both points share the L1 innervation and pubic landmark, but
KI11 is lateral (on the rectus abdominis border) while CV2 is
midline (on the linea alba).
→ Cross-ref: AP-08 KI11

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: CV2 (Qugu 曲骨). Urinary retention, dysuria, enuresis,
seminal emission, impotence, vaginal discharge, irregular menses,
hernia. Functions: benefits the Bladder, warms the Kidneys,
regulates the Lower Jiao. ✓
Trigger point: rectus abdominis inferior attachment at the pubic
crest develops trigger points in lower abdominal pain. Pyramidalis
muscle (small, inconstant) attaches to the pubic crest at this
level. 🟡
Ashi: suprapubic tenderness is common in cystitis, bladder
distension, and lower abdominal pain. ✓ as palpation finding. 🟡
Marma: no specific Marma mapped to the pubic symphysis midline.
Lohitaksha Marma is at the groin crease, ~5 cm lateral. ✗
Head zone: L1 dermatome covers the suprapubic region. L1
viscerotome: overlaps with bladder sensory territory (bladder pain
referred to suprapubic region via T11–L2 sympathetic afferents).
🟡
Anatomy Trains: Superficial Front Line (SFL) descends from sternum
→ rectus abdominis → pubic crest. CV2 sits at the inferior anchor
of the SFL. 🟡
Neurogenic: no specific data. ✗
Cupping: not applicable at the pubic symphysis. ✗

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: urinary retention, dysuria, enuresis, seminal
emission, impotence, vaginal discharge, irregular menses, hernia,
lower abdominal distension.

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: combined with CV3 and BL28 for urinary
disorders. Combined with LR3 for hernia (Liver channel meeting at
CV2). Often used as part of lower abdominal protocols rather than
as a standalone point.

#### An toàn

**Bladder puncture risk:** when the bladder is distended, it rises
above the pubic symphysis into the suprapubic space. Deep
perpendicular needling at CV2 with a full bladder risks bladder
wall puncture. **Patient should empty bladder before needling.**

**Pregnancy:** CV2 is in the lower abdominal region — traditionally
contraindicated during pregnancy (along with CV3, CV4, CV5).

### CV3 — Trung Cực (中极, Zhōngjí)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Central Pole" (中 = center/middle, 极 = pole/ultimate) — the
central axis point of the lower abdomen. On the anterior midline,
4 cun below the umbilicus, 1 cun above the pubic symphysis. The
**Front-Mu point of the Bladder (膀胱募穴)** and a meeting point of
CV with three Yin channels (SP + LR + KI). This "Three Yin Meeting"
(三阴交会) at the lower abdomen parallels the famous Three Yin
Meeting at the ankle (SP6) — but at a more proximal, visceral level.
Primary point for urinary disorders.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anterior midline, 4 cun
below the center of the umbilicus, 1 cun above the symphysis pubis.

Anatomical landmarks: midway between the umbilicus and the pubic
symphysis lies CV8 (umbilicus) to CV2 (pubic symphysis) = 5 cun.
CV3 at 4 cun below = 1 cun above CV2. The point lies over the
linea alba, between the medial borders of the two rectus abdominis
muscles.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.5 cun (~1.2–3.5 cm) perpendicular. The
needle passes through skin → subcutaneous fat → linea alba →
transversalis fascia → preperitoneal fat → parietal peritoneum.
The bladder lies posterior at this level (especially when distended).

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Anterior cutaneous branch of the iliohypogastric nerve (L1)**
  — provides sensory innervation to the suprapubic skin.
- **Ilioinguinal nerve (L1)** — may contribute cutaneous branches
  to the lower midline region.
- **Subcostal nerve (T12)** — anterior cutaneous branch may extend
  to this level.
- Spinal segments: T12–L1 (transitional zone between thoracic and
  lumbar segmental innervation).
- Deep: the bladder receives sensory innervation via S2–S4
  parasympathetic afferents (pelvic splanchnic nerves) and T11–L2
  sympathetic afferents (hypogastric plexus). CV3 overlies the
  bladder's projection area.

Fascial plane: linea alba — the midline raphe of the anterior
abdominal wall, formed by the aponeurotic intersection of the
external oblique, internal oblique, and transversus abdominis
muscles. Below the arcuate line (~halfway between umbilicus and
pubic symphysis), the rectus sheath has no posterior layer — the
rectus muscle rests directly on the transversalis fascia. CV3
is at or near this transition.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03

Vascular: superficial epigastric vessels (cutaneous). Deep:
inferior epigastric artery and vein (branches of external iliac)
run within the rectus sheath, lateral to the midline.

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → linea alba (at midline)
or rectus abdominis (if slightly lateral) → transversalis fascia
→ preperitoneal fat → peritoneum.

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Xi-Cleft: No. Back-Shu: No.
Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No. Luo-Connecting: No.

**Front-Mu of the Bladder (膀胱募穴).** CV3 is the anterior
diagnostic and treatment point for the Bladder organ.
Shu-Mu pairing: BL28 (Pangguangshu, S2 level) + CV3.
The Shu-Mu pair shows good segmental alignment: CV3 overlies the
bladder at the suprapubic surface (T12–L1 dermatome, with bladder
sensation primarily via S2–S4 parasympathetic afferents), while
BL28 at S2 accesses the bladder's parasympathetic innervation
directly. Both anterior (CV3) and posterior (BL28) approaches
converge on the S2–S4 pelvic parasympathetic system.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL28 (Back-Shu of Bladder), B01 S3.1

**Meeting point (交会穴):** CV3 is the meeting of CV + SP + LR + KI
(足三阴交会). The three Yin leg channels (Spleen, Liver, Kidney)
converge with CV at the lower abdomen. This "Three Yin Meeting"
pattern parallels SP6 (Sanyinjiao) at the ankle but at a visceral
level — SP6 accesses the three Yin channels at a distal point, CV3
accesses them where they approach their target organs (uterus,
bladder, reproductive organs).
→ Cross-ref: AP-04 SP6 (Three Yin Meeting at ankle), AP-08 KI12,
AP-12 LR internal pathway

#### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** CV3 (Zhongji 中极). Functions: regulates the
Bladder, benefits urination, resolves Damp-Heat in the Lower Jiao,
regulates menstruation, tonifies the Kidneys. Primary Front-Mu
point for ALL bladder pathology. ✓

**2. Trigger point:** rectus abdominis trigger points in the lower
segment (below umbilicus) refer pain to the suprapubic region and
can mimic bladder/urinary symptoms ("pseudo-visceral pain"). The
lower rectus TrP distribution overlaps with CV3 location. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** suprapubic tenderness is the cardinal
sign of cystitis, urinary retention, and bladder distension. CV3
location = standard clinical palpation point for bladder assessment.
🟡

**4. Marma point:** Basti Marma (बस्ति, "Bladder") — located in the
lower abdomen/suprapubic region. One of the 3 Sadyah Pranahara
(immediately lethal) abdominal Marmas. Size: 4 anguli. The
Ayurvedic tradition recognizes this area as governing urinary
function, directly paralleling CV3 as Front-Mu of Bladder. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** T12–L1 dermatome at CV3 surface. Bladder
viscerotome: bladder pain is referred to the suprapubic region via
T11–L2 sympathetic afferents, and to the perineum/urethra via
S2–S4. CV3's location overlaps the T12 anterior referred pain zone
of the bladder. Beissner (2011) mapping: bladder Front-Mu (CV3)
shows spatial overlap with the bladder Head zone maximum point. 🟡

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Front Line (SFL) passes through
rectus abdominis at this level. The Deep Front Line (DFL) includes
the bladder fascia deep to CV3. 🟡

**7. Neurogenic spot:** no specific data for the suprapubic
midline. ✗

**8. Cupping point:** not typically cupped at the lower abdomen
suprapubic region. ✗

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Marma 🟡, Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡). High convergence.
The suprapubic region is a multi-tradition recognized zone for
bladder/urinary function.

#### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Nanjing (Ch.66): names CV3 as the Front-Mu of the Bladder
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: urinary retention, dysuria, enuresis,
  frequent urination, turbid urine, lower abdominal pain, genital
  disorders, irregular menses, vaginal discharge, infertility
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: ALL urinary disorders — CV3 as the primary point.
  Also: seminal emission, impotence, hernia, uterine prolapse

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Urinary retention, dysuria, enuresis, frequent urination
- Cystitis, urethritis (Damp-Heat in the Bladder)
- Irregular menses, dysmenorrhea, amenorrhea, vaginal discharge
- Seminal emission, impotence, infertility
- Hernia, lower abdominal pain, uterine prolapse

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- MOST-prescribed single point for urinary disorders in TCM
- Shu-Mu combination: BL28+CV3 for acute/chronic cystitis
- CV3+SP6+SP9 for Damp-Heat urinary disorders
- CV3+CV4+SP6 for gynecological conditions (lower Yin channel
  protocol)
- CV3+BL32+BL33 for stress urinary incontinence (front-back
  approach combining Front-Mu with sacral foramen access)

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Stress urinary incontinence — 🟡 Plausible**

CV3 is included in EA protocols for stress urinary incontinence.
Liu et al. (2017, JAMA) landmark RCT of EA at BL33+BL35 (N=504)
showed significant reduction in incontinence episodes. While CV3
was not the primary point in this trial, it is frequently included
in clinical protocols as the Front-Mu anchor.

Low-frequency EA at CV3+CV4 lengthened ejaculatory latency and
improved sexual behavior in male rats (Chen et al. 2020, Chinese
J Physiol) — demonstrating pelvic floor neuromodulation from these
midline abdominal points.

**2. Overactive bladder — 🟡 Plausible**

Multiple RCTs include CV3 in EA protocols for overactive bladder.
CV3's role as Front-Mu of Bladder provides the theoretical basis.
The mechanism involves T12–L1 afferent input modulating sacral
parasympathetic (S2–S4) outflow to the detrusor muscle via
spinal-level convergence.

**3. Infertility / reproductive — 🔴 Unvalidated**

CV3 is commonly included in acupuncture protocols for infertility
(often combined with CV4+SP6+ST36), but no SR/MA isolates CV3's
specific contribution.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The suprapubic region (CV3 area) becomes sensitized in:
- Acute cystitis / urinary tract infection: suprapubic tenderness is
  a cardinal clinical sign — lowered pressure pain threshold at the
  bladder projection zone (T12–L1 viscerotome).
- Interstitial cystitis / painful bladder syndrome: chronic
  suprapubic allodynia and hyperalgesia, reflecting central
  sensitization of T12–L1 dorsal horn neurons receiving convergent
  bladder visceral afferents.
- Chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CPPS): suprapubic tenderness
  extends across CV2–CV4 zone.
- Dysmenorrhea: lower midline tenderness during menses, reflecting
  uterine afferent convergence at T12–L1.

The Front-Mu diagnostic function (palpating CV3 for bladder
pathology) is a clinical application of dynamic sensitization —
the point becomes tender when its associated organ is diseased.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: somato-visceral reflex via spinal convergence.**

Surface stimulation at CV3 (T12–L1 dermatome) → T12–L1 dorsal
horn → two output pathways:

1. **Sympathetic modulation:** T11–L2 sympathetic preganglionic
   neurons → hypogastric plexus → bladder internal sphincter
   (smooth muscle) + detrusor inhibition. Stimulation at CV3 may
   modulate sympathetic tone to the bladder, affecting storage and
   voiding.

2. **Parasympathetic facilitation (indirect):** T12–L1 segmental
   input can modulate S2–S4 parasympathetic outflow via
   propriospinal interneurons. This intersegmental pathway
   (thoracolumbar → sacral) provides a mechanism for suprapubic
   needling to influence detrusor contraction (parasympathetic S2–S4
   territory).

The Front-Mu mechanism: CV3 overlies the bladder's body-surface
projection zone. Bladder distension/inflammation generates visceral
afferents (T11–L2 + S2–S4) that converge in the dorsal horn with
somatic afferents from the suprapubic skin (T12–L1). Needling CV3
accesses this convergence zone, modulating viscero-somatic reflex
activity.

Gap: the "Three Yin Meeting" concept (SP+LR+KI at CV3) has no
clear anatomical correlate — the three Yin leg channels do not
physically converge at a single spinal segment. The traditional
concept may reflect the clinical observation that gynecological/
reproductive conditions (traditionally attributed to SP+LR+KI
imbalance) are effectively treated from the suprapubic region.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.2, C04 S3.2

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–1.5 cun perpendicular. De Qi: distending sensation
in the lower abdomen, may radiate to the urethra or perineum.
EA: 2 Hz low-frequency stimulation at CV3 shows pelvic floor
neuromodulation in animal models. EA at CV3+BL33 is a common
protocol for urinary disorders.

Bấm huyệt (B02): moderate pressure at the suprapubic midline.
Standard technique in TCM abdominal massage (fu guan).

Cứu (B08): indirect moxa for Cold-type urinary dysfunction
(frequent urination from Yang deficiency). Moxa stick 15–20 min.

Dry needling (B11): not standard at CV3. Abdominal trigger point
needling addresses rectus abdominis, not the midline linea alba.

#### An toàn

**Bladder puncture:** same risk as CV2 — patient must empty bladder
before needling. A distended bladder rises above the pubic
symphysis into the suprapubic space where CV3 is located. Deep
needling (>2 cun) with a full bladder risks bladder wall puncture.

**Peritoneal puncture:** below the arcuate line, the posterior
rectus sheath is absent — only transversalis fascia and peritoneum
separate the needle from the abdominal cavity. Risk of bowel
puncture is lower than at the umbilical level (bowel loops are
higher) but not zero.

**Pregnancy:** CV3 is traditionally CONTRAINDICATED during
pregnancy — lower abdominal needling may stimulate uterine
contractions.

### CV4 — Quan Nguyên (关元, Guānyuán)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**

"Gate of Origin" (关 = gate/barrier, 元 = origin/primary). On the
anterior midline, 3 cun below the umbilicus (2 cun above the pubic
symphysis). One of the MOST CLINICALLY IMPORTANT points on the
entire Conception Vessel and among the most important in the entire
361-point system. The **Front-Mu point of the Small Intestine
(小肠募穴)** and a meeting point of CV with three Yin channels
(SP + LR + KI) — same "Three Yin Meeting" pattern as CV3. More
importantly, CV4 is the classical root of Yuan Qi (Original Qi,
原气) — the fundamental constitutional energy in TCM. The name
"Gate of Origin" directly references this function: CV4 is the
"gate" through which Original Qi is accessed and tonified.

CV4 sits at the ANTERIOR counterpart of GV4 (Mingmen, "Gate of
Life") on the posterior midline — both at approximately the L2
vertebral level, both governing Kidney Yang tonification, both with
moxa as the primary technique. This anterior-posterior "Gate Pair"
at the level of the renal arteries and adrenal glands may represent
traditional recognition of the L2 adrenal-renal axis.

BL26 (Guanyuanshu, "Gate of Origin Shu") at L5 is named after CV4,
further emphasizing its importance. Moxibustion is the PRIMARY
technique — B08 documents CV4 extensively as one of the most
important moxa points (L435–445).

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anterior midline, 3 cun
below the center of the umbilicus.

Anatomical landmarks: 3/5 of the distance from umbilicus to pubic
symphysis (umbilicus-to-pubic distance = 5 cun). The point lies
on the linea alba, between the medial borders of the rectus
abdominis muscles. At this level, the peritoneal cavity contains
loops of small intestine (jejunum/ileum) — consistent with CV4's
designation as Front-Mu of Small Intestine. In females, the uterine
fundus lies at approximately this level in the non-gravid state.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.5 cun (~1.2–3.5 cm) perpendicular.
Similar anatomy to CV3 — skin → subcutaneous fat → linea alba →
transversalis fascia → preperitoneal fat → peritoneum. The
peritoneal cavity with small bowel loops lies deep.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Anterior cutaneous branch of the subcostal nerve (T12)** —
  the T12 nerve provides primary cutaneous innervation at the level
  3 cun below umbilicus.
- **Iliohypogastric nerve (T12–L1)** — may contribute branches to
  this level.
- Spinal segments: T12 (primary), with L1 contribution.
- Deep: small intestine and uterus receive visceral innervation via
  T9–T10 (SI sympathetic afferents via superior mesenteric plexus)
  and T10–L1 (uterine sympathetic via hypogastric plexus). The
  S2–S4 parasympathetic afferents reach pelvic organs including
  the uterine cervix and lower uterine segment.
- B08 documented CV4 innervation as T11–T12 (L435).

Fascial plane: linea alba at midline. Below the arcuate line
(approximately at this level), the rectus abdominis lies directly
on transversalis fascia without a posterior rectus sheath. The
thoracolumbar fascia's anterior continuation (transversalis fascia)
provides the deep fascial boundary.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03

Vascular: superficial: branches of superficial epigastric artery.
Deep: inferior epigastric artery and vein within the rectus sheath,
lateral to the midline. The abdominal aorta and inferior vena cava
lie at the L3–L4 vertebral level posterior to this region, separated
by the peritoneal cavity — not a direct safety concern.

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → linea alba (midline) →
transversalis fascia → preperitoneal fat → parietal peritoneum →
peritoneal cavity (small bowel loops, uterus in females).

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Xi-Cleft: No. Back-Shu: No.
Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No. Luo-Connecting: No.

**Front-Mu of the Small Intestine (小肠募穴).** CV4 is the
anterior diagnostic and treatment point for the Small Intestine
organ. Shu-Mu pairing: BL27 (Xiaochangshu, S1 level) + CV4.

The Shu-Mu pair shows POOR segmental alignment: CV4 at T12
dermatome level, BL27 at S1 — while the SI viscerotome is T9–T10
(superior mesenteric plexus). This is the SAME Dorsher non-matching
pattern documented for BL27 in AP-07: the SI Back-Shu is displaced
5–6 segments caudally from the SI viscerotome (the most extreme
displacement in the 12 Back-Shu). CV4's Front-Mu is also
non-matching (T12 vs T9–T10), though less extreme.

Importantly, BL27's actual clinical indications are urogenital (not
enteric) — and CV4's primary clinical use is also urogenital/
reproductive (not small intestine per se). The Front-Mu of SI
designation may reflect a historical naming convention rather than
functional SI specificity.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL27 (Dorsher non-matching, urogenital
indications), B01 S3.1

**Meeting point (交会穴):** CV4 is the meeting of CV + SP + LR + KI
(足三阴交会) — same three Yin channels as CV3. The lower abdomen
between CV3 (4 cun below) and CV4 (3 cun below) is the primary
"Three Yin Meeting zone" on the anterior body.
→ Cross-ref: AP-04 SP6 (distal Three Yin Meeting)

**KI corridor:** KI13 (Qixue 气穴, "Qi Hole") sits 0.5 cun
lateral to CV4. KI13 is a Chong Mai meeting point — the Chong Mai
passes alongside the Kidney channel through the abdomen. CV4
(midline, Ren Mai) and KI13 (0.5 cun lateral, Chong Mai pathway)
together bracket the lower abdominal midline with extraordinary
vessel influence from two directions.
→ Cross-ref: AP-08 KI13

#### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** CV4 (Guanyuan 关元). Functions: tonifies
Yuan Qi (Original Qi), tonifies Kidney Yang, warms the Lower Jiao,
benefits the Uterus, regulates menstruation, nourishes Blood,
benefits urination, rescues Yang collapse (emergency moxa). One
of the MOST MULTI-FUNCTIONAL points in TCM — clinical applications
span reproductive, urinary, digestive, immunological, and emergency
indications. ✓

**2. Trigger point:** rectus abdominis lower segment trigger points.
The inferior rectus TrPs refer pain to the lower abdomen, groin,
and occasionally to the back at the same level. The lower midline
(CV4 area) is a standard TrP examination zone for chronic abdominal
pain. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** lower abdominal tenderness at 3 cun
below the umbilicus is common in dysmenorrhea, endometriosis,
interstitial cystitis, and chronic pelvic pain. Palpation at CV4
location is part of standard gynecological abdominal examination. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Basti Marma (बस्ति) — the lower abdominal
Marma governing bladder/urinary function extends across the
suprapubic-to-infraumbilical region. CV4 falls within the Basti
Marma zone (4 anguli size). Therapeutically: governs Apana Vayu
(downward vital force), urinary function, reproductive capacity.
Strong functional parallel. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** T12 dermatome at the surface level. Viscerotome:
T10–L2 sympathetic territory covers the kidneys, ureters, upper
bladder, and uterus. The lower abdominal midline (CV4 area) is the
anterior projection zone for multiple pelvic organs. Beissner 2011
mapping supports midline abdominal Front-Mu points as Head zone
correspondents. 🟡

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SFL (Superficial Front Line) passes through
rectus abdominis at this level. DFL (Deep Front Line) includes the
peritoneal and visceral fascia deep to CV4. The DFL connects the
pelvic organs to the respiratory diaphragm via the psoas fascia —
CV4 sits at the surface projection of this deep fascial chain. 🟡

**7. Neurogenic spot:** no specific data for the lower abdominal
midline. Kim 2017 mapping focused on the trunk but did not
specifically report the infraumbilical midline. ✗

**8. Cupping point:** not typically cupped at the lower abdomen.
Some TCM practitioners apply flash cupping or gentle cupping at CV4
for chronic Cold-type conditions, but this is not a standard
cupping location. 🟡 (marginal)

**Convergence score: 7/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Marma 🟡, Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡, Cupping 🟡). Very high
convergence — among the highest on CV. The lower abdominal midline
is universally recognized as a critical zone across traditions for
reproductive and urinary function.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Primary dysmenorrhea — 🟡 Plausible**

Multiple SRs and data mining studies confirm CV4 as a core acupoint
for dysmenorrhea. Association rule analysis (JPR 2025): SP6+CV4+SP8
identified as the most consistent core combination across clinical
literature for primary dysmenorrhea, with CV4 appearing in the
highest-frequency acupoint selections.

Moxibustion RCTs: moxa at CV4+CV6+ST36 for dysmenorrhea showed
significant VAS reduction vs sham (Li et al. 2021, JPR), but
CV4-specific contribution cannot be isolated from the multi-point
protocol.

The mechanism: T12 somatic afferents at CV4 converge in the dorsal
horn with T10–L1 uterine sympathetic afferents → modulation of
uterine smooth muscle tone and menstrual pain. This
somato-visceral convergence at the thoracolumbar junction provides
a segmental rationale.

**2. Pelvic inflammatory disease — 🟡 Plausible**

Qu et al. (2025, Immunity, Inflammation and Disease): EA at CV4
(Guanyuan) improved pelvic inflammatory disease pain in a rat model
by inhibiting neuroinflammation and sympathetic activity.
Specifically: reduced behavioral pain scores, alleviated uterine
tissue pathological damage, and decreased expression of NLRP3,
IL-1β, and TNF-α in the dorsal root ganglia. 2/100 Hz alternating
frequency was optimal. The sympathoinhibitory mechanism parallels
GV14's immunomodulatory pathway (AP-13) but via the lumbar rather
than cervicothoracic sympathetic system.

**3. Lower back pain (moxa) — 🟡 Plausible**

RCT (Chiu et al. 2015): moxibustion at CV4 with 15, 30, or 60
minutes heating duration, 10 sessions over 2 weeks. The 60-minute
group showed significant VAS and Roland-Morris Questionnaire
improvement. This supports a dose-response relationship for moxa at
CV4 — longer heating duration → stronger effect. The mechanism:
T12–L1 segmental warming → paravertebral muscle relaxation +
sympathetic modulation at the lumbar level.

**4. Benign prostatic hyperplasia — 🟡 Plausible**

SR of acupoint selection for BPH (PMC 2025): among 85 included
studies, CV4 was among the highest-frequency acupoint selections.
Association rule analysis: CV4+CV6 identified as a key combination.
No efficacy data specific to CV4, but consistent inclusion across
studies supports clinical relevance.

**5. Cancer-related fatigue — 🟡 Plausible**

SR (Frontiers in Oncology 2026): acupuncture/moxibustion for
cancer-related fatigue. CV4+ST36 combination described as having
synergistic mechanism for immune modulation — enhancing NK cell
activity and regulating T lymphocyte subsets. RCT included
moxibustion at CV4+CV6+ST36 for chemotherapy-induced fatigue in
colorectal cancer patients.

**6. Postoperative dysuria — 🟡 Plausible**

Moxibustion at CV4 prevented postoperative dysuria after PPH
(procedure for prolapse and hemorrhoids) in an RCT (PMC 2013).
The mechanism: CV4 moxa → T12–L1 segmental warming → modulation
of bladder detrusor-sphincter coordination via sympathetic
relaxation.

**7. Uterine fibroids — 🟡 Plausible (aggregate)**

Narrative review (PMC 2025): CV4, SP6, CV3, and CV6 identified as
most frequently selected acupoints for uterine fibroids across the
literature, recommended for incorporation into treatment protocols.
No CV4-specific efficacy data.

**Evidence pattern:** CV4 appears as a core acupoint in SRs/data
mining studies across MULTIPLE conditions (dysmenorrhea, BPH,
cancer fatigue, uterine fibroids, postoperative dysuria). However,
NO SR/MA tests CV4 in isolation — it is always part of multi-point
protocols. The consistent inclusion across diverse conditions
reflects CV4's status as a "tonification anchor" point rather than
a condition-specific therapeutic point. This parallels ST36's
evidence pattern (AP-03): universally included, rarely isolated.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The lower abdominal midline (CV4 area) becomes sensitized in:
- Dysmenorrhea: menstrual-cycle-dependent sensitization of the
  infraumbilical region, with lowered pressure pain threshold during
  the luteal and menstrual phases. Multiple studies document
  menstrual hyperalgesia at CV4 location.
- Endometriosis: chronic pelvic sensitization with referred pain
  to the anterior abdominal wall, including the CV4 region.
- Chronic pelvic pain: central sensitization with expanded
  referred pain zones from pelvic organs to the abdominal surface.
- IBS: viscero-somatic convergence may sensitize the lower
  abdominal wall (T10–L2 territory) during flares.

Chen Rixin heat-sensitive moxibustion studies report enhanced heat
sensitivity at CV4 during gynecological conditions — patients
perceive deeper warmth penetration during dysmenorrhea compared to
asymptomatic controls, suggesting pathology-dependent changes in
thermal perception threshold.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: thoracolumbar sympathetic modulation.**

Surface stimulation at CV4 (T12 dermatome) → T12–L1 dorsal horn →
three output pathways:

1. **Sympathetic modulation to pelvic organs:** T10–L2 sympathetic
   preganglionic neurons → superior hypogastric plexus → inferior
   hypogastric plexus → uterus, bladder, prostate, vas deferens.
   This pathway mediates: (a) uterine smooth muscle relaxation
   (dysmenorrhea), (b) bladder internal sphincter relaxation
   (urinary retention), (c) reproductive organ vasodilation
   (fertility).

2. **Immune/inflammatory modulation:** T10–L2 sympathetic outflow
   reaches the mesenteric lymph nodes, spleen, and pelvic lymphatic
   tissue. The NLRP3/IL-1β/TNF-α suppression documented by Qu et
   al. (2025) for PID may involve this sympathetic-immune pathway.

3. **Segmental convergence (somato-visceral reflex):** T12 somatic
   afferents from the abdominal wall converge in the dorsal horn
   with T10–L2 visceral afferents from pelvic/lower abdominal
   organs. Needling/moxa at CV4 modulates this convergence zone,
   reducing visceral nociceptive transmission (gate-control
   mechanism at spinal level).

**CV4+GV4 "Gate Pair" mechanism:**

CV4 (anterior, T12) and GV4 (posterior, L2) sit on opposite
surfaces of the body at approximately the same vertebral level.
Both access the T12–L2 sympathetic system:
- CV4: via anterior cutaneous nerves (T12) → dorsal horn
- GV4: via posterior rami (L2) → dorsal horn

The L1–L2 spinal segments are the origin of the lumbar splanchnic
nerves, which provide sympathetic innervation to the adrenal glands
(preganglionic sympathetic → adrenal medulla → catecholamines) and
the kidneys (renal sympathetic). The TCM concept of "Kidney Yang
tonification" through CV4+GV4 moxa may reflect bilateral
(anterior + posterior) warming of the T12–L2 sympathetic system,
producing: (a) adrenal medullary activation (catecholamine
release), (b) renal sympathetic modulation, (c) pelvic organ
sympathetic regulation.

This is the same L2 adrenal-renal axis mechanism proposed for GV4
(AP-13 GV4): "GV4 moxa → paravertebral warming → sympathetic
activation → adrenal stimulation." CV4 adds the ANTERIOR approach
to the same system.

**Front-Mu of SI — functional mismatch:**

CV4's Front-Mu designation as "Small Intestine" is clinically
misleading: virtually none of CV4's primary indications relate to
the small intestine. The actual SI viscerotome (T9–T10) does not
match CV4's dermatome level (T12), and the corresponding Back-Shu
(BL27 at S1) is the most extreme Dorsher non-matching in the
Atlas. CV4's real clinical function is urogenital/reproductive/
tonification — driven by the T12–L1 sympathetic access to pelvic
organs, not the T9–T10 SI visceral pathway.

Gap: the "Yuan Qi" (Original Qi) concept in TCM has no direct
Western anatomical correlate. The adrenal-renal axis at L1–L2
provides the closest mechanistic approximation: adrenal function
(cortisol, catecholamines, androgens) broadly maps to the TCM
concept of constitutional "Kidney essence" stored at the "Gate of
Origin." However, this remains a 🟡 hypothesis, not a confirmed
mechanism.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A02 S3, A07 S4.2, AP-13 GV4, B08 L435–445

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–1.5 cun perpendicular. De Qi: distending, warm
sensation in the lower abdomen, may radiate to the perineum or
lumbosacral region. For tonification: use supplementing technique
(slow insertion, slow withdrawal, gentle rotation). For clearing:
use draining technique.

EA: 2 Hz low-frequency for tonification/reproductive effects
(Han: 2Hz → enkephalin pathway → tonic parasympathetic). 2/100 Hz
alternating for anti-inflammatory (Qu 2025 PID model). EA at
CV4+CV3 for pelvic floor neuromodulation (Chen 2020).

Bấm huyệt (B02): moderate sustained pressure at 3 cun below
umbilicus. Self-acupressure at CV4 is commonly taught for
dysmenorrhea self-management. Warmth (hot water bottle) combined
with CV4 pressure is a standard folk remedy.

Cứu (B08): **MOXA IS THE PRIMARY TECHNIQUE at CV4.** This is one
of the 3 most important moxa points in the entire system (CV4,
CV8, ST36). B08 documents CV4 moxa extensively:
- Moxa stick: 15–60 minutes (dose-response: 60 min > 30 min > 15
  min, Chiu 2015 RCT). Warming the lower abdomen.
- Direct moxa (small cones): traditional tonification technique,
  3–7 cones per session
- Salt moxa: sometimes applied at CV4 (similar to CV8 technique),
  though CV8 is the classical salt moxa point
- Preventive moxa at CV4: traditional longevity practice in East
  Asian medicine (monthly or seasonal moxa for health maintenance)
- Emergency moxa: heavy moxa (large cones, many repetitions) at
  CV4 for Yang collapse — "rescue Yang from prostration"
→ Cross-ref: B08 L435–445, 590, 611, 641, 704

Dry needling (B11): not standard at CV4. Lower abdominal rectus
TrPs are addressed lateral to the midline.

#### An toàn

**Peritoneal/bowel puncture:** same anatomical risk as CV3. Deep
perpendicular needling beyond the transversalis fascia enters the
peritoneal cavity with small bowel loops. Clinical safe depth:
<2 cun (~5 cm) in patients of normal body habitus. Obese patients
have greater safe depth; thin patients have less.

**Bladder distension:** less critical than CV3 (CV4 is 1 cun
higher, above the usual bladder dome) but still relevant.

**Pregnancy:** CV4 is STRONGLY CONTRAINDICATED during pregnancy.
Lower abdominal needling/moxa may stimulate uterine contractions.
CV4 is one of the classically prohibited points in pregnancy
(alongside LI4, SP6, BL67).

**Moxa caution:** prolonged moxa (>60 min) can cause skin burns,
especially with direct moxa cones. Standard moxa stick at safe
distance (2–3 cm) is low risk.

## Mid-Lower Abdomen Region (CV5–CV9)

Five points from 2 cun below the umbilicus (CV5) to 1 cun above
the umbilicus (CV9). This region spans the periumbilical zone of
the Ren Mai — the narrow band around the navel where the anterior
abdominal wall is thinnest and where the T10–T12 dermatomes
provide direct viscerotome overlap with gastrointestinal and
pelvic organs. Nerve supply transitions from the subcostal nerve
(T12) at CV5 through intercostal nerves T11 at CV6–CV7 to T10 at
CV8–CV9. The umbilicus (CV8) is the anatomical and symbolic center
of this region — the only point in the 361-point system where
needling is absolutely prohibited. The KI corridor continues
through this zone with partial gaps: KI14 parallels CV5, KI15
parallels CV7, KI16 parallels CV8; CV6 and CV9 have no direct
KI correspondent at their exact level. Three of five points carry
special designations: CV5 (Front-Mu of Triple Burner), CV6 (Sea
of Qi), CV8 (moxa-only + Spirit name).

### CV5 — Thạch Môn (石门, Shímén)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Stone Gate" (石 = stone, 门 = gate/door). On the anterior midline,
2 cun below the umbilicus. The **Front-Mu point of the Triple
Burner (三焦募穴)** — notable as the Front-Mu of the ONLY organ
system in TCM with no discrete anatomical counterpart. The name
"Stone Gate" carries a dual connotation: (a) a gate made of stone
(strong, immovable), and (b) a gate that "stones"/blocks —
specifically referencing the classical prohibition against
needling CV5 in women of childbearing age due to feared infertility.
This prohibition is one of the most debated safety claims in
classical acupuncture literature.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anterior midline, 2 cun
below the center of the umbilicus.

Anatomical landmarks: 2/5 of the distance from umbilicus down to
the pubic symphysis. The point lies on the linea alba, between the
medial borders of the rectus abdominis muscles. CV5 sits 1 cun
below CV6 (Qihai) and 1 cun above CV4 (Guanyuan) — three major
lower CV points within a 2-cun span.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.5 cun (~1.2–3.5 cm) perpendicular.
Same anatomical layers as CV4: skin → subcutaneous fat → linea
alba → transversalis fascia → preperitoneal fat → peritoneum.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Anterior cutaneous branch of the subcostal nerve (T12)** —
  primary cutaneous innervation at 2 cun below umbilicus.
- **Iliohypogastric nerve (T12–L1)** — may contribute branches.
- Spinal segments: T12 (primary).
- Deep: the Triple Burner has no specific visceral organ to
  innervate. The peritoneal cavity at this level contains small
  bowel loops. The uterus lies deep in females.

Fascial plane: linea alba at midline. Below the arcuate line
(approximately at or near this level), the posterior rectus
sheath is absent.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03

Vascular: superficial epigastric vessels cutaneously. Deep:
inferior epigastric artery and vein within the rectus sheath
lateral to midline.

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → linea alba →
transversalis fascia → preperitoneal fat → parietal peritoneum.

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Xi-Cleft: No. Back-Shu: No.
Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No. Luo-Connecting: No.

**Front-Mu of the Triple Burner (三焦募穴).** CV5 is the anterior
diagnostic and treatment point for the San Jiao organ system.
Shu-Mu pairing: BL22 (Sanjiaoshu, L1 level) + CV5.

The Triple Burner Front-Mu/Back-Shu alignment presents a unique theoretical
problem: the Triple Burner has NO discrete anatomical organ, so
there is no specific visceral innervation to check segmental
alignment against. BL22 at L1 and CV5 at T12 are nearly the same
spinal level — good "alignment" in terms of segmental access, but
alignment TO WHAT? The Triple Burner is classically described as a "functional
organ" governing water metabolism and Qi transformation across the
three body cavities (thorax, upper abdomen, lower abdomen). This
makes the Triple Burner Shu-Mu pair the only pair that cannot be evaluated
by Dorsher's viscerotome matching criteria.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL22 (Back-Shu of the Triple Burner), B01 S3.1

**KI corridor:** KI14 (Siman 四满, "Fourfold Fullness") sits 0.5
cun lateral to CV5. KI14 is a Chong Mai meeting point. The name
"Fourfold Fullness" refers to abdominal distension — consistent
with Triple Burner water metabolism disorders at this level.
→ Cross-ref: AP-08 KI14

#### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** CV5 (Shimen 石门). Functions: regulates the
San Jiao, benefits the water passages, regulates Qi
transformation. Used for: abdominal pain, diarrhea, edema, urinary
disorders, amenorrhea, uterine bleeding, postpartum hemorrhage,
hernia. ✓

**2. Trigger point:** rectus abdominis lower-middle segment trigger
points at this level. Lower rectus TrPs refer pain to the lower
abdomen and groin. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** infraumbilical tenderness at 2 cun
below the navel is common in lower abdominal pain, menstrual
disorders, and urinary conditions. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Basti Marma zone extends across the suprapubic-
to-infraumbilical region (4 anguli). CV5 falls within the upper
portion of the Basti zone, though the center is closer to CV3–CV4.
🟡 (marginal)

**5. Head zone:** T12 dermatome at the surface. Viscerotome: T12
sympathetic territory overlaps with pelvic organ innervation (upper
bladder, proximal ureter). 🟡

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Front Line passes through rectus
abdominis at this level. 🟡

**7. Neurogenic spot:** no specific data. ✗

**8. Cupping point:** not standard at the lower abdomen. ✗

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Marma 🟡, Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Nanjing (Ch.66): names CV5 as the Front-Mu of the San Jiao
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: abdominal pain, diarrhea, edema, dysuria,
  hernia, postpartum hemorrhage, uterine bleeding
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: water metabolism disorders — edema, ascites,
  urinary retention. WARNING on fertility

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Abdominal pain, distension, diarrhea, borborygmus
- Edema, urinary retention, dysuria
- Amenorrhea, irregular menses, vaginal discharge
- Postpartum hemorrhage, uterine bleeding
- Hernia

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- LESS commonly used than CV4 (1 cun below) or CV6 (0.5 cun above)
  — flanked by two more prominent points
- Often substituted by CV4 or CV6 in clinical protocols
- The fertility prohibition (see An toàn) has reduced clinical use
  of CV5, particularly in East Asian practice
- CV5+SP9 for edema (Triple Burner water metabolism + SP dampness resolution)

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. No condition-specific evidence — 🔴 Unvalidated**

No SR, MA, or significant RCT specifically tests CV5 for any
indication. CV5 is frequently omitted from modern clinical
protocols in favor of CV4 (more evidence, stronger traditional
authority) or CV6 (Qi tonification, no prohibition). The
fertility prohibition may have contributed to decreased clinical
use and therefore decreased research interest.

**2. Fertility prohibition — 🔴 Unvalidated (as risk)**

No modern evidence supports the classical claim that needling CV5
causes infertility. The prohibition is documented in several
classical texts (Zhenjiu Dacheng, Biao You Fu 标幽赋) but is NOT
universally accepted — many classical and modern authorities
consider it unfounded. The anatomical depth at CV5 (2 cun below
navel) does not differ meaningfully from CV4 or CV6, and there
is no identified mechanism by which needling at this specific
level (vs 1 cun higher or lower) would selectively impair
fertility.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The infraumbilical region (CV5 area) shares the same
sensitization profile as CV3–CV4: menstrual-cycle-dependent
hyperalgesia, endometriosis-related tenderness, and IBS-related
viscerosomatic convergence in the T12 territory. No specific
published data distinguishes CV5 sensitization from that of
adjacent CV4 or CV6.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: T12 segmental access — identical to CV4.**

CV5 accesses the same T12 dorsal horn segments as CV4 (1 cun
below) and CV6 (0.5 cun above). The mechanism is the same
somato-visceral reflex through the thoracolumbar sympathetic
system: T12 somatic afferents → dorsal horn convergence with
T10–L2 visceral afferents → modulation of sympathetic outflow
to pelvic and lower abdominal organs.

**Front-Mu of the Triple Burner — the "no organ" problem:**
The Triple Burner is the only organ system in TCM without a
discrete anatomical counterpart. The Triple Burner concept most closely
maps to the body's fluid metabolism and thermoregulatory systems
— distributed functions rather than a single organ. CV5's
Front-Mu designation may reflect:
(a) T12 segmental access to the hypogastric plexus (lower jiao
    water metabolism — bladder, kidney function), or
(b) the periumbilical region's role as the abdominal center from
    which sympathetic outflow regulates water balance across
    multiple organ systems simultaneously.

The "no organ" problem means CV5 has LESS clinical specificity
than other Front-Mu points (CV3/bladder, CV4/SI, CV12/stomach,
CV14/heart, CV17/pericardium) — consistent with its reduced
clinical use.

Gap: the fertility prohibition has no identified mechanism.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.2, B01 S3.1

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–1.5 cun perpendicular. De Qi: distending
sensation in the lower abdomen. Technique and De Qi are
indistinguishable from CV4 or CV6 at this level.

Bấm huyệt (B02): moderate pressure at infraumbilical midline.

Cứu (B08): indirect moxa for water metabolism disorders and Cold-
type abdominal conditions. Moxa stick 15–20 min. Less commonly
used for moxa than CV4 (tonification) or CV8 (salt moxa).

Dry needling (B11): not standard at CV5.

#### An toàn

**Classical fertility prohibition (禁忌 for women):**
Multiple classical texts (Zhenjiu Dacheng, Biao You Fu) warn
against needling or moxa at CV5 in women of childbearing age,
claiming it causes infertility. The specific claim in Biao You
Fu: "石门针灸应须忌" (CV5 needling and moxa must be avoided).

This prohibition is CONTROVERSIAL:
- **For:** Several authoritative classical texts include it.
  The name "Stone Gate" itself may encode the warning (gate that
  "stones"/blocks fertility).
- **Against:** (a) No modern evidence supports the claim. (b) Not
  all classical texts agree — some omit the prohibition entirely.
  (c) No anatomical mechanism differentiates CV5 from CV4 or CV6
  for reproductive effects. (d) The prohibition may reflect
  confusion with nearby points or an ancient clinical correlation
  that was not well controlled.

Modern clinical practice: most practitioners either ignore the
prohibition or avoid CV5 in favor of CV4/CV6 for pragmatic
reasons (stronger evidence at adjacent points, no prohibition
concerns).

**Peritoneal/bowel puncture:** same risk as CV3–CV4. Deep
needling risks peritoneal entry.

**Pregnancy:** CV5 is CONTRAINDICATED during pregnancy (same as
all lower CV points CV2–CV7).

### CV6 — Khí Hải (气海, Qìhǎi)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Sea of Qi" (气 = Qi/vital energy, 海 = sea/ocean). On the
anterior midline, 1.5 cun below the umbilicus. One of the most
important tonification points in TCM — second only to CV4 among
the lower Conception Vessel points. The name "Sea of Qi"
designates CV6 as a primary reservoir and source of Qi in the
body. CV6 is one of the two "Sea of Qi" points in the Four Seas
system (Ling Shu Ch.33): CV17 (upper Sea of Qi, at chest level)
and CV6 (lower Sea of Qi, at lower abdomen). BL24 (Qihaishu,
"Sea of Qi Shu") on the posterior body is named after CV6,
emphasizing its importance.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anterior midline, 1.5
cun below the center of the umbilicus.

Anatomical landmarks: 1.5/5 of the distance from umbilicus to
pubic symphysis. Midway between CV7 (1 cun below) and CV5 (2 cun
below). On the linea alba, between the medial borders of the
rectus abdominis muscles.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.5 cun (~1.2–3.5 cm) perpendicular.
Same anatomical layers as CV4–CV5.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Anterior cutaneous branch of the subcostal nerve (T12)** or
  the 11th intercostal nerve (T11) — the T11–T12 transitional
  zone. The exact cutaneous territory at 1.5 cun below the
  umbilicus may receive contributions from both T11 and T12.
- Spinal segments: T11–T12.
- Deep: peritoneal cavity with small bowel loops. In females,
  the uterine fundus lies approximately at this level.

Fascial plane: linea alba at midline. Below the arcuate line.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03

Vascular: superficial epigastric vessels. Deep: inferior
epigastric artery/vein lateral to midline within rectus sheath.

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → linea alba →
transversalis fascia → preperitoneal fat → peritoneum.

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Xi-Cleft: No. Back-Shu: No.
Front-Mu: No. Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No.
Luo-Connecting: No.

**Sea of Qi (气海) — Four Seas system.** CV6 is the lower Sea of
Qi point, as designated in Ling Shu Chapter 33 (海论). The Four
Seas system maps four physiological "seas" to specific acupoints:
- Sea of Qi (气海): CV17 (upper) + CV6 (lower)
- Sea of Nourishment (水谷之海): ST30 (lower) + ST36 (upper)
- Sea of Blood (血海): BL11 (upper) + ST37, ST39 (lower)
- Sea of Marrow (髓海): GV16 (posterior) + GV20 (superior)

CV6 and CV17 together represent the Qi axis of the body — CV6
at the lower abdomen (origin of Qi from the lower Dantian) and
CV17 at the chest (gathering of Qi from respiration). Both are
on the Conception Vessel.
→ Cross-ref: AP-03 ST36+ST30 (Sea of Nourishment), AP-07 BL11
(Sea of Blood), AP-13 GV16+GV20 (Sea of Marrow)

**No Front-Mu, no Meeting point designation.** Despite being
one of the most important lower CV points clinically, CV6 has
NO special category designation (no Front-Mu, no Hui-Meeting, no
Meeting point). Its importance derives entirely from the Four
Seas designation and empirical clinical observation — unlike
adjacent CV4 (Front-Mu SI, Three Yin meeting) and CV5 (Front-Mu
Triple Burner). This makes CV6 an example of a point whose clinical
importance exceeds its categorical designation.

**KI corridor gap:** CV6 at 1.5 cun below the umbilicus has NO
corresponding KI point at its exact level. The KI corridor jumps
from KI15 (at CV7 level, 1 cun below) to KI14 (at CV5 level,
2 cun below). CV6 falls between two KI corridor pairs.
→ Cross-ref: AP-08 KI14, KI15

**BL24 (Qihaishu) — named correspondent.** BL24 at L3 is named
after CV6: "Qihaishu" = "Sea of Qi Shu." This is NOT a formal
Front-Mu/Back-Shu pairing (CV6 is not a Front-Mu point), but the
naming convention indicates a recognized anterior-posterior
relationship.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL24

#### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** CV6 (Qihai 气海). Functions: tonifies Qi,
tonifies Yuan Qi, regulates Qi, rescues Yang. Primary point for
Qi deficiency of ANY origin. Used for: general weakness, fatigue,
chronic Qi deficiency, prolapse (Qi sinking), urinary disorders,
gynecological conditions, hernia, lower abdominal pain. ✓

**2. Trigger point:** rectus abdominis infraumbilical trigger
points. Consistent with the general lower rectus TrP zone. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** infraumbilical tenderness common in
Qi deficiency patterns (bloating, fatigue, prolapse). Periumbilical
palpation tenderness in functional GI disorders. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Nabhi Marma (नाभि, "Navel") — the navel
region Marma extends several anguli around the umbilicus. CV6 at
1.5 cun below falls within the Nabhi zone. Nabhi Marma governs
Apana Vayu (downward vital force) and Samana Vayu (digestive
force) — functional parallel to CV6's Qi regulation. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** T11–T12 dermatome. Viscerotome: T11–T12
sympathetic territory covers the ureters, upper bladder, and
the lower GI tract (ileocecal junction). 🟡

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Front Line passes through
rectus abdominis. Deep Front Line includes the peritoneal and
visceral fascia deep to CV6. 🟡

**7. Neurogenic spot:** no specific data. ✗

**8. Cupping point:** not standard at this location. ✗

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Marma 🟡, Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Neijing (Ling Shu Ch.33 海论): CV6 named as lower Sea of Qi
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: Qi deficiency, fatigue, lower abdominal
  pain, hernia, urinary disorders, menstrual irregularity
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: PRIMARY TONIFICATION POINT — Qi deficiency
  across all organs, Yang sinking (prolapse), chronic fatigue,
  postpartum weakness

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Tonification: chronic fatigue, Qi deficiency, general weakness,
  post-illness recovery, immunodeficiency
- Prolapse: gastric prolapse, uterine prolapse, rectal prolapse
  (Qi sinking pattern)
- Urinary: frequent urination, enuresis, urinary retention
- Gynecological: irregular menses, amenorrhea, dysmenorrhea,
  vaginal discharge, postpartum disorders
- Digestive: abdominal distension, diarrhea, constipation
- Hernia

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- **CV6+CV4+ST36 tonification triad:** the classical lower body
  Qi tonification combination. Moxa strongly preferred.
- **CV6+ST36 moxa:** the "two pillars" of Qi tonification in
  East Asian medicine. Monthly or seasonal preventive moxa at both
  points is a traditional health maintenance practice.
- CV6+SP6+ST36 for prolapse disorders (Qi lifting protocol)
- CV6+CV4 for BPH (identified as key combination in SR, cross-ref
  CV4 entry)
- Less frequently used as standalone point compared to CV4 —
  typically appears in combination protocols

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. General Qi deficiency / fatigue — 🔴 Unvalidated (as single
point)**

CV6 appears frequently in clinical protocols for fatigue and
weakness, but always in multi-point combinations (CV6+ST36,
CV6+CV4+ST36). No SR/MA isolates CV6's specific contribution.
The consistent inclusion across studies supports clinical
relevance but does not constitute independent evidence.

**2. Prolapse disorders — 🟡 Plausible (mechanism)**

The segmental rationale for prolapse treatment: T11–T12 somatic
afferents at CV6 modulate sympathetic outflow to pelvic organs
(via the T10–L2 sympathetic system), potentially increasing
pelvic floor tone and supporting organ position. Combined with
ST36 (L4–L5, lower limb proprioception → central integration),
the protocol addresses both central Qi regulation and peripheral
pelvic support. Limited direct evidence, but mechanistic
plausibility.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The periumbilical-infraumbilical region (CV6 area) shows
sensitization in the same conditions as CV4: dysmenorrhea,
endometriosis, chronic pelvic pain, IBS. The T11–T12 dermatome
at CV6 overlaps with visceral afferent convergence from the lower
GI tract and pelvic organs, providing a basis for pathology-
dependent threshold changes.

No CV6-specific sensitization data published. Chen Rixin heat-
sensitive moxa studies report enhanced heat sensitivity throughout
the infraumbilical zone, not distinguishing between CV4, CV5, and
CV6 individually.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: T11–T12 somato-visceral reflex — shared with
CV4 and CV5.**

CV6 accesses essentially the same T11–T12 dorsal horn segments
as CV4 (1.5 cun below) and CV5 (0.5 cun below). The clinical
differentiation between CV4, CV5, and CV6 is primarily TRADITION-
DRIVEN rather than mechanism-driven: all three share the same
spinal segmental access and produce effects through the same
sympathetic outflow pathways to pelvic and lower abdominal organs.

**The "Sea of Qi" concept — Qi pooling at the lower Dantian:**
CV6 is located at the traditional "lower Dantian" (下丹田) — the
region below the navel considered the body's Qi reservoir in
Chinese and East Asian traditions. The closest mechanistic
correlate:
(a) The infraumbilical abdominal wall receives bilateral T11–T12
    innervation, providing dense segmental access to the thoraco-
    lumbar sympathetic outflow (T10–L2) — the primary autonomic
    regulator of internal organ function.
(b) The "Qi reservoir" metaphor may map to the high density of
    sympathetic preganglionic neurons at the thoracolumbar
    junction, which collectively regulate metabolism, circulation,
    and organ function.

Gap: the clinical distinction between CV4, CV5, and CV6 — all
within a 2-cun zone sharing the same segmental innervation —
remains empirical rather than mechanistically justified.
→ Cross-ref: A02 S3, A07 S4.2

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–1.5 cun perpendicular. De Qi: distending, warm
sensation in the lower abdomen. Supplementing technique for
tonification.

Bấm huyệt (B02): moderate sustained pressure at 1.5 cun below
the umbilicus. Self-acupressure commonly taught for general Qi
strengthening.

Cứu (B08): **MOXA STRONGLY PREFERRED.** CV6 is one of the major
moxa tonification points. Moxa stick 15–30 minutes. Indirect moxa
(ginger interposed) for Yang deficiency. Direct moxa (small cones)
for chronic Qi deficiency — traditional protocol: 3–7 cones.
Preventive moxa at CV6 is standard practice alongside CV4 and
ST36.
→ Cross-ref: B08 §4.6, §5.2

Dry needling (B11): not standard at CV6.

#### An toàn

**Peritoneal/bowel puncture:** same risk as CV3–CV5. Deep needling
at midline risks peritoneal entry. Safe depth: <2 cun in normal
body habitus.

**Pregnancy:** CV6 is CONTRAINDICATED during pregnancy (same as
all lower CV points).

### CV7 — Âm Giao (阴交, Yīnjiāo)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Yin Intersection" (阴 = Yin, 交 = intersection/crossing). On the
anterior midline, 1 cun below the umbilicus. Meeting point of
CV + Chong Mai + KI — the triple convergence of three
extraordinary/Yin-associated channels at the infraumbilical
midline. The Chong Mai (Penetrating Vessel, 冲脉, "Sea of Blood")
intersects CV at this level, adding a Blood-regulating dimension.
Gynecological and lower abdominal pain.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anterior midline, 1 cun
below the center of the umbilicus.

Anatomical landmarks: 1/5 of the distance from umbilicus to pubic
symphysis. On the linea alba. At this level, the abdominal wall
begins to thin compared to the umbilical level.

Needle depth: 0.5–1.5 cun perpendicular.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: anterior cutaneous branch of the 11th intercostal nerve
(T11). Spinal segment: T11.
Vascular: superficial epigastric vessels. Deep: inferior epigastric
artery/vein lateral to midline.
Fascial: linea alba. Below the arcuate line.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03

#### Phân loại huyệt

No Five Shu, Yuan-Source, Xi-Cleft, Back-Shu, Front-Mu, Eight
Confluent, Hui-Meeting, or Luo-Connecting designation.

**Meeting point (交会穴):** CV7 is the meeting of CV + Chong Mai
+ KI (冲任交会). The Chong Mai ("Penetrating Vessel" / "Sea of
Blood") passes through the interior of the abdomen alongside the
Kidney channel. This triple meeting at CV7 adds the Chong Mai
Blood-regulating function to the Ren Mai Yin-regulating function.
In gynecological TCM, the Chong-Ren axis (冲任) governs
menstruation and fertility.
→ Cross-ref: AP-08 KI15 (Chong Mai meeting)

**KI corridor:** KI15 (Zhongzhu 中注, "Central Flow") sits 0.5
cun lateral to CV7. KI15 is also a Chong Mai meeting point — both
CV7 (midline) and KI15 (0.5 lateral) share the Chong Mai
intersection at this level.
→ Cross-ref: AP-08 KI15

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: CV7 (Yinjiao 阴交). Functions: regulates Chong Mai and Ren
Mai, regulates menstruation, resolves stagnation in the Lower
Jiao. Used for: irregular menses, postpartum hemorrhage, vaginal
discharge, lower abdominal pain, hernia. ✓
Trigger point: rectus abdominis infraumbilical TrPs. 🟡
Ashi: infraumbilical tenderness in gynecological conditions. 🟡
Marma: within the broader Nabhi Marma zone. 🟡
Head zone: T11 dermatome. 🟡
Anatomy Trains: SFL through rectus abdominis. 🟡
Neurogenic: no specific data. ✗
Cupping: not standard. ✗

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Marma 🟡, Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: irregular menses, postpartum hemorrhage,
postpartum abdominal pain, vaginal discharge, hernia, lower
abdominal masses, edema.

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: less commonly used as a standalone point —
typically included in lower abdominal protocols alongside CV4,
CV6, SP6. The Chong Mai meeting makes CV7 theoretically relevant
for Blood disorders (Chong = "Sea of Blood"), but CV4 and SP6
are more commonly selected in practice.

#### An toàn

**Peritoneal/bowel puncture:** same risk as other lower CV points.
**Pregnancy:** contraindicated during pregnancy.

### CV8 — Thần Khuyết (神阙, Shénquè)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Spirit Gate Palace" (神 = spirit/divine, 阙 = palace gate/tower).
The center of the umbilicus. THE MOST UNIQUE POINT IN THE ENTIRE
361-POINT SYSTEM — the ONLY point where needling is ABSOLUTELY
PROHIBITED. While other points have cautions or relative
contraindications, CV8's needle prohibition is universal across
all classical and modern authorities without exception. The
traditional technique is SALT-FILLED MOXIBUSTION (隔盐灸, ge yan
jiu): salt fills the navel concavity, a moxa cone is placed on
the salt, and the salt conducts and moderates the heat to the
periumbilical tissues.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Acute/chronic diarrhea — 🟡 Plausible**

Traditional primary indication with the strongest mechanistic
rationale: T10 dermatome at CV8 directly overlaps the small
intestine and proximal colon viscerotome. Thermal stimulation at
T10 → T10 dorsal horn → sympathetic modulation of GI motility
(T10 sympathetic outflow → superior mesenteric ganglion → small
bowel + ascending colon). This is the same somato-visceral reflex
documented for ST25 at the same level (AP-03).

No SR/MA specifically tests CV8 moxa for diarrhea. The classical
indication is supported by mechanistic plausibility (T10
segmental access to GI tract) but lacks rigorous clinical
evidence.

**2. Emergency resuscitation — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Historical emergency moxa indication. No modern evidence supports
moxa at CV8 for hemodynamic collapse. The classical "Yang
collapse" may have included various conditions now differentiated
(septic shock, cardiogenic shock, dehydration, hypothermia). The
intense thermal nociceptive stimulus from salt moxa may have
provided a strong arousal/alerting signal via spinothalamic
activation, but this is 🔴 speculation.

**3. Neonatal/infantile diarrhea — 🟡 Plausible (limited)**

Several Chinese clinical studies report moxa at CV8 for infantile
diarrhea. The thin abdominal wall in neonates makes the T10
segmental thermal stimulus more direct. Limited controlled
evidence.

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: T10 somato-visceral reflex — the most
anatomically coherent mechanism on the lower CV.**

Thermal stimulation at CV8 (T10 dermatome) → T10 dorsal horn →
two pathways:

1. **Sympathetic modulation to GI tract:** T10 sympathetic
   preganglionic neurons → superior mesenteric ganglion → small
   intestine (ileum), cecum, ascending colon. T10 is the PRIMARY
   sympathetic outflow level for the mid-gut. Moxa at CV8 may
   modulate GI motility, secretion, and blood flow via this
   direct segmental pathway.

2. **Parasympathetic interaction:** the vagus nerve provides
   parasympathetic innervation to the GI tract down to the
   splenic flexure. Vagal afferents reach the NTS in the
   brainstem. While T10 somatic stimulation does not directly
   activate the vagus, supraspinal convergence in the brainstem
   (spinothalamic → reticular formation ← NTS) may allow T10
   input to modulate vagal tone indirectly. 🟡

**Needle prohibition mechanism — anatomical, not cultural:**
The needle prohibition at CV8 is FULLY JUSTIFIED by anatomy:
the umbilical center has the thinnest abdominal wall (~0.5–1.0 cm
in thin patients), no protective muscle layer, and a direct path
to the peritoneal cavity containing bowel loops. A needle inserted
at CV8 would almost certainly penetrate the peritoneum within
1 cm. This is a genuine anatomical hazard — the classical
prohibition encodes an accurate anatomical safety assessment.
Salt moxa avoids this risk entirely: thermal energy transfers
through intact skin without puncture.

**Spirit name significance:**
The 神 (Spirit) designation and the navel's embryological status
as the vestige of the umbilical cord connection may reflect
recognition of the navel as a psychologically and somatically
significant site. The navel is:
(a) the body's embryological "origin point" — the first physical
    connection to sustenance and life
(b) anatomically central — at the midpoint of the trunk, the
    midline, the T10 level (midpoint of the thoracolumbar
    sympathetic outflow)
(c) viscerally connected — T10 provides the most balanced
    sympathetic access to both upper GI (stomach/duodenum via
    T6–T10) and lower GI (ileum/cecum/colon via T10–L2)

Gap: the "Spirit" function (consciousness, psychological effects)
attributed to CV8 has no mechanism from the T10 segmental pathway
alone. Unlike GV24 (Spirit Court, frontal cortex access) or GV26
(Spirit, V2→ARAS), CV8's Spirit name may be metaphorical/
embryological rather than neurologically functional.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A02 S3, A07 S4.2, B08 §4.6/§5.2

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

**NEEDLING IS ABSOLUTELY PROHIBITED at CV8.** This is the ONLY
absolute needle prohibition in the entire 361-point system. All
classical and modern authorities agree without exception.

Cứu (B08): **SALT MOXA (隔盐灸) = CLASSICAL PRIMARY TECHNIQUE.**
Procedure:
1. Patient supine, navel exposed and cleaned
2. Fine table salt fills the umbilical depression, creating a
   level surface (~0.5 cm salt layer)
3. Small moxa cone (grain-of-rice to pea-sized) placed on the
   salt surface
4. Cone is ignited; heat conducts through salt into periumbilical
   tissues
5. When patient reports strong warmth, cone is replaced (3–7
   cones per session traditionally)
6. Salt is removed after treatment

Alternative: moxa stick warming — moxa stick held 2–3 cm above
the navel, 15–30 minutes. Simpler, safer, more commonly used in
modern practice. The moxa stick technique does not require salt.

Moxa box: a perforated box placed over the navel containing
burning moxa — provides sustained, distributed warming over a
wider area.

Ginger moxa: a slice of ginger placed over the navel instead of
salt, with moxa cone on top — used for Cold-type abdominal
conditions.
→ Cross-ref: B08 §2.3 (classification B3 — salt moxa), §4.6,
§5.2

Bấm huyệt (B02): gentle circular pressure around the umbilical
ring. Abdominal massage (fu guan 腹关) techniques include the
navel region as a central reference point. Self-massage around
CV8 is a common wellness practice in East Asian traditions.

Dry needling (B11): PROHIBITED — same as acupuncture needling.

#### An toàn

**NEEDLE PROHIBITION — ABSOLUTE.**
CV8 is the ONLY point in the 361-point system with an absolute
needle prohibition accepted by ALL authorities (classical and
modern). The prohibition is fully justified by anatomy:
- Umbilical wall thickness: ~0.5–1.0 cm (thinnest abdominal wall
  point)
- No protective muscle layer (rectus abdominis absent at center)
- Peritoneum directly deep to umbilical scar tissue
- Bowel loops immediately deep to peritoneum
- Needle insertion = almost certain peritoneal penetration with
  bowel perforation risk

This is the best example in the Atlas of traditional safety
knowledge encoding genuine anatomical risk.

**Salt moxa safety:**
- Fire risk: moxa cone on salt can ignite surrounding materials.
  Monitor continuously.
- Burn risk: if salt layer is too thin, heat transfers too quickly
  to skin → burns at the thin umbilical skin. Adequate salt depth
  (~0.5 cm) is essential.
- Skin sensitivity: umbilical skin is thinner than surrounding
  abdominal skin — thermal tolerance is lower.

**No pregnancy prohibition specific to CV8** (unlike CV2–CV7).
Historically, gentle moxa warming at CV8 is not contraindicated
in pregnancy. However, most practitioners avoid lower abdominal
moxa during pregnancy as a general precaution.

## Upper Abdomen Region (CV10–CV14)

Five points from 2 cun above the umbilicus (CV10) to 6 cun above
the umbilicus (CV14, at the sub-xiphoid level). This region spans
the epigastric zone of the Ren Mai — the projection surface of the
stomach, duodenum, pancreas, and the inferior heart. The Three Wan
series (CV10 Lower Wan, CV12 Middle Wan, CV13 Upper Wan) maps the
vertical axis of the stomach cavity. Nerve supply transitions from
T10 at CV10 through T9 at CV11–CV12 to T7–T8 at CV13–CV14. This
is the zone where the dermatome-viscerotome alignment for the
stomach (T6–T10 via greater splanchnic → celiac ganglion) begins
to match: CV12 at T8–T9 sits within the stomach viscerotome, unlike
the lower CV points where T10–T12 accesses pelvic organs rather
than gastric structures. The KI corridor continues: KI17 parallels
CV10, KI18 parallels CV11, KI19 parallels CV12, KI20 parallels
CV13, KI21 parallels CV14 — five consecutive, unbroken pairs (no
gaps, unlike the CV6/CV9 gaps below). Two of five points carry
major designations: CV12 (Front-Mu of Stomach + Hui-Meeting of Fu
Organs — DUAL designation) and CV14 (Front-Mu of Heart).

### CV12 — Trung Quản (中脘, Zhōngwǎn)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**

"Middle Stomach Cavity" (中 = middle/center, 脘 = stomach cavity).
On the anterior midline, 4 cun above the umbilicus (midway between
the umbilicus and the sternocostal junction). THE HIGHEST-PROFILE
DIGESTIVE POINT ON THE CONCEPTION VESSEL and one of the most
clinically important acupoints in the entire 361-point system for
gastrointestinal disorders. CV12 carries DUAL special designation:

1. **Front-Mu of the Stomach (胃募穴)** — the anterior diagnostic
   and treatment point for the Stomach organ. Shu-Mu pair: BL21
   (Weishu, T12) + CV12.
2. **Hui-Meeting of Fu Organs (腑会, Fuhui)** — the theoretical
   command point for ALL six Fu (Yang hollow) organs: Stomach,
   Small Intestine, Large Intestine, Bladder, Gallbladder, Triple
   Burner. This makes CV12 the single most important point for Fu
   organ disorders in TCM point-category theory.

This DUAL designation (Front-Mu + Hui-Meeting) is shared only with
CV17 (Front-Mu of Pericardium + Hui-Meeting of Qi) on the entire
Conception Vessel. Meeting point of CV + ST + SI + TE — three Yang
channels converge at the epigastric midline.

CV12 is the center of the Three Wan series (CV10 Lower, CV12
Middle, CV13 Upper) — the "Middle" of the stomach cavity,
corresponding approximately to the gastric body and antrum.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anterior midline, 4 cun
above the center of the umbilicus.

Anatomical landmarks: midpoint between the umbilicus (CV8) and the
sternocostal junction (CV15 region). 4/8 of the umbilicus-to-
sternocostal distance. On the linea alba, between the medial
borders of the rectus abdominis muscles. At this level, the stomach
body lies directly deep to the anterior abdominal wall — CV12's
location overlies the gastric projection area on the body surface.
The stomach body occupies the left upper quadrant with the greater
curvature projecting toward the midline at approximately this level.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.5 cun (~1.2–3.5 cm) perpendicular.
Layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → linea alba → posterior rectus
sheath → transversalis fascia → preperitoneal fat → peritoneum →
greater omentum → stomach (if directly deep). The greater omentum
provides a protective fatty layer between the peritoneum and the
stomach wall, creating a somewhat greater safety margin than at the
lower abdominal level.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Anterior cutaneous branch of the 8th intercostal nerve (T8)**
  — primary cutaneous innervation at 4 cun above the umbilicus.
  Some sources assign T7–T8 transitional zone.
- Spinal segments: T8 (primary), T7–T9 range.
- Deep: the stomach receives visceral innervation via:
  (a) **Greater splanchnic nerve (T5–T9)** → celiac ganglion →
      gastric plexus. This provides sympathetic innervation to the
      stomach (inhibits motility, reduces secretion, constricts
      blood vessels).
  (b) **Vagus nerve (CN X)** → anterior and posterior vagal trunks
      → gastric branches. Parasympathetic innervation (stimulates
      motility, increases secretion).
  CV12 at T8 sits WITHIN the stomach's sympathetic viscerotome
  (T5–T9) — this is the critical alignment that distinguishes CV12
  from the lower CV points (CV4–CV9, T10–T12), which access pelvic
  organs rather than the stomach.
- B01 documents CV12 as the Front-Mu of Stomach (L1112).
- A02 documents CV12 HRV effects: "prolonged ↑ vagal activity"
  (L1066).
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A02 S4.2 (L1066–1077), A07 S4.2

Fascial plane: linea alba at midline. Above the arcuate line —
full posterior rectus sheath present. The linea alba at the
epigastric level is narrower than at the infraumbilical level
(rectus muscles closer together).
→ Cross-ref: A03

Vascular: superior epigastric artery and vein within the rectus
sheath. These are the terminal branches of the internal thoracic
artery/vein. Deep: left gastric artery (branch of celiac trunk)
supplies the stomach at this level.

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → linea alba (thin) →
rectus abdominis (if slightly off midline) → posterior rectus
sheath → transversalis fascia → preperitoneal fat → peritoneum →
greater omentum → stomach wall.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Functional dyspepsia — 🟡 Plausible (strong)**

Updated MA (Frontiers in Medicine 2026): acupuncture for functional
dyspepsia — efficacy confirmed across multiple RCTs. ST36 and CV12
identified as the core acupoint combination via data mining of
clinical literature and validated by animal experiments. EA at
ST36+CV12 significantly alleviated duodenal low-grade inflammation
and mucosal barrier damage in FD rats (Frontiers in Medicine 2026).
Application frequency data: ST36 (19.77%), PC6 (16.86%), CV12
(12.21%) — CV12 is the third most-used acupoint for FD. The ST36+
CV12 He-Mu combination is the most consistently prescribed across
studies.

SR/MA (Frontiers in Medicine 2026): comprehensive meta-analysis
confirmed efficacy and safety of acupuncture for FD, with treatment
protocols typically 3–5 sessions/week over ~4 weeks.

**2. Gastric motility regulation — 🟡 Plausible (mechanism)**

**Dorsal Vagal Complex pathway:** EA at CV12 (and BL21) regulates
gastric motility through the Dorsal Vagal Complex (DVC) — the
brainstem parasympathetic center composed of the Dorsal Motor
Nucleus of the Vagus (DMNV) and the Nucleus of the Solitary Tract
(NTS). The DVC mediates gastric motility regulation by increasing
gastrointestinal hormone levels via vagal efferent output. This
establishes a specific neural pathway: CV12 surface stimulation →
T8 afferents → spinal cord → brainstem DVC → vagal efferent →
gastric smooth muscle (Yu et al. 2013, Evidence-Based CAM).

**TRPV1 receptor mechanism:** EA at CV12 inhibits gastric motility
at 2–4 mA in an intensity-dependent manner. TRPV1 (Transient
Receptor Potential Vanilloid 1) is partially involved in this
EA-mediated modulation. TRPV1 is a nociceptive/thermosensitive
receptor expressed on sensory nerve endings — its involvement at
CV12 suggests the mechanism engages capsaicin-sensitive C-fiber
afferents in the abdominal wall, which then relay to the DVC
(Yu et al. 2013, Evidence-Based CAM).

**Intensity-dependent bidirectional effect:** EA at CV12 can both
stimulate and inhibit gastric motility depending on parameters —
supporting the acupuncture concept of "regulating" (调节) rather
than simply stimulating or inhibiting. Low-intensity may
facilitate motility (parasympathetic), while higher intensity may
inhibit (sympathetic). This is consistent with the A02 principle:
intensity matters — low → vagal, high → sympathetic (A02 L1061).

**3. Rapid gastric emptying — 🟡 Plausible**

EA at ST36+CV12 vs CV12 alone for rapid gastric emptying: both
effective, but the combination was MORE effective than CV12 alone
(Li et al. 2021, PubMed). This validates the He-Mu combination
principle: the distal Lower He-Sea (ST36) adds to the local
Front-Mu (CV12) effect.

**4. HRV / autonomic modulation — 🟡 Plausible**

A02 documents CV12 as producing "prolonged ↑ vagal activity"
measured by HRV (L1066). The specific autonomic pathway for CV12:
"segmental sympathetic → GI" (A02 L1077). CV12 stimulation
activates vagal activity, inhibits sympathetic nerve activity, and
improves delayed gastric emptying. This HRV evidence is important
because it provides an objective, measurable autonomic outcome
rather than subjective symptom reports.

**5. Gastroesophageal reflux-related chronic cough — 🟡 Plausible**

SR/MA (Frontiers in Medicine 2026): acupuncture-related therapies
for GERD-related chronic cough, with CV12 included in treatment
protocols. The mechanism: CV12 addresses the gastric component
(reduce reflux via gastric motility regulation), while other
points address the cough reflex. Limited CV12-specific data within
the multi-point protocols.

**6. Ulcerative colitis — 🟡 Plausible (aggregate)**

SR/network MA (PMC 2025): CV12 among the most commonly used
acupoints for ulcerative colitis. The Hui-Meeting of Fu
designation provides the theoretical basis — UC is a Fu organ
(Large Intestine) disorder, and CV12 theoretically commands all
Fu organs. No CV12-specific efficacy data.

**Evidence pattern:** CV12 has STRONGER specific evidence than any
other CV point. The DVC pathway mechanism (CV12 → T8 → brainstem
→ vagal → stomach) is the most well-characterized neural pathway
for any Front-Mu point. The ST36+CV12 combination has the most
extensive data mining validation of any two-point formula in
clinical acupuncture. However, like CV4 and ST36, CV12 is rarely
tested in ISOLATION — it appears in multi-point protocols. The He-
Mu combination study (ST36+CV12 > CV12 alone) is one of the few
comparative designs that partially addresses this.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The epigastric region (CV12 area) becomes sensitized in:
- Gastritis: epigastric pressure pain threshold decreases during
  acute gastric inflammation — the classical clinical sign of
  epigastric tenderness.
- Functional dyspepsia: viscero-somatic hyperalgesia at the
  epigastric dermatome (T7–T9). Multiple studies document lowered
  pain threshold at the T8 level in FD patients.
- Peptic ulcer: epigastric deep tenderness, sometimes with
  guarding — reflecting T7–T9 viscero-somatic reflex activation.
- GERD: epigastric and substernal sensitization during acid
  reflux episodes.

Chen Rixin heat-sensitive moxibustion studies report enhanced heat
sensitivity at CV12 during gastric disorders — patients perceive
deeper warmth penetration and radiating heat sensation during active
gastritis compared to healthy controls. This is consistent with
C04's dynamic acupoint model: organ pathology sensitizes the
corresponding dermatome, lowering the stimulation threshold at the
overlying acupoint.

The T8 Head zone for stomach pathology (Beissner 2011) directly
predicts CV12 sensitization during gastric disease — CV12 sits at
the Head zone maximum for the stomach.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon), A07 L928

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: T8 somato-visceral reflex via the greater
splanchnic nerve — the first TRULY MATCHED segmental mechanism
on the upper CV.**

Surface stimulation at CV12 (T8 dermatome) → T8 dorsal horn →
three output pathways:

1. **Sympathetic modulation to gastric system:** T8 dorsal horn →
   T5–T9 sympathetic preganglionic neurons → greater splanchnic
   nerve → celiac ganglion → gastric plexus → stomach smooth
   muscle, secretory glands, and blood vessels. This is the
   DIRECT segmental pathway: T8 somatic input modulates T5–T9
   sympathetic output to the stomach. Unlike CV4–CV6 (which
   access T10–T12, pelvic organs), CV12 accesses the GASTRIC
   sympathetic system directly.

2. **Vagal modulation via DVC:** T8 afferent input → ascending
   spinoreticular pathway → brainstem Dorsal Vagal Complex (NTS +
   DMNV) → vagal efferent → anterior/posterior vagal trunks →
   gastric branches. This supraspinal relay mediates the
   parasympathetic component of CV12's effects: increased gastric
   motility, enhanced secretion, improved gastric emptying. The
   A02 HRV evidence (prolonged vagal activation at CV12)
   documents this pathway's activation.

3. **TRPV1-mediated sensory pathway:** TRPV1+ C-fiber afferents
   in the abdominal wall at T8 → dorsal horn → intensity-dependent
   bidirectional effect. TRPV1 involvement suggests the mechanism
   engages capsaicin-sensitive nociceptors. These same receptors
   are activated by heat (moxa at CV12) and by inflammatory
   mediators (explaining dynamic sensitization in gastritis).

**Front-Mu mechanism — CV12 MATCHES, BL21 does NOT:**

CV12 at T8 provides direct access to the stomach's sympathetic
viscerotome (T6–T9). This is the FIRST Front-Mu point on the CV
that matches its organ's segmental innervation (CV3/Bladder: T12
vs S2–S4 parasympathetic = indirect match; CV4/SI: T12 vs T9–T10
= non-matching; CV5/Triple Burner: "no organ"). The alignment at CV12
validates the Beissner principle: Front-Mu points fall within the
Head zone of the corresponding organ.

BL21 (Back-Shu) at T12 does NOT match the stomach viscerotome
(T6–T9). The clinical combination BL21+CV12 works because CV12
provides the segmentally correct gastric access, while BL21 may
contribute via:
(a) T12 sympathetic modulation of intestinal function (lesser
    splanchnic → superior mesenteric ganglion) — accessing the
    small intestine rather than the stomach
(b) Non-segmental mechanisms: paravertebral myofascial input,
    referred pain modulation, or central sensitization modification

**Hui-Meeting of Fu mechanism:**

The Hui-Meeting concept implies CV12 can treat ALL Fu organ
disorders. The mechanistic basis: T8 sympathetic access via the
celiac ganglion provides indirect connections to the hepatic
plexus (liver, gallbladder), splenic plexus (spleen), and
superior mesenteric plexus (SI, LI). While the PRIMARY target is
gastric, the celiac ganglion serves as a distribution hub for
upper abdominal autonomic innervation. This provides a limited
mechanistic basis for the Hui-Meeting claim — T8 access reaches
multiple upper abdominal Fu organs via the celiac hub. However,
the Hui-Meeting concept extends to Bladder (S2–S4) and Triple
Burner ("no organ") — these are NOT accessible from T8 and have
no segmental justification from CV12.

Gap: the Hui-Meeting of Fu claim is partially supported (upper
abdominal Fu via celiac ganglion) but NOT fully supported (pelvic
Fu organs are unreachable from T8). The traditional concept
overstates CV12's reach.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A02 S4.2 (L1066–1077), A07 S4.2 (L928),
AP-07 BL21 (L4166–4265), B01 S3.1

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5–1.5 cun perpendicular. De Qi: distending, warm
sensation in the epigastric region, may radiate toward the stomach
area or laterally along the costal margin. De Qi at CV12 is
typically described as a deep, spreading warmth — consistent with
engaging the deep visceral C-fiber (TRPV1+) afferents.

EA: 2 Hz for gastric motility promotion (parasympathetic
enhancement). Higher intensity (2–4 mA) for gastric motility
inhibition (sympathetic dominance) — Yu et al. 2013 TRPV1 study.
EA at CV12 shows intensity-dependent bidirectional effects:
subthreshold → facilitation, suprathreshold → inhibition.
ST36+CV12 EA combination > CV12 alone for regulating gastric
emptying (Li 2021).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2 (EA frequency mapping: 2Hz → enkephalin,
100Hz → dynorphin — Han)

Bấm huyệt (B02): moderate sustained pressure at epigastric
midline. Self-acupressure at CV12 for dyspepsia/nausea is a
commonly taught technique. Circular pressure (clockwise for
constipation, counterclockwise for diarrhea — traditional
abdominal massage directions, mechanistic basis unclear).

Cứu (B08): indirect moxa for Cold-type gastric disorders
(epigastric pain with Cold preference, improved by warmth).
Moxa stick 15–30 minutes. Ginger moxa (ginger slice interposed)
at CV12 is a common technique for nausea and chronic gastritis.
LR13+CV12 moxa = Hui-Meeting of Zang + Hui-Meeting of Fu combined
moxa protocol.
→ Cross-ref: B08 §4.6

Dry needling (B11): not standard at CV12. Epigastric rectus
abdominis trigger point needling targets lateral to the midline.
However, the upper rectus TrP zone at this level is recognized
in Travell & Simons as a source of pseudo-visceral epigastric pain.

#### An toàn

**Peritoneal/visceral puncture:** deep perpendicular needling at
CV12 risks peritoneal entry. The greater omentum lies superficial
to the stomach at this level, providing some protection. In thin
patients, the stomach wall may be <3 cm from the skin surface.
Clinical safe depth: 1–1.5 cun in normal body habitus.

**No specific vascular risk** at the midline. The internal
thoracic/superior epigastric vessels run within the rectus sheath,
lateral to the linea alba.

**Pregnancy:** CV12 is generally NOT specifically contraindicated
during pregnancy (unlike lower CV points CV2–CV7). Mild acupressure
at CV12 for morning sickness/nausea is used in some clinical
protocols during pregnancy (with PC6).

### CV13 — Thượng Quản (上脘, Shàngwǎn)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

"Upper Stomach Cavity" (上 = upper, 脘 = stomach cavity). On the
anterior midline, 5 cun above the umbilicus. The THIRD and
uppermost of the Three Wan series: CV10 (Lower), CV12 (Middle),
CV13 (Upper). Meeting point of CV + ST + SI. The "Upper Wan"
corresponds approximately to the gastric cardia and fundus — the
entry zone where food enters the stomach from the esophagus.
Traditionally associated with nausea, vomiting, hiccup, and acid
reflux — symptoms of the esophagogastric junction and upper
stomach. 1 cun above CV12 (Front-Mu ST).

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anterior midline, 5 cun
above the center of the umbilicus.

Anatomical landmarks: 5/8 of the distance from umbilicus to the
sternocostal junction. On the linea alba. 1 cun above CV12, 1 cun
below CV14. At this level, the stomach's cardia and fundus project
to the left upper abdominal surface, slightly left of the midline.
The left lobe of the liver may overlie the stomach at the midline.

Needle depth: 0.5–1.0 cun perpendicular. Somewhat shallower
recommended depth than lower CV points — the stomach and liver lie
closer to the surface at this level.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve: anterior cutaneous branch of the 7th or 8th intercostal
nerve (T7–T8 transitional zone). Spinal segment: T7–T8.
Vascular: superior epigastric artery/vein.
Fascial: linea alba at midline. Above the arcuate line.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03

#### Phân loại huyệt

No Five Shu, Yuan-Source, Xi-Cleft, Back-Shu, Front-Mu, Eight
Confluent, Hui-Meeting, or Luo-Connecting designation.

**Meeting point (交会穴):** CV13 is the meeting of CV + ST + SI
(足阳明 + 手太阳). Two Yang channels converge with CV at the
upper epigastric level (compare CV12 which adds TE for a triple
Yang meeting).

**Three Wan series — Upper (上脘):** CV13 is the "Upper Stomach
Cavity" — the uppermost of the three gastric mapping points.
Anatomically closest to the gastric cardia and esophagogastric
junction. Traditional indications emphasize ENTRY symptoms:
nausea, vomiting, hiccup (functions of the upper stomach and
cardia). This is clinically consistent: the cardia/fundus region
is responsible for receptive relaxation and preventing reflux;
dysfunction at this level produces nausea, vomiting, and hiccup.

**KI corridor:** KI20 (Futonggu 腹通谷, "Abdominal Open Valley")
sits 0.5 cun lateral to CV13. The name "Abdominal Open Valley"
suggests opening/clearing the GI passage — consistent with CV13's
upper gastric function.
→ Cross-ref: AP-08 KI20

#### Cross-tradition mapping

TCM: CV13 (Shangwan 上脘). Functions: harmonizes the Stomach,
descends counterflow Qi (降逆), resolves Phlegm. Used for: nausea,
vomiting, hiccup, acid reflux, epigastric pain, food stagnation
in the upper stomach, epilepsy. ✓
Trigger point: upper rectus abdominis TrPs at the epigastric level.
🟡
Ashi: upper epigastric tenderness in GERD, esophagitis, cardia
disorders. 🟡
Marma: no specific Marma at this level. ✗
Head zone: T7–T8 dermatome. Viscerotome: T7–T8 covers the upper
stomach, esophageal hiatus, and gastric cardia via greater
splanchnic. 🟡
Anatomy Trains: SFL through rectus abdominis. 🟡
Neurogenic: no specific data. ✗
Cupping: not standard. ✗

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡).

#### Chỉ định truyền thống

Jiayi Jing, Dacheng: nausea, vomiting, hiccup (呃逆), acid
reflux, epigastric pain, food stagnation in the upper stomach,
epilepsy, palpitations (Stomach-Heart connection at the
epigastric-subxiphoid transition).

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng:
- CV13+PC6 for nausea/vomiting: upper gastric (CV13) + anti-emetic
  (PC6) = combined protocol for persistent vomiting
- CV13+CV12+CV10 (Three Wan series): see CV10 entry
- CV13 for hiccup (呃逆): the upper gastric/cardia location makes
  CV13 the most anatomically relevant of the Three Wan for
  diaphragmatic and cardia-related hiccup
- Less commonly used than CV12 — CV13 serves as an adjunct for
  symptoms specifically referable to the upper stomach

#### An toàn

**Peritoneal/visceral puncture:** the stomach and left lobe of the
liver lie close to the surface at this level. Deep needling risks
hepatic or gastric puncture. Clinical safe depth: 0.5–1.0 cun
in most patients.
**No specific pregnancy prohibition** for CV13.

### CV14 — Cự Khuyết (巨阙, Jùquē)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

"Great Tower Gate" (巨 = great/giant, 阙 = tower gate/palace
gate). On the anterior midline, 6 cun above the umbilicus (2 cun
below the sternocostal junction). The **Front-Mu point of the
Heart (心募穴)** — the anterior diagnostic and treatment point for
the Heart organ. The name 巨阙 also refers to a famous ancient
sword (the Juque sword) — metaphorically, the "great weapon" or
"great gateway" at the subxiphoid level. CV14 is the LAST
Front-Mu point on the abdomen before the thoracic transition.
It sits at the epigastric-to-substernal boundary, where the
anterior body transitions from the soft abdominal wall to the
rigid xiphoid process and sternum.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anterior midline, 6 cun
above the center of the umbilicus.

Anatomical landmarks: 6/8 of the distance from umbilicus to the
sternocostal junction. 2 cun below the xiphisternal junction
(sternocostal angle where xiphoid meets the body of the sternum).
On the linea alba at the subxiphoid level. The xiphoid process
begins approximately 1–2 cun above CV14. At this level, the
abdominal wall is relatively thin — the rectus abdominis tapers
toward its sternal/costal attachment, and the linea alba narrows.
The diaphragm lies deep to the peritoneum, separating the
abdominal and thoracic cavities. The heart's inferior surface
rests on the diaphragm — CV14 is the closest anterior midline
point to the heart via the transdiaphragmatic route.

Needle depth range: 0.5–1.0 cun (~1.2–2.5 cm) perpendicular or
slightly inferiorly oblique. CAUTION: this is the subxiphoid
region — deeper needling risks diaphragmatic penetration and, in
extreme cases, cardiac puncture (pericardial access via the
subxiphoid route — the same approach used for pericardiocentesis).
Standard clinical depth is safe; excessive depth is dangerous.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Anterior cutaneous branch of the 7th intercostal nerve (T7)**
  — primary cutaneous innervation at 6 cun above the umbilicus.
  Some sources assign T6–T7 transition.
- Spinal segments: T7 (primary), T6–T7 range.
- Deep: the heart receives innervation via:
  (a) **Cardiac sympathetic nerves:** T1–T5 (primarily T2–T4)
      via stellate ganglion and upper thoracic sympathetic chain →
      cardiac plexus. The sympathetic innervation of the heart
      originates ABOVE CV14's segmental level (T7).
  (b) **Vagus nerve (CN X):** cardiac vagal branches → cardiac
      plexus. Parasympathetic.
  (c) **Cardiac referred pain:** classically T1–T5 (left arm,
      jaw, chest). The anterior midline at T7 (CV14) is at the
      INFERIOR margin of cardiac referred pain distribution.
  CV14 at T7 is BELOW the heart's primary sympathetic viscerotome
  (T1–T5) but the subxiphoid position provides the closest anterior
  midline surface access to the heart via the diaphragm. The
  Front-Mu mechanism at CV14 may rely on the diaphragmatic route
  (anatomical proximity) rather than direct segmental match.
- BL15 (Xinshu, Back-Shu of Heart) at T5 provides the segmentally
  correct posterior access to cardiac sympathetic innervation.

Fascial plane: linea alba at the subxiphoid level, narrowing toward
the sternal attachment. The rectus abdominis origin inserts on the
xiphoid process and costal cartilages of ribs 5–7.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A03

Vascular: superior epigastric artery/vein (terminal internal
thoracic branches) within the rectus sheath. Deep: the inferior
phrenic arteries (diaphragmatic surface) and the pericardial
blood supply lie deep to the diaphragm.

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous fat → linea alba/rectus
abdominis (tapering) → posterior rectus sheath → transversalis
fascia → preperitoneal fat → peritoneum → diaphragm (deep).
The diaphragm's central tendon lies directly deep at the midline
subxiphoid level — the same anatomical window used for subxiphoid
echocardiography and pericardiocentesis.

#### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Xi-Cleft: No. Back-Shu: No.
Eight Confluent: No. Hui-Meeting: No. Luo-Connecting: No.

**Front-Mu of the Heart (心募穴).** CV14 is the anterior
diagnostic and treatment point for the Heart organ.
Shu-Mu pairing: BL15 (Xinshu, T5 level) + CV14.

The BL15+CV14 Shu-Mu pair shows an INTERESTING alignment pattern:
- **BL15 (Back-Shu)** at T5 — directly WITHIN the cardiac
  sympathetic viscerotome (T1–T5). The Back-Shu MATCHES the
  organ's segmental innervation. T5 accesses the cardiac
  sympathetic chain via the thoracic paravertebral ganglia.
- **CV14 (Front-Mu)** at T7 — BELOW the cardiac viscerotome
  (T1–T5). The Front-Mu does NOT match segmentally.

This is the REVERSE of the CV12/BL21 pattern (where CV12
Front-Mu matched but BL21 Back-Shu did not). For Heart:
BL15 matches, CV14 does not.

CV14's Front-Mu designation may reflect ANATOMICAL PROXIMITY
rather than segmental innervation: CV14 at the subxiphoid level
is the closest anterior midline point to the heart (via the
diaphragmatic/pericardial route), even though its T7 dermatome
is below the cardiac sympathetic outflow. The heart projects to
the subxiphoid region via the pericardial attachment to the
central diaphragm.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL15 (Back-Shu of Heart, T5, L3014–3080),
AP-05 HT6+BL15+CV14 (acute cardiac protocol), B01 S3.1

**KI corridor:** KI21 (Youmen 幽门, "Dark Gate" or "Pyloric Gate")
sits 0.5 cun lateral to CV14. KI21 is the HIGHEST abdominal KI
point — above this level, the KI channel shifts to 2 cun lateral
on the chest (KI22 at CV16 level). The name "Youmen" (幽门)
literally means "pyloric gate" — the same term used in modern
Chinese anatomy for the pylorus. KI21 is the Chong Mai terminus
in the abdomen before the chest transition.
→ Cross-ref: AP-08 KI21

#### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** CV14 (Juque 巨阙). Functions: calms the
Heart, clears Heart Fire, transforms Phlegm obstructing the
Heart, regulates Heart Qi. Primary point for cardiac and
psychiatric disorders via the Heart-Shen connection (Heart houses
the Spirit/Shen in TCM). Used for: chest pain, palpitations,
cardiac pain, vomiting, acid reflux, epilepsy, mania, anxiety,
poor memory. ✓

**2. Trigger point:** rectus abdominis trigger points at the
subxiphoid level. The most superior rectus TrPs refer pain to the
substernal and epigastric region — mimicking cardiac and upper GI
symptoms ("pseudo-cardiac" abdominal wall pain). This is a
clinically important differential diagnosis: subxiphoid TrP pain
can mimic angina pectoris. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** subxiphoid tenderness is a clinical
sign in:
- Pericarditis (subxiphoid pressure reproduces pain)
- GERD/esophagitis (epigastric-substernal pain)
- Xiphoidalgia (xiphoid process tenderness syndrome)
- Functional chest pain (costochondral/muscular)
The CV14 area is a standard palpation zone for differentiating
cardiac from non-cardiac chest pain. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Hridaya Marma (हृदय, "Heart") — located in the
chest/substernal region. One of the 3 Sadyah Pranahara (immediately
lethal) thoracic Marmas. Governs Prana Vayu (vital breath),
consciousness, and cardiac function. CV14 at the subxiphoid level
falls at the inferior boundary of the Hridaya Marma zone. The
Ayurvedic recognition of this region as governing cardiac function
parallels CV14's Front-Mu of Heart designation. ~2–3 cm. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** T7 dermatome at the surface. Viscerotome: T7
contributes to the hepatic and gastric sympathetic territory (via
greater splanchnic nerve). The cardiac viscerotome (T1–T5) extends
its referred pain zone inferiorly to include the subxiphoid region
in some patients (atypical cardiac pain). Beissner 2011: BL15
Back-Shu at T5 matches the cardiac Head zone precisely; CV14
Front-Mu at T7 falls at the inferior edge of the extended cardiac
projection. 🟡

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SFL through rectus abdominis (superior
attachment at xiphoid and costal cartilage). The DFL includes the
pericardial fascia, which attaches to the central diaphragm — CV14
sits at the surface projection of this deep pericardial-
diaphragmatic fascial connection. 🟡

**7. Neurogenic spot:** no specific data at the subxiphoid midline.
Kim 2017 documented cardiac-associated spots at C8–T2 (wrist/
forearm level), not at the subxiphoid region. ✗

**8. Cupping point:** not standard at the subxiphoid midline. ✗

**Convergence score: 6/8** (TCM ✓, Trigger point 🟡, Ashi 🟡,
Marma 🟡, Head zone 🟡, Anatomy Trains 🟡). High convergence. The
subxiphoid region is recognized across multiple traditions as a
critical zone for cardiac and upper GI function.

#### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Nanjing (Ch.66): names CV14 as the Front-Mu of the Heart
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: cardiac pain, palpitations, chest
  oppression, vomiting, acid reflux, abdominal fullness, epilepsy,
  mania, fearfulness, poor memory
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: ALL Heart-related disorders — cardiac pain,
  palpitations, anxiety, mania, epilepsy, insomnia, dreaminess.
  Also: vomiting, hiccup, chest fullness. The psychiatric
  indications (mania, epilepsy, anxiety) reflect TCM's Heart-Shen
  model: the Heart houses consciousness, so Heart-Front-Mu treats
  Shen disturbance.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Cardiac: chest pain, palpitations, cardiac pain
- Psychiatric: mania, epilepsy, anxiety, poor memory, insomnia
- Upper GI: vomiting, acid reflux, hiccup, nausea, abdominal
  distension
- Respiratory: cough, dyspnea (subxiphoid access to diaphragm)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- **HT6+BL15+CV14 acute cardiac pain protocol:** the classical
  triad for acute cardiac/chest pain. HT6 (Xi-Cleft of Heart,
  distal = acute conditions) + BL15 (Back-Shu of Heart, posterior)
  + CV14 (Front-Mu of Heart, anterior). This combination addresses
  the Heart from three angles: distal Xi-Cleft for acute arrest
  of pain, posterior Shu for autonomic regulation, anterior Mu for
  local cardiac access.
  → Cross-ref: AP-05 HT6 (L1627–1694)
- **CV14+PC6 for chest pain:** Front-Mu of Heart + Luo-Connecting
  of PC (anatomically = median nerve access to cardiac vagal
  pathway). The PC6+CV14 combination addresses both local (CV14)
  and distal vagal (PC6) mechanisms.
- **CV14+CV12 for epigastric-cardiac pain:** the subxiphoid-
  epigastric zone where cardiac and gastric symptoms overlap.
  CV14 (Heart Front-Mu) + CV12 (Stomach Front-Mu) = differential
  treatment of the Heart-Stomach overlap zone.
- **CV14+GV20 for mania/epilepsy:** Front-Mu of Heart (anterior
  midline) + vertex point (posterior midline, cranial) = combined
  anterior-posterior approach for psychiatric conditions via the
  Heart-Brain axis.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Angina pectoris / cardiac pain — 🟡 Plausible**

SR/network MA (PMC 2025): acupuncture combined with multiple
therapies for angina pectoris. CV14 included in treatment protocols
as the Front-Mu of Heart. No CV14-specific efficacy data isolated
from multi-point protocols. The Shu-Mu pair (BL15+CV14) is the
most commonly cited acupuncture combination for angina in Chinese
clinical literature.

**2. Psychiatric conditions (anxiety, mania) — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Traditional Heart-Shen indications (mania, epilepsy, anxiety) are
attributed to CV14 as Front-Mu of Heart. No SR/MA tests CV14
specifically for psychiatric conditions. The mechanism for
psychiatric effects from subxiphoid stimulation is unclear: T7
segmental access does not directly project to limbic or cortical
structures. The "Heart-Shen" model is a TCM explanatory framework,
not a validated mechanism.

**3. GERD / acid reflux — 🟡 Plausible (mechanism)**

CV14's subxiphoid location overlies the gastroesophageal junction.
T7 segmental access reaches the lower esophageal sphincter and
gastric cardia via the greater splanchnic nerve. Mechanistically,
CV14 may modulate esophagogastric junction tone through T6–T7
sympathetic output. However, CV12 and PC6 are more commonly
studied for GERD than CV14.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The subxiphoid region (CV14 area) becomes sensitized in:
- Angina pectoris: subxiphoid/epigastric pain can be a
  presentation of inferior myocardial ischemia (diaphragmatic
  surface of the heart refers pain to T7 territory). Subxiphoid
  tenderness in cardiac disease is clinically documented.
- Pericarditis: subxiphoid pressure reproduces pericardial pain.
  The subxiphoid pericardial window (used for pericardiocentesis)
  exploits the same anatomical proximity that CV14 accesses.
- GERD: epigastric-substernal sensitization during acid reflux.
- Xiphoidalgia: local tenderness of the xiphoid process, often
  post-traumatic or inflammatory.

The dynamic sensitization at CV14 reflects dual visceral
convergence: cardiac afferents (T1–T5, extended to T6–T7 in
inferior MI) AND gastric afferents (T6–T9) both project to the
subxiphoid area. This overlap explains why CV14 is used for both
cardiac and gastric disorders — and why patients often cannot
distinguish "heartburn" (GERD) from "heart pain" (angina) at this
anatomical level.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: subxiphoid anatomical proximity to the heart
via the diaphragm — NOT direct segmental match.**

Unlike BL15 (T5, direct cardiac sympathetic access), CV14 at T7
does NOT directly access the cardiac sympathetic viscerotome
(T1–T5). The mechanism at CV14 likely operates through:

1. **Anatomical proximity:** the subxiphoid approach provides the
   closest anterior midline surface access to the pericardium.
   The diaphragm's central tendon lies directly deep to CV14,
   and the heart rests on the diaphragm's superior surface.
   Needling at CV14 may stimulate the phrenic nerve (C3–C5)
   terminals in the diaphragmatic peritoneum, with ascending
   input reaching the cervical spinal cord and brainstem.

2. **Vagal modulation via diaphragmatic stimulation:** the vagus
   nerve passes through the esophageal hiatus of the diaphragm at
   approximately this level. Subxiphoid stimulation may indirectly
   modulate vagal tone by affecting the diaphragmatic/periesophageal
   tissues where vagal trunks pass. This would provide an indirect
   cardiac parasympathetic effect.

3. **T7 segmental contribution (partial):** while T7 is below the
   primary cardiac viscerotome, T6–T7 sympathetic fibers contribute
   to the greater splanchnic nerve, which passes through the
   diaphragm near the subxiphoid level. Some T7 sympathetic output
   may reach the cardiac plexus via interganglion fibers between
   the celiac and thoracic cardiac ganglia — a minor but anatomically
   real pathway.

**Front-Mu of Heart — proximity vs segmental mechanism:**

CV14 represents a DIFFERENT Front-Mu mechanism than CV12:
- CV12 (Front-Mu of Stomach) works via SEGMENTAL match (T8 = within
  stomach viscerotome T6–T9)
- CV14 (Front-Mu of Heart) works via ANATOMICAL PROXIMITY (sub-
  xiphoid = closest anterior midline point to heart, despite T7
  being below the cardiac viscerotome T1–T5)

This distinction suggests Front-Mu points use AT LEAST TWO
different mechanisms: segmental (when the Front-Mu falls within
the organ's viscerotome) and proximity-based (when the Front-Mu
sits at the closest surface projection of the organ, regardless
of segmental innervation).

Gap: the psychiatric indications (mania, anxiety, epilepsy) from
CV14 have no clear mechanism via T7 segmental access or subxiphoid
proximity. Unlike GV20 (vertex, direct cortical access) or GV26
(V2 → ARAS), CV14 has no pathway to consciousness-related brain
structures. The "Heart houses Shen" model is TCM theory, not
validated mechanism.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2, A02 S3, A07 S4.2, AP-05 HT6 (L1627–1694),
AP-07 BL15 (L3014–3080)

## Chest / Sternal Region (CV15–CV21)

Seven points from the xiphoid process (CV15) ascending through the
body of the sternum (CV16–CV20) to the manubrium (CV21). This
region marks a fundamental anatomical transition from the muscular
abdominal wall to the rigid bony thorax.

Key regional features:
- **Sternal midline = bone surface.** Unlike abdominal CV points
  (which overlie soft tissue → viscera), sternal CV points sit on
  the periosteum of the sternum. This eliminates peritoneal
  puncture risk (abdominal) and pneumothorax risk (intercostal).
  Depth is limited to sternal thickness (~1–2 cm in adults), and
  oblique/transverse needling is standard technique.
- **Sternal foramen (foramen sternale):** congenital defect (4–8%
  prevalence) creating a perforation in the lower sternal body,
  most commonly at the 3rd–4th sternebra (approximately CV16–CV17
  level). If present, perpendicular deep needling at midline could
  penetrate the anterior mediastinum — risk of pneumomediastinum
  or pericardial puncture. Always use oblique/transverse technique
  at sternal points.
- **KI corridor shift:** the Kidney channel corridor widens from
  0.5 cun lateral (abdomen, KI11–KI21) to 2 cun lateral (chest,
  KI22–KI27) at the diaphragm level. CV15 at the xiphoid sits at
  this transition. From CV16 upward, KI points sit 2 cun lateral
  to the sternum, over the costal cartilages.
- **Nerve supply:** anterior cutaneous branches of intercostal
  nerves provide ascending segmental innervation: T7 (CV15) → T5
  (CV16) → T4 (CV17) → T3 (CV18) → T2 (CV19) → T1 (CV20–CV21).
  The cardiac sympathetic viscerotome (T1–T5) overlaps with the
  upper sternal points (CV17–CV21).
- **Naming pattern:** sternal CV points carry an imperial-celestial
  theme ascending from the xiphoid: Dove Tail (鸠尾) → Central
  Court (中庭) → Chest Center (膻中) → Jade Hall (玉堂) → Purple
  Palace (紫宫) → Flowery Canopy (华盖) → Jade Pivot (璇玑). This
  vertical "ascent through the imperial court" metaphor reflects
  the chest as the domain of the Heart-Emperor (心为君主之官).

### CV15 — Cưu Vĩ (鸠尾, Jiūwěi)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry) — Luo-Connecting of Ren Mai**

Name 鸠尾 (Jiūwěi) = "Dove Tail" — the xiphoid process, whose
tapering cartilaginous shape resembles the tail feathers of a
turtledove (鸠). The xiphoid is the smallest and most variable

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

The xiphoid/sub-xiphoid region (CV15 area) becomes sensitized in:
- Cardiac conditions: xiphoid tenderness (xiphodynia) is reported

  activation.

  territory) extends toward the midline.
- Diaphragmatic irritation: phrenic nerve afferents at the central
  tendon → xiphoid tenderness as referred phrenic pain. Hiccup and
  persistent singultus may sensitize this zone.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Primary pathway: T7 intercostal segmental + phrenic nerve
diaphragmatic.**

Two afferent sources converge at CV15:

1. **T7 intercostal:** anterior cutaneous branch → T7 dorsal horn.
   Provides the same cardiac-gastric transitional access as CV14
   (also T7), though CV15 sits higher (xiphoid vs sub-xiphoid).

2. **Phrenic nerve:** the diaphragm's central tendon attaches to
   the posterior xiphoid process. Phrenic nerve (C3–C5) terminals
   innervate this attachment. Stimulation at CV15 may activate
   ascending phrenic afferents → cervical spinal cord → brainstem.
   This phrenic pathway is unique among CV points — only CV15 (and
   possibly CV14) can access the phrenic system from the anterior
   midline.

**Luo dispersal mechanism:** the traditional Luo "scattering across

SI1+CV17+ST18 classical triad plus TCM-based additions vs

months: 35% vs 15%. Unblinded, but large effect size.

🟡 — two SRs positive with large effect sizes, one large RCT with
single-point efficacy. Evidence quality low by GRADE (mostly Chinese
trials, limited blinding, high heterogeneity). Prolactin pathway
documented.

**2. COPD / respiratory — 🟡 Plausible**

SR/MA (Chen et al. 2025, Journal of Thoracic Disease, 15 RCTs,
N=1,076): electroacupuncture for COPD. CV17 among the most
frequently used points alongside ST36, CV12, LU9, BL13. FVC:
WMD=0.28L (95% CI 0.14–0.43, p<0.001). FEV1%: WMD=3.99% (95% CI
2.30–5.67, p<0.001). FEV1/FVC%: WMD=3.77% (95% CI 2.38–5.17,
p<0.001). Treatment duration ≥8 weeks needed for significant
FEV1/FVC improvement. CV17 specifically noted to "improve
diaphragmatic mobility, enhance pulmonary function and oxygenation."

However, for asthma specifically, network analyses rank BL13, GV14,
EX-B1, and ST36 above CV17. CV17 is a supporting point, not the
primary point for asthma.

🟡 — included in positive SRs for COPD and respiratory conditions,
but always as part of multi-point protocols. Cannot isolate CV17's
specific contribution.

**3. Cardiac autonomic modulation — 🟡 Plausible (BEST MECHANISTIC
EVIDENCE)**

Crossover study (Kurono et al. 2011, Autonomic Neuroscience,
N=14 healthy males): epifascial acupunctural stimulation at CV17
(Danzhong) vs CV16 (Zhongting, one intercostal space below =
control). **CV17 decreased heart rate (p=0.01) and increased high-
frequency HRV component (cardiac vagal index, p=0.01). CV16 showed
NO such effect.** LF/HF ratio (sympathovagal balance) unchanged.

This is the **SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT MECHANISTIC STUDY for CV17**
because it demonstrates **POINT SPECIFICITY** — the cardiac vagal
effect is unique to CV17 at T4, not a generic sternal stimulation
response. CV16 at T5 (one segment lower) produced no cardiac
autonomic change, despite being on the same bone surface, ruling
out nonspecific sternal periosteal stimulation.

Follow-up (Kurono et al. 2013): confirmed "site-specific organ-
selective effect of epifascial acupuncture on cardiac and gastric
autonomic functions."

🟡 — strong mechanistic evidence for point-specific cardiac vagal
modulation, but small sample (N=14) and healthy volunteers only.
No disease-state replication.

**4. Anti-inflammatory / asthma (animal) — 🟡 Plausible**

Animal study (Tang et al. 2022, Acupuncture Research, N=40 Wistar
rats): catgut embedding at CV17 + BL13 + EX-B1 in asthmatic rats
reduced p38MAPK, ICAM-1, IL-4 mRNA expression in lung tissue.
Decreased eosinophil infiltration and promoted eosinophil apoptosis.
Effects comparable to dexamethasone group.

🟡 — animal data supporting anti-inflammatory mechanism, but CV17
was not isolated (part of 3-point protocol).

**5. Angina / coronary — 🟡 Plausible**

SRs for angina (Huang et al. 2019, N=1,916; Kong et al. 2025,
N=3,976) show positive results for acupuncture in angina, but
neither catalogued individual acupoint contributions. CV17 included
in standard angina protocols. Cannot isolate CV17-specific effect.

🟡 — part of positive angina evidence, no CV17-specific data.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization at T4 sternal midline:
- **Angina pectoris:** precordial chest pain and sternal tenderness
  at T4 level in cardiac ischemia. The sternal midline at the
  nipple line is a classical location for angina pain. CV17
  sensitization expected in cardiac patients.
- **Insufficient lactation:** breast engorgement and distension in
  postpartum may create palpable fullness at the CV17 region.
  Mechanical tissue changes (breast swelling) alter local tissue
  tension around the sternal attachment of breast fascia.
- **Asthma / COPD:** chest tightness and dyspnea produce muscular
  hypertonicity of the intercostal and accessory respiratory
  muscles at T4. Patient-reported "chest tightness" maps to the
  CV17 territory.
- **Costochondritis:** tenderness at the 4th costochondral junction

  pain. CV17 sits at the midline immediately medial to this
  junction.

The Kurono 2011 finding that CV17 (not CV16) modulates cardiac
vagal tone suggests T4 has a specific threshold for cardiac
autonomic access — possibly related to the density or type of
cardiac-specific afferent projections at T4 compared to T5.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

**Three mechanisms at CV17:**

1. **T4 cardiac somatovisceral reflex (confirmed):** Kurono 2011

   cardiac vagal tone (HF-HRV), while T5 (CV16) does not. This

   autonomic output. Pathway: T4 intercostal afferents → T4 dorsal
   horn → converging on cardiac sympathetic neurons → cardiac
   plexus. Ascending T4 afferents may also reach brainstem NTS via
   spinoreticular pathways, influencing parasympathetic (vagal)
   cardiac output.

2. **Lactation via T4 mammary innervation:** the 4th intercostal
   nerve provides primary sensory innervation to the nipple-areola

   mammary gland milk production. The SR evidence (prolactin

   hypothalamic-pituitary axis. This parallels the SI1 mechanism
   (AP-06), where SI1 at C8/T1 was proposed to act via a similar
   hypothalamic-pituitary pathway but from a more distant segment.
   → Cross-ref: AP-06 SI1 (L291 — ⚡ hypothalamic-hormonal)

3. **Respiratory modulation via T4 bronchial access:** bronchial
   tree receives sympathetic innervation from T2–T4. Stimulation
   at CV17 (T4) may modulate bronchomotor tone via T4 sympathetic
   neurons → stellate ganglion/upper thoracic ganglia → bronchial
   smooth muscle. This provides a direct segmental mechanism for
   CV17's respiratory indications, though this specific pathway has
   NOT been individually demonstrated (unlike the cardiac effect).

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis) — Window of Heaven, Meeting

study on CV22 and ST11 acupuncture anatomy).

- **Supraclavicular nerves (C3-C4):** branches of the cervical

→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A01 S2.3

fascial plane, staying superficial to the trachea.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

The depression lies between the two sternal heads of the SCM.
Deep to the fascia: infrahyoid strap muscles (sternohyoid,
sternothyroid) flank the trachea bilaterally but do not cross the
midline at this level.

#### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**Post-stroke dysphagia:**
🟡 Plausible. CV22 is the most frequently selected throat point
in acupuncture protocols for post-stroke dysphagia. Deep
acupuncture at CV22 (80 mm posteroinferior insertion along
posterior manubrium) combined with swallowing rehabilitation
showed higher effective rate (88.57% vs 74.29%) than conventional
acupuncture in an RCT of 70 patients (Pan 2019).

Overview of 31 SRs on acupuncture for post-stroke dysphagia
(Ye 2019): consistently positive direction but evidence quality
mostly critically low per AMSTAR2 (22/31 critically low, 5 low,
4 moderate). Updated overview 2025 confirmed: all SRs positive
direction, quality remains critically low. MA on aspiration
specifically (2024, 16 articles N=1,284): acupuncture reduced
penetration-aspiration scale scores.

Note: the "deep acupuncture" variant (80 mm) uses a fundamentally
different trajectory and depth than standard clinical technique
(15-25 mm). These two approaches should be evaluated separately.

**Globus pharyngeus (plum-pit Qi):**
🟡 Plausible. Randomized single-blind crossover trial (N=80):
transcutaneous electroacupuncture at CV22 significantly improved
globus symptoms compared to control (Zhang 2020). The globus
sensation (梅核气, plum-pit Qi) maps closely to functional globus
pharyngeus in modern gastroenterology — a diagnosis of exclusion
for persistent sensation of a lump in the throat without
structural pathology. CV22 provides the most direct local access
to the suprahyoid-infrahyoid junction where globus sensation
localizes.

**Bronchial asthma:**
🟡 Plausible. CV22 appears in >80% of acupuncture protocols for
asthma in data-mining studies — the most-selected throat point.
Network MA 2023 (BMC Pulmonary Medicine): acupuncture modalities
for bronchial asthma improved clinical response rate and
pulmonary function. SR/MA 2025 (Journal of Asthma): acupuncture
vs sham improved quality of life, FEV1%, symptom scores, and
reduced exacerbation frequency. However, no study isolates
CV22's individual contribution from multi-point protocols.

**Cough (chronic):**
🟡 Plausible. CV22 is the most-selected point for chronic cough
in TCM data-mining analyses. No single-point RCT. Protocol-level
evidence only.

**Voice/speech disorders:**
🔴 Unvalidated. Traditional use for hoarseness and sudden voice
loss. No modern evidence specifically for CV22 in voice
disorders.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

**Globus pharyngeus:** patients with globus sensation report
increased sensitivity and tenderness at CV22 when the subjective
throat lump feeling is active. The suprasternal region may show
lowered pressure-pain thresholds during acute globus episodes
(clinical observation, not formally studied in dynamic acupoint
literature).

**Asthma exacerbation:** during acute asthma, the suprasternal
notch shows visible retraction (suprasternal retractions = sign
of increased respiratory effort and airway obstruction). This is
a VISIBLE dynamic change at the point's exact anatomical
location — the soft tissue configuration physically changes
during the pathological state, providing a macroscopic parallel
to the microscopic sensitization phenomena documented at other
dynamic acupoints.

**Cough reflex:** the suprasternal area becomes hyperreflexive
during upper respiratory infection — light touch or pressure at
CV22 can trigger coughing in patients with active bronchitis.
This increased mechanical sensitivity represents a form of
peripheral sensitization specific to the respiratory pathology.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Respiratory effects (cough, asthma, dyspnea):**
CV22 at the suprasternal notch is directly anterior to the
extrathoracic trachea. The posteroinferior needling technique
directs the needle along the retrosternal space where the vagus
nerves descend.

Potential mechanisms:
1. **Tracheal mucosal reflex:** needle approaching tracheal
   cartilage (at ~15 mm depth) stimulates tracheal afferents →
   vagal reflex arc → modulation of bronchomotor tone. This is
   a local segmental reflex distinct from the deeper vagal
   modulation.
2. **Vagal afferent convergence:** C3-C4 supraclavicular nerve
   stimulation converges with vagal afferents at the brainstem
   NTS (nucleus tractus solitarius). The NTS is the central hub
   for respiratory rhythm, cough reflex, and bronchospasm
   threshold. Needle-induced C3-C4 input may modulate NTS
   processing of vagal respiratory signals.
3. **Infrahyoid muscle relaxation:** strap muscle tension
   (sternohyoid, sternothyroid) contributes to throat
   constriction sensation. Needle-induced local muscle relaxation
   reduces perceived dyspnea and globus.

Gap: no study has directly measured vagal nerve activation or NTS
activity during CV22 stimulation. The vagal access hypothesis is
anatomically plausible but mechanistically unconfirmed. 🟡

**Dysphagia/globus (swallowing and throat sensation):**
1. **Cricopharyngeal access:** the cricopharyngeus (upper
   esophageal sphincter) sits at C6 level, just above the
   suprasternal notch. Deep needling trajectories may influence
   the retropharyngeal space near this muscle, reducing spasm
   and improving esophageal inlet opening.
2. **Recurrent laryngeal nerve:** RLN innervates the posterior
   cricoarytenoid (the only laryngeal abductor) and other
   intrinsic laryngeal muscles. RLN stimulation via deep
   needling could improve laryngeal closure during swallowing →
   reduced aspiration. This mechanism aligns with the deep
   acupuncture variant (80 mm) that specifically targets
   retrosternal RLN proximity.
3. **Hyoid complex:** the infrahyoid muscles accessible from
   CV22 (sternohyoid, sternothyroid) control hyoid depression.
   Modulating their tone affects hyoid excursion during the
   pharyngeal phase of swallowing.

Gap: the "deep acupuncture" technique (80 mm along posterior
manubrium) in dysphagia studies involves a fundamentally
different mechanism than standard-depth needling (15-25 mm). The
two should not be conflated — deep insertion accesses the
retrosternal space near the brachiocephalic vessels and vagal
trunks, while standard depth stays within superficial tissues.
🟡

**Window of Heaven mechanism — positional uniqueness:**
CV22 is the LOWEST Window of Heaven point, positioned at the thoracic inlet.
This is the anatomical "gateway" where all head-bound
neurovascular structures transit between thorax and neck: both
vagus nerves, both common carotid arteries (deep), both internal
jugular veins, the trachea, and the esophagus all pass through
the thoracic inlet at CV22's level.

The Window of Heaven cervical ring (C1-C4) provides multi-angle access to
the cervical neurovascular bundle from the NECK level. CV22
provides access to the MEDIASTINAL portion of these same
structures — the vascular and neural "roots" before they ascend
into the cervical ring. This explains CV22's unique Window of Heaven role:
it treats the source (thoracic trunk → cervical → head) while
cervical Window of Heaven points treat the transit zone.

→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3, A02 S3.5, C04 S3.2.1

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): the MOST TECHNIQUE-CRITICAL standard point alongside
GV15/GV16. Two-step insertion:
Step 1: Insert perpendicular to skin 0.2 cun (5 mm) — penetrate
  skin and superficial fascia. Pause at this depth to assess.
Step 2: Redirect needle tip posteroinferiorly (toward the
  posterior surface of the manubrium) and advance 0.5-1.0 cun
  (15-25 mm) along the retrosternal trajectory.
NEVER insert straight down beyond 5 mm (tracheal cartilage at
15 mm perpendicular).

De Qi: local distension at the suprasternal notch, sometimes a
descending sensation into the chest (retrosternal awareness).
Coughing or throat clearing is common — indicates tracheal
stimulation. If coughing persists, reduce depth.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

**Deep acupuncture variant (dysphagia-specific):** 80 mm needle
along posterior manubrium surface, reaching the deep retrosternal
space. This is a SPECIALIST technique requiring advanced training
and ideally imaging (ultrasound/CT) validation. Not standard
clinical practice. Used specifically in post-stroke dysphagia
research protocols.

EA: rarely applied directly at CV22 due to proximity to vital
structures. When used, low frequency (2 Hz), minimal intensity.
One electrode at CV22, the other at a less dangerous distal
point (LU7 or LI4). Never pair bilateral electrodes across the
midline at throat level (risk of vagal stimulation → bradycardia).

Bấm huyệt (B02): gentle finger pressure into the suprasternal
notch. Safe and accessible for self-treatment of throat
discomfort and dyspnea. Press gently with fingertip, directing
pressure posteriorly.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S2

Cứu (B08): indirect moxa (moxa stick held at distance) for
chronic cough, chronic pharyngitis, and throat conditions with
cold pattern. Direct moxa CONTRAINDICATED (thin skin, proximity
to trachea).

#### An toàn

**🔴 DANGEROUS POINT — THE MOST DANGEROUS CV POINT**

CV22 is one of the most dangerous points in the standard
361-point system, comparable to GV15 and GV16 on the posterior
midline. The suprasternal notch provides direct access to the
superior mediastinum and its vital structures.

**Structures at risk (superficial → deep):**
1. **Anterior jugular veins + jugular venous arch** — surface
   vasculature crossing the suprasternal fossa. Moderate
   bleeding risk.
2. **Inferior thyroid veins** — variable midline course. Can
   cross the suprasternal region unpredictably.
3. **Trachea** — directly deep to CV22 at ~15 mm perpendicular
   depth. Tracheal puncture → subcutaneous emphysema,
   pneumomediastinum.
4. **Brachiocephalic trunk (innominate artery)** — behind the
   manubrium, RIGHT of midline. Inadvertent arterial puncture →
   massive hemorrhage.
5. **Left brachiocephalic vein** — behind the manubrium, crossing
   from left to right.
6. **Aortic arch** — deeper in the superior mediastinum.
7. **Pleural domes** — bilateral lung apices project above the
   clavicles. Lateral needle deviation could reach the pleural
   dome → pneumothorax.

**Safety protocols:**
- The TWO-STEP technique (perpendicular 5 mm, then redirect
  posteroinferiorly) keeps the needle on the posterior manubrial
  surface — AWAY from the trachea and mediastinal vessels.
- Zhang 2007 cadaver study: documented safe angles and depths.
  Posteroinferior trajectory along the manubrium posterior surface
  is safe up to 25 mm.
- EXPERIENCED PRACTITIONERS ONLY. Contraindicated for students
  or trainees without direct supervision.
- If patient coughs persistently → withdraw immediately
  (tracheal cartilage contact indicates anterior deviation).
- Avoid in patients with: goiter (displaced anatomy), thoracic
  aortic aneurysm, superior mediastinal masses, tracheostomy.

### CV23 — Liêm Tuyền (廉泉, Liánquán)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry) — Meeting CV + Yin Wei Mai**

Name 廉泉 (Liánquán) = "Ridge Spring." 廉 (ridge/edge/angle) +
泉 (spring/fountain). The "ridge" is the hyoid bone; the "spring"
is the flow of saliva and speech from the sublingual region above
the hyoid. The hyoid as a bony ridge from which the tongue's
"spring" — speech, saliva, swallowing — originates.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anterior midline, in
the depression superior to the upper border of the hyoid bone.

Anatomical landmarks: tilt the head slightly back. The hyoid bone
is palpable at C3 vertebral level as a mobile U-shaped bone in
the anterior neck. CV23 is in the midline depression ABOVE the
hyoid body, between the two anterior bellies of the digastric
muscles. Below the chin, above the throat.

Spatial relationships:
- CV22 (Tiantu) is approximately 3-4 cun below — at the
  suprasternal notch
- CV24 (Chengjiang) is at the mentolabial groove — approximately
  2 cun above
- ST9 (Renying) is lateral and inferior, at the thyroid
  cartilage level
- The hyoid bone divides the throat (infrahyoid = below) from
  the submental region (suprahyoid = above)

Needle depth: 0.5-1.0 cun (15-25 mm), directed posterosuperiorly
toward the tongue root. The needle passes through the suprahyoid
soft tissues toward the floor of the mouth.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII):** motor to ALL extrinsic and
  intrinsic tongue muscles (except palatoglossus). CN XII exits
  the hypoglossal canal at the skull base, descends between the
  internal carotid and internal jugular vein, loops anteriorly
  below the posterior belly of the digastric, and runs on the
  surface of the hyoglossus to enter the tongue. At CV23 level
  (above hyoid), the needle directed toward the tongue root
  approaches CN XII's terminal distribution in the tongue
  musculature.
- **Mylohyoid nerve (V3):** branch of the inferior alveolar nerve
  (mandibular division). Motor to mylohyoid and anterior belly of
  digastric.
- **Transverse cervical nerve (C2-C3):** cutaneous branch of the
  cervical plexus providing sensation to the anterior neck
  including the suprahyoid region.
- **Lingual nerve (V3):** sensory to the anterior 2/3 of the
  tongue. Located lateral and superior to the hyoglossus. Deep to
  CV23 direction.
- Spinal segment: C2-C3 (cutaneous), CN XII + CN V3 (deep)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Fascial plane: skin → platysma → investing layer of deep cervical
fascia → mylohyoid muscle (the muscular "diaphragm" of the floor
of the mouth, separating the submental space from the oral
cavity).
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: submental artery (branch of facial artery) runs along
the inferior mandibular border toward the midline chin, passing
superficial to mylohyoid. Moderate bleeding risk.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → platysma → anterior bellies of digastric (flanking
  midline)
- Mylohyoid (floor of mouth)
- Geniohyoid (deep to mylohyoid)
- Deep: hyoglossus and genioglossus (tongue muscles)

Post-stroke oropharyngeal dysfunction: patients with post-stroke
dysphagia show measurably reduced hyoid excursion during
swallowing (assessed by videofluoroscopy). The hyoid bone at
CV23's anatomical landmark moves less during the pharyngeal
phase — representing a functional dynamic change at this point's
exact location. Patients with pseudobulbar palsy may show
hyperactive gag reflex (increased sensitivity at CV23), while
those with bulbar palsy may show reduced sensation (decreased
sensitivity) — directional changes depending on the neurological
lesion type.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Speech and tongue motor function:**
CV23 above the hyoid accesses the floor of the mouth where CN XII
distributes to the tongue muscles. Needling directed
posterosuperiorly toward the tongue root may:
1. Stimulate CN XII terminal branches → enhance tongue motor
   control → improve articulation
2. Activate lingual sensory afferents (lingual nerve, V3) →
   enhance proprioceptive feedback for speech
3. Modulate suprahyoid muscle tone (mylohyoid, geniohyoid) →
   improve hyoid elevation during swallowing

**The HT5+CV23 mechanism gap:** HT5 at C8-T1 (ulnar nerve/FCU
region) has no anatomically verified pathway to CN XII (medulla,
hypoglossal nucleus). The classical rationale — "Heart opens to
the tongue" (心开窍于舌) via the Heart Luo vessel ascending to
the tongue root — lacks a modern neuroanatomical correlate.
C8-T1 afferents project to spinal dorsal horn, not to the
medullary hypoglossal nucleus. The HT5→speech mechanism remains
the most significant unexplained traditional distal-local
pairing for this body region. 🔴
→ Cross-ref: AP-05 HT5, A01 S2.3

**Dysphagia mechanism:**
CV23 stimulation activates the suprahyoid muscle complex
(mylohyoid, geniohyoid, anterior digastric). These muscles
elevate the hyoid bone during the pharyngeal phase of
swallowing — a critical biomechanical event for epiglottic
closure over the airway. Reduced hyoid excursion is a hallmark
of post-stroke dysphagia. CV23 stimulation may enhance hyoid
elevation → improved airway protection during swallowing. 🟡

Gap: whether needle stimulation at CV23 actually increases hyoid
excursion (measurable by videofluoroscopy) has not been directly
tested. The biomechanical pathway is plausible but the
therapeutic efficacy of the point-specific mechanism is unknown.

→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A08 S2

#### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01): 0.5-1.0 cun (15-25 mm), directed posterosuperiorly
toward the tongue root. The needle passes through the submental
soft tissues, penetrating the mylohyoid toward the sublingual
space. De Qi: heavy sensation in the floor of the mouth,
sometimes referred to the tongue. If the patient gags → too deep
(approaching epiglottis/valleculae). Withdraw slightly.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

EA: commonly applied in post-stroke aphasia and dysphagia
protocols. Pair: CV23 to CV22 (throat pair), or CV23 to a
submental extra point. Low frequency (2 Hz) for motor
stimulation of suprahyoid muscles.

Bấm huyệt (B02): finger pressure in the submental midline, above
the hyoid bone. Used for throat discomfort, voice strain.
Accessible for self-treatment.

Cứu (B08): indirect moxa at CV23 for chronic throat conditions
with cold pattern (chronic pharyngitis, chronic hoarseness). Not
commonly used — throat skin is thin.

#### An toàn

Moderate risk — less dangerous than CV22 but not without hazards.
- DO NOT needle excessively deep: the epiglottis and valleculae
  lie deep to the sublingual space. Excessive depth risks
  pharyngeal wall penetration.
- Submental artery (branch of facial artery): moderate bleeding
  risk. Apply pressure post-withdrawal.
- Gagging: common if the needle reaches the posterior floor of
  the mouth. Indicates excessive depth — withdraw slightly.
- The hyoid bone is NOT directly needled — CV23 is in the
  depression ABOVE the hyoid body. Some texts recommend gently
  pressing the hyoid down during needle insertion to increase
  the suprahyoid tissue space.

### CV24 — Thừa Tương (承浆, Chéngjiāng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry) — Ghost Point #8, Meeting
CV + GV + ST + LI, CV ENDPOINT**

Name 承浆 (Chéngjiāng) = "Sauce Receptacle." 承 (to receive/
catch) + 浆 (juice/sauce/liquid). The mentolabial groove
"catches" drool, saliva, or food that falls from the lower lip —
a practical anatomical observation that identifies CV24 as the
point where gravity collects facial fluids.

#### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the face, on the anterior
midline, in the depression of the mentolabial groove (the crease
between the lower lip and chin).

Anatomical landmarks: the mentolabial groove (labiomental sulcus)
is the horizontal crease between the lower lip (orbicularis oris)
and the chin prominence (mentalis muscle). CV24 is at the midpoint
of this groove, on the midline.

Spatial relationships:
- CV23 (Lianquan) is approximately 2 cun below — above the
  hyoid bone
- GV28 (Yinjiao) is the GV endpoint at the upper labial frenulum
  (inside the mouth) — the CV24↔GV28 "gap" is bridged by the
  tongue (搭鹊桥)
- GV26 (Renzhong) is at the philtrum — the nearest GV surface
  point
- ST4 (Dicang) is at the oral commissure, approximately 0.5 cun
  lateral and slightly superior
- LI20 terminus is at the nasolabial fold above

Needle depth: 0.2-0.3 cun (5-8 mm), perpendicular or oblique
superiorly toward the lower lip. Shallow — minimal tissue over
the mandible.

#### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Mental nerve (V3):** terminal branch of the inferior alveolar
  nerve (from the mandibular division of CN V). Exits through the
  mental foramen on the mandible (below the 2nd premolar, ~1 cm
  lateral to midline) and supplies sensory innervation to the chin,
  lower lip, and labial gingiva. At CV24 midline, bilateral mental
  nerve terminal branches converge.
- **Marginal mandibular branch of facial nerve (CN VII):** motor
  to depressor labii inferioris, depressor anguli oris, and
  mentalis. Controls lower lip depression and chin movements.
- **Inferior labial nerve (from V3):** sensory to the lower lip
  mucosa and skin.
- Spinal segment: CN V3 (sensory), CN VII (motor)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Fascial plane: skin → subcutaneous tissue → orbicularis oris
(upper border of groove) / mentalis (lower border). The
mentolabial groove sits at the JUNCTION between these two muscles.

Vascular: inferior labial artery (branch of facial artery)
supplies the lower lip, crosses or approaches the midline.
Moderate bleeding risk in this vascular facial region.

**Meeting point (交会穴) — CV + GV + ST + LI (4 channels):**
CV24 is the meeting point of FOUR channels:
1. **Conception Vessel (CV)** — CV24 is the ENDPOINT. The channel
   ascends from CV1 (perineum) through the entire anterior midline
   to terminate here at the chin.
2. **Governing Vessel (GV)** — GV's internal pathway meets CV at
   the chin region. The external GV terminates at GV28 (upper
   gum), but its internal branch descends to connect at the chin.
3. **Stomach (ST)** — the ST channel descends the face through
   ST4 (oral commissure) to the mandible. Its internal pathway
   connects to the chin area.
4. **Large Intestine (LI)** — the LI channel ascends to LI20
   (nasolabial fold), crosses the midline, and connects to the
   contralateral ST1 via the lower jaw area.

This 4-channel meeting creates a convergence of BOTH
extraordinary midline vessels (CV+GV) and BOTH Yangming channels
(ST+LI). CV24 is the ONLY CV point where the Governing Vessel
meets — making it the single location where both midline vessels
converge on the CV channel.

**CV ENDPOINT and Small Heavenly Circuit (小周天):**
CV24 is the terminal point of the Conception Vessel. The Small
Heavenly Circuit from the CV direction:
- CV1 (perineum) → ascending anterior → CV24 (chin) = CV endpoint
- The internal pathway continues upward through the oral cavity
  to meet GV at GV28 (upper labial frenulum)
- GV1 (coccyx) → ascending posterior → GV28 (upper gum) =
  GV endpoint
- The "gap" between CV24 (external, chin) and GV28 (internal,
  upper gum) is bridged by the tongue touching the palate — the
  "magpie bridge" (搭鹊桥, dā quèqiáo) in Qigong practice.

#### Dynamic acupoint properties

Facial paralysis: in Bell's palsy, the affected side shows
weakened mentalis contraction → inability to wrinkle the chin
skin, loss of the chin dimple. The mentolabial groove depth
changes with mentalis tone — deeper in hypertonic recovery phase,
flattened in flaccid phase. These motor nerve changes are
clinically observable but not formally studied as dynamic acupoint
sensitization in the Chen/Kim sense.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

#### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Facial paralysis (perioral motor function):**
CV24 at the mentolabial groove overlies the junction of
orbicularis oris and mentalis — both innervated by the CN VII
marginal mandibular branch. Needling stimulates this motor
convergence zone. The midline position allows bilateral CN VII
access from a single point (both left and right marginal
mandibular branches send terminal fibers to the chin midline).
Mechanism: local CN VII motor branch stimulation → maintaining
muscle tone during denervation → facilitating reinnervation
during recovery. 🟡

**Ghost Point mechanism:**
The psychiatric use of CV24 (Ghost Market) has no established
modern mechanism. The V3 trigeminal territory at the chin
connects to the spinal trigeminal nucleus → reticular formation,
but no demonstrated pathway to emotional or behavioral
regulation exists. The Ghost Point system may encode empirical
observations about the mouth/chin region's involvement in
psychiatric presentations — abnormal oral behaviors such as teeth
grinding, jaw clenching, drooling, mutism, and incoherent speech
are common in psychotic and manic states. The chin point may have
been selected as a treatment site because it was an observable
site of psychiatric symptom manifestation. 🔴

**Small Heavenly Circuit neuroanatomy:**
The CV24→GV28 tongue bridge has a cranial nerve substrate: tongue
tip touching the palate connects the lingual nerve (V3, anterior
tongue) to palatine nerves (V2, hard palate). This creates a
trigeminal-mediated bridge between V3 territory (ending at
CV24/chin) and V2 territory (at GV28/upper gum). Whether this
trigeminal sensory continuity has physiological significance
beyond simple somatosensory awareness is unknown. The Qigong
claim of "energy circulation" through this bridge remains
unvalidated. 🔴
→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV28, A01 S1
