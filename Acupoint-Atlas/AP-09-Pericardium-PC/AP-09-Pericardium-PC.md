# AP-09 — Pericardium Meridian (PC) — Hand Jueyin (手厥阴心包经)

> **Status:** COMPLETE — all 9 standard points. PC6 (Phase 1 Tier A),
> PC3, PC4, PC7, PC8 (Phase 2 Tier B), PC1, PC2, PC5, PC9 (Phase 2
> Tier C). First complete meridian in the Atlas.
>
> **Meridian overview:** The Pericardium channel (Hand Jueyin) runs from
> the lateral chest (PC1) → axilla → medial upper arm → forearm between
> the two tendons (palmaris longus and flexor carpi radialis) → palm →
> tip of the middle finger (PC9). 9 points total. Paired with Triple
> Burner (Hand Shaoyang). In TCM, the Pericardium is the "outer defense
> of the Heart" (心之外卫), protecting the Heart organ from pathogenic
> invasion. Peak time in Chinese clock: 19:00-21:00. Five Element:
> Ministerial Fire (相火), distinguished from Heart's Sovereign Fire.
>
> **Total points:** 9 standard | Entries: PC1-PC9 (all)

## PC6 — Nội Quan (内关, Nèi Guān)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**
The single strongest per-indication evidence in acupuncture: Cochrane
review for PONV with 59 RCTs and 7,667 participants. PC6 ≈ antiemetic
drugs (NNT≈4-5). Mechanism identified: median nerve → brainstem
NTS/DMV → vagal modulation. Luo-Connecting point + Eight Confluent
point (Chong Mai + Yin Wei Mai).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): 2 cun above the transverse crease
of the wrist (PC7 Daling), between the tendons of palmaris longus and
flexor carpi radialis.

Anatomical landmarks: on the anterior (palmar) surface of the forearm,
approximately 3 cm proximal to the wrist crease. Between the two most
prominent tendons visible when the fist is clenched with wrist flexed.

Needle depth range: 13-25 mm perpendicular insertion. Standard clinical
depth: 15-20 mm (0.5-1 cun).

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply — three-layer neural architecture:
- Superficial: medial and lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerves
  (sensory, C8-T1)
- Middle: palmar cutaneous branch of the median nerve (C6-T1),
  between flexor digitorum superficialis and flexor digitorum profundus.
  The MAIN TRUNK of the median nerve lies radially at this depth.
- Deep: anterior interosseous nerve (motor branch of median nerve)
- Spinal segment: C8-T1 (primary median nerve contribution)

Fascial plane: between flexor digitorum superficialis (FDS) and flexor
digitorum profundus (FDP). Three connective tissue layers identified
(CNKI 2010 morphological study): surface layer (skin → FDS, containing
cutaneous nerves), middle layer (between FDS and FDP, containing median
nerve trunk and palmar branch), deep layer (FDP → interosseous membrane).
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: median antebrachial vein (superficial). Anterior interosseous
artery and vein at deep level.
→ Cross-ref: A04

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Palmaris longus tendon (medial) / flexor carpi radialis tendon (lateral)
  — PC6 lies in the cleft BETWEEN these tendons
- Flexor digitorum superficialis muscle
- Flexor digitorum profundus muscle
- Interosseous membrane (deepest safe layer)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. PC6 falls between PC7 Daling (Shu-Stream/Earth) and
PC5 Jianshi (Jing-River/Metal) but is not itself a Five Shu point.
The Five Shu points of Pericardium are: PC9 (Jing-Well/Wood),
PC8 (Ying-Spring/Fire), PC7 (Shu-Stream/Earth, also Yuan-Source),
PC5 (Jing-River/Metal), PC3 (He-Sea/Water).

Yuan-Source: No (PC7 Daling is Yuan-Source of Pericardium)

**Luo-Connecting: YES — Luo-Connecting point of the Pericardium (心包络穴)**
Connects interiorly-exteriorly paired channels: Pericardium (Hand Jueyin)
↔ Triple Burner (Hand Shaoyang). Luo-Connecting points are where a
branch separates from the main channel to connect with its paired channel.
Traditional use: treats disorders of BOTH the Pericardium and Triple
Burner channels. The Luo vessel from PC6 ascends along the arm to
the chest, connecting with the Heart-Pericardium system.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Xi-Cleft: No (PC4 Ximen is Xi-Cleft of Pericardium)

Back-Shu: No (BL14 Jueyinshu at T4 is Back-Shu of Pericardium)

Front-Mu: No (CV17 Shanzhong at midline sternum is Front-Mu of
Pericardium)

**Eight Confluent: YES — Confluent point of Chong Mai + Yin Wei Mai
(冲脉 + 阴维脉)**
Paired with SP4 (Gongsun) — together they open/regulate the Chong Mai
(Penetrating Vessel) and Yin Wei Mai (Yin Linking Vessel).
Chong Mai = "Sea of Blood" (血海), associated with the chest, stomach,
and heart — aligns with PC6's cardiac and anti-emetic clinical actions.
Yin Wei Mai = links all Yin channels, associated with chest/cardiac
pain — aligns with PC6's use for chest oppression and cardiac disorders.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Hui-Meeting: No

Special designations: None in the standard designation systems (not a
Command Point, Ma Dan-yang Star, Sun Si-miao Ghost Point, or Lower
He-Sea Point). However, PC6 is arguably the most clinically validated
single point in modern evidence-based acupuncture.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** PC6 Neiguan — Luo-Connecting point of Pericardium,
Eight Confluent point (Chong Mai + Yin Wei Mai). Name "Neiguan" (Inner
Gate/Pass) refers to the point's traditional role as a gateway to the
interior — regulating the Heart, chest, and Stomach from the forearm.
One of the most commonly prescribed points across all TCM specialties.

**2. Trigger point:** Flexor digitorum superficialis trigger point.
The forearm flexor muscles (FDS, FDP, pronator teres) have well-
documented trigger points in the proximal-to-mid forearm. Travell &
Simons: FDS trigger points refer pain to the middle finger and palmar
forearm. The PC6 location overlaps with the FDS muscle belly where
trigger points are commonly found. Dorsher mapping: correspondence
confirmed for forearm flexor trigger points.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.3

**3. Ashi correspondence:** The PC6 region is a common tender point in
patients with carpal tunnel syndrome, repetitive strain injury, and
forearm overuse. Palpation frequently reveals tenderness between the
two tendons at this level. Also tender in patients with anxiety-related
forearm tension.

**4. Marma point:** Manibandha Marma (wrist Marma, 107-point Ayurvedic
system, Frawley). Approximate correspondence — Manibandha covers the
wrist joint area. PC6 at 2 cun (≈3 cm) above the wrist crease is at
the proximal boundary of this Marma. Possible correspondence within
2-3 cm. Both traditions identify this wrist-forearm region as
therapeutically significant for cardiac and emotional conditions.

**5. Head zone:** C8-T1 dermatome (median nerve territory on forearm).
**PARTIAL CONVERGENCE for cardiac effects:** Heart viscerotome = T1-T5.
The T1 component of the median nerve overlaps with the upper boundary
of cardiac autonomic innervation. This provides a partial segmental
basis for PC6's cardiac effects.
**DIVERGENCE for anti-emetic effects:** Stomach viscerotome = T6-T9.
NO segmental convergence between PC6's spinal level (C8-T1) and the
stomach. PC6's PONV effect is non-segmental — it operates through
NTS brainstem convergence, not dermatomal overlap.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2, S8

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Arm Line (Myers 2001). The forearm
flexors (FDS, FDP) are key stations on this myofascial line, which runs
from the anterior chest → medial upper arm → forearm flexors → hand.
The Deep Front Arm Line partially overlaps with the Pericardium meridian
pathway.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** Weak correspondence. For cardiac
pathology, neurogenic spots would appear at T1-T5 dermatome
(chest/medial arm) — NOT at C8-T1 forearm. For GI pathology,
neurogenic spots at T6-T9 abdomen — also NOT at PC6. The neurogenic
inflammation model does not predict PC6 as a reactive point for either
cardiac or GI disease. This confirms PC6's therapeutic effects operate
through supraspinal convergence, not segmental neurogenic inflammation.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3, C04 S3.2.3

**8. Cupping point:** Not a standard cupping point. Forearm location is
not typical for cupping application (primarily back/shoulder in both
TCM and Sunnah traditions).

**Convergence score: 5/8**
(TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Marma approximate, Anatomy Trains)

The Head zone divergence for GI effects and weak neurogenic spot
correspondence are NOT weaknesses — they are critically important
datapoints confirming that PC6's most validated indication (PONV) is
non-segmental. The partial Head zone convergence for cardiac effects
(via T1) suggests a dual mechanism: partial segmental basis for cardiac
indications + non-segmental brainstem convergence for anti-emetic effects.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV)**
Confidence: 🟢 Confirmed

Cochrane review (Lee, Chan & Fan 2015, CD003281.pub4): 59 RCTs,
7,667 participants — THE largest dedicated single-acupoint Cochrane
review in the field.

PC6 vs sham:
- Nausea: RR 0.68 (95% CI 0.60-0.77) — 32% reduction
  (40 trials, 4,742 participants)
- Vomiting: RR 0.60 (95% CI 0.51-0.71) — 40% reduction
  (45 trials, 5,147 participants)
- Rescue antiemetics: RR 0.64 (95% CI 0.55-0.73)
- NNT at 60% baseline risk: nausea 5, vomiting 4

PC6 vs antiemetic drugs:
- Nausea: RR 0.91 (95% CI 0.75-1.10) — NO significant difference
- Vomiting: RR 0.93 (95% CI 0.74-1.17) — NO significant difference
- Authors' conclusion: further comparative trials "futile"

PC6 + antiemetic vs antiemetic alone:
- Vomiting: RR 0.56 (95% CI 0.35-0.91) — ADDITIVE benefit
- Rescue antiemetics: RR 0.61 (95% CI 0.44-0.86)

Cochrane update (Lee et al. 2025, CD003281.pub5): network meta-analysis
comparing DIFFERENT PC6 stimulation techniques (acupuncture, EA, TENS,
acupressure, laser, capsicum plaster, acu-stimulation device).
Expanded analysis confirming effect across multiple delivery methods.

Wearable P6 stimulation SR+MA (2026): pooled RR 0.57 (95% CI 0.49-0.67)
— 43% PONV reduction. NNT≈4.

Critical observation: the Cochrane review includes ALL PC6 stimulation
methods, not just needling. Acupuncture, EA, acupressure wristbands,
TENS — all effective. This means the therapeutic effect is primarily
about ACTIVATING MEDIAN NERVE AFFERENTS AT THIS LOCATION, not about
the specific technique used to activate them.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.2

Sham comparison: PC6 > sham at same point for PONV. Effect is
location-dependent — stimulating the correct nerve territory matters.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S3.4

**2. Pregnancy nausea and vomiting (NVP)**
Confidence: 🟢 Confirmed

2023 meta-analysis: 33 RCTs, 3,390 pregnant patients. P6 acupressure
produces meaningful reductions in nausea and vomiting vs placebo.
Acupressure wristbands (Sea-Bands) are the most studied delivery
method for NVP.

Safety in pregnancy: PC6 is NOT among contraindicated pregnancy points
(unlike LI4, SP6, BL67). The PONV and NVP evidence makes PC6 one of
the few acupoints with positive evidence specifically IN pregnancy.

Mechanism likely identical to PONV — median nerve → NTS → anti-emetic
brainstem pathway. The stimulus (acupressure) is gentler but targets
the same nerve territory.

**3. Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV)**
Confidence: 🟡 Plausible

Multiple RCTs positive for PC6/P6 stimulation as adjunct to standard
antiemetic regimens. Evidence less extensive than PONV. Journal of
Palliative and Supportive Care: P6 acupressure effective for CINV
in breast cancer patients — reduced nausea, vomiting, AND anxiety.

Mechanism: likely same NTS-mediated anti-emetic pathway, but CINV
involves additional central mechanisms (chemoreceptor trigger zone,
area postrema) that may respond differently to PC6 modulation.

Sham comparison: some positive, some equivocal. Multi-point protocols
complicate isolation of PC6-specific contribution.

**4. Cardiac effects (arrhythmia, HRV, coronary heart disease)**
Confidence: 🟡 Plausible

Yue et al. 2025 (scoping review): PC6 for coronary heart disease —
most commonly studied cardiac acupoint.

SR (Frontiers in Physiology 2017): comparison of acupuncture vs
antiarrhythmic drugs. PC6 is the most frequently selected acupoint
for arrhythmia treatment in the included studies.

HRV evidence: mixed. Some studies show EA at PC6-PC7 balances
sympathovagal ratio (↑ parasympathetic, ↓ sympathetic). One RCT
found laser acupuncture at PC6 did NOT affect HRV in healthy young
men — suggesting the effect may require needling depth or be
disease-state-dependent.

Animal evidence: EA at PC6 improved mean arterial pressure, heart rate,
stroke volume, cardiac output in dogs during open-chest surgery.
Opioid-mediated modulation in NTS confirmed (Nature Scientific
Reports 2018).
→ Cross-ref: A02 S4.2

**5. Gastric motility (vagovagal mechanism)**
Confidence: 🟡 Plausible

Lu et al. 2019 (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine):
EA at PC6 promotes gastric motility via brainstem vagovagal neurocircuits.

Mechanism identified with precision:
- EA at PC6 → median nerve afferents → spinal cord → NTS
- NTS → inhibits GABA release in DMV
- Reduced GABAergic inhibition → disinhibits vagal motor neurons
- Activated vagal efferents → gastric smooth muscle → motility increase
- Gastric motility increase within 30 seconds (p<0.05)

Vagotomy experiment: after cutting the vagus nerve, EA at PC6 had
NO effect on gastric motility. = Vagus nerve is ESSENTIAL.
Sympathectomy: cutting splanchnic nerve did NOT diminish the effect.
= Sympathetic pathway NOT involved (contrast with ST25).

Neurotransmitter specificity: GABA microinjection into DMV suppressed
gastric contractions; EA at PC6 reversed this suppression. Glutamate
microinjection increased contractions; EA showed minimal additional
effect above glutamate.

**6. Anxiety and insomnia**
Confidence: 🟡 Plausible

Multiple small RCTs positive for PC6 in anxiety and insomnia protocols.
Traditional "Shen-calming" indication (calm the spirit, open the chest,
regulate emotions). P6 acupressure found to reduce anxiety in
perioperative and CINV contexts (dual benefit: anti-anxiety + anti-nausea).

Evidence base less extensive than PONV. Most anxiety/insomnia protocols
use PC6 as part of multi-point combinations (commonly with HT7, GV20,
Yintang), making PC6-specific contribution difficult to isolate.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization: limited data specifically for PC6.
Neurogenic inflammatory spots for cardiac pathology appear at T1-T5
dermatome (chest/medial arm), NOT at the C8-T1 forearm location of PC6
(Kim 2017 model). For GI pathology, neurogenic spots at T6-T9 abdomen.

PC6's therapeutic effects operate through SUPRASPINAL convergence at
NTS, not through segmental neurogenic inflammation. The dynamic
acupoint model (disease → local sensitization → treatment at sensitive
point) does NOT fully apply to PC6 for its most validated indications
(PONV, cardiac). Local forearm sensitization at PC6 may occur with
carpal tunnel or forearm overuse (musculoskeletal trigger point
activation), but this relates to its LOCAL function, not its
visceral organ modulation.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Track A systems activated at PC6:**

A01/A02 (Neural — PRIMARY):
- Median nerve (C8-T1): the primary therapeutic target. All PC6
  stimulation methods (needling, EA, acupressure, TENS) work by
  activating median nerve afferents at this location.
- Afferent pathway: median nerve → dorsal root ganglion (C8-T1) →
  spinal cord → spinomedullary tract → brainstem NTS
- NTS modulation: EA at PC6 inhibits GABAergic transmission to DMV
  (Lu 2019). Reduced GABA → disinhibits vagal motor neurons → increased
  vagal efferent output
- Anti-emetic pathway: median nerve afferents converge at NTS with
  vagal visceral afferents from stomach. NTS modulation → influences
  the vomiting reflex center (NTS/area postrema/CTZ complex)
- Cardiac pathway: median nerve C8-T1 afferents → NTS. NTS also
  receives cardiac vagal afferents. Convergence at NTS allows somatic
  input to modulate cardiac vagal output. Opioid-mediated mechanism
  in NTS confirmed (Scientific Reports 2018).

A03 (Fascia):
- Three-layer fascial architecture at PC6 provides
  mechanotransduction substrate
- Needle manipulation at intermuscular cleft (between FDS and FDP)
  → collagen winding → mechanical signal → fibroblast activation
  (Langevin mechanism)
- The fascial plane location maximizes tissue engagement with needle
  rotation (needle grasp phenomenon)

A06 (Chemical):
- EA at PC6 activates endogenous opioid system in NTS
  (opioid-mediated modulation of brainstem vagal circuits)
- Han frequency-opioid mapping applies: 2 Hz → enkephalin, 100 Hz →
  dynorphin (general EA mechanism, not PC6-specific)

A07 (Segmental — PARTIAL for cardiac, NOT for GI):
- C8-T1 segment. T1 overlaps with upper boundary of cardiac
  autonomic innervation (T1-T5) → partial segmental basis for
  cardiac effects
- For GI effects (stomach T6-T9): NO segmental convergence. Anti-emetic
  effect is entirely non-segmental, operating through NTS brainstem relay

**WHY this location produces anti-emetic effects (the central question):**

PC6 is at C8-T1 dermatome. Stomach is at T6-T9 viscerotome.
No segmental convergence. How does forearm stimulation suppress nausea?

Resolution:
1. Median nerve afferents from PC6 (C8-T1) → dorsal horn → ascending
   spinomedullary pathway → brainstem NTS
2. NTS is the PRIMARY relay nucleus for visceral afferents from the
   stomach (via vagus nerve)
3. Somatic afferents (from median nerve) and visceral afferents (from
   vagus) CONVERGE at NTS
4. EA at PC6 modulates GABAergic transmission within NTS/DMV circuit:
   inhibits GABA → disinhibits vagal motor neurons
5. Modified vagal output → modulates gastric motility and the vomiting
   reflex

This is the SECOND strongest non-segmental mechanism identified in
the Atlas (after ST36's PROKR2-vagal pathway). Both converge at the
same brainstem relay (NTS/DMV), but through different afferent
pathways and different molecular mechanisms:
- ST36: PROKR2+ neurons → vagal-adrenal → anti-inflammatory
- PC6: median nerve → NTS GABAergic modulation → anti-emetic

**Gap:** The precise neurochemical mechanism by which somatic
afferents from the median nerve reach NTS (rather than their expected
target in cuneate nucleus) requires further anatomical tracing.
Convergence at NTS is functionally demonstrated (vagotomy experiments,
GABA microinjection) but the ascending pathway from C8-T1 spinal
segments to NTS is not fully mapped anatomically. Also, PC6's
traditional indications extend beyond GI and cardiac (mental disorders,
epilepsy, malaria) — mechanisms for these are unknown.

⚡ Framework note: PC6 NTS-GABAergic mechanism provides the SECOND
identified non-segmental acupoint pathway (after ST36 PROKR2-vagal).
Both terminate at NTS/DMV brainstem relay but use different afferent
molecules and pathways. A09 S4.3's "30-40% non-segmental gap" now
has TWO point-specific resolutions. The emerging pattern: non-segmental
acupoint effects converge at NTS through diverse afferent routes —
suggesting NTS is a common integration hub for somatic-visceral
convergence, not just a vagal relay.

⚡ Framework note: PC6 PONV evidence (NNT≈4, PC6 ≈ antiemetic drugs,
59 RCTs) represents the strongest per-indication evidence for ANY
single acupoint in the entire Atlas. This exceeds the evidence strength
of ST36 for any single indication. The Cochrane conclusion that further
PC6 vs antiemetic comparative trials are "futile" (no expected
difference) is unprecedented in acupuncture research. This challenges
the general "modest effect size" characterization in A09 S3.4 — for
PONV, the effect is clinically equivalent to pharmaceuticals.

⚡ Framework note: ALL PC6 stimulation methods are effective for PONV
(needling, EA, acupressure, TENS, laser, capsicum). This is uniquely
strong evidence that the mechanism is NEURAL ACTIVATION AT A SPECIFIC
LOCATION, not technique-specific. It validates C04's central thesis
that location determines therapeutic access — different techniques
are different ways to activate the same median nerve afferents at
the same anatomical site. This has implications for the entire Atlas:
technique comparison may matter less than precise anatomical targeting.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

**Châm (B01):**
Needle depth: 13-25 mm perpendicular. Standard: 15-20 mm to reach
the fascial plane between FDS and FDP. Deeper needling approaches
the median nerve trunk — eliciting electric sensation radiating to
fingers (not De Qi but nerve contact — should be avoided).
De Qi characteristics: typically distension (zhang) and heaviness
(zhong) at the forearm, sometimes radiating proximally toward the
elbow or distally to the palm. Needle grasp commonly felt.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S2.2, S2.3

**EA (B01):**
PC6-PC7 is a common electrode pair (Luo-Connecting + Yuan-Source on
same channel). Han frequency mapping applies (2 Hz → enkephalin,
100 Hz → dynorphin). For PONV: both low and mixed frequencies studied.
The Cochrane review includes EA studies — effective for PONV at
this location.
Lu 2019: EA at PC6 → GABA inhibition in DMV → gastric motility within
30 seconds. Vagotomy abolishes effect.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S2.4

**Bấm huyệt (B02):**
PC6 is THE most extensively validated acupressure point in all of
evidence-based medicine. Acupressure wristbands (Sea-Bands) apply
sustained pressure at PC6. Cochrane review includes acupressure
studies — effective for PONV. NVP 2023 meta-analysis: 33 RCTs
specifically studying acupressure at P6 for pregnancy nausea.

PROKR2 depth question: LESS relevant for PC6 than for ST36. PC6's
mechanism is median nerve activation, not PROKR2. The median nerve
trunk is at ~15-20 mm depth, but its palmar cutaneous branch and
antebrachial cutaneous nerves are superficial — surface pressure
likely sufficient to activate nerve afferents. This explains why
acupressure wristbands work at PC6 (unlike the PROKR2 depth problem
at ST36).
→ Cross-ref: B02, C04 S3.2.1

**Cứu (B08):**
Moxa at PC6 used traditionally for cardiac conditions and chest
oppression (cold-pattern presentations). Less studied than
needling/acupressure for PONV. Warm needle technique (needle with
moxa cone on handle) occasionally used.

**Dry needling (B11):**
Forearm flexor trigger point treatment. FDS trigger points at the
PC6 region — dry needling targets motor endplate zone in FDS.
Local twitch response may be obtained. This addresses the LOCAL
musculoskeletal function (forearm pain, carpal tunnel), NOT the
anti-emetic function.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.3

**So sánh:**
For PONV, ALL stimulation methods appear similarly effective (Cochrane
2025 network MA comparing techniques). This is exceptional — most
acupoints do not have cross-technique comparison data. The consistency
across methods supports the conclusion that the therapeutic target is
the MEDIAN NERVE AFFERENTS, not any specific tissue response to a
specific technique. Acupressure has the practical advantage of being
self-applicable (wristbands).

### An toàn

Pneumothorax risk: None — forearm location, no thoracic cavity proximity.

Vascular risk: Median antebrachial vein at superficial level — standard
precaution for superficial veins. Anterior interosseous vessels at deep
level — not typically reached at standard needling depth.

**Median nerve injury risk:** Primary safety concern unique to PC6.
The median nerve trunk lies radially in the middle fascial layer. Deep
or off-angle needling can contact the nerve trunk, producing electric
shooting sensation to the fingers. If this occurs: withdraw slightly
and redirect. Sustained direct needle contact with the nerve trunk can
cause temporary paresthesia. Permanent nerve injury is extremely rare
with standard technique.

Pregnancy: **NOT contraindicated.** PC6 is one of the few acupoints
with POSITIVE evidence specifically during pregnancy (NVP treatment).
AAFP, NICE, and multiple clinical guidelines recommend P6 acupressure
for pregnancy nausea. Contrast with LI4, SP6, and BL67 which are
traditionally contraindicated during pregnancy.

Special restriction: None. Cochrane review adverse events (14 trials):
minor skin irritation, blistering, redness, and pain (primarily from
wristbands). No serious or long-term complications documented across
7,667+ participants.

## PC7 — Đại Lăng (大陵, Dà Líng)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
Shu-Stream/Earth + Yuan-Source + Sun Si-miao Ghost Point #4 (鬼心,
Ghost Heart). Triple designation makes PC7 one of the most categorically
significant points on the Pericardium channel. Located directly over
the median nerve at the carpal tunnel entrance — key point for carpal
tunnel syndrome treatment alongside PC6.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): at the midpoint of the transverse
wrist crease, between the tendons of palmaris longus and flexor carpi
radialis.

Anatomical landmarks: with wrist slightly flexed and fist loosely
clenched, the two tendons (palmaris longus medially, flexor carpi
radialis laterally) become prominent. PC7 is in the depression between
them at the wrist crease level. Approximately 3 cm distal to PC6.

Needle depth range: 5-8 mm perpendicular insertion. Shallow — the
median nerve lies directly beneath at this level.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Carpal tunnel syndrome**
Confidence: 🟡 Plausible

Chen et al. 2021 systematic review + meta-analysis: 22 randomized
controlled trials, 1,958 patients. Acupuncture significantly reduced
pain intensity and improved symptom severity in carpal tunnel syndrome
vs sham/no treatment. PC7 was the most commonly employed point across
included studies.

Treatment protocols: PC7 + PC6 is the standard acupoint combination.
Additional points: TE5 (Waiguan), TE6 (Zhigou), LI4 (Hegu).

Nerve conduction studies: acupuncture at PC6-PC7 showed lasting
improvements in median nerve motor and sensory conduction velocities
and reduced distal motor latency at 1-year follow-up.

Mechanism: LOCAL — PC7 sits directly over the median nerve at the
carpal tunnel entrance. Needling may reduce local inflammation, promote
nerve gliding, or modulate nociceptive processing. This is distinct from
PC6's supraspinal NTS mechanism for visceral effects. Carpal tunnel
treatment at PC7 is fundamentally a LOCAL neural intervention.

**2. Insomnia**
Confidence: 🟡 Plausible

Used in multi-point insomnia protocols, typically combined with HT7,
PC6, GV20, Yintang. PC7-specific evidence limited — most insomnia
randomized controlled trials use multi-point prescriptions without
isolating individual point contributions.

Traditional distinction: PC7 for excess-type insomnia (Heart Fire,
agitation, racing thoughts) vs HT7 for deficiency-type insomnia (Heart
Blood/Yin deficiency, anxiety).

**3. Mental disorders (mania, epilepsy)**
Confidence: 🔴 Unvalidated (individual point)

Strong traditional designation (Ghost Point #4) but no modern randomized
controlled trials testing PC7 alone for mental health conditions. Ghost
Point protocols use multiple points simultaneously.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Carpal tunnel syndrome: PC7 shows local sensitization — increased
tenderness, lowered mechanical threshold in carpal tunnel syndrome
patients. Aligns with
the dynamic acupoint model (C04 §4).

CARDIAC disease-state sensitization: Kim 2017 demonstrated that
hypertensive rats develop neurogenic inflammatory spots at C8-T2
dermatome, with 67% matching acupoint locations including PC7. This
provides objective evidence that PC7 becomes physiologically reactive
during cardiac pathology — the body CREATES a reactive point at PC7
when the heart is diseased.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3, C04 S4

For GI indications (stomach T6-T9): no disease-state sensitization
expected at PC7 (non-segmental pathway).

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Track A systems at PC7:**

A01/A02 (Neural):
- Median nerve at carpal tunnel entrance — same nerve as PC6 but
  MORE DISTAL and MORE SUPERFICIAL access point
- For carpal tunnel: LOCAL mechanism — direct median nerve territory
  stimulation at the compression site. Anti-inflammatory, nerve gliding,
  nociceptive modulation
- For visceral effects (cardiac, GI): same supraspinal pathway as
  PC6 — median nerve → spinal cord C6-T1 → NTS. PC7 accesses the
  median nerve trunk MORE directly than PC6 (nerve is more superficial
  at wrist crease than at 2 cun proximal)

A03 (Fascia):
- Between palmaris longus and flexor carpi radialis tendons at wrist —
  fascial transition zone (forearm fascia → flexor retinaculum)
- Less intermuscular tissue than PC6 (tendons rather than muscle bellies)

**Dual mechanism at PC7:**
- LOCAL: carpal tunnel decompression (nerve-specific, segmental)
- DISTANT: cardiac/GI effects via NTS (supraspinal, non-segmental for
  GI, partially segmental for cardiac)

Same point, two mechanism classes — a pattern seen at other PC points
(PC6 also has local forearm and distant visceral applications).

**Gap: Ghost Point mechanism**
The 13 Ghost Points span 8 different meridians and diverse anatomical
locations. No unified neural pathway explains why these specific 13
points were selected for mental disorder treatment. The two Pericardium
Ghost Points (PC7 + PC8) both access the median nerve — but so do PC4,
PC5, and PC6, which are NOT Ghost Points. The Ghost designation cannot
be explained by nerve identity alone.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the transverse cubital crease,
at the ulnar side of the tendon of biceps brachii.

prominent at the cubital fossa. PC3 is immediately ulnar (medial) to

brachial artery lies adjacent.

- Spinal segment: C5-T1 (brachial plexus territory at elbow)

Fascial plane: bicipital aponeurosis (lacertus fibrosus) bridges from
biceps tendon medially over the cubital fossa structures. PC3 lies
deep to this aponeurosis.
→ Cross-ref: A03

Vascular: **brachial artery** — runs immediately medial to the biceps
tendon at the cubital fossa. The median cubital vein crosses
superficially (the standard venipuncture site). CRITICAL vascular
anatomy — brachial artery is the main arterial supply to the forearm
and hand.
→ Cross-ref: A04

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Median cubital vein (superficial)
- Bicipital aponeurosis (lacertus fibrosus)

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Cardiac conditions**
Confidence: 🟡 Plausible

Part of Pericardium channel protocols for cardiac conditions. Combined

dedicated randomized controlled trials isolating PC3 specifically.

**2. Gastrointestinal — vomiting, gastroenteritis**
Confidence: 🟡 Plausible

Traditional use for acute vomiting and gastroenteritis. Neuroprotective

Confidence: 🔴 Unvalidated

Classical bloodletting indication without modern controlled trials.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

He-Sea points at the elbow access the median nerve trunk at a
proximal level, before it branches into forearm branches. Disease-state
sensitization likely follows the same C8-T2 cardiac pattern as
PC6/PC7 (Kim 2017).

Elbow overuse conditions produce local tenderness at PC3 (standard
musculoskeletal dynamic acupoint response).
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Track A systems at PC3:**

A01/A02 (Neural):
- Median nerve TRUNK at cubital fossa — the most proximal access to
  the median nerve on the Pericardium channel (PC4-PC9 access
  progressively more distal branches)
- Same NTS convergence pathway as PC6/PC7 for visceral effects
- Proximal needle insertion accesses the nerve before it divides —
  theoretically activating the full nerve territory (all forearm + hand
  median branches) from a single proximal point
- C5-T1 spinal segments (broader than distal points at C6-T1)

A04 (Vascular — UNIQUE at PC3):
- The brachial artery is immediately adjacent. Needling near (not into)
  the artery may produce local vascular effects — vasodilation,
  microcirculation changes. This vascular proximity is unique among PC
  points and may contribute to the bloodletting tradition
- Bloodletting releases venous blood from the median cubital vein —
  traditional "clearing Heat" may correspond to local vascular effects

**Gap:** The mechanism of bloodletting as treatment is poorly understood.
Classical theory attributes it to "releasing pathogenic Heat from the
Blood level." Modern hypotheses: local vasodilation, inflammatory
mediator release, or simply placebo/cultural practice. No controlled
studies comparing bloodletting at PC3 vs sham bloodletting.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

**Châm (B01):**
Needle depth: 10-15 mm perpendicular. AVOID the brachial artery —
palpate pulse first, insert to the ulnar side of the biceps tendon.
De Qi: distension at the cubital fossa, may radiate down the forearm.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S2.2

**Bloodletting (classical):**
Three-edged needle pricking at superficial veins of the cubital fossa
(median cubital vein). Release 3-5 drops of blood. Classical indication:
acute Heat in Ying/Blood level. Combined with BL40 bloodletting.
Modern practice: still used in TCM emergency protocols for heatstroke.

**Bấm huyệt (B02):**
Applicable but less commonly used than PC6. Firm pressure at the
cubital fossa, ulnar to the biceps tendon.

**Cứu (B08):**
Moxa at PC3 generally CONTRAINDICATED — classical prohibition against
moxibustion at He-Sea/Water points used for clearing Heat. The point's
primary function is cooling; heating contradicts its traditional action.

### An toàn

Pneumothorax risk: None — elbow location.

**Vascular risk: HIGH — brachial artery immediately adjacent.** The
brachial artery is the main arterial supply to the forearm and hand.
Inadvertent arterial puncture is the primary safety concern at PC3.
Palpate pulse before needling. Insert to the ulnar side of the biceps
tendon, away from the arterial pulse. For bloodletting: target the
superficial VENOUS structures (median cubital vein), never the artery.

Nerve risk: median nerve trunk lies deep. Standard perpendicular
insertion at 10-15 mm is generally safe, but deep needling risks
nerve contact.

Pregnancy: Not specifically contraindicated.

## PC4 — Khích Môn (郄门, Xì Mén)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
Xi-Cleft point of the Pericardium channel. Xi-Cleft points are where
Qi and Blood accumulate deeply — classically indicated for ACUTE
conditions along the channel. On Yin channels, Xi-Cleft points
specifically treat Blood disorders (bleeding, hemorrhage, blood stasis).
PC4 is the primary ACUTE cardiac emergency point on the Pericardium
channel.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): 5 cun above the transverse crease
of the wrist (PC7 Daling), on the line connecting PC3 (Quze) and PC7
(Daling), between the tendons of palmaris longus and flexor carpi
radialis.

Anatomical landmarks: same inter-tendon cleft as PC5, PC6, and PC7
but more proximal — 5 cun (approximately 7.5 cm) above the wrist
crease, roughly at the midpoint of the forearm.

Needle depth range: 13-25 mm perpendicular insertion.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Superficial: medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (C8-T1)
- Middle: palmar cutaneous branch of median nerve (C6-T1). The median
  nerve trunk lies in the same intermuscular plane as at PC6, but
  slightly more radial at this proximal level.
- Deep: anterior interosseous nerve (motor branch of median nerve)
- Spinal segment: C6-T1 (median nerve territory)

Fascial plane: between flexor digitorum superficialis and flexor
digitorum profundus — same intermuscular plane as PC6 and PC5.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: anterior interosseous artery and vein at deep level. Median
antebrachial vein superficially.
→ Cross-ref: A04

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Palmaris longus (medial) / flexor carpi radialis (lateral)
- Flexor digitorum superficialis
- Flexor digitorum profundus
- Interosseous membrane

NOTE: PC4 lies in the SAME anatomical plane as PC5, PC6, and PC7 —
all four points share the inter-tendon cleft between palmaris longus
and flexor carpi radialis, accessing the median nerve at progressively
different levels of the forearm.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Xi-Cleft: YES — Xi-Cleft point of Pericardium (郄穴)**
One of 16 Xi-Cleft points (12 regular meridian + 4 extraordinary
vessel). Xi-Cleft points are where Qi and Blood accumulate deeply.
Used for acute pain and acute organ conditions along the channel.

YIN CHANNEL Xi-CLEFT SUB-RULE: Yin channel Xi-Cleft points specifically
treat BLOOD disorders — bleeding, hemorrhage, blood stasis. PC4 as a
Yin Xi-Cleft treats cardiac Blood stasis (acute chest pain, angina)
and hemorrhage (hematemesis, hemoptysis, epistaxis).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Five Shu: No (PC4 falls between PC5 Jianshi and PC3 Quze but is not
a Five Shu point)
Yuan-Source: No (PC7 Daling)
Luo-Connecting: No (PC6 Neiguan)
Back-Shu: No (BL14 Jueyinshu at T4)
Front-Mu: No (CV17 Shanzhong)
Eight Confluent: No
Hui-Meeting: No
Special: Not a Ghost Point, Command Point, or Ma Dan-yang Star.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** PC4 Ximen — Xi-Cleft of Pericardium. Name
"Ximen" (郄门, Cleft Gate) — the "cleft" (郄) refers to the deep
space where Qi and Blood accumulate; the "gate" (门) implies an access
point to this deep reservoir. Primary ACUTE cardiac point. Classical
formula: PC4 + PC6 for acute cardiac pain (Xi-Cleft + Luo-Connecting
on same channel — addresses both Blood stasis and Qi regulation
simultaneously).

**2. Trigger point:** Forearm flexor trigger points. Flexor digitorum
superficialis trigger points in the mid-forearm. Referred pain pattern:
forearm ache, finger stiffness. Anatomical correspondence similar to
PC6 (same inter-tendon location, slightly more proximal).
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.3

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Mid-forearm tenderness in overuse
conditions. Less commonly a primary Ashi point than PC6 or PC7.

**4. Marma point:** No precise Marma correspondence at the mid-forearm.
Manibandha (wrist) and Kurpara (elbow) are the nearest Marma points.

**5. Head zone:** C6-T1 dermatome. Same partial cardiac convergence as
PC5/PC6/PC7 (T1 overlaps cardiac T1-T5). No GI convergence.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Arm Line (Myers 2001). The flexor
muscle bellies in the mid-forearm are central stations on this line.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** Same partial cardiac pattern as
PC6/PC7 — C8-T2 territory includes the T1 component of the median
nerve at PC4. Kim 2017 did not specifically test PC4.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable — mid-forearm not a cupping site.

**Convergence score: 4/8**
(TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi weak, Anatomy Trains)

Lower convergence than PC6 (5/8) or PC7 (5/8), primarily because
PC4 lacks a specific Marma correspondence and has a weaker Ashi
profile. The point's clinical significance derives from its Xi-Cleft
FUNCTIONAL designation rather than anatomical convergence across
traditions.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing (Huangfu Mi, ~260 CE): cardiac pain, chest
  tightness, palpitations, hematemesis, epistaxis, fright
- Nanjing: Xi-Cleft point theory — for acute conditions of the
  channel
- Zhenjiu Dacheng (Yang Jizhou, 1601): acute cardiac pain, chest
  oppression, vomiting blood, nosebleed, epilepsy, skin boils

Nguồn hiện đại:
- Deadman (Manual of Acupuncture): invigorates Blood, calms Shen,
  clears Heat from Blood. Acute cardiac pain, palpitations, chest
  oppression, hematemesis, hemoptysis, epistaxis, epilepsy
- WHO 2008: cardiac conditions, hemorrhagic conditions

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- PRIMARY acute cardiac point on the Pericardium channel — selected
  when onset is sudden (angina attack, acute palpitations). PC6 is
  preferred for CHRONIC cardiac regulation
- **ACUTE-CHRONIC DISTINCTION:** PC4 (Xi-Cleft, acute Blood stasis)
  vs PC6 (Luo-Connecting, chronic Qi regulation). Classical rule:
  Xi-Cleft for emergency, Luo-Connecting for ongoing treatment
- Hemostatic function: Xi-Cleft of Yin channels stop bleeding.
  PC4 for hematemesis, hemoptysis, epistaxis — not for menstrual
  bleeding (→ SP8 Diji, Xi-Cleft of Spleen)
- Classical combinations: PC4 + PC3 + PC7 (Qianjin Yaofang, all-PC
  channel combination for acute cardiac); PC4 + PC6 (Xi-Cleft + Luo
  for cardiac pain); PC4 + BL15 (Pericardium channel + Heart Back-Shu
  for dual-pathway cardiac access)

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Cardioprotection during cardiac surgery**
Confidence: 🟡 Plausible

Randomized controlled trial: electroacupuncture at Ximen (PC4) for
enhanced recovery after heart valve replacement surgery with
cardiopulmonary bypass. Results showed cardioprotective effects —
decreased myocardial ischemia markers. Published in Evidence-Based
Complementary and Alternative Medicine (2017).

**2. Acute cardiac pain / angina**
Confidence: 🟡 Plausible

Used in clinical protocols for acute cardiac presentations. PC4 +
PC6 is a standard acupuncture emergency combination for acute chest
pain. Individual PC4 contribution difficult to isolate from multi-point
protocols.

Traditional claim that PC4 can "regulate the autonomic nervous system"
and "reduce the impact of myocardial infarction" is reported in TCM
databases. Mechanism plausible via median nerve → NTS → vagal cardiac
modulation (same pathway as PC6). No dedicated systematic reviews.

**3. Hemorrhagic conditions (hematemesis, hemoptysis)**
Confidence: 🔴 Unvalidated

Classical Yin Xi-Cleft indication without modern controlled trials.
The hemostatic mechanism of acupuncture at Xi-Cleft points is unknown.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Same anatomical plane and nerve territory as PC6. Disease-state
sensitization for cardiac conditions plausible based on Kim 2017
C8-T2 neurogenic spot data (PC4 shares the C6-T1 territory where
cardiac neurogenic spots appear in hypertensive models).

Mid-forearm tenderness in overuse conditions (standard musculoskeletal
sensitization).
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

**Track A systems at PC4:**

A01/A02 (Neural):
- Same median nerve access as PC6 but 3 cun (approximately 4.5 cm)
  more proximal. The median nerve trunk and anterior interosseous
  nerve are both accessible at this level.
- Supraspinal pathway identical to PC6: median nerve → C6-T1 spinal
  cord → NTS → vagal cardiac modulation
- The claim that PC4 is specifically "acute" while PC6 is "chronic"
  has NO KNOWN NEUROANATOMICAL BASIS — both access the same nerve
  at similar levels. The acute-chronic distinction may reflect
  clinical convention rather than a mechanistic difference.

**Gap: Xi-Cleft specificity**
Traditional theory holds that Xi-Cleft points are anatomically distinct
— sites where Qi and Blood accumulate in a "deep cleft." At PC4, there
is no obvious anatomical "cleft" that distinguishes it from the
surrounding inter-tendon space (PC5 and PC6 have the same anatomy).
The Xi-Cleft designation may reflect empirical observation of clinical
effectiveness at this exact distance (5 cun above wrist) rather than
a unique anatomical feature. The functional difference between PC4
(acute) and PC6 (chronic) at the same nerve requires further
investigation.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

**Châm (B01):**
Needle depth: 13-25 mm perpendicular. Same technique as PC6 — insert
between the two tendons (palmaris longus and flexor carpi radialis).
De Qi: distension, heaviness at mid-forearm. May radiate distally.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S2.2

**EA (B01):**
PC4-PC6 electrode pairing occasionally used (Xi-Cleft + Luo-Connecting
on same channel). More commonly PC6-PC7 is used for EA.

**Bấm huyệt (B02):**
Same inter-tendon technique as PC6 but at the mid-forearm level. Less
commonly used for acupressure than PC6.

**Cứu (B08):**
Applicable for cold-pattern cardiac presentations. Less commonly
moxibusted than needled.

### An toàn

Pneumothorax risk: None — forearm location.

Vascular risk: anterior interosseous vessels at deep level — not
typically reached at standard needling depth. Superficial veins
present (median antebrachial vein).

Nerve risk: median nerve trunk lies in the mid-layer. Same precautions
as PC6 — deep needling may elicit electric sensation. Withdraw if
nerve contact occurs.

Pregnancy: Not specifically contraindicated.

## PC1 — Thiên Trì (天池, Tiān Chí)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**
No special point category designation. The most proximal and ONLY
thoracic point on the Pericardium channel. Pneumothorax risk.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

1 cun lateral to the nipple, 5 cun lateral to the anterior midline.

Anatomical landmarks: on the lateral chest wall, one finger-breadth
lateral to the nipple (in males). In the 4th intercostal space between
the 4th and 5th ribs.

Needle depth range: 5-10 mm oblique or transverse insertion. SHALLOW
ONLY — lung tissue beneath.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- 4th intercostal nerve (T4) — sensory branch
- Lateral thoracic nerve (from brachial plexus, C5-C7) — innervates
  pectoralis minor
- Spinal segment: T4 (primary), C5-C7 (secondary via lateral thoracic)

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Pectoralis major muscle
- Pectoralis minor muscle
- External intercostal muscle → internal intercostal muscle
- Pleura → LUNG (at depth — pneumothorax risk)

### Phân loại huyệt

Five Shu: No
Yuan-Source: No | Luo-Connecting: No | Xi-Cleft: No
Back-Shu: No | Front-Mu: No | Eight Confluent: No
Hui-Meeting: No

PC1 is where the Pericardium channel emerges on the body surface after

**3. Ashi:** Tender in pectoral overuse, shoulder impingement syndromes.

**4. Marma:** Stanamuula Marma (root of the breast) — approximate
correspondence in the lateral chest region.

**5-8. Head zone / Anatomy Trains / Neurogenic spot / Cupping:** T4

**Convergence score: 4/8**
(TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Marma approximate)

### Chỉ định truyền thống

- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: chest fullness, lateral costal pain, axillary
  swelling, cough, malaria
- Deadman: unbinds the chest, transforms Phlegm, benefits the breasts.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic modern study of PC1 as an individual acupoint.

### Cơ chế giải thích

T4 segmental innervation aligns with cardiac autonomic territory
(T1-T5). PC1 is the ONLY Pericardium channel point with DIRECT
segmental cardiac convergence — T4 is the same level as the Back-Shu
of the Pericardium (BL14 Jueyinshu, also T4). This is a purely
segmental mechanism, unlike the supraspinal NTS pathway at distal
points (PC3-PC9).

### An toàn

**Pneumothorax risk: YES — 4th intercostal space.** The lung apex
extends superiorly; lung tissue lies deep to the intercostal muscles
at this location. Maximum safe depth approximately 10 mm. Oblique
or transverse insertion ONLY — never deep perpendicular. This is
the ONLY Pericardium channel point with pneumothorax risk.

Pregnancy: Not specifically contraindicated, but breast/chest
needling avoided during lactation.

(TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi)

### Chỉ định truyền thống

- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: cardiac pain, lateral costal fullness, cough,
  pain and swelling of the medial arm
- Deadman: unbinds the chest, activates Blood. Cardiac pain, cough,
  chest oppression, pain in the chest/back/medial arm

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic modern study.

### Cơ chế giải thích

PC2 accesses the musculocutaneous nerve (C5-C7), NOT the median nerve.
This is the NEURAL TRANSITION point on the Pericardium channel: PC1
(T4 intercostal + C5-C7 lateral thoracic) → **PC2 (C5-C7
musculocutaneous)** → PC3 onward (C6-T1 median nerve). The channel's
anatomical pathway transitions from one nerve territory to another at
the upper arm.

### An toàn

No pneumothorax risk. Brachial artery lies medial — avoid medial
deviation. No pregnancy contraindication.

## PC5 — Giản Sử (间使, Jiān Shǐ)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**
Jing-River/Metal point — fourth of the Five Shu on the Pericardium
channel. Located 3 cun above the wrist crease in the same inter-tendon
cleft as PC4, PC6, and PC7.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): 3 cun above the transverse crease
of the wrist, between the tendons of palmaris longus and flexor carpi
radialis.

Anatomical landmarks: 1 cun (approximately 1.5 cm) proximal to PC6,
2 cun distal to PC4. Same inter-tendon cleft.

Needle depth range: 13-20 mm perpendicular insertion.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Same as PC6: median nerve territory (C6-T1). Palmar cutaneous
  branch superficially, median nerve trunk at middle depth, anterior
  interosseous nerve deep.
- Spinal segment: C6-T1

Vascular: same as PC6 — median antebrachial vein superficially,
anterior interosseous vessels deep.

Muscle layers: identical to PC6 — palmaris longus / flexor carpi
radialis → flexor digitorum superficialis → flexor digitorum profundus
→ interosseous membrane.

NOTE: PC5 shares the SAME anatomical plane, nerve supply, and tissue
layers as PC4, PC6, and PC7. Four consecutive points (PC4-PC7) lie
in a single inter-tendon cleft, each 1-2 cun apart, accessing the
same median nerve at slightly different levels.

### Phân loại huyệt

**Five Shu: Jing-River/Metal point (经金穴)**
Fourth of the Five Shu points. Jing-River points are in the wrist/
ankle area and traditionally treat cough, dyspnea, chills and fever.
Metal element on a Fire channel — Metal controls Fire via the
controlling cycle.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Yuan-Source: No | Luo-Connecting: No | Xi-Cleft: No
Back-Shu: No | Front-Mu: No | Eight Confluent: No
Hui-Meeting: No
Special: No special designation.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM:** PC5 Jianshi — "Intermediary." Name suggests the point
serves as an intermediary/envoy between paired channels (Pericardium
↔ Triple Burner). Treats cardiac pain, palpitation, stomach pain,
vomiting, malaria, mania, epilepsy, arm pain.

**2-8:** Same cross-tradition profile as PC6 (forearm inter-tendon
location): trigger point correspondence with forearm flexors (moderate),
Ashi tenderness in forearm overuse (moderate), no specific Marma, C6-T1
Head zone, Deep Front Arm Line station, Kim 2017 neurogenic pattern not
directly tested, no cupping.

**Convergence score: 4/8**
(TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Anatomy Trains)

### Chỉ định truyền thống

- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: cardiac pain, palpitations, stomach pain,
  vomiting, malaria, mania, epilepsy, elbow/arm contraction
- Deadman: resolves Phlegm, opens the chest, calms Shen, treats
  malaria. Cardiac pain, palpitations, epigastric pain, vomiting,
  mania, epilepsy, malaria, arm pain, loss of voice

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- Traditional primary point for malaria (now historically relevant
  rather than clinically current)
- Combined with PC7 and PC3 for myocarditis (modern clinical use)
- Combined with LU7 for loss of voice (Jing-River points treat cough/
  dyspnea/voice conditions per Five Shu theory)

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05279404: electroacupuncture study using PC5 +
PC6 to evaluate cardiac and gastric function simultaneously. Status
and results not yet available. This is the only identified clinical
trial specifically including PC5.

No systematic modern study of PC5 as an individual acupoint.

### Cơ chế giải thích

Same median nerve → NTS pathway as PC6. Given that PC5 lies only
1 cun (approximately 1.5 cm) from PC6 in the SAME anatomical plane
with the SAME nerve supply, the mechanistic distinction between PC5
and PC6 is unclear. Traditional Five Shu theory assigns different
clinical profiles (Jing-River for cough/fever vs PC6 Luo-Connecting
for cardiac/GI), but the neural pathway is identical.

### An toàn

Same as PC6 — median nerve injury risk with deep needling. No
pneumothorax risk. No pregnancy contraindication.

## PC9 — Trung Xung (中冲, Zhōng Chōng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**
Jing-Well/Wood point — first (most distal) of the Five Shu on the
Pericardium channel. Located at the tip of the middle finger. Classical
emergency resuscitation point.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): at the center of the tip of the
middle finger, 0.1 cun proximal to the fingernail.

Anatomical landmarks: center of the fleshy pad at the tip of the
middle (3rd) finger.

Needle depth range: 1-2 mm shallow prick, or bloodletting prick.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Proper palmar digital nerves (median nerve terminal branches, C7-C8)
  — the fingertip has extremely high receptor density (Meissner
  corpuscles, Merkel cells, free nerve endings). The middle finger
  is exclusively median nerve territory.
- Spinal segment: C7-C8

Vascular: proper palmar digital arteries (terminal branches of the
common palmar digital artery).

Tissue: thick skin (stratum corneum), dense fingertip pulp tissue,
high concentration of encapsulated mechanoreceptors. No muscle layer
— only skin, subcutaneous tissue, and distal phalanx bone.

### Phân loại huyệt

**Five Shu: Jing-Well/Wood point (井木穴)**
First and most distal of the Five Shu points. Jing-Well points are
at the fingertip/toe tip and traditionally treat acute conditions,
loss of consciousness, and fullness below the Heart. Wood element
on a Fire channel — Wood generates Fire via the generating cycle
(= the "mother" point for tonifying the Pericardium).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Yuan-Source: No | Luo-Connecting: No | Xi-Cleft: No
Back-Shu: No | Front-Mu: No | Eight Confluent: No
Hui-Meeting: No
Special: No additional special designation. However, Jing-Well points
as a class are traditionally considered emergency points across ALL
12 channels. The clinical convention of pricking the fingertips
(十宣, Shixuan — "Ten Diffusions") for loss of consciousness uses
all 10 fingertip Jing-Well points including PC9.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM:** PC9 Zhongchong — "Central Rush." Name refers to the
rushing sensation of Qi at the fingertip. Terminal point of the
Pericardium channel where it connects to the Triple Burner channel
(PC9 → TE1 transition at the ring finger tip in classical channel
theory).

**2-8:** Fingertip location has limited cross-tradition correspondence.
No specific trigger point, Ashi, or Marma at the middle fingertip.
No Head zone convergence for visceral conditions. Not a cupping or
Anatomy Trains point.

**Convergence score: 1/8**
(TCM only)

The low convergence score is expected for fingertip points — the
traditions that independently identified body points (trigger points,
Marma, Head zones) do not map the fingertips as clinically significant
sites. The fingertip's therapeutic significance is unique to the
meridian-based systems.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing: loss of consciousness, heatstroke, fainting,
  stiff tongue, children's convulsions, fever
- Deadman: clears Heat, restores consciousness, expels Wind. Loss of
  consciousness, febrile convulsions in children, tongue swelling/
  stiffness, cardiac pain, irritability
- Classical emergency use: pricked to bleed at the fingertip to
  restore consciousness — one of the oldest emergency acupuncture
  techniques

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic modern study of PC9 as an individual acupoint.

Jing-Well points as a class: fingertip stimulation (including PC9)
produces STRONG sensory input due to extreme receptor density at
the fingertip. The arousal/consciousness effect of fingertip pricking
may operate through intense nociceptive input → reticular activating
system → cortical arousal. This is a general mechanism for ALL
fingertip Jing-Well points, not specific to PC9.

### Cơ chế giải thích

Proper palmar digital nerves (median nerve terminal branches, C7-C8)
carry high-density sensory input. Fingertip pricking produces intense
nociceptive input that may activate ascending reticular pathways for
arousal. The mechanism is NON-SPECIFIC — any fingertip stimulation
would produce similar effects, which aligns with the traditional
Shixuan (Ten Diffusions) practice of pricking all 10 fingertips
rather than relying on PC9 alone.

### An toàn

No pneumothorax risk. Minimal vascular risk (digital arteries are
small). Infection risk with bloodletting — standard aseptic technique.
No pregnancy contraindication.
