>
> **Meridian overview:** The Liver channel (Foot Jueyin) starts at the
> lateral nail corner of the big toe (LR1 Dadun), crosses the dorsum of
> the foot (LR2-LR4), ascends the medial leg along the tibia, continues
> up the medial thigh through the adductor group, curves around the
> external genitalia, enters the lower abdomen, reaches the lateral
> abdomen (LR13 Zhangmen — Front-Mu of Spleen), and terminates at the
> subcostal area (LR14 Qimen — Front-Mu of Liver). Internal pathway
> ascends through the diaphragm, spreads in the ribs, follows the
> throat, connects to the eye system, and reaches the vertex (connecting
> to GV20). 14 points total. Paired with Gallbladder (GB, Foot
> Shaoyang). Peak time in Chinese clock: 01:00-03:00.

## LR3 — Thái Xung (太冲, Tài Chōng)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**
Yuan-Source point of the Liver channel. Shu-Stream (Earth) point — for
Yin channels, Yuan-Source and Shu-Stream coincide at the same point.
One half of the "Four Gates" (四关, Si Guan) with LI4 (Hegu), the most
commonly used point combination in clinical acupuncture for pain, stress,
and Liver Qi Stagnation. Primary point for Liver Qi Stagnation — the
most frequently diagnosed TCM pattern in modern clinical practice.
Most-used acupoint in hypertension acupuncture RCTs (12/23 trials).
Third most-used point in depression acupuncture studies (after GV20 and
Yintang).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the dorsum of the foot, in the
depression distal to the junction of the 1st and 2nd metatarsal bones.

Anatomical landmarks: in the first intermetatarsal space, approximately
2 cun (finger-widths) proximal to the web margin of the 1st and 2nd
toes. The point lies in the depression where the 1st and 2nd metatarsal
bones begin to separate. Palpation: slide a finger proximally along the
1st-2nd intermetatarsal groove until it falls into a distinct depression
before the bones converge at their bases.

Needle depth range: 5-10 mm perpendicular insertion. Some classical
techniques describe oblique insertion toward KI1 (Yongquan) on the
plantar surface.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply (Umemoto et al. 2020, Evidence-Based Complementary and
Alternative Medicine — cadaveric dissection):
- **Deep peroneal nerve (deep fibular nerve)**, medial branch — primary
  innervation at LR3. Vascular branches of the medial branch reaching
  the dorsal pedis artery found in 100% of specimens, mean distance
  from LR3 = 3.2 ± 2.6 mm.
- Spinal segments: **L4-L5** (deep peroneal nerve). Additionally, the
  medial plantar nerve (tibial nerve, L5-S1) provides sensory innervation
  to the plantar surface of the same interspace — deeper needle insertion
  may recruit this pathway.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A09 S1.2

Fascial plane: between the 1st and 2nd metatarsal bones, within or
superficial to the first dorsal interosseous muscle of the foot.
Intermuscular connective tissue between the first dorsal interosseous
and the adjacent extensor hallucis brevis tendon.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: dorsal pedis artery (continuation of anterior tibial artery)
runs along the lateral border of the extensor hallucis longus tendon,
crossing near or through the LR3 area. First dorsal metatarsal artery
branches from the dorsal pedis at the proximal first intermetatarsal
space.

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Extensor hallucis brevis tendon (lateral edge)
- First dorsal interosseous muscle of foot
- Plantar interosseous (deep)
→ Cross-ref: A08 S4

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Shu-Stream (Earth)** — 3rd of the Five Shu points on the
Liver channel. For Yin channels, the Five Shu element assignment:
Jing-Well = Wood (LR1), Ying-Spring = Fire (LR2), Shu-Stream = Earth
(LR3), Jing-River = Metal (LR4), He-Sea = Water (LR8).
Shu-Stream points treat "heaviness of body and pain of joints" (Neijing
Lingshu Ch.1).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Yuan-Source: **Yes.** Yuan-Source point (原穴) of the Liver channel. One
of 12 Yuan-Source points (one per regular meridian). For Yin channels,
the Yuan-Source point coincides with the Shu-Stream point — LR3 holds
both designations simultaneously. Yuan-Source points access Yuan
(Original) Qi; used diagnostically (tenderness = organ dysfunction)
and therapeutically (tonify constitutional energy). Combined with
Luo-Connecting point of paired channel in Host-Guest technique
(主客配穴法): Liver as host → LR3 (Yuan) + GB37 (Luo of paired
Gallbladder, Guangming).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Luo-Connecting: No (LR5 Ligou is Luo of Liver)

Xi-Cleft: No (LR6 Zhongdu is Xi-Cleft of Liver)

Back-Shu: No (BL18 Ganshu at T9 is Back-Shu of Liver)

Front-Mu: No (LR14 Qimen is Front-Mu of Liver — note: LR14 is the
Front-Mu point on its OWN channel, unlike most Front-Mu points which
are on Ren Mai or other channels. LR13 Zhangmen is also notable as
Front-Mu of Spleen despite being on the Liver channel.)

Eight Confluent: No

Hui-Meeting: No

Special designations:
- **"Four Gates" (四关, Si Guan)** — LR3 (Taichong) combined with LI4
  (Hegu) bilaterally = four needles = "Four Gates." One of the most
  commonly used point combinations in clinical acupuncture for pain,
  stress, emotional regulation, and Liver Qi Stagnation. Both are
  Yuan-Source points of their respective channels (Liver and Large
  Intestine), and both are anatomically homologous: the first
  interosseous space of foot (LR3) mirrors the first interosseous
  space of hand (LI4).
→ Cross-ref: AP-02 (LI4), B01 S3.1, B01 S3.2

### Cross-tradition mapping

**TCM acupoint:** LR3 Taichong (太冲). Yuan-Source of Liver. Primary
functions: spreads Liver Qi (shu xie, 疏泄 — the Liver's most important
function), subdues Liver Yang rising, extinguishes Liver Wind, nourishes
Liver Blood and Yin, clears Liver Fire, activates the channel. The
single most prescribed point for Liver Qi Stagnation, which is the
most commonly diagnosed TCM pattern in modern clinical practice.
Treatment formula: Liver Qi Stagnation → LR3, LR14, PC6, GB34.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2

**Trigger point:** First dorsal interosseous of foot trigger point
(Dorsher 2008 correspondence mapping). Referred pain pattern: dorsum
of foot in the 1st-2nd metatarsal area. The 93.3% acupoint-trigger
point overlap (Dorsher 2008) includes LR3 as a correspondent. Note:
trigger point referred pain covers LOCAL foot pain, not the distal
headache/emotional effects — the cross-tradition convergence is for
location identification, not for all clinical applications.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**Ashi correspondence:** Commonly tender on palpation in patients with
headache, hypertension, stress, and Liver Qi Stagnation patterns.
Clinically used as a diagnostic palpation point — tenderness at LR3
supports Liver pattern diagnosis. Reactivity increases under emotional
stress and in tension-type headache patients.

**Marma point:** **Kshipra** (क्षिप्र) — Marma point located between
the big toe and second toe of the foot (and correspondingly between
thumb and index finger on the hand). One of the 107 Marma points in
the Ayurvedic system (Sushruta Samhita). Classified as a Snayu (tendon/ligament)
Marma in most classical Ayurvedic sources (Sushruta Samhita); some
modern references classify the hand Kshipra as Sira (vascular) — see
AP-02 note. Dimension: half Angula. Classification: Kalantara Pranahara
(delayed fatal on trauma — indicating vital significance). Traditional
function: controls cardiovascular and respiratory systems, influences
Rasa Vaha Srotas (lymphatic) and Prana Vaha Srotas (respiratory).
Anatomical correspondence with LR3 is within 1-2 cm.

Notable: Kshipra exists on BOTH foot (matching LR3) and hand (matching
LI4). The "Four Gates" combination pairs two anatomically homologous
points that INDEPENDENTLY correspond to the SAME Marma across two
unrelated medical traditions.
→ Cross-ref: AP-02 (LI4 ≈ hand Kshipra)

**Head zone:** Liver viscerotome = T7-T9 (A07 S1.6, Beissner 2011).
LR3 is at L4-L5 dermatome (deep peroneal nerve). **No match.** The
5-segment gap (L4-L5 → T7-T9) means LR3's hepatic effects CANNOT be
explained by segmental convergence. Same non-segmental gap as ST36
(L4-L5 → T6-T9 gastric). Back-Shu point BL18 at T9 DOES match Liver
Head zone; LR3 does not.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S1.6, C04 S3.5.6

**Anatomy Trains:** **Deep Front Line (DFL)** — the Liver meridian
path closely parallels the DFL, particularly through the medial leg
and adductor group (Dorsher 2009, Medical Acupuncture). The DFL runs:
inner arch of foot → tibialis posterior → adductor group → psoas →
diaphragm → deep anterior spine. LR3 at the 1st-2nd metatarsal
interspace sits at the distal origin of this line on the dorsal foot
surface. The Liver channel's ascent through medial leg → adductors →
genital area → abdomen closely tracks the DFL's deep medial pathway.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.3, A09 S1.3

**Neurogenic spot:** No data. Kim 2017 primarily studied trunk/back
neurogenic inflammatory spots correlating with organ disease states
(cardiac, pulmonary). Foot acupoints not included in study design.
Liver-specific neurogenic spot study does not exist.

**Cupping point:** Not applicable. Foot dorsum too small and curved for
standard cupping. No traditional cupping protocol at LR3.

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Marma, Anatomy
Trains). Head zone = no match (non-segmental). Neurogenic spot = no
data. Cupping = not applicable. High convergence confirms strong
anatomical significance across independent traditions — same score as
LI4 (AP-02) and BL18 (AP-07).

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:

Neijing Lingshu (Chapter 1, Ben Shu — 本输): identified as Shu-Stream
and Yuan-Source point of the Liver channel.

Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing (针灸甲乙经, Huangfu Mi, 259 CE): headache, vertex
headache, dizziness, blurred vision, hypochondriac pain, abdominal
distension, hernia, uterine bleeding, urinary retention, pain and
swelling of the dorsal foot.

Nanjing (难经, Classic of Difficulties): elaborates Yuan-Source theory.
Yuan points access the source between the kidneys (Ming Men).

Zhenjiu Dacheng (针灸大成, Yang Jizhou, 1601): vertex headache,
dizziness, epilepsy, convulsions, deviation of mouth (facial palsy),
abdominal distension, jaundice. Contains the "Four Gates" prescription:
bilateral LI4 + LR3 for pain, emotional disturbance, and stagnation.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, Manual of Acupuncture):
- Spreads Liver Qi (depression, frustration, sighing, chest/costal pain,
  menstrual irregularity, PMS — the broadest clinical application)
- Subdues Liver Yang rising (headache, dizziness, tinnitus, hypertension)
- Extinguishes Liver Wind (tremors, convulsions, numbness, stroke sequelae)
- Nourishes Liver Blood and Yin (blurred vision, dry eyes, dizziness,
  muscle cramps)
- Clears Liver Fire (red eyes, tinnitus, irritability, bitter taste)
- Activates the channel (foot dorsum pain, lower limb pain)
- Regulates menstruation (amenorrhea, dysmenorrhea, irregular menses)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật: consistent across ALL classical sources
as the primary point for Liver conditions. LR3 is the "command point"
for Liver Qi Stagnation — the single most frequently diagnosed TCM
pattern, estimated to account for 20-30% of all TCM diagnoses in modern
practice. The "Four Gates" combination (LI4 + LR3) has been the
standard point pair for pain, stress, and emotional regulation for
centuries. In BL18 (Ganshu) clinical practice: Back-Shu (BL18) + distal
Yuan-Source (LR3) = comprehensive Liver treatment — one of the most
common Shu-Mu/Shu-Yuan pairings.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2, AP-02 (LI4), AP-07 (BL18)

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Migraine — Four Gates combination — 🟡 Plausible**

Siguan SR/MA (2025, European Journal of Integrative Medicine — 19 RCTs,
1,312 participants): Siguan points-based acupuncture treatment (SPBAT)
demonstrated significant improvements:
- Clinical effective rate: RR **1.22** [1.14, 1.31] — significant
- VAS headache intensity: MD **-1.11** [-1.50, -0.73] — significant
- Attack frequency: SMD **-0.81** [-1.49, -0.14] — significant
- Safety profile comparable to standard treatments
- No significant differences in adverse events between groups

Cochrane TTH prevention (Linde 2016): general acupuncture vs usual
care: 48% vs 17% responders (RR 2.5). Acupuncture vs sham: 51% vs 43%
(RR 1.3) — 11 additional responders per 100 patients over sham.
Moderate quality evidence. Not LR3-specific but LR3 included in
majority of trial protocols.

Cochrane migraine prevention (Linde 2016): acupuncture ≥ prophylactic
drugs with better safety. Migraine prevention NNT=4 vs no treatment,
NNT=11 vs sham.

Confidence: 🟡 — positive SR/MA for Four Gates combination with
clinically significant effect sizes. However, LR3 always tested as
part of Four Gates (with LI4) or multi-point protocols — cannot isolate
LR3's individual contribution to migraine relief. Individual LR3-only
migraine trial does not exist.

**2. Hypertension — 🟡 Plausible**

Sham-controlled RCT (Sajjadi et al. 2020, Journal of Bodywork and
Movement Therapies — N=90, 2 arms): LR3 as SOLE acupoint vs sham
(1st metatarsal, non-acupoint). Results:
- Experimental group: SBP 145.0 → 132.2 mmHg, DBP 92.4 → 78.6 mmHg
  (significant decrease at 20 min and 30 min post-acupuncture)
- Sham control: no significant change at any time point
- Between-group difference: significant at immediately and 30 min
  post-acupuncture for both SBP and DBP
- Limitation: single session, acute effect only, no long-term follow-up

General acupuncture for hypertension (overview of 15 SRs, C02 data):
LR3 = most frequently selected acupoint (12/23 RCTs), followed by
LI11, GB20, ST36. Evidence for acupuncture+medication > medication
alone. Evidence for acupuncture alone = inconsistent.

Acupressure for hypertension SR (2023): LR3 among most-used points.
Evidence supports acute BP reduction.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S5.3, C02 Domain 5

Confidence: 🟡 — one positive sham-controlled RCT with LR3 as sole
point, consistent with LR3 being the most-selected point in
hypertension trials. Acute effect demonstrated. Long-term efficacy and
dose-response data lacking.

**3. Depression / Emotional regulation — 🟡 Plausible**

LR3 = 3rd most frequently used acupoint in depression acupuncture fMRI
studies (7/31 studies, after GV20 and Yintang). Always part of
multi-point protocols. No sole-point depression RCT.

B01 S3.2 formula: Liver Qi Stagnation (depression, frustration,
irritability) → LR3, LR14, PC6, GB34.

fMRI evidence for emotional regulation (Rao et al. 2024 meta-analysis):
LR3 specifically modulates anterior cingulate cortex, middle frontal
gyrus, and superior frontal gyrus — regions central to emotion
processing and regulation.

True vs sham fMRI (Wu et al. 2014, N=15): LR3 specifically activates/
deactivates brain areas related to vision, movement, sensation, emotion,
and analgesia. Sham needle does not produce these changes.

Confidence: 🟡 — strong traditional rationale (Liver Qi Stagnation =
most common depression pattern in TCM), consistent inclusion in
depression protocols, and fMRI evidence for emotion-related brain
modulation. But no isolated LR3 depression outcome data.

**4. Alzheimer's disease / cognitive — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Liang et al. 2014: Four Gates (LI4 + LR3) in AD patients modulated
default mode network activity on fMRI. Zheng et al. 2021: Four Gates
acupuncture affected resting-state networks beyond the default mode
network in AD. Both studies are mechanistic neuroimaging — no clinical
outcome endpoints (cognition, function, behavior).

Confidence: 🔴 — neuroimaging data only, no clinical outcome RCTs.
Direction of brain changes is consistent with potential cognitive benefit
but needs clinical validation.

**5. GI motility — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Crossover clinical trial: Four Gates (LI4 + LR3) showed bidirectional
regulatory effect on gastric motility — promoting peristalsis in low-
motility subjects while suppressing it in high-motility subjects.
Single study, mechanism-focused, not a clinical outcome trial.

Confidence: 🔴 — preliminary. Bidirectional regulation is consistent
with autonomic balancing model but insufficient clinical evidence.

**Sham comparison note:** The hypertension RCT (Sajjadi 2020) is the
only trial testing LR3 as a SOLE acupoint against sham — and showed
significant effects. In all other conditions, LR3 is tested as part of
multi-point protocols (Four Gates, or broader formulas), making it
impossible to attribute effects specifically to LR3 vs the combination.
This is a common methodological limitation for acupoints other than the
"landmark" points (ST36, PC6, LI4) which have more sole-point studies.

**Negative evidence note:** No published sole-point LR3 trial with null
result was found in the literature search. This may reflect the absence
of sole-point trials rather than absence of negative findings.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization: TCM palpation tradition reports increased
tenderness at LR3 in Liver Qi Stagnation patterns (emotional stress,
headache, hypertension, PMS). This is consistent with the dynamic
acupoint model (Kim 2017, AP-00 S1.2) but specific published data for
LR3 pressure pain threshold under pathological conditions is limited.
Liver-specific neurogenic spot study does not exist.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon)

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at LR3:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): deep peroneal nerve (L4-L5), medial
  branch — primary afferent pathway. Activation of A-delta fibers
  (De Qi sensation) and potentially A-beta fibers (pressure/touch
  mechanoreceptors in first dorsal interosseous).
- A02 (Autonomic Nervous System): sympathetic modulation → ↓BP (Sajjadi
  2020). Mechanism not fully mapped; likely supraspinal relay given
  non-segmental location.
- A04 (Vascular-Lymphatic): proximity to dorsal pedis artery (3.2 mm,
  Umemoto 2020). Local axon reflex at needling site.
- A07 (Pain-Nociception): gate control at spinal level (L4-L5
  segmental); supraspinal modulation for headache/migraine effects
  (similar to LI4's PAG/RVM — periaqueductal gray / rostroventromedial
  medulla — descending pathway; see contrast below).

**Central mechanism question — WHY does a foot point (L4-L5) affect
the liver (T7-T9) and treat headache (trigeminal/cervical)?**

This is the fundamental non-segmental question for LR3. The 5-segment
gap between L4-L5 (deep peroneal nerve) and T7-T9 (liver viscerotome)
mirrors ST36's gap (L4-L5 → T6-T9 gastric). For headache effects, the
gap from L4-L5 to trigeminal/cervical territory is even larger.

```
NON-SEGMENTAL GAP:
  LR3 (L4-L5) → Liver effects (T7-T9): 5-segment gap
  LR3 (L4-L5) → Headache effects (C1-C3 trigeminal): 14+ segment gap
  Compare: ST36 (L4-L5) → Gastric (T6-T9): 4-segment gap

  ST36 solution: PROKR2+ neurons → specific vagal-adrenal pathway (Liu
  2021 Nature). Molecular mechanism identified.

  LR3 solution: UNKNOWN. No PROKR2 study at LR3. No identified molecular
  pathway. Must operate through supraspinal relay, but specific pathway
  not mapped.
```

**fMRI meta-analysis provides partial answer (Rao et al. 2024):**

```
BRAIN ACTIVATION PATTERN (10 studies, N=319, seed-based d-mapping):

  LR3 acupuncture → activation of:
  - Right postcentral gyrus (somatosensory cortex — S1)
  - Left thalamus (relay station for ALL ascending somatosensory)
  - Left middle frontal gyrus (prefrontal — executive/emotional)
  - Right superior frontal gyrus (prefrontal — cognitive control)

  Meta-regression: longer acupuncture duration → stronger activation
  of right superior frontal gyrus (dose-dependent response).

  True vs sham (Wu et al. 2014): LR3 activates/deactivates brain
  areas related to vision, movement, sensation, emotion, and analgesia.
  Sham needle = no change.

  INTERPRETATION: LR3 accesses a widespread cortical and subcortical
  network via deep peroneal nerve afferents → thalamic relay →
  cortical distribution. The thalamic activation is key — thalamus
  as relay hub can distribute ascending signals to prefrontal
  (emotion/cognition), somatosensory (pain modulation), and
  potentially hypothalamic (autonomic/visceral) targets.
```

**Contrast with other Phase 1 non-segmental mechanisms:**

```
MECHANISM PATTERNS — 8 documented across Phase 1:

  ① ST36:  PROKR2 → vagal-adrenal (molecular, NTS/DMV)
  ② PC6:  median nerve → NTS → GABA inhibition (vagal disinhibition)
  ③ LI4:  radial nerve → PAG/RVM → descending modulation
  ④ SP6:  dual S2-S4 sacral + PAG pathway
  ⑤ BL67: UNKNOWN (S1 → uterus T10-L1 = no segmental link)
  ⑥ Back-Shu: somatovisceral reflex (segmental, ~100%)
  ⑦ HT7:  A-beta → dorsal column → cuneate → limbic modulation
  ⑧ LR3:  deep peroneal → thalamus → prefrontal/cortical
           (NON-SEGMENTAL, pathway partially mapped by fMRI,
           molecular mechanism UNKNOWN)

  LR3 adds the 8th pattern: a THALAMIC-CORTICAL relay pathway
  identified only by neuroimaging, without molecular characterization.
  Closest parallel is LI4 (also supraspinal, also non-segmental) but
  LI4's PAG/RVM descending modulation is better characterized than
  LR3's thalamic-cortical pattern.
```

**Gap:** the precise mechanism by which L4-L5 afferents from LR3 reach
the thalamus and produce hepatic/emotional effects is not mapped at the
molecular level. fMRI shows the endpoint (brain activation), not the
pathway. Key unanswered questions: (1) Does LR3 also use PAG/RVM
descending modulation (like LI4) for its analgesic effects? (2) Is
there a specific spinal ascending tract preferentially activated? (3)
Does the dorsal pedis artery proximity (Umemoto 2020) play a functional
role beyond anatomical landmark? The ST36 PROKR2 breakthrough required
molecular-level investigation — LR3 awaits equivalent research.

⚡ Framework note 1: LR3 adds the 8th distinct mechanism pattern to
Phase 1: thalamic-cortical relay via deep peroneal nerve, identified by
fMRI meta-analysis but without molecular characterization. The non-
segmental gap (L4-L5 → T7-T9) parallels ST36 exactly. ST36 was solved
by PROKR2 (molecular). LR3 remains at neuroimaging level only. The
growing catalog of distinct mechanisms (8 patterns across 12 points)
confirms that there is NO universal acupuncture mechanism — each point
accesses a DIFFERENT neural pathway determined by local anatomy.
Extends A09 S3.1 evidence scorecard and C04 S3.5.6 non-segmental gap.

⚡ Framework note 2: LR3 fMRI meta-analysis (Rao 2024, 10 studies,
N=319) provides the 3rd acupoint-specific cortical fingerprint in Phase
1, after ST36 (AP-03) and HT7 (AP-05). Each shows a DISTINCT brain
activation pattern: ST36 → visual network/noradrenaline maps; HT7 →
widest deactivation; LR3 → thalamus + prefrontal cortex. This
converges with the conclusion from AP-05 ⚡ note 2: different acupoints
produce different cortical signatures. Functional brain imaging may
become a key tool for validating acupoint specificity (A09 S3.3). With
3 acupoints now meta-analyzed, the pattern is strengthening from
"observed" to "systematic."
Extends A09 S3.3 fMRI specificity evidence.

⚡ Framework note 3: Kshipra Marma — deepest cross-tradition convergence
yet documented. The "Four Gates" (LR3 + LI4) pairs two anatomically
homologous points: both at the 1st interosseous space (foot and hand),
both Yuan-Source points of their respective channels, and BOTH
correspond to the SAME Marma (Kshipra) in the independent Ayurvedic
system. Kshipra Marma traditionally controls cardiovascular and
respiratory function — matching the clinical applications of both LR3
(hypertension) and LI4 (autonomic regulation). This triple convergence
— location, function, and clinical pairing — across two traditions that
developed without historical contact is the strongest cross-tradition
correspondence in the Atlas. Extends A09 S2.2 convergence zone model.

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 5-10 mm perpendicular. Standard 0.5-0.8 cun depth.
- De Qi: typically produces distention, soreness, or heaviness at the
  dorsum of the foot, sometimes radiating along the big toe or up
  toward the ankle. De Qi quality variable due to relatively thin
  tissue layer at this location.
- EA (electroacupuncture): commonly used in hypertension protocols
  (2 Hz most common in published RCTs). Also used in headache and
  depression protocols. Frequency mapping follows Han model:
  2 Hz → enkephalin/endorphin;
  100 Hz → dynorphin; 2/100 Hz alternating → maximum opioid synergy.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02):
- B02 Tier 2 point (10-50 acupressure-specific RCTs). Self-accessible:
  contralateral hand presses into the 1st-2nd metatarsal groove.
- Pressure: firm thumb pressure directed between the metatarsal bones,
  30-60 seconds sustained. For stress/headache relief.
- Conditions: headache, stress, hypertension. Part of gynecological
  acupressure protocol alongside SP6 and auricular Uterus point.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4, B02 S5

Cứu (B08):
- Direct or indirect moxa applicable. Used for cold-type Liver
  conditions and Blood stasis patterns. Not a primary moxa point.
→ Cross-ref: B08

Dry needling (B11):
- First dorsal interosseous of foot trigger point: needle into the
  muscle belly between 1st-2nd metatarsals. Local twitch response
  possible but often less pronounced than hand/forearm trigger points.
- Effects limited to local foot pain — does NOT reproduce the distal
  headache, emotional, or visceral effects that require activation of
  supraspinal pathways.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

So sánh: for hypertension, acupuncture (with EA) has the strongest
evidence (sole-point sham-controlled RCT). For headache, Four Gates
(needle) has SR/MA support. For stress/self-care, acupressure at LR3
offers accessible home protocol (Tier 2 evidence). For local foot pain,
dry needling addresses the trigger point component only.

### An toàn

Vascular risk: dorsal pedis artery runs adjacent to LR3. Avoid direct
arterial puncture — pulse check before needling is standard practice.
Apply pressure post-needling if bleeding occurs. Caution with
therapeutic anticoagulation.

Neural risk: deep peroneal nerve medial branch passes through the area.

## LR8 — Khúc Tuyền (曲泉, Qū Quán)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Crooked Spring" — He-Sea (Water) point of the Liver channel. Water
is the MOTHER of Wood in the Five Element generating cycle, making
LR8 the **tonification point** of the Liver channel. Primary point
for Liver Blood deficiency and Liver Yin deficiency — the nourishing
counterpart to LR3's Qi-moving function. Located at the medial knee
crease in the depression anterior to the hamstring tendons. Notable
preclinical evidence: electroacupuncture at LR8 specifically increases
hepatic blood flow but not splenic blood flow, demonstrating
organ-specific hemodynamic effects along the Liver meridian.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): when the knee is flexed, LR8 is
at the medial end of the transverse popliteal crease, in the
depression anterior to the tendons of semimembranosus and
semitendinosus, posterior to the medial epicondyle of the femur.

Anatomical landmarks: with the knee flexed to approximately 90°,
locate the medial end of the popliteal crease. The point is in the
depression between the posterior border of the medial femoral
epicondyle (anteriorly) and the tendinous insertions of
semimembranosus and semitendinosus (posteriorly). Approximately 1 cun
anterior to KI10 (Yingu), which lies between the tendons of
semimembranosus and semitendinosus. Palpate by flexing the knee — the
depression is most evident in this position.

Needle depth range: 10–15 mm perpendicular insertion. The great
saphenous vein runs superficially in this region — if pulsation or
venous resistance is felt, stop and redirect.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Saphenous nerve (branch of femoral nerve, L3-L4) — primary sensory
  innervation of the medial knee. The saphenous nerve is the largest
  purely sensory branch of the femoral nerve, descending through the
  adductor canal then emerging to supply the medial knee, leg, ankle,
  and foot
- Infrapatellar branch of saphenous nerve may contribute to anterior
  knee sensation in this region
- DIFFERENT from LR3: LR3 is innervated by deep peroneal nerve
  (L4-L5), while LR8 is innervated by saphenous nerve (L3-L4).
  Frontiers study (Wang et al. 2021) confirmed independent sensory

- Spinal segments: L3-L4
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: superficial fascia over the medial knee. Deep to the
point: the joint capsule of the knee and the posterior medial

→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: medial inferior genicular artery (branch of popliteal

→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (moderate thickness at medial knee)
- Sartorius muscle (superficial, crossing medially)
- Gracilis tendon (part of pes anserinus group)

Liver's OWN channel)."

Ling Shu chapter 1 (Ben Shu): "the He point is where Qi enters
deeply, like water flowing into the sea." He-Sea points on Yin

clinical paradox: the He-Sea classical role (treating fullness) meets

posterior compartment. The Liver meridian trajectory from LR5 through
LR8 closely parallels the DFL's medial line.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Hepatic blood flow — organ-specific effects — 🟡 Plausible**

Kim et al. 2012 (American Journal of Chinese Medicine): 2 Hz EA at
bilateral LR8 (Ququan) in 18 Sprague-Dawley rats increased mean blood
flow/perfusion in the liver but NOT in the spleen. Conversely, EA at
SP9 (Yinlingquan, He-Sea of Spleen) increased splenic blood flow but
NOT hepatic flow. Sham EA at non-acupoints did not increase blood
flow in either organ. Measurement: laser Doppler blood flow monitor
at liver and spleen surfaces, recorded before, during, and after EA.

Dong et al. 2014 (Journal of Acupuncture and Tuina Science): EA at
LR3 + LR8 in patients with chronic hepatic disease increased portal
vein blood flow velocity, total liver blood flow, and hepatic artery
resistance index. Clinical observation, not sham-controlled.

Confidence: 🟡 — the organ-specific finding (LR8 → liver flow, SP9 →
spleen flow) is striking and supports meridian specificity at the
He-Sea level. However: (1) preclinical only for the specific study;
(2) the clinical study combined LR3 + LR8 without isolating LR8;
(3) no sham-controlled human RCT for LR8 alone.

**2. Knee osteoarthritis — 🔴 Unvalidated**

LR8 appears in multi-point knee acupuncture protocols for medial knee
osteoarthritis, but no systematic review or RCT has isolated LR8 as
a sole intervention. The medial knee location makes it anatomically
relevant, but clinical evidence is embedded in multi-point protocols
(typically combined with SP9, ST35, EX-LE5 Xiyan).

Confidence: 🔴 — anatomically logical but no controlled evidence for
LR8 specifically.

**3. Neural pathway comparison (LR3 vs LR8) — 🟢 Confirmed (anatomical)**

Wang et al. 2021 (Frontiers in Integrative Neuroscience): compared
sensory and motor innervation between LR3 and LR8 in rats using
regional anatomy and neural tract tracing (cholera toxin subunit B
conjugates). Key finding: LR3 and LR8 are innervated by DIFFERENT
peripheral nerves (deep peroneal nerve at LR3, saphenous nerve at
LR8), originating from and terminating in DIFFERENT corresponding
spinal segments (L4-L5 vs L3-L4) and target regions. The two points
operate through independent sensory and motor pathways despite being
on the same meridian.

Confidence: 🟢 — well-designed anatomical study with tract tracing
confirmation. This is anatomical fact, not a clinical claim.

**Sham comparison note:** No sham-controlled clinical trial exists for
LR8 as a sole point. The hepatic blood flow study used sham EA at
non-acupoints as control (positive for specificity). The neural
pathway study is anatomical — no sham needed.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization: no specific published data on LR8
sensitization under hepatic pathology. The saphenous nerve territory
(L3-L4) does not overlap with the liver viscerotome (T7-T9), so
viscerocutaneous reflex-mediated sensitization at the medial knee
during liver disease would require supraspinal mechanisms — no data
exists on this.

Clinical observation: LR8 is frequently tender on palpation in
patients with medial knee pathology (osteoarthritis, meniscal injury,
pes anserine bursitis). Whether Liver-pattern patients show
sensitization at LR8 (e.g., Liver Blood deficiency → tender medial
knee) has not been studied.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at LR8:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): saphenous nerve (L3-L4) — purely
  sensory. Different nerve from LR3 (deep peroneal, L4-L5). Wang
  et al. 2021 demonstrated independent pathways. Fiber type: NOT
  studied at LR8. The saphenous nerve carries A-beta (touch/pressure)
  and A-delta (pain/temperature) fibers — which predominates during
  acupuncture at LR8 is unknown.
- A02 (Autonomic): L3-L4 spinal segments do NOT directly correspond
  to hepatic sympathetic innervation (T7-T9). The hepatic blood flow
  increase at LR8 requires NON-SEGMENTAL mediation — likely a
  supraspinal reflex pathway. This parallels the LR3 non-segmental
  gap (L4-L5 → T7-T9, 5 segments).
- A03 (Fascial): medial knee = transition zone between thigh and leg
  compartments. Multiple fascial layers converge: pes anserinus
  (sartorius + gracilis + semitendinosus tendons), joint capsule,
  deep fascia of the leg.

**Five Element tonification mechanism:**
LR8's traditional logic (Water generating Wood) maps to Liver Blood/
Yin deficiency conditions: dry eyes, blurred vision, scanty menses,
brittle nails, dizziness, pale complexion. The "nourishing" metaphor
(Water feeds Wood) has no known mechanistic basis — why stimulating
the saphenous nerve at the medial knee would specifically affect
hepatic blood production or storage is unexplained by current anatomy.

However: the Kim 2012 preclinical data showing LR8-specific hepatic
blood flow increase is consistent with the traditional concept of
LR8 "nourishing" the Liver, even though the mechanism (supraspinal
reflex? autonomic mediation?) is unknown.

**LR8 vs HT3 — same He-Sea/Water position, different logic:**
- HT3 (Water on Fire): CONTROL cycle (Water extinguishes Fire) →
  used for Heart Fire excess
- LR8 (Water on Wood): GENERATING cycle (Water nourishes Wood) →
  used for Liver Blood/Yin deficiency
This demonstrates how the Five Element system creates OPPOSITE
therapeutic applications from the same Five Shu position depending
on the channel's own element. Both are He-Sea/Water, both at major
joints (elbow/knee), but clinical indications are entirely different.

**Gap:** LR8 has no fiber-type study, no fMRI data, and no mapped
supraspinal pathway. The hepatic blood flow effect is documented
(preclinical) but the mechanism from saphenous nerve (L3-L4) to
hepatic vasculature (T7-T9) is unknown. Whether this non-segmental
effect uses the same class of supraspinal relay as LR3's thalamic-
cortical pathway (Rao 2024) is an open question.

  Insert into the depression between the medial epicondyle and

Bấm huyệt (B02):

Cứu (B08):

### An toàn

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Hemodynamic effects (blood pressure / cerebral blood flow) —
🔴 Unvalidated (clinically)**

Preclinical: Korean study (Journal of Acupuncture Research, 2003) —
acupuncture at LR2 with reduction technique in normal rats decreased
mean arterial blood pressure (MABP). LR2 with reinforcement technique
also decreased MABP but increased regional cerebral blood flow (rCBF)
when combined with HT8. Effects varied with needle technique
(reinforcing vs reducing) and with point combination order (LR2 first
vs HT8 first).

Clinical: LR2 appears in multi-point hypertension acupuncture
protocols (typically combined with LR3, GV20, and other points), but
no systematic review or RCT has isolated LR2 as a sole intervention
for hypertension. The blood pressure-lowering effect is embedded in
protocols where LR3 is the primary point.

Confidence: 🔴 — preclinical hemodynamic data exists but is limited
to a single animal study in normal (not hypertensive) rats. No
clinical evidence for LR2 alone. LR3 has much stronger hypertension
evidence (Sajjadi 2020, sham-controlled RCT) — LR2's individual
contribution is unknown.

**2. Headache — 🔴 Unvalidated**

LR2 is traditionally the primary point for vertex and temporal
headache attributed to Liver Fire/Yang rising. One case report
describes through-needling LR2 → LR3 for syndrome-differentiated
headache as more effective than conventional point protocols. No RCT
or systematic review exists for LR2 specifically for headache.

Confidence: 🔴 — strong classical indication supported by the channel
pathway reaching the vertex, but zero controlled evidence for LR2
as a sole or primary point.

**3. Insomnia — 🔴 Unvalidated**

One clinical observation (Zhongguo Zhen Jiu, 2013): needling LR2
penetrated to KI1 (Yongquan) for insomnia. Technique-specific study
(through-needling between two points on different channels), not an
assessment of LR2 alone. No sham control.

Confidence: 🔴 — single uncontrolled observation using a combined-
point technique. Not evidence for LR2 individually.

**Sham comparison note:** No sham-controlled clinical data exists for
LR2 as a sole point for any indication. The point's clinical
importance is derived entirely from classical tradition and the Five
Element sedation-point logic. This parallels HT8 (also a
Ying-Spring/Fire point with zero controlled evidence) — suggesting
that Ying-Spring points in general are under-studied relative to
Yuan-Source and He-Sea points.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization: no specific published data on LR2
sensitization under Liver Fire or Liver Yang rising conditions.
The dorsal foot web space (L4-L5 dermatome) is the same territory
as LR3. Whether Liver pathology generates tenderness at LR2 (in the
way that cardiac pathology generates neurogenic spots at HT7, Kim
2017) has not been studied.

Clinical observation: LR2 may be tender in acute Liver Fire
presentations (acute red eyes, severe headache, acute irritability),
but this is anecdotal — no systematic palpation study exists for
LR2 sensitization.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at LR2:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): dorsal digital branches of deep
  peroneal nerve (L4-L5). Same nerve as LR3 but at the terminal
  digital branches rather than the main trunk. Fiber type: NOT
  studied at LR2. The dorsal foot between toes has relatively thin
  skin with moderate mechanoreceptor density — likely A-beta and
  A-delta fibers, but which predominates during needling is unknown.
- A02 (Autonomic): L4-L5 spinal segments do NOT correspond to
  hepatic sympathetic innervation (T7-T9). Same non-segmental gap
  as LR3 and LR8. Any organ-level effects require supraspinal
  mediation.
- A03 (Fascial): minimal fascial complexity at this location — thin
  dorsal foot fascia over the interosseous muscle. Not a fascial
  convergence zone.

**Five Element sedation mechanism:**
LR2's traditional logic (Fire draining Wood) maps to Liver Fire
excess conditions: red eyes, vertex headache, irritability with
outbursts, bitter taste, insomnia with vivid dreams. The "draining"
metaphor (child absorbs parent's excess) has no known mechanistic
basis — why stimulating the deep peroneal terminal branches at the
toe web would specifically reduce Liver Fire symptoms is unexplained.

**LR2 vs HT8 — same Ying-Spring/Fire position, different logic:**
- HT8 (Fire on Fire): HORARY point (same element as organ) → clears
  Heart Fire directly (same-element pathology)
- LR2 (Fire on Wood): SEDATION point (child drains parent) → drains
  Liver Fire by diverting excess into the generating cycle
Both clear "Fire," but through different Five Element mechanisms.
Whether this traditional distinction reflects different neural
mechanisms is unknown — both are on the dorsal hand/foot with
similar innervation depth.

**LR2 vs LR3 — adjacent points, different clinical roles:**
Both innervated by deep peroneal nerve (L4-L5). LR3 is at the main
nerve trunk area (deeper, near dorsalis pedis artery); LR2 is at
the terminal digital branches (shallower, thinner skin). Traditional
distinction: LR3 moves Qi stagnation; LR2 clears Fire. Whether the
anatomical difference (trunk vs terminal branches, deeper vs
shallower) produces different afferent fiber engagement is an
interesting question — analogous to the HT4-7 FCU quartet question
on the Heart channel.

**Gap:** LR2 has no fiber-type study, no fMRI data, no mechanism
mapping. The blood pressure effect (preclinical, single study) uses
a different pathway than the hepatic blood flow effect at LR8 (also
preclinical). Both points are on the same channel but produce
different organ-level effects through different (unknown) mechanisms.
The vertex headache mechanism (Liver channel internal pathway to
vertex) has no anatomical correlate — the deep peroneal nerve (L4-L5)
has no known connection to vertex sensation (trigeminal V1/C2-C3).

### Kỹ thuật kích thích + dose-response

Châm (B01):
- Needle depth: 5–8 mm oblique insertion directed proximally toward
  LR3. Through-needling LR2 → LR3 is a classical technique for
  combined Qi-moving and Fire-clearing effects.
- De Qi: mild distention or sharp sensation at the toe web. The
  thin skin at the web margin makes needling sensations more acute
  than at thicker-skinned locations.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

Bấm huyệt (B02):
- Self-accessible: opposite thumb and index finger pinch the 1st
  web space, pressing into the depression at the web margin.
- Pressure: moderate sustained pressure, 30–60 seconds.
- The toe web is easily accessible — convenient for self-treatment.

Cứu (B08):
- Applicable but with clinical caveat: LR2 is primarily a
  Fire-CLEARING point. Moxa (warming technique) at LR2 is
  contraindicated in Liver Fire patterns — the exact conditions for
  which LR2 is most commonly used. Moxa at LR2 would only be
  appropriate in rare cold-stagnation patterns of the Liver channel.
  Same contraindication logic as HT8 (Fire-clearing point where moxa
  is usually inappropriate).

### An toàn

Vascular risk: 1st dorsal metatarsal artery and dorsal digital
arteries are small caliber. Low risk of significant hemorrhage.
Brief post-needling pressure sufficient.

Neural risk: dorsal digital nerves are superficial at the toe web —
the needle may contact them, producing sharp or electric sensation.
Generally well-tolerated at shallow depth.

Pneumothorax risk: none (foot location).

Pregnancy: LR2 is NOT listed among traditional forbidden points in
pregnancy (LI4, SP6, BL60, BL67, GB21). However, strong stimulation
with reduction technique is traditionally cautioned against during
pregnancy due to the point's strong Fire-clearing and Blood-cooling
effects.

Overall safety: LOW complication risk. Shallow insertion in thin
dorsal foot skin. The primary practical consideration is patient
comfort — the toe web is sensitive, and needling can produce sharp
sensations.

## LR5 — Lệ Câu (蠡溝, Lì Gōu)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Woodworm Canal" — Luo-Connecting point of the Liver channel. The
Liver Luo vessel branches from LR5, connects to the paired
Gallbladder (GB) channel, then descends to the dorsum of the foot.
Importantly, the Luo vessel also ascends along the channel pathway
to the genitalia — this ascending connection explains LR5's
prominent role in genitourinary conditions. Located 5 cun above the
medial malleolus on the medial surface of the tibia. LR5 is the
guest Luo point in the Host-Guest technique when Gallbladder is the
primary affected channel (GB40 Yuan + LR5 Luo).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the medial aspect of the
lower leg, 5 cun proximal to the highest prominence of the medial
malleolus, on the medial surface of the tibia, posterior to the
medial crest (border) of the tibia.

Anatomical landmarks: from the medial malleolus tip, measure 5 cun
proximally along the medial tibial surface. The point lies on the
flat medial face of the tibia, just posterior to the tibial crest
(the sharp anterior border). The tibial bone is subcutaneous here —
minimal soft tissue overlies the point. Palpate by running the
finger along the medial tibial surface until the tibial crest is
felt anteriorly.

Needle depth range: 5–10 mm oblique insertion posteriorly along the
tibial surface, or transverse insertion. Perpendicular insertion
is shallow due to the subcutaneous tibial bone directly beneath.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Saphenous nerve (branch of femoral nerve, L3-L4) — primary sensory
  innervation of the medial leg. At 5 cun above the malleolus, the
  saphenous nerve runs along the medial tibial surface, often
  accompanied by the great saphenous vein
- This is the SAME nerve as LR8 (saphenous, L3-L4) — both are in the
  medial leg neural zone. LR5 through LR7 share this innervation.
  DIFFERENT from LR1-LR4 (deep peroneal, L4-L5)
- Key anatomical distinction from SP6: SP6 (Sanyinjiao, 3 cun above
  malleolus) is innervated by the posterior tibial nerve (L4-S3),
  NOT the saphenous nerve. Despite both being on the "medial leg,"
  LR5 and SP6 access DIFFERENT neural pathways
- Spinal segments: L3-L4
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: minimal fascial complexity. The medial tibial surface
is subcutaneous — the tibia has no muscle covering on its medial
face. Skin → thin subcutaneous tissue → periosteum.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: great saphenous vein runs posteriorly to LR5 along the
medial leg. Avoid needling directly posteriorly into the vein.
Posterior tibial artery is deeper and more posterior (not directly
at LR5).
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → thin subcutaneous tissue
- Tibial periosteum (medial surface — no muscle covering)
- If needled posteriorly: soleus muscle, flexor digitorum longus
  (posterior compartment, accessed by angled insertion)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **No.** LR5 is not one of the Five Shu points. (LR4
Zhongfeng is the Jing-River/Metal point at the ankle.)

Luo-Connecting: **Yes.** Luo-Connecting point (络穴) of the Liver
channel. The Liver Luo vessel branches from LR5 and has two
pathways:
1. Descends to connect with the paired Gallbladder (GB) channel →
   reaches the dorsum of the foot
2. Ascends along the Liver channel pathway to the genitalia

Lingshu Chapter 10 (Jing Mai) describes the Liver Luo pathology:
- Excess: genital swelling, penile erection (priapism)
- Deficiency: genital itching

This ascending pathway to the genitalia is the classical basis for
LR5's role in genitourinary conditions — a more specific genital
connection than the Liver channel's main pathway, which also
encircles the external genitalia.

Host-Guest technique application:
- Liver as host → LR3 (Yuan) + GB37 (Luo of paired GB, Guangming)
- GB as host → GB40 (Yuan, Qiuxu) + LR5 (Luo of paired Liver)
LR5 is used as the guest Luo point when Gallbladder is the primary
affected channel — treating GB conditions via the Liver Luo vessel.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Yuan-Source: No (LR3)

Xi-Cleft: No (LR6)

Back-Shu: No (BL18 at T9)

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LR5 Ligou (蠡溝, "Woodworm Canal"). Luo-
Connecting of Liver. Primary functions: spreads Liver Qi, resolves
Damp-Heat in the lower jiao, benefits the genitalia, regulates
menstruation. The Luo vessel ascending to the genitalia provides
the specific rationale for genital disorders — hernia, genital
itching, vaginal discharge, testicular swelling. Combined with LR3
(Yuan) + REN-6 (Qihai) for hernia and testicular pain. Distinguished
from LR3 (which moves Qi broadly) — LR5 specifically targets the
Liver-genital connection via the Luo pathway.

**2. Trigger point:** Limited trigger point correspondence at the
medial tibial surface — the tibia has no muscle covering here.
The closest trigger point zone is the distal soleus muscle (posterior
compartment, accessed by oblique insertion), which refers pain to
the medial ankle and heel. Dorsher mapping does not identify a
strong correspondence at LR5's exact location.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender on palpation in periostitis of
the medial tibia (shin splints, medial tibial stress syndrome),
and in patients with varicose veins of the great saphenous vein
territory. The subcutaneous tibial surface is sensitive to
periosteal pressure.

**4. Marma point:** Indrabasti Marma (इन्द्रबस्ति) — located in the
middle of the calf (posterior aspect). Classified as Mamsa (muscle)
Marma, size: 0.5 anguli. LR5 on the MEDIAL tibial surface is
adjacent to but not exactly at Indrabasti (which is more posterior/
central on the calf). Weak correspondence — the Marma is in the
gastrocnemius/soleus, while LR5 is on the tibial bone.

**5. Head zone:** L3-L4 dermatome (saphenous nerve territory at
medial leg). Liver viscerotome = T7-T9. Same non-segmental gap as
LR8 and LR3. Genital innervation = S2-S4 (pudendal nerve) — also
non-segmental from L3-L4. LR5's genital effects require supraspinal
mediation whether directed at the liver OR the genitalia.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2, S4.4

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Back Line (SBL, Myers 2001) does
not pass through the medial tibial surface. The Deep Front Line
(DFL) passes more posteriorly through the deep posterior compartment.
LR5 on the subcutaneous tibial bone is not on a primary myofascial
line — it is between lines rather than on one.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** Not individually confirmed. L3-L4
dermatome at the medial leg was not examined in Kim et al. 2017. No
data on whether liver pathology generates neurogenic spots at the
medial tibial surface.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S4.3

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable. Subcutaneous tibial bone —
insufficient soft tissue for cupping seal.

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi). Lowest convergence on the
Liver channel so far. The medial tibial surface is a structurally
sparse location — subcutaneous bone with minimal soft tissue — which
limits cross-tradition overlap. This is consistent with the Atlas
pattern: points on bony prominences or subcutaneous bone (tibial
shaft, ulnar styloid) show fewer cross-tradition matches than
points at muscle bellies, joint creases, or fascial convergence zones.

1. Spinal cord convergence — L3-L4 dorsal horn interneurons

2. Supraspinal relay — somatosensory input at L3-L4 reaching

traditional pathways to distant anatomical targets with no known

nerves. LR5 = saphenous nerve (L3-L4, purely sensory, femoral nerve

sciatic nerve branch). Despite being only 2 cun apart vertically on
the medial leg, they access fundamentally different neural pathways
with different spinal segment ranges. SP6's posterior tibial nerve
actually HAS a segmental connection to the pelvic floor (S2-S4),
while LR5's saphenous nerve does NOT — yet both traditionally treat
genital/menstrual conditions.

describes a specific ascending pathway to the genitalia, but no

3. No specific mechanism — LR6 may function through non-specific

## LR14 — Kỳ Môn (期门, Qī Mén)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
Front-Mu point of the Liver (肝之募穴) — located on the Liver
channel itself. This is uncommon: of 12 Front-Mu points, only 3
are on the organ's own channel (LU1 for Lung, LR14 for Liver,
GB24 for Gallbladder). Most Front-Mu points are on the Conception
Vessel (CV) midline. LR14 is the terminal point of the Liver
channel — after LR14, the internal pathway ascends through the
diaphragm to the ribs, throat, eye system, and vertex. Intersection
point of Foot Jueyin (Liver), Foot Taiyin (Spleen), and Yin Wei
Mai. Part of the classical Liver Qi Stagnation formula (LR3 +
LR14 + PC6 + GB34) — B01 S3.2. Paired with BL18 (Liver Back-Shu,
T9) in the Shu-Mu combination for comprehensive Liver treatment.
Prominently featured in the Shanghan Lun for febrile disease
patterns involving Jueyin (terminal Yin) stage.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the chest, directly below
the nipple, in the 6th intercostal space, 4 cun lateral to the
anterior midline.

Anatomical landmarks: locate the nipple (typically at the 4th
intercostal space in males), count 2 intercostal spaces inferiorly
to the 6th intercostal space. In females, locate relative to the
inframammary fold (below the breast). 4 cun lateral to the midline
= mammillary line.

Right side: overlies the dome of the liver (right lobe).
Left side: overlies the fundus of the stomach.

Needle depth range: 3–8 mm MAXIMUM. Oblique or transverse insertion
ONLY, directed along the intercostal space. NEVER perpendicular
deep insertion. In thin patients, the pleural cavity may be only
10–20 mm from the skin surface.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **6th intercostal nerve (T6)** — anterior cutaneous branch
  (medial) and lateral cutaneous branch. This is the primary
  sensory innervation at the point.
- T6 segment falls within the broader hepatic visceral afferent
  range (T5-T9), though the core liver innervation is T7-T9.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A07 S1.2

Fascial plane: multiple layers in the intercostal space —
- Superficial: skin → subcutaneous fat → pectoralis major (lateral
  fibers)
- Intercostal layers: external intercostal → internal intercostal
  → innermost intercostal / transversus thoracis
- Deep: endothoracic fascia → parietal pleura → pleural cavity
The total soft tissue depth from skin to pleura varies by body
habitus: ~10-20 mm in thin individuals, ~20-40 mm in normal
habitus, >40 mm in obese individuals.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: 6th intercostal artery and vein — run along the inferior
margin of the 6th rib in the costal groove. Needle insertion at the
SUPERIOR margin of the 7th rib avoids the neurovascular bundle.

Muscle layer (superficial → deep): skin → subcutaneous tissue →
pectoralis major (inferolateral fibers) → external intercostal
muscle → internal intercostal muscle → innermost intercostal /
transversus thoracis → endothoracic fascia → parietal pleura →
pleural cavity. Beyond: lung (if inspiratory excursion reaches this
level) or diaphragm → liver (right side) / stomach (left side).

→ Cross-ref: A01, A03, A05

**Ashi correspondence:** Intercostal tenderness in hepatobiliary
disease is a standard clinical finding. Right subcostal and lower
chest wall tenderness is assessed in Murphy's sign (gallbladder)

**Marma point:** Stanamula Marma (base of breast region) — a Mamsa
(muscle) Marma in the anterior chest. Regional correspondence but

segments within it.

**Neurogenic spot:** No specific data for 6th intercostal space.

is below the cardiac focus and above the gastric focus.

**Cupping point:** Technically applicable but DANGEROUS at the 6th
intercostal space. Cupping creates negative pressure that could
theoretically exacerbate intercostal distortion in thin patients.
Not a standard cupping site. If applied: dry cupping only, light
suction, brief duration.

**Convergence score: 5/8** — TCM ✓, Trigger Point ✓, Ashi ✓,
Marma ~ (regional), Head Zone ✓, Anatomy Trains ✓, Neurogenic ✗,
Cupping ~ (applicable but dangerous). Same score as LR13.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

Systematic review / Meta-analysis: LR14 appears as a component
point in acupuncture for liver cirrhosis (Qi et al. 2020 meta-
analysis, Gastroenterology Research and Practice). The meta-analysis
found acupuncture beneficial as adjunctive therapy for cirrhosis
but cannot isolate LR14's individual contribution from multi-point
protocols.

RCT data: no RCT specifically examining LR14 as sole intervention.

Preclinical: Lin et al. 2014 (BMC Complementary and Alternative
Medicine) — preconditioning somatothermal stimulation on right
LR14 reduced hepatic ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury in rats
(n=18, 3 groups of 6). Somatothermal stimulation on right LR14 12 hours before hepatic
ischemia reduced markers of liver injury. This is the only point-
specific preclinical study at LR14. Key finding: thermal stimulation
at the Front-Mu point of the Liver produced hepatoprotective
effects — consistent with a somatovisceral reflex mechanism
through T6 intercostal afferents modulating hepatic sympathetic
outflow.

Sham comparison: the Lin 2014 study used untreated controls and
I/R-only controls, not sham-point controls. Point-specificity not
tested.

Confidence:
- Hepatoprotective effect (I/R): 🟡 Plausible (single preclinical
  study with mechanistically consistent results, but no replication
  and no sham control)
- Liver Qi Stagnation symptoms: 🔴 Unvalidated (clinical tradition
  only, included in meta-analysis protocols but not isolated)
- Hepatitis/jaundice: 🔴 Unvalidated
- Intercostal neuralgia: 🔴 Unvalidated (local mechanism plausible
  but no data)

### Cơ chế giải thích

**Somatovisceral reflex — T6 intercostal pathway:**
LR14 at the 6th intercostal space → T6 intercostal nerve afferents
→ dorsal horn at T6 → sympathetic preganglionic neurons → greater
splanchnic nerve → celiac ganglia → hepatic plexus. The T6 segment
is at the UPPER range of hepatic visceral innervation (core: T7-T9,
extended: T5-T9). This provides a plausible segmental pathway for
somatovisceral effects on the liver.

**Shu-Mu pair — segmental offset:**
BL18 (T9) + LR14 (T6) = 3-segment offset. Compare with the Spleen
Shu-Mu pair: BL20 (T11) + LR13 (T10-T11) = precise overlap at
T11. The Liver Shu-Mu pair has WIDER segmental separation than
the Spleen pair:
- Spleen: BL20 (T11) ↔ LR13 (T10-T11) = 0-1 segment offset
- Liver: BL18 (T9) ↔ LR14 (T6) = 3 segment offset

Both Front-Mu points (LR13, LR14) fall WITHIN their respective
organ's visceral afferent range, but the tightness of segmental
alignment varies. This suggests the Shu-Mu system is not
uniformly precise in segmental matching — some pairs are tighter
than others.

**Lin 2014 — hepatoprotective mechanism:**
Somatothermal stimulation at LR14 → T6 intercostal thermoreceptors
→ afferent signaling → spinal cord → descending modulation →
hepatic sympathetic tone adjustment → reduced oxidative stress
and inflammatory response during I/R. The study supports the
somatovisceral reflex model: skin-level thermal stimulation at
a dermatomal point corresponding to the liver produces organ-
level protective effects. This parallels the Kim 2012 finding at
LR8 (organ-specific hepatic blood flow increase) — both suggest
liver-specific effects mediated through liver-relevant neural
pathways at different points on the same channel.

**Neural zone transition — the Liver channel's biggest jump:**
LR13-14 are innervated by T6-T11 intercostal nerves. The previous
Liver points (LR5-8) were innervated by saphenous nerve (L3-L4).
This is a gap of 8+ spinal segments (L3 → T11 = 8 segments; L4
→ T6 = 10 segments). The Liver channel traverses the LARGEST
spinal segment range of any channel documented so far:
- Foot: L4-L5 (deep peroneal)
- Medial leg: L3-L4 (saphenous)
- Trunk: T6-T11 (intercostal)
- Total span: T6 → L5 = ~11 segments

Compare: HT = C5-T1 (~5 segments), PC = C5-T1 (~5 segments).
The Liver channel spans more than TWICE the segmental range.
The channel pathway through the inguinal region (LR9-12, L2-L4,
obturator/femoral) partially bridges the gap, but there remains
no continuous neural pathway connecting LR8 (L3-L4) to LR13
(T10-T11). The "channel" crosses neural territories that have no
anatomical connection at the peripheral nerve level.

**Gap:** The Lin 2014 study needs replication with sham-point
controls. The 3-segment Shu-Mu offset (T6 vs T9) is wider than
ideal — does the broader T5-T9 hepatic range compensate?

### An toàn

**Pneumothorax risk: HIGH.** This is the primary safety concern.
The 6th intercostal space overlies the pleural cavity. In thin
patients, the lung/pleura may be only 10-20 mm from the skin
surface. Pneumothorax is a DOCUMENTED complication of intercostal
acupuncture — 90+ incidents reported in global literature across
all intercostal points.

Strict rules:
- NEVER perpendicular insertion
- NEVER exceed 0.8 cun (approximately 16 mm) depth
- OBLIQUE or TRANSVERSE insertion along the intercostal space ONLY
- In thin/cachectic patients: reduce to 3-5 mm maximum
- If in doubt about depth: use acupressure instead of needling

Organ injury risk:
- RIGHT side: liver dome directly deep. Hepatic puncture possible
  with excessively deep perpendicular insertion. Presents as right
  upper quadrant pain, potential hemorrhage.
- LEFT side: stomach fundus. Gastric perforation theoretically
  possible but extremely rare.

Vascular risk: 6th intercostal artery and vein in the costal
groove. Needle at the SUPERIOR border of the lower rib to avoid
the neurovascular bundle. Intercostal artery laceration can cause
hemothorax.

Neural risk: 6th intercostal nerve. Direct contact causes sharp
pain along the dermatome. Not dangerous but painful — indicates
incorrect needle placement.

Pregnancy: no specific classical contraindication, but the chest
wall point with pneumothorax risk warrants conservative approach.

Overall safety: **HIGH complication risk** — the highest-risk
point on the Liver channel. Pneumothorax, hepatic injury (right),
gastric injury (left), intercostal vessel damage. Requires trained
practitioner, precise technique, and appropriate body habitus
assessment before needling.

## LR4 — Trung Phong (中封, Zhōng Fēng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Central Seal" — Jing-River (Metal) point of the Liver channel and
the **Ke cycle point** (Metal controls Wood — the restraining
element). Located anterior to the medial malleolus, medial to the
tibialis anterior tendon. Completes the Five Shu set for the Liver
channel — the third complete set in the Atlas after Pericardium and
Heart.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): anterior to the medial malleolus,
midway between SP5 (Shangqiu) and ST41 (Jiexi), in the depression
on the medial side of the tendon of tibialis anterior.

Anatomical landmarks: dorsiflex the foot to visualize the tibialis
anterior tendon. LR4 is in the depression medial to this tendon,
approximately 1 cun anterior to the tip of the medial malleolus.
Nearby: SP5 (Shangqiu) is posterior-inferior to medial malleolus
(~0.5-1 cun from LR4), KI3 (Taixi) is posterior to medial
malleolus. Three Yin ankle points on different sides of the same
bony prominence.

Needle depth: 3–6 mm perpendicular, or oblique 5–10 mm toward
SP5 or ST41.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Saphenous nerve** (L3-L4) — branch of femoral nerve, primary
  cutaneous nerve at the anteromedial ankle
- **Medial dorsal cutaneous nerve of foot** (L4-L5-S1) — terminal
  branch of the superficial peroneal nerve
- NOT deep peroneal nerve at this location — the deep peroneal
  runs deeper and more laterally under the extensor retinaculum
- LR4 is the TRANSITION point where the channel shifts from deep
  peroneal territory (foot dorsum, LR1-3) to saphenous territory
  (medial leg, LR5-8), with superficial peroneal as bridge
- Dermatome: L4
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Fascial plane: deep to the superior extensor retinaculum, in the
groove between the tibialis anterior tendon (lateral) and medial
malleolus (medial). Extensor hallucis longus tendon nearby.
→ Cross-ref: A03

Vascular: anterior medial malleolar artery (branch of anterior
tibial artery). Dorsal venous network of foot superficially. Great
saphenous vein in the area.
→ Cross-ref: A04

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue (saphenous nerve, dorsal
venous network) → superior extensor retinaculum → tibialis anterior
tendon sheath. At standard depth (3-6 mm), needle reaches tendon/
retinacular level.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Jing-River (Metal)** — 4th of the Five Shu on the
Liver channel. Nan Jing Difficulty 64: Yin channels assign Metal to
Jing-River points.

**Ke cycle significance:** Metal on Wood channel = Metal CONTROLS
Wood in the restraining cycle. Metal's nature: descending,
contracting, clarifying. Wood's nature: ascending, expanding,
spreading. LR4 provides a built-in "brake" — the channel's own
internal restraining mechanism. Name "Central Seal" (中封) suggests
containment — Metal constraining Wood's spreading tendency.

Nan Jing citations:
- Ch. 68: "经主喘咳寒热" — Jing-River points treat cough, dyspnea,
  alternating chills/fever. Metal element gives LR4 a bridge to
  respiratory symptoms (especially "Liver invading Lung" patterns).
- Ch. 74: "秋刺经" — needle Jing-River points in autumn (Metal
  season).

Sa-Am acupuncture (Korean, ~1644 CE): LR4 is sedated in BOTH
Liver tonification AND sedation four-needle protocols. In
tonification: LR4 + LU8 sedated to prevent over-nourishment. In
sedation: LR4 + LU8 sedated to directly restrain excess. Always
plays the restraining role — appearing in every Liver protocol.

Five Shu completion for Liver:
LR1 Well/Wood → LR2 Spring/Fire → LR3 Stream/Earth → **LR4
River/Metal** → LR8 Sea/Water = COMPLETE SET.
Third complete Five Shu in Atlas (after PC and HT).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Yuan-Source: No (LR3)
Luo-Connecting: No (LR5)
Xi-Cleft: No (LR6)
Special: none. First recorded in Ling Shu Chapter 2 (本输).

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LR4 Zhongfeng. Jing-River/Metal of Liver.

→ Cross-ref: B11

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in anterior ankle sprains,

**4. Marma point:** Gulpha Marma (गुल्फ, "ankle") — Sandhi (joint)
Marma, size 2 anguli, covering the ankle circumference. LR4 falls
within its anterior-medial sector. Shared indications: Gulpha

LR4's classical genital/lower limb functions.

**5. Head zone:** L4 dermatome. No direct viscerotome overlap with
Liver or genitourinary organs.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Front Line (SFL, Myers) —

medial to the TA tendon at the ankle retinacular station. The Liver
channel's medial leg trajectory aligns more with the Deep Front
Line (DFL), which passes posterior to the medial malleolus.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** Not confirmed at ankle.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Anatomy
Trains partial). Gulpha Marma partial. Moderate convergence — higher
than toe tip (LR1 = 2/8) but lower than dorsal foot (LR3 = 5/8).

Nguồn cổ:
- Ling Shu Ch. 2 (Ben Shu): first recorded.

  exhalations, 3 moxa cones.
- Yizong Jinjian: nocturnal emission, genital retraction, urinary
  dysfunction, abdominal distension.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Spreads Liver Qi — hypochondriac pain, sighing, depression,

- Clears damp-heat from Lower Jiao — urinary retention, difficult

- Treats shan disorder — hernia, lower abdominal pain, testicular
  conditions
- Benefits the sinews — ankle and foot pain, difficulty walking

Classical combinations:
- LR4 + GB34 + LR3 + ST44: jaundice
- LR4 + LR3 + ST36: difficulty walking
- LR4 + LR10 + KI14: acute infectious hepatitis

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for LR4 specifically. LR4 appears in

## LR7 — Tất Quan (膝关, Xī Guān)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Knee Gate" — located on the medial aspect of the knee, in the
upper portion of the medial head of gastrocnemius, 1 cun posterior

(Ququan) is approximately 1 cun superior, at the medial end of the

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No — LR7 is located between ankle (Jing-River) and knee

## LR10 — Túc Ngũ Lý (足五里, Zú Wǔ Lǐ)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Foot Five Li" — located on the medial thigh, 3 cun below ST30
(Qichong), on the lateral border of the adductor longus. No
special point category designation. Shares the 五里 (Five Li)
naming pattern with LI13 (手五里, Arm Five Li) on the upper arm —
both are near major vessels, both carry classical needling
prohibition. The Ling Shu Chapter 60 lists 五里 among the "Great

LR10 receives an extended prohibition by name association and
genuine vascular danger.

of the pubic symphysis, 2 cun lateral to midline. Measure 3 cun
inferiorly along the medial thigh. The point is on the lateral
(anterior) border of the adductor longus — palpable when the
patient abducts against resistance. LR11 (Yinlian) is 1 cun
superior (2 cun below ST30). LR12 (Jimai) is in the inguinal
crease above.

most prominent on the medial surface is the adductor longus. The
point lies just anterior to it.

Intersection point: No.

Special: **Classical needling prohibition/restriction.**

Ling Shu Chapter 60 (玉版, "Jade Inscription"): lists 五里 among
the "Great Prohibitions" (大禁) — "气尽而死" (Qi exhausted, then
death). The primary reference is to LI13 (手五里); LR10 (足五里)
receives extended prohibition by name association.

Jiayi Jing (282 CE): formally lists 手五里 (LI13) among 17
forbidden acupuncture points — "五里在肘上三寸，大脉中，禁不可刺"
(Wuli is 3 cun above the elbow, in the middle of the great
vessel, forbidden to needle). LR10 is not separately listed but
the shared naming convention implies shared prohibition.

Modern status: the prohibition is NOT strictly observed. Shallow
needling (5–15 mm) is practiced with extreme caution. Moxibustion
is generally preferred over needling.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LR10 Zuwuli. Functions: clears Damp-Heat
from the Lower Jiao, regulates the Lower Jiao, relaxes sinews.

**2. Trigger point:** No corresponding trigger point mapped by
Dorsher. LR10 is among a small set of classical acupoints with
no corresponding clinical pain indication in the trigger point
literature.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Not typically palpated due to
proximity to femoral vessels.

**4. Marma point:** Between Urvi Marma (mid-thigh) and Lohitaksha
Marma (groin) — no precise Marma at this location.

**5. Head zone:** L2 dermatome. Kidney/ureter referred pain zone
(T10-L1) is adjacent — partial overlap with urogenital
indications.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Line (DFL, Myers). The adductor
compartment at the proximal thigh is a core DFL component. At
this level, the DFL is transitioning toward the pelvic floor
and psoas complex.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable — vascular risk.

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Anatomy Trains). Lowest
convergence of the Liver thigh group. No trigger point, no Ashi,
no Marma. Consistent with clinically prohibited/restricted status.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Despite the classical prohibition, traditional sources document
indications:

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: lower abdominal distension and pain, retention of
  urine, testicular swelling and pain, scrofula
  (lymphadenopathy), excessive sleepiness.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Clears Damp-Heat from the Lower Jiao — urinary retention,
  genital eczema and itching, excessive vaginal discharge
- Regulates the Lower Jiao — lower abdominal pain, uterine
  prolapse
- Local: medial thigh pain

Classical combinations:
- LR10 + LR11 (Yinlian): female infertility
- LR10 + LR12 (Jimai) + CV3 (Zhongji): urogenital disorders
- LR4 + LR10 + KI14: acute infectious hepatitis

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: rarely used. Clinicians report using it
primarily to supplement other points on the channel, not as a
primary point.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No clinical trials exist for LR10. The least studied point on
the Liver channel alongside LR11.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**HIGH risk — one of the highest-risk acupuncture points in the
lower limb.**

Classical prohibition: Ling Shu Ch. 60 — Great Prohibitions.
Modern practice does not strictly observe but maintains extreme
caution.

Vascular risk:
- Femoral artery and vein ANTERIOR to the point — relatively
  superficial at this level
- Great saphenous vein superficially
- Direct puncture of femoral artery/vein: hemorrhage, hematoma,
  pseudoaneurysm

Mandatory precautions:
- ALWAYS palpate for arterial pulsation before insertion
- Insert on the lateral border of adductor longus, angling AWAY
  from femoral vessels
- Shallow insertion ONLY (5–15 mm maximum)
- Moxibustion generally preferred over needling
- Deep needling CONTRAINDICATED

## LR11 — Âm Liêm (阴廉, Yīn Lián)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Yin Corner" — located on the medial thigh, 2 cun below ST30
(Qichong), on the lateral border of the adductor longus. No
special point category designation. One cun superior to LR10,
positioned just distal to the apex of the femoral triangle.
Functionally described as regulating the Chong and Ren vessels —
a functional rather than formal intersection point classification.
Primarily a gynecological point with specific moxa recommendation
for infertility.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the medial aspect of the
thigh, 2 cun directly below ST30 (Qichong), on the lateral
border of the adductor longus muscle.

Anatomical landmarks: locate ST30 (Qichong) at the upper border
of the pubic symphysis, 2 cun lateral to midline. ST30 sits
essentially at the inguinal crease. Measure 2 cun inferiorly
along the medial thigh. The point is on the lateral (anterior)
border of the adductor longus. LR12 (Jimai) is in the inguinal
crease, 2 cun superior. LR10 (Zuwuli) is 1 cun inferior.

Needle depth: 10–30 mm perpendicular or oblique.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Anterior branch of the obturator nerve** (L2-L4) — primary
  deep nerve, innervates adductor longus
- **Medial cutaneous branch of the femoral nerve** (L2-L3) —
  superficial sensory innervation
- **Genitofemoral nerve** (L1-L2) — femoral branch provides
  sensation to upper medial thigh
- **Ilioinguinal nerve** (L1) — contributes to superficial groin
  area sensation
- Dermatome: L1-L2
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular: medial circumflex femoral artery and vein (branch of
profunda femoris artery) pass through this region. Femoral artery
and vein run LATERAL to the point at this level, partially
protected by the adductor longus muscle. Great saphenous vein
superficially.
→ Cross-ref: A04

Muscle layers: skin → subcutaneous tissue (medial femoral
cutaneous nerve, genitofemoral femoral branch) → fascia lata →
lateral border of **adductor longus** (obturator nerve L2-L4) →
**pectineus** (femoral nerve L2-L3). Deep: femoral vessels
(lateral).

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No.
Yuan-Source: No (LR3)
Luo-Connecting: No (LR5)
Xi-Cleft: No (LR6)
Intersection point: No — despite the functional description of
"regulating the Chong and Ren vessels," LR11 is NOT formally
listed as an intersection point for either extraordinary vessel.
Special: none.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LR11 Yinlian. Functions: regulates the Chong
and Ren vessels, benefits the uterus, resolves Damp-Heat from the
Lower Jiao, relaxes sinews.

**2. Trigger point:** Adductor longus TrPs are anatomically close.
Referred pain pattern: deep groin, anteromedial thigh, inner knee.
Partial correspondence for groin/thigh pain indications.
→ Cross-ref: B11

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in adductor strain, groin pain,
hip adductor overuse.

**4. Marma point:** Lohitaksha Marma (लोहिताक्ष) — Sira (blood
vessel) Marma at the root of the thigh, 1/2 angula size.
Approximate correspondence — Lohitaksha maps more precisely to
LR12 at the inguinal crease. LR11 at 2 cun below is slightly
distal but within the broader zone.

**5. Head zone:** L1-L2 dermatome. Kidney/ureter referred pain
zone (T10-L1) partially overlaps. L1 inguinal zone approaches
the genitourinary viscerotome.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Line (DFL, Myers). The adductor
longus is a core DFL component. The Liver sinew channel (Jing
Jin) parallels the DFL path through the adductors, pectineus,
and psoas.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Ashi, Anatomy Trains). Trigger
point partial. Lohitaksha Marma approximate.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: disorders of menstruation, leukorrhea (vaginal
  discharge).

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Regulates Chong and Ren vessels — irregular menstruation,
  leukorrhea, female infertility
- Benefits the uterus — lower abdominal pain
- Local: medial thigh and leg pain

Classical combinations:
- LR11 + LR10 (Zuwuli): female infertility
- LR11 + ST30 + REN2 + BL32 + SP6 + LR3: Damp-Heat disorders
  and infertility
- LR11 + REN4 + SP6: gynecological indications

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng: moxibustion at LR11 specifically
recommended for infertility. The area between LR9-LR11 may be
reactive (tender on palpation) in women with fertility problems.
Moxa generally preferred over needling.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for LR11 as an individual point.
The least studied Liver channel point alongside LR10.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

MODERATE risk. Femoral vessels are LATERAL to the point at this
level, partially protected by the adductor longus muscle — safer
than LR10 (where vessels are more anterior) and LR12 (where
vessels are immediately adjacent).

Precautions:
- Avoid needling laterally — femoral vein passes nearby lateral
  to adductor longus
- Great saphenous vein superficially — avoid prominent veins
- Medial circumflex femoral artery in the region
- Moxibustion preferred over needling for most indications
- Pregnancy caution advised

## LR12 — Cấp Mạch (急脉, Jí Mài)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Urgent Pulse" — located in the inguinal groove, 2.5 cun lateral
to the anterior midline, medial to the femoral vein where the
femoral artery pulsation is palpable. No special point category
designation. The name is unique among all 361+ standard
acupoints — the only point named directly after a palpable
arterial pulsation (急 = urgent/rapid, 脉 = pulse/vessel). The
name simultaneously serves as a location guide ("find the urgent
pulse") and a safety warning (major vessel immediately adjacent).
Su Wen classically recommends moxibustion only, no needling — a
recommendation that modern practice has reversed.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): in the inguinal region, 2.5
cun lateral to the anterior midline (lateral to CV2 Qugu), at
the inguinal groove, medial to the femoral vein, where the
pulsation of the femoral artery is palpable.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the femoral artery pulse in the
inguinal crease — the strong pulsation is the primary landmark
(the "urgent pulse" of the name). The point is MEDIAL to the
pulsation. Nearby: CV2 (Qugu) is on the midline at the upper
border of the pubic symphysis. ST30 (Qichong) is 2 cun lateral
to CV2 — LR12 is just lateral and slightly inferior to ST30.
SP12 (Chongmen) is 3.5 cun lateral to CV2, on the LATERAL side
of the femoral artery.

Arrangement from lateral to medial at the inguinal crease:
femoral Nerve → femoral Artery → femoral Vein → **LR12**. SP12
(Spleen) is lateral to the nerve/artery; LR12 (Liver) is medial
to the vein — two different channels flanking the same vessels.

Needle depth: 5–10 mm oblique insertion directed MEDIALLY (away
from vessels). Shallow only.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- **Ilioinguinal nerve** (L1) — traverses the inguinal canal,
  emerges from external ring, sensory to inguinal region
- **Genitofemoral nerve** (L1-L2) — genital branch through
  inguinal canal alongside spermatic cord; femoral branch to
  skin over femoral triangle
- **Anterior femoral cutaneous nerve** (L2-L3) — cutaneous
  sensation to anterior/medial upper thigh
- Dermatome: L1 — the most proximal Liver channel point on the
  lower limb. Closest to the liver viscerotome (T7-T9) of any
  lower limb Liver point, but still a 6–7 segment non-segmental
  gap.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular: **femoral artery immediately lateral** — strong
pulsation palpable. **Femoral vein between the artery and the
point.** Medial circumflex femoral artery (branch of profunda
femoris) passes through the region. **VASCULAR RISK: HIGHEST ON
THE LIVER CHANNEL.**
→ Cross-ref: A04

Tissue layers: thin at the inguinal crease. Skin → subcutaneous
fat (Camper's and Scarpa's fascia) → external oblique aponeurosis
/ inguinal ligament. Pectineus muscle deep and slightly lateral.
Spermatic cord (males) or round ligament (females) emerges from
the nearby external inguinal ring — anatomically consistent with
classical hernia indications.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No.
Yuan-Source: No (LR3)
Luo-Connecting: No (LR5)
Xi-Cleft: No (LR6)
Intersection point: No — LR12 is NOT a formal intersection point
with Yin Wei Mai or Chong Mai. LR14 is the Liver channel's
intersection with Yin Wei Mai. ST30 (nearby) is a Chong Mai
intersection, but LR12 is not.
Special: none. Classical moxibustion-preferred point (Su Wen).

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** LR12 Jimai. Functions: disperses Cold from
the Liver channel, benefits the Lower Jiao, treats shan disorder
(hernia).

**2. Trigger point:** No Dorsher mapping for the inguinal crease.
Not a standard trigger point zone in Travell & Simons. Functionally
related: iliopsoas TrP (refers to groin), pectineus TrP (pain
below inguinal ligament).

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in inguinal hernia, groin
strain, adductor tendinopathy at the pubic insertion.

**4. Marma point:** **Lohitaksha Marma** (लोहिताक्ष) — the
strongest Marma correspondence for any Liver thigh point. Sira
(blood vessel) Marma at the root of the thigh, 1/2 angula size.
Vaikalyakara (deformity-causing if injured). Anatomical substrate:
femoral artery/vein origin zone. Both Lohitaksha and LR12 are
defined by the palpable femoral arterial pulse. Both are
vascular-dominant. Both carry HIGH risk — Lohitaksha can cause
"death from blood loss" (Sushruta Samhita). One of the strongest
Marma-acupoint correspondences in the Atlas.

**5. Head zone:** L1 dermatome. Kidney/ureter referred zone
(T10-L1) overlaps. The inguinal L1 zone approaches the
genitourinary viscerotome.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Line (DFL, Myers). LR12 sits
at the critical DFL transition zone where the lower limb component
(adductors → pectineus) merges with the trunk component (psoas →
iliacus). The conjoined tendon of adductors and pectineus crosses
the front of the pelvis at this level. One of the DFL's most
important structural junctions.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Contraindicated — vascular risk.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Anatomy Trains, Marma).
Lohitaksha is a strong correspondence — both at the femoral
pulse, both vascular-classified. Ashi partial.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Ling Shu: the Liver channel "enters the pubic hair region,
  encircles the genitalia, reaches the lower abdomen" — LR12
  sits at this transition point where the channel enters the
  trunk from the lower limb.
- Jiayi Jing: hernia (shan qi), genital pain, lower abdominal
  disorders.
- Su Wen: recommends moxibustion rather than needling — one of
  the classical needling contraindications.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Disperses Cold from the Liver channel — hernia (primary
  classical indication), genital pain
- Benefits the Lower Jiao — penile/vulvar pain, testicular
  swelling, impotence
- Treats shan disorder — inguinal hernia anatomically consistent
  (proximity to inguinal canal and external ring)
- Local: inner thigh pain, vulvar itching

Classical combinations:
- LR12 + LR1 (Dadun): hernia, uterine prolapse, penile pain,
  impotence
- LR12 + REN3 (Zhongji) + ST30 (Qichong): warms Lower Jiao,
  disperses Cold from Liver channel (Cold-type hernia)
- LR12 + LR10 (Zuwuli) + CV3: urogenital disorders

Classical vs modern reversal:
- **Classical (Su Wen): moxibustion YES, needling NO** — feared
  vessel puncture from needles
- **Modern: needling YES (oblique, shallow), moxibustion NO** —
  fire risk near pubic hair, heat over femoral vessels
- The only point in the Atlas where classical and modern safety
  recommendations are completely opposite

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review, RCT, or case series for LR12. Complete
evidence desert.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**HIGH risk — HIGHEST vascular risk point on the Liver channel.**

Femoral vessel proximity: the femoral artery is immediately
lateral (strong pulsation palpable). The femoral vein lies between
the artery and the point. Accidental puncture: hemorrhage,
hematoma, arteriovenous fistula, pseudoaneurysm.

Classical contraindication: Su Wen — moxibustion only, no needling.

Modern needling technique (for practitioners who choose to needle):
1. Patient supine with legs slightly abducted
2. ALWAYS palpate femoral artery pulse FIRST — this is the safety
   landmark (the "urgent pulse" of the name)
3. Needle MEDIAL to the pulsation, at least 1 fingerbreadth from
   the artery
4. Oblique insertion directed MEDIALLY (away from vessels)
5. Depth: 5–10 mm maximum
6. NEVER needle deeply or laterally

Moxibustion: modern sources generally contraindicate (fire risk
near pubic hair, heat over femoral vessels) — reversing the
classical recommendation.

Additional contraindications:
- Deep needling CONTRAINDICATED
- Cupping CONTRAINDICATED
- Gua sha CONTRAINDICATED
- Pregnancy caution
