## SP6 — Tam Âm Giao (三阴交, Sān Yīn Jiāo)

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**
The "Three Yin Crossing" — meeting point of all three leg Yin meridians
(Spleen, Liver, Kidney). One of the 3-4 most frequently prescribed
acupoints in modern clinical practice worldwide. NOT a member of any
standard point category (not Five Shu, not Yuan-Source, not Eight
Confluent) yet clinically one of the most important points in TCM. The
point where PTNS (posterior tibial nerve stimulation) — a modern
urological second-line treatment for overactive bladder — independently
converges with traditional acupuncture location. Primary gynecological
point across all classical sources. One of the "forbidden points" in
pregnancy alongside LI4 (see evidence below). Uniquely, the ONLY known
point where acupressure OUTPERFORMS acupuncture for a specific condition
(dysmenorrhea — C02 §3.3).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the medial side of the lower leg,
3 cun (approximately 4 finger-breadths) above the prominence of the
medial malleolus, on the posterior border of the medial aspect of the
tibia.

Anatomical landmarks: in the depression posterior to the medial border of
the tibia, at the transition zone where the tibialis posterior muscle
emerges from under the flexor digitorum longus to become the most medial
deep muscle. Palpation: slide four fingers above the medial malleolus
along the posterior tibial border — the point is in the muscular
depression between the tibia and the calf muscles.

Needle depth range: 15-25 mm perpendicular insertion, directed slightly
posteriorly toward the tibial nerve. Deeper insertion (25-40 mm) reaches
tibialis posterior muscle.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Superficial: saphenous nerve (L3-L4) — sensory branch of the femoral
  nerve, providing cutaneous innervation to the medial leg and ankle.
  This is the primary nerve reached by superficial needling.
- Deep: posterior tibial nerve (L4-S3) — major branch of the sciatic
  nerve, running deep between flexor digitorum longus and tibialis
  posterior. Carries both motor and sensory fibers. This nerve also
  innervates the pelvic floor and lower urogenital region via sacral
  segments S2-S4.
- Spinal segments: L3-L4 (saphenous, superficial sensory); L4-S3
  (tibial, deep motor/sensory — critically, shares S2-S4 with pelvic
  parasympathetic supply)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: within the crural fascia of the medial lower leg. A03
identifies this region as PROKR2-rich fascial tissue (A03 S3.4 table:
"crural fascia — contains acupoints ST36, SP6. PROKR2-rich"). The
crural fascia at SP6 represents a convergence of fascial planes from
three muscular compartments.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: posterior tibial artery (deep) — runs alongside the tibial
nerve in the deep compartment. Great saphenous vein (superficial) —
major superficial vein of the medial leg, crosses near SP6.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue → crural fascia
- Flexor digitorum longus (primary, superficial deep compartment)
- Tibialis posterior (deep — part of the Deep Front Line, Myers)
- Soleus (posterior, deep to gastrocnemius)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **No.** SP6 is not a Five Shu point. The Five Shu of the
Spleen meridian are: SP1 (Jing-Well/Wood), SP2 (Ying-Spring/Fire), SP3
(Shu-Stream/Earth), SP5 (Jing-River/Metal), SP9 (He-Sea/Water).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

Yuan-Source: No (SP3 Taibai is Yuan-Source of Spleen)

Luo-Connecting: No (SP4 Gongsun is Luo-Connecting of Spleen)

Xi-Cleft: No (SP8 Diji is Xi-Cleft of Spleen)

Back-Shu: No (BL20 Pishu is Back-Shu of Spleen)

Front-Mu: No (LR13 Zhangmen is Front-Mu of Spleen)

Eight Confluent: No

Hui-Meeting: No

Special designations:
- **Crossing Point (交会穴) of Three Leg Yin Meridians** — this is
  SP6's defining identity. The three channels that cross at this location:
  1. Spleen (Foot Taiyin, 足太阴) — the host meridian
  2. Liver (Foot Jueyin, 足厥阴)
  3. Kidney (Foot Shaoyin, 足少阴)

  The crossing point concept (交会穴) is distinct from the 8 standard
  point categories. Crossing points are locations where two or more
  meridians intersect — the point "belongs" to one channel but is
  influenced by all channels that pass through it. SP6 is the most
  important crossing point because:
  - It crosses THREE meridians (most crossing points connect only two)
  - The three meridians represent the three Yin organ systems most
    associated with blood, reproduction, and fluid metabolism
  - Traditional theory: stimulating SP6 simultaneously accesses Spleen
    (blood production, digestion), Liver (blood storage, Qi flow,
    menstruation), and Kidney (reproduction, constitutional essence)
  - Anatomical correlate: the three meridian trajectories on the medial
    leg DO converge near SP6 — the Spleen, Liver, and Kidney channels
    all pass through the medial leg, and their pathways approach a
    common zone above the medial malleolus

  This is DATA — recording the traditional classification. Whether the
  "crossing" carries mechanistic significance beyond the anatomical
  convergence of fascial planes and nerve territories is unresolved.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Cross-tradition mapping

**TCM acupoint:** SP6 Sanyinjiao (三阴交). Crossing point of three Yin
meridians. Primary function: tonifies Spleen and Stomach, resolves
dampness; promotes smooth flow of Liver Qi, regulates menstruation;
tonifies Kidney, nourishes Yin and Blood; promotes labor; calms the
Mind (Shen); benefits urination. One of the 3-4 most prescribed
acupoints across all TCM clinical practice worldwide.

**Trigger point:** Flexor digitorum longus trigger point (Dorsher 2008
correspondence mapping). This trigger point produces referred pain to
the medial lower leg and plantar surface of the foot, overlapping with
the Spleen and Kidney meridian trajectories on the medial leg. The
tibialis posterior trigger point (deeper layer) refers pain to the
Achilles tendon area and medial ankle. The 93.3% acupoint-trigger point
overlap (Dorsher 2008) includes this region. Note: trigger point
referred pain addresses local musculoskeletal conditions, not the
distant gynecological effects — cross-tradition convergence confirms
location significance, not therapeutic equivalence.
→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

**Ashi correspondence:** Commonly tender on palpation in dysmenorrhea
patients. Medial leg tenderness during menstruation is a well-documented
clinical finding. This aligns with the dynamic acupoint phenomenon
(AP-00 §1.2): disease-state sensitization at acupoints associated with
the affected organ system.

**Marma point:** **Gulpha** (गुल्फ) — ankle Marma point in the
Ayurvedic 107-point system (Sushruta Samhita). Located on the medial
ankle region, anatomical proximity to SP6 within 2-3 cm. Classified as
a Snayu (ligament/tendon) Marma. Governs joints, reproductive system,
and urinary function in Ayurvedic theory — functional correspondence
with SP6's gynecological and urological applications. Alternative
correspondence: **Indrabasti** (calf midpoint Marma) — slightly
proximal to SP6 but in the same functional zone.

**Head zone:** L4 dermatome on medial lower leg (Beissner 2011). Uterine
visceral innervation: sympathetic via T10-L1 (superior hypogastric
plexus); parasympathetic via S2-S4 (pelvic splanchnic nerves). The
tibial nerve at SP6 (L4-S3) shares spinal segments S2-S4 with the
pelvic parasympathetic supply — this is PARTIAL segmental overlap,
providing a Head-zone-level viscerotome convergence for pelvic/
urogenital organs. Stronger segmental basis than LI4 (no trigeminal
overlap) or PC6 (partial cardiac overlap).
→ Cross-ref: C04 S3.4, A07 S4.2

**Anatomy Trains:** **Deep Front Line (DFL)** per Myers — the DFL runs
from tibialis posterior → through the popliteal fascia → adductors →
pelvic floor → psoas → diaphragm → pericardium → pharynx. SP6 is at
the surface access point for the tibialis posterior, which is the
inferior anchor of the DFL. This provides a fascial continuity pathway
from the medial leg DIRECTLY to the pelvic floor and pelvic organs.
The DFL is one of the most clinically relevant Anatomy Trains lines
because it physically connects lower limb points to pelvic and
abdominal viscera. Dorsher 2009 confirmed significant acupoint-Anatomy
Trains correspondence.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

**Neurogenic spot:** Insufficient data for medial leg points. Kim 2017
primarily studied trunk/back neurogenic inflammatory spots correlating
with organ disease. No specific prediction for lower leg acupoints.

**Cupping point:** Not a primary cupping location. Some TCM protocols
use sliding cupping on the medial leg, but the medial tibial border
anatomy makes standard cupping difficult at this specific point.

**Convergence score: 5/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Marma, Anatomy
Trains). High convergence. Additionally, PTNS (posterior tibial nerve
stimulation) represents an independent MODERN MEDICAL tradition
identifying this same location for pelvic conditions — if counted as a
9th tradition, convergence would be 6/9. See ⚡ Framework note 2.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:

Neijing Lingshu: identified as the crossing point of three Yin channels
on the leg. The concept of "three Yin meeting" establishes SP6 as a
single access point to three organ systems simultaneously.

Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing (针灸甲乙经, Huangfu Mi, 259 CE): irregular
menstruation, difficult labor, uterine bleeding, abdominal distension,
digestive disorders (borborygmus, diarrhea), urinary disorders
(difficult urination, enuresis), lower limb pain.

Nanjing (难经): elaborates crossing point theory — points where multiple
channels converge have broader therapeutic scope than single-channel
points. SP6 is cited as the primary example.

Zhenjiu Dacheng (针灸大成, Yang Jizhou, 1601): dysmenorrhea, irregular
menstruation, amenorrhea, difficult labor (促进分娩), uterine prolapse,
seminal emission, impotence, insomnia, digestive weakness, edema,
abdominal pain.

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, Manual of Acupuncture):
- Tonifies Spleen, resolves dampness (digestive disorders, fatigue)
- Promotes smooth flow of Liver Qi (emotional regulation, menstruation)
- Tonifies Kidney (reproductive function, constitutional weakness)
- Nourishes Blood and Yin (anemia, dry skin, insomnia)
- Regulates menstruation (dysmenorrhea, irregular menses, amenorrhea)
- Promotes labor, expels the fetus (traditional labor induction use)
- Benefits urination (urinary retention, incontinence, enuresis)
- Calms the Mind (insomnia, anxiety, palpitations)

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật: SP6 is THE primary point for all
gynecological conditions in clinical practice. The "three Yin crossing"
concept makes it the default choice whenever Spleen, Liver, OR Kidney
pathology is involved — which in TCM covers most internal medicine. SP6
is consistently among the top 3-4 most frequently prescribed acupoints
worldwide alongside ST36, LI4, and PC6. The LI4 + SP6 combination is
the standard labor pain protocol (partner-administered acupressure
during contractions). SP6 + CV4 (Guanyuan) + ST36 is the classical
gynecological tonification formula.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.2, B02 S4

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Primary dysmenorrhea — 🟡 Plausible**

Chen & Chien 2013 (MA, 3 acupuncture + 4 acupressure trials): SP6
acupressure vs control: **p=0.001** (significant, favoring SP6). SP6
acupuncture vs active control (GB39): **p=0.371** (NOT significant).
This is the study that revealed acupressure outperforming acupuncture
at the same point — unique in the literature.

Abaraogu et al. 2016 (SR/MA, 6 studies, 461 participants): SP6
acupressure decreased pain intensity immediately after intervention
(effect size **-0.718**, CI -0.951 to -0.585, p=0.000). Pain relief
maintained up to 3 hours post-intervention. SP6 acupressure delivered by
trained personnel was significantly effective. However, studies generally
low quality with high risk of bias per Cochrane criteria.

Ravi et al. 2024 (updated SR/MA, Medical Acupuncture, literature
through March 2023): both acupuncture AND acupressure on SP6 effective
for primary dysmenorrhea pain reduction. This updated the 2013 finding
by including more recent trials that showed acupuncture effectiveness
when compared to no-treatment rather than active-control acupoints.

Yang et al. 2017 (Complement Ther Med, SR, 7 RCTs): moxibustion at
CV4+SP6+ST36 formula superior to pharmacotherapy for dysmenorrhea pain.
BUT: all studies from China, high risk of bias.
→ Cross-ref: B08 S4.3

B02 notes the dysmenorrhea finding as one of the most significant
anomalies in comparative therapy evidence: SP6 acupressure (p=0.001)
vs SP6 acupuncture (p=0.371) is the ONLY condition where acupressure
outperforms acupuncture at the same point. C02 §3.3 records this as
one of the key findings in cross-therapy comparison.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4, C02 S3.3

Confidence: 🟡 — multiple MAs positive for acupressure specifically;
the acupuncture evidence is weaker (not significant vs active control
point). Overall evidence quality remains low (high risk of bias, mostly
Chinese studies). No dedicated Cochrane review for SP6 alone in
dysmenorrhea. The paradoxical acupressure > acupuncture finding needs
head-to-head confirmation.

**2. Labor pain — 🟡 Plausible**

Cochrane 2020 (Smith et al., CD009232 — 28 trials, 3,960 women): SP6
is the MOST frequently used acupoint in labor pain protocols alongside
LI4. Reduced pharmacological analgesia use: RR **0.75** (moderate
certainty). Satisfaction: RR 2.38. Pattern: significant vs no
treatment; NOT significant vs sham (same as LI4).

Türkmen 2020 (RCT, Japan Journal of Nursing Science): SP6 acupressure
during labor reduced pain intensity and shortened labor duration
(significant).

B02 labor acupressure evidence (Larki et al. 2025 MA, 37 RCTs, ~3,900
participants): LI4+SP6+BL32+GB21 protocol. Pain reduction vs
touch/sham/no-intervention: MD **-1.19 to -2.32** (GRADE: low to
moderate certainty). SP6 and LI4 are the two core points in virtually
all labor acupressure protocols.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Practical note: SP6+LI4 acupressure is partner-administrable during
contractions. High clinical feasibility, no equipment needed. Caregiver
presses SP6 and LI4 during contractions.
→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

Teimoori 2015 (Iran, n=288, labeled "Shiatsu" but B13 correctly
identifies as acupressure): GB21+LI4+SP6 (30 sec each). Spontaneous
labor: **56.9% vs 8.3%** control. Oxytocin needed: **54.5% vs 93.1%**
(p<0.001). Analgesia use: **38.1% vs 93.5%** (p<0.001). Strong effect
but multi-point protocol — cannot isolate SP6 contribution.
→ Cross-ref: B13 S4.2

Confidence: 🟡 — clinically useful (reduces drug use, partner-
administrable, high satisfaction) but specific SP6-alone effect vs sham
not isolated. Always used in combination with LI4 and other points.

**3. Insomnia — 🟡 Plausible**

SR (4 RCTs, 288 participants): single-point SP6 stimulation improved
sleep quality, lengthened deep sleep duration, and REM duration.
Clinical effect improvement significant.

B02 lists SP6 alongside HT7 and auricular Shen Men as primary
acupressure points for insomnia. The "calming the Mind" traditional
indication is partially supported.

Confidence: 🟡 — positive direction but quality limited, small sample
sizes, no dedicated Cochrane review. Traditional indication partially
supported.

**4. Overactive bladder / urinary incontinence — 🟡 Plausible**

PTNS (Posterior Tibial Nerve Stimulation) at SP6 location:
- Second-line treatment for refractory overactive bladder (OAB) in
  modern urology guidelines
- Success rates: **37-82%** for OAB symptoms across studies
- 4 RCTs comparing PTNS vs sham: significant difference favoring PTNS
- Comparable to antimuscarinics with BETTER side effect profile
- Mechanism: electrical stimulation of the posterior tibial nerve (at
  SP6 location) → sacral S2-S4 segments → modulation of bladder
  detrusor muscle activity via pelvic parasympathetic pathways

The urology literature explicitly notes that the PTNS stimulation site
(posterior and superior to medial malleolus) corresponds to the
acupuncture point SP6 (Sanyinjiao). This represents INDEPENDENT
VALIDATION of a traditional acupoint location by a modern medical
discipline that arrived at the same anatomical site through empirical
clinical observation of pelvic symptom relief.

Confidence: 🟡 — strong evidence for PTNS specifically (which IS SP6
stimulation), but the evidence base treats it as neuromodulation rather
than acupuncture. The convergence of evidence is compelling but spans
different research traditions.

**5. Labor induction — 🔴 Unvalidated**

Traditional "forbidden point" in pregnancy — same category as LI4,
BL60, BL67. The same Carr 2015 data (>6,700 women at "forbidden
points") showing no increased adverse event rates applies to SP6.

No strong evidence that SP6 stimulation alone induces labor. Multi-point
protocols (Teimoori 2015) showed increased spontaneous labor but cannot
isolate SP6's contribution.

Confidence: 🔴 — similar to LI4, the labor induction claim as stated
("SP6 stimulation causes contractions") is not supported for SP6 alone.
Traditional pregnancy contraindication is precautionary principle, not
evidence-based.

**Sham comparison note:** Like LI4, SP6 shows large effects vs no
treatment and modest-to-null effects vs sham. The dysmenorrhea finding
is notable: SP6 acupuncture did NOT outperform acupuncture at GB39
(active control), suggesting the acupuncture effect may be largely
non-point-specific. However, SP6 ACUPRESSURE was significant vs control
— raising the question of whether sustained pressure activates a
different mechanism than brief needle insertion at this location.

**Negative evidence:** Acupuncture at SP6 did NOT outperform acupuncture
at an unrelated acupoint (GB39) for dysmenorrhea (Chen & Chien 2013,
p=0.371). This negative finding for point-specificity in acupuncture is
balanced by the positive finding for acupressure point-specificity at
the same location (p=0.001). Recorded per AP-00 §3.2 rule 4.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

Disease-state sensitization: clinically observed increased tenderness at
SP6 in dysmenorrhea patients during menstruation. This is consistent
with the dynamic acupoint model (Kim 2017): organ pathology → increased
sensitivity at associated acupoints. The three-yin-crossing identity
makes SP6 sensitive to Spleen, Liver, OR Kidney dysfunction.

PROKR2 at SP6: A03 specifically identifies the crural fascia containing
SP6 as "PROKR2-rich." If PROKR2+ neurons are present at SP6 as they are
at ST36, this would provide a molecular basis for the point's broad
effects. However, no SP6-specific PROKR2 study has been published (Liu
2020/2021 studied ST36 and ST25 specifically). This is a gap.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4 (Dynamic Acupoint Phenomenon), A03 S3.4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems activated at SP6:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): saphenous nerve (L3-L4 sensory) +
  posterior tibial nerve (L4-S3 motor/sensory). Afferent activation of
  A-beta (touch/pressure) and A-delta (De Qi) fibers.
- A02 (Autonomic Nervous System): somatovisceral reflex via S2-S4
  parasympathetic convergence → modulation of pelvic organ function
  (uterus, bladder, rectum).
- A03 (Fascia-Connective Tissue): PROKR2-rich crural fascia. Deep Front
  Line fascial continuity from tibialis posterior → pelvic floor.
- A04 (Vascular-Lymphatic): posterior tibial artery region. Local
  vasodilation via axon reflex.
- A05 (Immune): IL-10 mediated anti-inflammatory effect demonstrated in
  mouse peritonitis model (PMC3135881). SP6 EA promoted IL-10 release
  with anti-inflammatory properties.
- A06 (Chemical Signaling): endorphin release via standard pain
  modulation pathways (Han JS model applies).
- A07 (Pain-Nociception): segmental gate control at L4-S3 level; DNIC
  for heterosegmental effects.

**Central mechanism question — WHY does SP6 (medial leg, L4-S3) affect
gynecological/pelvic conditions?**

Unlike LI4 (C6-T1 → trigeminal CN V, NO segmental overlap → fully
non-segmental via PAG descending modulation), SP6 has PARTIAL segmental
overlap with pelvic organs:

```
SP6 afferents → tibial nerve → L4-S3 spinal segments

Uterine/pelvic innervation:
  Sympathetic: T10-L1 (superior hypogastric plexus)
  Parasympathetic: S2-S4 (pelvic splanchnic nerves)

OVERLAP at S2-S3(-S4): tibial nerve afferents converge with pelvic
parasympathetic pathways at sacral spinal segments.
```

**Resolution — partial segmental convergence + fascial continuity:**

```
Mechanism 1: SEGMENTAL SOMATOVISCERAL REFLEX (S2-S4)
  Needling/pressure at SP6 activates tibial nerve afferents (L4-S3)
  → afferents enter spinal cord at S2-S4 levels
  → converge with pelvic parasympathetic neurons at same spinal
    segments
  → somatovisceral reflex modulates uterine/bladder smooth muscle
    tone
  → PTNS for OAB operates via this exact pathway

  Key evidence:
  - PTNS 4 RCTs: significant vs sham for overactive bladder
  - Shared S2-S4 segments between tibial nerve and pelvic splanchnic
    nerves is established neuroanatomy
  - Uterine smooth muscle receives parasympathetic innervation via
    S2-S4

Mechanism 2: DEEP FRONT LINE FASCIAL CONTINUITY (A03)
  Tibialis posterior (at SP6) is the inferior anchor of the Deep
  Front Line (DFL) per Myers:
    tibialis posterior → popliteal fascia → adductors → pelvic floor
    → psoas → diaphragm
  → sustained pressure at SP6 transmits mechanical signal through
    DFL → pelvic floor modulation
  → may explain why ACUPRESSURE > ACUPUNCTURE for dysmenorrhea:
    sustained broad pressure engages fascial chain more effectively
    than focal needle penetration

Mechanism 3: SUPRASPINAL DESCENDING MODULATION
  Same PAG/RVM pathway as LI4 (non-segmental):
  → tibial nerve afferents ascend → brainstem PAG/RVM
  → descending inhibition → spinal segments including T10-L1
    (uterine sympathetic supply)
  → endorphin/enkephalin release (Han model)
  This mechanism handles the T10-L1 gap: SP6 (L4-S3) does NOT
  directly overlap with uterine sympathetic supply (T10-L1), so
  sympathetic modulation requires supraspinal relay.
```

**SP6 = DUAL-PATHWAY mechanism:**
- Pelvic parasympathetic effects → segmental (S2-S4 convergence)
- Uterine sympathetic/pain effects → non-segmental (supraspinal PAG)

This is the FIRST Tier 1 point where BOTH segmental AND non-segmental
mechanisms contribute substantially. Compare:
- ST36: primarily PROKR2-molecular (non-segmental vagal)
- PC6: primarily NTS convergence (non-segmental brainstem)
- LI4: primarily PAG descending modulation (non-segmental)
- **SP6: segmental (S2-S4) + non-segmental (PAG) — dual pathway**

**Gap:** Why does acupressure outperform acupuncture at SP6 for
dysmenorrhea? The C02 hypothesis (sustained somatovisceral reflex
producing prolonged smooth muscle relaxation) is plausible but
untested. A head-to-head RCT comparing needle vs pressure at SP6
for dysmenorrhea with mechanistic biomarkers (uterine blood flow,
EMG, endorphin levels) would resolve this. No such study exists.

**Gap:** PROKR2 status at SP6. A03 identifies the crural fascia at SP6
as "PROKR2-rich" but no direct PROKR2 study at SP6 has been published.
If PROKR2+ neurons are confirmed at SP6, this would be a third
PROKR2-verified acupoint (after ST36 and ST25) and would connect the
molecular pathway data to a point with extensive clinical evidence.

⚡ Framework note 1: SP6 represents the FOURTH distinct mechanism
pattern in Phase 1 — PARTIAL segmental convergence + non-segmental
dual-pathway. Unlike the three previous points (ST36=PROKR2-vagal,
PC6=NTS-GABAergic, LI4=PAG-descending — all primarily non-segmental),
SP6's effects on pelvic organs are PARTIALLY explained by segmental
S2-S4 overlap between tibial nerve and pelvic parasympathetic supply.
This is the first point where segmental convergence (C04 S3.4)
provides a substantial part of the explanation. The emerging pattern
from Phases 1-3 is now: the highest-evidence points use DIFFERENT
combinations of segmental and non-segmental mechanisms, not a single
universal pathway. C04's "60-70% segmental" estimate is too simplistic
— it varies dramatically by point. Extends C04 S3.4, A07 S1.

⚡ Framework note 2: PTNS-SP6 convergence represents INDEPENDENT
MODERN MEDICAL VALIDATION of a traditional acupoint location. Urology
arrived at the SP6 site empirically for pelvic/bladder conditions
through clinical observation — identical to how TCM arrived at the
same site through 2000+ years of clinical observation. This parallels:
- Dorsher 2008: trigger point-acupoint overlap (93.3%)
- Beissner 2011: Head zone-Mu/Shu convergence
- Kim 2017: neurogenic spot-acupoint correlation
- Langevin 2002: fascial plane-acupoint overlap (80%)
PTNS adds another independent line of convergent evidence. If these
are counted alongside TCM, SP6 has 6+ independent traditions/

⚡ Framework note 3: SP6 acupressure > acupuncture for dysmenorrhea

So sánh: for dysmenorrhea, acupressure may be SUPERIOR to acupuncture
at this specific point (Chen & Chien 2013). For labor pain, acupressure
(partner-administered) is the most practical and evidence-supported
technique. For overactive bladder, electrical stimulation (PTNS) has
the strongest evidence base. For insomnia, both needling and
acupressure have limited evidence.

### An toàn

Vascular risk: posterior tibial artery runs deep alongside the tibial
nerve — excessive depth (>40 mm) risks arterial puncture. Great
saphenous vein is superficial — minor bleeding or bruising possible.
Apply pressure post-needling.

Neural risk: posterior tibial nerve runs deep between flexor digitorum
longus and tibialis posterior — standard depth (15-25 mm) is safe.
Deeper needling (>30 mm) with aggressive manipulation could affect the
nerve. Risk is LOW at standard depth.

Pregnancy: **traditionally contraindicated** (one of the "forbidden
points" in pregnancy alongside LI4, BL60, BL67, GB21). Same evidence
as LI4 applies: Carr 2015 (>6,700 women at "forbidden points") showed
NO increased adverse events. The traditional contraindication is
precautionary principle, not evidence-based. However, intentional use of
SP6 acupressure during ACTIVE LABOR is different — this is a supported
clinical intervention for labor pain management (reduces analgesic use,
RR 0.75; partner-administrable). Distinction: avoid in early/mid
pregnancy as precaution → use intentionally during active labor.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S7.3, B02 S7

Moxibustion at SP6: standard precautions for thermal burns. Avoid
excessive heat duration at medial leg (thin subcutaneous tissue over
tibia). B08 lists SP6 as contraindicated for moxa during pregnancy.
→ Cross-ref: B08 S7

Infection: standard clean needle technique. Single-use disposable
needles. Adverse events from acupuncture at SP6 are rare and minor
across published trials.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Cerebral effects (fMRI) — 🔴 Unvalidated**
Wang et al. 2021 (Brain and Behavior, PMC8035461): exploratory
resting-state fMRI study, 15 healthy volunteers. Effective acupuncture
at SP3 vs sham acupuncture showed decreased regional homogeneity (ReHo)
in bilateral visual cortices (BA17, BA18, cuneus) and right

The visual cortex deactivation pattern is consistent with findings

Confidence: 🔴 — exploratory, tiny sample (n=15), healthy volunteers

Confidence: 🔴 — combination-only evidence, no point-specific data.

## SP4 — Công Tôn (公孙, Gōng Sūn)

  plantar/plantar)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

head (SP3).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Gastrointestinal motility — 🟡 Plausible (combination)**
Electroacupuncture at SP4+PC6 has been shown to regulate
gastrointestinal motility in animal and clinical studies: promotes
gastric emptying, improves intestinal peristalsis, modulates
inflammatory markers. EA at SP4+PC6+ST36 demonstrated superior
effects compared to SP4+PC6 alone — including enhanced epidermal
growth factor in gastric mucosa and increased nitric oxide synthesis
(gastric mucosal protection). However, SP4's individual contribution
cannot be isolated from these multi-point protocols. PC6 and ST36
each have independent evidence bases (PC6: Cochrane PONV; ST36:
PROKR2-vagal); SP4's addition may provide incremental benefit
through the Luo vessel's abdominal pathway, but this is inferred,
not proven.
Confidence: 🟡 for combination (SP4+PC6 or SP4+PC6+ST36) — positive
results in multiple studies. 🔴 for SP4 alone — no isolated evidence.

**2. Fertility and reproductive conditions — 🔴 Unvalidated**
SP4 is included in acupuncture protocols for IVF support and PCOS
treatment based on Chong Mai theory. A randomized clinical trial
studied acupuncture (including SP4) with clomiphene for PCOS. Stage-
by-stage acupuncture/moxibustion based on "Chong channel as Sea of
Blood" theory was tested for repeated IVF-ET failure. Results are
preliminary and SP4's individual contribution is not isolatable from
multi-point protocols.
Confidence: 🔴 — combination-only, theoretical basis (Chong Mai)
not mechanistically validated.

**3. Postoperative nausea and vomiting — 🔴 Unvalidated (SP4-specific)**
SP4 appears in some PONV protocols alongside PC6, but the Cochrane
evidence (Lee et al. 2015, 59 trials) is for PC6 stimulation
specifically. SP4 is listed as an adjunct point in some trial
protocols but has not been independently evaluated. The anti-emetic
evidence belongs to PC6, not to the SP4+PC6 combination as a unit.
Confidence: 🔴 for SP4-specific contribution.

All point-specific indications: 🔴 Unvalidated. Evidence for
SP4 exists only as part of combinations, primarily SP4+PC6.

similar to the dual-pathway mechanism documented at SP6.

**Luo-Connecting mechanism question:**
The Lingshu describes SP4's Luo vessel entering the abdomen
directly — a claim that has no identified anatomical correlate.
No known nerve pathway from the medial foot (L3-S1) projects
directly to the abdominal viscera without spinal relay. The
"abdominal Luo pathway" is a traditional anatomical claim, not
a confirmed structure. If SP4 affects gastrointestinal function,
the mechanism is most likely: afferent activation at L3-S1 →
spinal relay → supraspinal (brainstem/PAG) → descending
modulation of T6-T12 visceral segments. This is the standard
non-segmental pathway applicable to all distal points affecting
visceral function.

**SP4+PC6 pairing mechanism:**
When SP4 (foot, L3-S1) and PC6 (wrist, C6-T1) are stimulated
simultaneously, they provide afferent input at two widely separated
spinal levels. PC6 activates median nerve → NTS/DMV brainstem relay
(documented mechanism for PONV). SP4 activates tibial-branch
afferents → separate spinal entry. The combined stimulation may
produce stronger supraspinal activation than either alone through
temporal summation at brainstem relay nuclei. This "bilateral
spinal input" hypothesis is consistent with the EA finding that
SP4+PC6+ST36 > SP4+PC6 alone (three spinal input levels > two).

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Dynamic acupoint sensitization — 🟡 Plausible**
Chen et al. 2015 (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative
Medicine, PMC4487270): studied SP8 tenderness in primary
dysmenorrhea patients using finger pressing and algometry.
Key findings:
- Tenderness occurrence rate at SP8 SIGNIFICANTLY HIGHER during
  menstrual period vs non-menstrual period
- VAS score of the tenderest point increased during menstruation
- The tenderest point SHIFTED LOCATION within the SP8 area between
  menstrual and non-menstrual periods — the point is not spatially
  fixed
- Conclusion: SP8 exhibits "dynamic characteristics" — varying
  with physiological/pathological status in both sensitivity and
  location

This is the FIRST Xi-Cleft point in the Atlas with experimental
disease-state sensitization data. Validates the C04 S4 dynamic
acupoint model at a Xi-Cleft specifically, and supports the TCM
teaching that Xi-Cleft tenderness serves a diagnostic function.
Confidence: 🟡 — controlled comparison (menstrual vs non-menstrual
in same patients), but small sample, single study, no replication.

**2. Dysmenorrhea — 🔴 Unvalidated (SP8-specific)**
SP8 appears as a core acupoint for primary dysmenorrhea in
network meta-analysis (IJWH, Dove Medical Press): identified
alongside SP6, CV4, ST36, BL32 as the most frequently prescribed
points. Acupressure at SP6 + SP8 reduces pain severity for up to
2 hours after application. However, NO RCT has tested SP8 as a
sole intervention for dysmenorrhea — SP8 always appears in
multi-point protocols alongside SP6 (which has its own independent
evidence base). SP8's individual contribution cannot be isolated.
Confidence: 🔴 — combination-only, no point-specific evidence.

**3. Uterine bleeding (崩漏) — 🔴 Unvalidated**
Classical primary indication for SP8 as Yin Xi-Cleft blood point.
No modern controlled study for SP8 alone in uterine bleeding.
Compare: LR6 (Liver Xi-Cleft) also has zero evidence for its
uterine bleeding indication. Xi-Cleft points for bleeding remain
an untested hypothesis across all documented Yin Xi-Cleft points.

**Xi-Cleft evidence desert continues:** SP8 joins LU6, HT6, LR6
as Yin Xi-Cleft points with zero controlled point-specific
evidence for their primary traditional indications. Only PC4
(Pericardium Xi-Cleft) has any preliminary controlled data (for
angina). The dynamic acupoint study (Chen 2015) addresses
SENSITIZATION, not therapeutic efficacy — it confirms SP8
responds to pathology but does not prove that stimulating SP8
treats dysmenorrhea.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

**EXPERIMENTALLY CONFIRMED at SP8 — unique among Xi-Cleft points.**

Chen et al. 2015 directly tested the dynamic acupoint hypothesis
at SP8 in primary dysmenorrhea patients:
- SP8 tenderness increases during menstrual period (disease-state
  sensitization confirmed)
- The tenderest point location SHIFTS within the SP8 area — the
  point behaves as a dynamic zone, not a fixed coordinate
- Pain threshold decreases during pathological state

This aligns with the C04 S4 model: organ pathology → acupoint
sensitization → increased tenderness at associated points. SP8 is
the strongest experimental confirmation of this model at a
specific named acupoint, because: (1) the comparison (menstrual
vs non-menstrual) creates a natural within-subject control; (2)
the sensitization is reversible (returns to baseline after
menstruation); (3) the location shift supports the traditional
concept that acupoints are portals with spatial variability, not
fixed anatomical addresses.

The diagnostic application (palpate SP8 → if tender, suspect
acute Spleen Blood pathology) is the traditional Xi-Cleft
palpation method, now partially validated.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

## SP9 — Âm Lăng Tuyền (阴陵泉, Yīn Líng Quán)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
He-Sea/Water (合穴/水) point of the Spleen channel — fifth and
final transporting point in the Five Shu system. Water on Earth
(Spleen = Earth organ) = Ke SUBJECT in the controlling cycle
(Earth controls Water). SP9 is the PRIMARY point for resolving
dampness (利湿) in TCM — arguably the single most-prescribed
point for all dampness-related conditions across clinical
practice. "Yinlingquan" (阴陵泉) = "Yin Mound Spring" — Yin
(medial/inner) + Ling (mound/prominence, referring to the
medial tibial condyle) + Quan (spring, referring to the
depression at the base of the "mound" where Qi pools like water
at a spring). Located in the depression posterior and inferior to
the medial condyle of the tibia, at the level of the pes
anserinus. Fifth He-Sea/Water point documented across Yin organs
(after LU5, HT3, PC3, LR8), and notably the ONLY He-Sea point
with organ-specific hemodynamic evidence: EA at SP9 increases
splenic blood flow without affecting hepatic flow (Kim 2012).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the tibial aspect of the
leg, in the depression posterior and inferior to the medial
condyle of the tibia, at the junction of the shaft and the
medial condyle.

Anatomical landmarks: with the knee slightly flexed, slide the
thumb along the posterior border of the medial tibial surface from
below, upward toward the knee. The thumb drops into a depression
at the junction where the tibial shaft meets the tibial condyle —
this is SP9. The pes anserinus (conjoined insertion of sartorius,
gracilis, semitendinosus) is anterior and slightly superior. The
medial head of gastrocnemius origin is posterior.

Architectural note: SP9 sits at the TRANSITION ZONE between the
tibial shaft and the knee joint proper. Anterior to it: pes
anserinus (the "goose foot" tendon insertion). Posterior: medial
gastrocnemius origin. Deep: the proximal posterior tibial
neurovascular bundle emerges from the popliteal fossa. This is
the most anatomically complex point on the medial tibial border —
where muscular/tendinous structures from three compartments
(anterior thigh via sartorius, medial thigh via gracilis/
semitendinosus, posterior leg via gastrocnemius) converge.

Needle depth range: 15-30 mm perpendicular insertion. Greater
soft tissue depth than SP8 or SP6 due to the muscular convergence
at the knee level.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Saphenous nerve (L3-L4) — primary cutaneous nerve at the medial
  knee. The infrapatellar branch of the saphenous nerve (L3) passes
  across the anterior-medial knee region, proximal to SP9 — at risk
  during knee surgery (known iatrogenic injury in orthopedics).
- Deep: tibial nerve (L4-S3) — the main trunk emerging from the
  popliteal fossa, now entering the proximal leg deep to soleus.
  At SP9's level, the tibial nerve is deep to gastrocnemius/soleus,
  more accessible than at mid-leg (SP8, SP6) because the muscle
  bulk is thinner at the tibial condyle junction.
- Medial genicular nerve (branch of saphenous or tibial) —
  periarticular branch supplying the medial knee joint capsule.
  SP9's proximity to the medial joint line means needle stimulation
  may activate joint capsule afferents.
- Spinal segments: L3-L4 (saphenous/superficial) + L4-S3 (tibial
  nerve/deep)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Fascial plane: between the pes anserinus (anterosuperior) and the
gastrocnemius aponeurosis (posterior). The crural fascia at this
level is reinforced by the tendinous insertions converging at the
proximal medial tibia — a denser fascial environment than at
mid-leg points (SP8, SP6).
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: medial inferior genicular artery (branch of popliteal
artery) — courses around the proximal medial tibia. Great
saphenous vein runs superficially anterior to the point. Deep:
proximal posterior tibial artery (continuing from popliteal
artery) enters the posterior compartment at this level.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle/tendon layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (saphenous nerve, great saphenous vein)
- Crural fascia (reinforced by pes anserinus insertions)
- Pes anserinus tendons: sartorius (from ASIS), gracilis (from
  pubic symphysis), semitendinosus (from ischial tuberosity) —
  three muscles from three different origins converging to insert
  on the proximal medial tibia
- Medial head of gastrocnemius (posterior, originating from medial
  femoral condyle)
- Deep: soleus origin (from proximal posterior tibia)
- Tibial nerve and posterior tibial vessels (deepest)

**THREE-YIN DIFFERENTIATION AT MEDIAL KNEE:**
SP9, LR7, and LR8 are all within 1-2 cun of each other on the
medial knee. Their anatomical differentiation:
- **SP9** (anterior): depression at tibial shaft-condyle junction,
  in pes anserinus territory. Needle enters between tendon
  insertions and tibial periosteum.
- **LR7** (posterior, 1 cun behind SP9): in the upper medial
  gastrocnemius belly. Needle enters muscle directly.
- **LR8** (superior-posterior, medial popliteal crease): between
  semitendinosus and semimembranosus tendons. Needle enters inter-
  tendinous space.

All three share saphenous nerve (L3-L4) cutaneous innervation.
The differentiation is by MUSCULAR COMPARTMENT and FASCIAL PLANE,
not by cutaneous nerve territory. SP = anterior/tibial border
(pes anserinus fascia), LR = posterior/gastrocnemius (SBL
territory), KI channel points at this level would be most
posterior (popliteal). This answers the AP-12 question about
Three-Yin anatomical differentiation: the channels separate by
deep target, not surface nerve.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Organ-specific hemodynamic effects — 🟡 Plausible**
Kim et al. 2012 (American Journal of Chinese Medicine): 2 Hz EA
at bilateral SP9 in Sprague-Dawley rats INCREASED splenic blood
flow/perfusion but did NOT increase hepatic blood flow. Conversely,
EA at LR8 (He-Sea of Liver) increased hepatic but NOT splenic
blood flow. Sham EA at non-acupoints produced no change in either
organ. Measurement: laser Doppler blood flow monitor at organ
surfaces.

This is the STRONGEST organ-specificity evidence at He-Sea points
in the Atlas. Two He-Sea points (SP9 and LR8), located within
1-2 cun of each other on the medial knee, produce OPPOSITE
organ-selective hemodynamic effects matching their channel
assignments. The spatial proximity makes this finding especially
significant — it is not the gross anatomical location that
determines the effect, but the precise point accessed.

Confidence: 🟡 — preclinical (rat model), small sample, single
study. But the double-dissociation design (SP9→spleen not liver,
LR8→liver not spleen, sham→neither) is methodologically strong
for a mechanistic study.
→ Cross-ref: AP-12 LR8 entry

**2. Knee osteoarthritis — 🔴 Unvalidated (SP9-specific)**
SP9 appears in most multi-point knee acupuncture protocols for
medial knee osteoarthritis, typically combined with GB34
(Yanglingquan, lateral knee), EX-LE2/ST35 (knee eyes), and
various local points. A 2021 RCT (PMC8387169) found that distal
acupoints (including SP9) outperformed proximal acupoints in
treating knee OA. fMRI studies (Frontiers in Integrative
Neuroscience 2019) detected limbic network connectivity changes
during acupuncture at SP9+GB34 for KOA. However, SP9's individual
contribution is not isolatable from multi-point protocols.
Confidence: 🔴 — combination-only evidence.

**3. Overactive bladder / urinary conditions — 🔴 Unvalidated
(SP9-specific)**
Systematic reviews (Frontiers in Neurology 2022, PMC9878288):
acupuncture showed favorable effects vs sham for OAB symptoms
(reduced micturition frequency, incontinence episodes, nocturia).
An umbrella review (PMC11703375, 2024) confirmed moderate evidence.
SP9 is a frequently included point in OAB acupuncture protocols.
However, the primary PTNS evidence for OAB belongs to SP6 location
(documented in SP6 entry), and SP9's individual contribution to
multi-point OAB protocols is unknown.
Confidence: 🔴 — included in positive protocols but not isolated.

**4. Dampness-resolving mechanism — 🔴 Unvalidated**
SP9's primary traditional function (resolving dampness) has no
direct modern experimental study. "Dampness" in TCM maps loosely
to fluid metabolism disorders (edema, effusions, excessive
secretions), but no study has tested whether SP9 stimulation
specifically affects fluid balance, lymphatic drainage, aquaporin
expression, or renal function. The organ-specific splenic blood
flow finding (Kim 2012) does not directly address the dampness
mechanism — it demonstrates organ specificity, not fluid
regulation.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No systematic sensitization study published specifically for SP9.
SP9 is clinically noted to be "normally sensitive when pressed"
even in healthy individuals — baseline tenderness that may reflect
the dense pes anserinus fascial environment. In patients with
dampness patterns (edema, heavy limbs), SP9 tenderness reportedly
increases, consistent with disease-state sensitization. SP9
tenderness as a diagnostic sign for dampness conditions is widely
taught in TCM palpation but has not been formally studied.

The Chen 2015 SP8 study (see above) focused on SP8 specifically
and did not compare SP9 tenderness. A comparative study of
sensitization across adjacent SP points (SP6, SP8, SP9) during
different pathological states would help clarify whether
sensitization patterns differ by point category (Xi-Cleft vs
He-Sea vs Crossing Point) or follow a more uniform spatial
distribution.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems at SP9:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): saphenous nerve (L3-L4) +
  infrapatellar branch of saphenous (L3). Deep: tibial nerve
  (L4-S3). Additionally, medial genicular nerve branches
  supplying knee joint capsule afferents. More complex innervation
  than mid-leg points due to the knee joint's dense periarticular
  nerve supply. Combined spinal access: L3-S3.
- A02 (Autonomic Nervous System): periarticular sympathetic
  innervation at the medial knee — relevant to local inflammatory
  regulation and joint capsule vasomotor control.
- A03 (Fascia-Connective Tissue): multi-line convergence (SBL +
  DFL + Spiral Line). Dense fascial environment from pes
  anserinus + gastrocnemius insertions. Higher mechanical
  complexity than mid-limb points.
- A04 (Vascular): medial inferior genicular artery. Significant
  lymphatic drainage at the medial knee — popliteal lymph nodes
  are posterior, and lymphatic channels from the medial leg
  converge in this region.

**Organ-specific mechanism question — WHY does SP9 affect
splenic blood flow specifically (Kim 2012)?**

SP9 (L3-S3) is 6-8 segments from the Spleen viscerotome (T6-T8).
No direct segmental somatovisceral reflex is possible. The
organ-specific hemodynamic effect MUST operate through supraspinal
pathways:
- Tibial nerve afferents at L4-S3 → ascend to brainstem
  (NTS/rostral ventrolateral medulla)
- Supraspinal processing → descending autonomic modulation
  specifically to splenic vasculature (T6-T8 sympathetic
  control of splenic artery)
- The specificity (spleen not liver) despite spatial proximity
  to LR8 suggests that the PRECISE neural target (different
  fascial plane → different afferent fiber population →
  different central processing pattern) determines organ
  selectivity

This is consistent with the fascial plane hypothesis (Langevin
2002): two points 1-2 cun apart on the medial knee access
DIFFERENT fascial compartments (SP9 = pes anserinus/tibial
border fascia, LR8 = inter-hamstring/gastrocnemius fascia),
activating different afferent fiber populations despite sharing
saphenous cutaneous territory.

**"Dampness" mechanism hypothesis:**
TCM's "resolving dampness" function at SP9 may map to:
(1) local lymphatic stimulation (medial knee = lymphatic
convergence zone → popliteal nodes → inguinal nodes), (2)
autonomic modulation of renal function (via supraspinal
brainstem relay → renal sympathetic nerve → diuretic effect),
or (3) splenic vascular regulation (Kim 2012 evidence → spleen
plays a role in fluid filtration and immune-mediated edema).
None of these pathways has been tested for SP9 specifically.

→ Cross-ref: B01 S4.3

depression below the medial tibial condyle; press firmly with

→ Cross-ref: B02 S4

with cold sensation, loose stools with undigested food). Indirect

→ Cross-ref: B08 S4.3

→ Cross-ref: B11 S3.2

### An toàn

Vascular risk: LOW-MODERATE. Medial inferior genicular artery
at the point level — small caliber branch, usually not
clinically significant. Great saphenous vein superficially,

position and typically anterior to SP9.

Pneumothorax: none (knee location).

Overall safety: LOW complication risk. Standard medial knee
point with good anatomical landmarks and adequate tissue depth.

## SP10 — Huyết Hải (血海, Xuè Hǎi)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Sea of Blood" (血海) — one of the most clinically important
points on the Spleen channel despite having NO formal point
category membership (not Five Shu, not Yuan-Source, not Luo,
not Xi-Cleft, not any standard designation). The name directly
announces its function: governing Blood. SP10 is the PRIMARY
point in TCM clinical practice for all blood-related conditions
— dermatological (eczema, urticaria, pruritus), menstrual
(irregular menstruation, excessive bleeding, amenorrhea), and
hematological (blood stagnation, purpura). Located on the
vastus medialis muscle, 2 cun above the superior border of the
patella — a MUSCLE-BELLY location unusual for high-importance
acupoints, which typically localize to tendons, bony prominences,
or inter-muscular grooves. SP10 represents the Spleen's
traditional role of "governing Blood" (脾统血). The "Sea of
Blood" name creates a thematic link with SP4 (Gongsun), which
opens Chong Mai (Penetrating Vessel, also called the "Sea of
Blood" 血海 in Lingshu Ch. 33). Two different "Sea of Blood"
concepts — point-level (SP10) and vessel-level (SP4/Chong Mai)
— converge on the Spleen channel, reflecting the Spleen's
central position in TCM blood physiology.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the anteromedial aspect
of the thigh, in the depression of the vastus medialis muscle,
2 cun above the medial superior border of the patella.

Anatomical landmarks: with the knee slightly flexed, locate the
superior border of the patella. Measure 2 cun (approximately 2
finger-breadths) proximally along the medial border. Ask the
patient to contract the quadriceps (extend the knee against
resistance) — the prominent bulge of the vastus medialis muscle
becomes visible. SP10 is at the highest point of this muscular
bulge, in a palpable depression when the muscle is relaxed.
Alternative method: with the knee flexed, cup the patient's
knee with the right hand (for right knee), thumb abducted. The
tip of the thumb indicates SP10.

Architectural note: SP10 marks the transition from the tibial
border (SP6-SP9) to the anterior thigh compartment. Unlike SP9
(bony depression at the tibial condyle), SP10 sits entirely on
MUSCLE BELLY — the vastus medialis obliquus (VMO) at this level.
SP10 and LR9 (Yinbao) are at approximately the SAME HEIGHT on
the medial thigh (SP10 = 2 cun above medial patella border;
LR9 = 4 cun above medial epicondyle ≈ similar level). SP10 is
more ANTERIOR (on VMO bulge) while LR9 is more MEDIAL-POSTERIOR
(between VMO and sartorius). Same-height, different-compartment
differentiation — parallels SP9/LR7/LR8 at the knee.

Needle depth range: 15-25 mm perpendicular insertion into the
vastus medialis muscle belly. Adequate muscle cushion over femur.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Saphenous nerve (L3-L4) — cutaneous, continues from the leg
  into the medial thigh. At SP10 level, the saphenous nerve has
  emerged from the adductor canal (subsartorial canal) and runs
  superficially along the medial knee/thigh region.
- Medial femoral cutaneous nerve (L2-L3) — branch of femoral
  nerve. At SP10's level, this nerve overlaps with the saphenous
  territory, creating a DUAL CUTANEOUS ZONE.
- Deep: nerve to vastus medialis (branch of femoral nerve,
  L2-L4) — motor nerve entering the vastus medialis from its
  posterior surface. Intramuscular nerve branches are present at
  SP10 level. Needling into the muscle belly directly accesses
  these motor nerve branches.
- Spinal segments: L2-L4 (both cutaneous and motor via femoral
  nerve branches)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

**NEURAL TRANSITION NOTE:** SP10 marks the shift from the
saphenous-dominant zone (SP4-SP9, saphenous L3-L4 as primary
cutaneous nerve, tibial L4-S3 deep) to the DUAL cutaneous zone
(saphenous L3-L4 + medial femoral cutaneous L2-L3), with deep
innervation switching from tibial nerve (posterior compartment)
to femoral motor nerve (anterior compartment). This neural
transition corresponds to the anatomical boundary between leg
and thigh — the knee as a neural gateway.

Fascial plane: vastus medialis muscle belly → covered by fascia
lata (deep fascia of thigh). At the medial border, the
vastoadductor membrane (medial intermuscular septum) separates
the vastus medialis (anterior compartment) from the adductor
group (medial compartment). SP10's needle path penetrates fascia
lata into the muscle belly.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: descending genicular artery (from femoral artery) —
its saphenous branch descends medially near SP10. Medial
superior genicular artery (branch of popliteal artery) at or
slightly below SP10 level. Great saphenous vein superficially,
visible and avoidable.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle/tendon layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (saphenous nerve, medial femoral
  cutaneous nerve, saphenous vein tributaries)
- Fascia lata (deep fascia of thigh)
- Vastus medialis muscle belly — the PRIMARY TARGET. VMO fibers
  at this level are oriented at approximately 50-55° to the long
  axis of the femur
- Vastoadductor membrane (medial intermuscular septum) —
  separating vastus medialis from adductor group
- Femur periosteum at depth (not reached at standard needle depth)

"Sea of Blood" (血海) is a CLINICAL DESIGNATION encoded in the

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SP10 Xuehai (血海, "Sea of Blood"). THE
primary point for all blood-related conditions in TCM. Functions:
invigorates Blood and dispels stasis, cools Blood and clears
Heat, nourishes Blood and regulates menstruation, harmonizes
the Ying (nutritive) level. SP10 is the first point selected
whenever "Blood" appears in a TCM pattern diagnosis — Blood
stasis, Blood Heat, Blood deficiency. 🟢

**2. Trigger point:** Vastus medialis TrP — well-documented in
Travell & Simons. The VMO TrP refers pain to the anteromedial
knee, associated with patellofemoral pain syndrome. Dorsher
(2009): SP10 corresponds to the distal vastus medialis TrP.
SP10 is a standard dry needling target for medial knee pain
and quadriceps imbalance. 🟢

**3. Ashi correspondence:** tenderness at SP10's location is a
common clinical finding in patellofemoral pain syndrome and
anterior knee pain. The vastus medialis area becomes tender and
palpably tense in quadriceps imbalance conditions. SP10 is one
of the acupoints where tenderness assists localization — the
exact point is found partly by palpation for the tender
depression on the VMO. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Urvi (ऊर्वि) — thigh Marma, classified as
Mamsa (muscle) Marma. Located on the medial-anterior thigh.
Several texts place Urvi at the lower medial thigh, within the
vicinity of SP10. Governs blood circulation in the lower limb.
Approximate correspondence — less precise than knee-level Janu
Marma. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** L2-L3 dermatome (medial suprapatellar thigh).
Spleen viscerotome = T6-T8. Segmental gap: 4-6 segments. For
dermatological indications (systemic skin conditions), segmental
innervation is NOT the mechanism — whole-body pruritus and
generalized urticaria treated by SP10 are not dermatome-
restricted. Supraspinal mediation required.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2 🔴

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Front Line (SFL): vastus
medialis is part of the quadriceps group, a major station on
Myers' SFL (tibial tuberosity → quadriceps → ASIS → rectus
abdominis → sternum). At the medial border, the vastoadductor
membrane connects to the adductor group (Deep Front Line, DFL).
SP10 sits at the SFL-DFL INTERFACE on the medial thigh.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4, A09 S2.2 🟡

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No specific data. The
medial suprapatellar region has not been studied in Kim 2017's
neurogenic spot model (focused on trunk). 🔴

**8. Cupping point:** The medial suprapatellar thigh provides a
reasonably flat surface for medium cups. Cupping at SP10 area
is used in some TCM protocols for blood stasis conditions. Wet
cupping (bloodletting + cupping) at SP10 is a specific
traditional technique for blood stasis + heat affecting the
skin. 🟡

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Anatomy
Trains confirmed). Same convergence as SP9. The muscle-belly
location provides strong TrP correspondence — vastus medialis
TrPs are among the best-documented in the lower limb.

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Atopic dermatitis — 🟡 Plausible (combination)**
Zhang et al. 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis
(PMC11624827): acupuncture for atopic dermatitis. SP10
(Xuehai), LI11 (Quchi), ST36 (Zusanli) = the three most
frequently used acupoints across included studies. Concluded
acupuncture "may be helpful for treating atopic dermatitis
with no indication of serious potential harm." SP10's individual
contribution not isolated — all studies used multi-point
protocols.
Confidence: 🟡 — SP10 consistently included in positive
protocols with plausible mechanism (see mast cell study below),
but individual effect unknown.

**2. Urticaria — mast cell mechanism — 🟡 Plausible
(preclinical)**
Wang et al. 2023 (Zhen Ci Yan Jiu / Acupuncture Research):
EA pretreatment at LI11 + SP10 (2 Hz/15 Hz, 1 mA, 20 min
daily × 7 days) in a rat urticaria model reduced mast cell
degranulation, decreased IL-33/ST2 pathway activation, and
lowered histamine release in skin tissue. N=8/group, 4 groups
(blank, model, EA, medication).
Confidence: 🟡 — preclinical (rat), small sample, combination
(SP10+LI11 not SP10 alone). But the IL-33/ST2 pathway is
directly relevant to urticaria/atopic dermatitis in humans,
and the finding mechanistically supports the TCM "Blood Heat
→ skin" concept.

**3. Dysmenorrhea (acupressure) — 🔴 Unvalidated (SP10-specific)**
Khayati et al. (Jurnal Aisyah, Indonesia): acupressure at SP10
reduced menstrual pain in students (p<0.001). Single non-blinded
study without sham control, low-impact journal.
Confidence: 🔴 — single uncontrolled study.

**4. Blood regulation mechanism — 🔴 Unvalidated**
SP10's primary traditional functions (invigorating/cooling/
stopping Blood) have no direct experimental study examining
effects on coagulation factors, platelet function, or regional
blood flow at SP10 specifically.

## SP21 — Đại Bao (大包, Dà Bāo)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**
"Great Envelope" (大包, also translated "Great Wrapping" or
"Great Embracement") — the ONLY point in the entire meridian
system classified as "Great Luo of Spleen" (脾之大络). This is
a UNIQUE category: while every channel has one Luo-Connecting
point (15 total including Ren and Du), the Spleen alone has a
SECOND Luo designation at SP21. Traditional theory states that
the Great Luo of the Spleen "connects to all 15 Luo vessels"
and governs the net-like vessels (孙络) of the entire body.
SP21 is the LAST point on the Spleen channel's surface
trajectory: the channel begins at the medial big toe (SP1) and
ends at the lateral chest (SP21), giving the Spleen channel
the LONGEST anatomical span of any Yin meridian — from toe to
axillary chest wall. Located in the 6th intercostal space on
the mid-axillary line, with PNEUMOTHORAX RISK requiring oblique
shallow insertion only. SP21 is also the EFT "Under Arm"
tapping point (B12), independently selected by a modern
therapeutic system for emotional regulation — a cross-system
convergence at a TCM "systemic connection" point.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral side of the
chest, on the mid-axillary line, in the 6th intercostal space.

Anatomical landmarks: locate GB22 (Yuanye) in the mid-axillary
line at the level of the 4th intercostal space (nipple level in
males). SP21 is 3 cun inferior to GB22. Alternative: from the
axillary fold, palpate inferiorly along the mid-axillary line,
counting rib interspaces to the 6th intercostal space.

Architectural note: SP21 is the TERMINAL POINT of the Spleen
channel's surface trajectory — the pathway ends here before the
internal branch ascends. The mid-axillary line at the 6th
intercostal space is where the chest wall transitions from
pectoralis major/minor territory (anterior) to latissimus dorsi
territory (posterior), with serratus anterior occupying the
lateral strip between them. SP21 sits directly on the serratus
anterior — the "boxer's muscle" — which attaches from the
lateral ribs to the medial scapular border, linking the ribcage
to the shoulder girdle.

Needle depth: oblique insertion 0.3-0.5 cun (8-15 mm) along
the intercostal space. **PERPENDICULAR INSERTION CONTRAINDICATED
— pneumothorax risk.**

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Lateral cutaneous branch of the 6th intercostal nerve (T6)
  — primary innervation at the 6th ICS on the mid-axillary line.
- Long thoracic nerve (C5-C7) — motor nerve to serratus
  anterior, runs along the lateral chest wall on the superficial
  surface of serratus anterior. In close proximity to SP21 at
  the 6th ICS level.
- Variable: terminal branches of the intercostobrachial nerve
  (T2) may extend inferiorly toward the mid-axillary region in
  some individuals, but T2 territory is primarily at the upper
  axilla and medial arm level, not at the 6th ICS.
- Spinal segments: T6 (primary cutaneous) + C5-C7 (long
  thoracic motor to serratus anterior)
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

NOTE ON B12-EFT NERVE ATTRIBUTION: The B12-EFT file attributes
the intercostobrachial nerve (T2) to the "Under Arm" tapping
point (mapped to SP21). Standard anatomical innervation at the
6th ICS is the lateral cutaneous branch of T6, not T2. The
discrepancy likely reflects that the EFT tapping zone (~10 cm
below axilla) may be slightly superior to the WHO-standard SP21
location, overlapping with variable T2 cutaneous extension. For
this Atlas entry, T6 is the primary segmental attribution.

Fascial plane: the lateral chest wall at the 6th ICS has a
layered fascial structure. Serratus anterior fascia covers the
lateral ribcage, with muscle fibers running anterosuperior to
posteroinferior (from rib digitations to scapular medial border).
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4

Vascular: intercostal artery (branch of thoracic aorta) runs
along the INFERIOR border of each rib in the costal groove —
the standard "danger zone" for intercostal procedures. Lateral
thoracic artery (branch of axillary artery) descends more
superiorly. Insert needle SUPERIOR to the lower rib border to
avoid the neurovascular bundle.
→ Cross-ref: A04 S3.2

Muscle/tendon layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (lateral cutaneous nerve of T6)
- Serratus anterior (digitation from 6th rib, C5-C7 long
  thoracic nerve) — primary muscle at this location
- External intercostals (between ribs 6 and 7)
- Internal intercostals
- Innermost intercostals
- Endothoracic fascia → parietal pleura → **PLEURAL CAVITY
  (LUNG)**

**DEPTH TO PLEURA:** At the mid-axillary line, 6th ICS, chest
wall thickness is typically 15-25 mm in adults (varies
significantly with body habitus). In thin individuals, as little
as 10-15 mm. The pleural cavity extends down to the 10th ICS
at the mid-axillary line. Perpendicular needling at standard
acupuncture depths (25+ mm) WILL penetrate the pleural cavity
in most patients.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

**Great Luo of Spleen (脾之大络, Pí Zhī Dà Luò)**

UNIQUE CATEGORY — SP21 is the only point in the meridian system
with the "Great Luo" designation. The standard Luo system
includes 15 Luo-Connecting points (12 regular + Ren Mai + Du Mai
+ Great Luo of Spleen). SP21 is the 15th, occupying a DIFFERENT
functional tier from the regular 14.

Regular Luo-Connecting points (including SP4): connect PAIRED
channels (interior-exterior relationship). SP4 connects Spleen
↔ Stomach specifically.

Great Luo of Spleen (SP21): connects to ALL Luo vessels — a
SYSTEMIC connecting function rather than channel-specific. The
Lingshu states: "The Great Luo of the Spleen is called Dabao.
It emerges 3 cun below Yuanye (GB-22) and spreads over the
chest and lateral costal region."

Symptom differentiation:
- Excess (实): pain throughout the ENTIRE body (全身尽痛)
- Deficiency (虚): all hundred joints are FLACCID/lax
  (百节尽皆纵)

This excess/deficiency pattern is UNIQUE among all acupoints
— whole-BODY symptoms rather than channel-specific or organ-
specific patterns. No other single point claims governance over
the entire musculoskeletal system's pain and tone.

DUAL LUO ON SPLEEN (architectural note):
- SP4 = regular Luo-Connecting (local: Spleen ↔ Stomach)
- SP21 = Great Luo (systemic: all vessels)
LOCAL → GLOBAL Luo architecture: SP4 (foot) → SP21 (chest).
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1, SP4 entry above

Five Shu: No (beyond elbow/knee)

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SP21 Dabao (大包, "Great Wrapping").
Great Luo-Connecting point of the Spleen. Functions: regulates
Qi and Blood of the whole body, firms sinews and joints, benefits
the lateral costal region, unbinds the chest. Excess = whole-
body aching, deficiency = all joints flaccid. A "master
regulator" concept for connective tissue integrity. 🟢

**2. Trigger point:** Serratus anterior TrP — Travell & Simons
document TrPs in the serratus anterior that refer pain along the
lateral chest wall, to the inferior angle of the scapula, and
sometimes to the medial arm. The 6th rib digitation TrP
corresponds closely to SP21. Associated with painful breathing,
"side stitches," and difficulty reaching overhead. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** lateral chest wall tenderness at
SP21's location is common in intercostal neuralgia,
costochondritis, and post-thoracotomy pain. Also reported in
patients with hepatobiliary conditions (referred pain via T6-T8
intercostal nerves from the hepatobiliary region). 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Stanarohita (स्तनरोहित) — breast/chest Marma,
classified as Sira (vascular) Marma, size 2 angula. Multiple
texts place this Marma at the lateral chest wall near the 5th-6th
rib level. Approximate functional proximity to SP21. Governs
respiratory function and chest vitality. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** T6 dermatome (lateral chest wall at 6th ICS).
Spleen viscerotome = T6-T8. **DIRECT SEGMENTAL OVERLAP** — T6 at
SP21 falls within the Spleen viscerotome range. This is the FIRST
and ONLY SP channel point with direct segmental correspondence to
its own organ's viscerotome. All medial leg and foot SP points
(SP1-SP12 at L2-S1) are 4-8 segments away, requiring supraspinal
mediation. SP21 at T6 can access the Spleen via DIRECT
somatovisceral reflex.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2 🟢

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line (LL) — the lateral chest wall
intercostals are part of Myers' Lateral Line (lateral foot/
peroneus longus → ITB → lateral obliques → intercostals → SCM).
SP21 directly accesses the LL. Additionally, serratus anterior
at SP21 connects to the scapular stabilization system, linking
the Lateral Line to the Arm Lines via the rib-to-scapula chain.
→ Cross-ref: A03 S3.4, A09 S2.2 🟡

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No specific data for lateral
chest wall. Kim 2017's trunk model focused on the paraspinal
region, not the mid-axillary line. 🔴

**8. Cupping point:** Lateral chest wall provides moderate
cupping surface between ribs. Flash cupping at SP21 area is used
for chest fullness and hypochondriac pain. Cup adhesion moderate
due to rib contour. 🟡

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Head Zone, Trigger Point).
Lower than limb points. The Head Zone convergence is particularly
significant — DIRECT segmental overlap with the Spleen viscerotome,
unique among all SP channel points.

### Chỉ định truyền thống (DATA — ghi nhận, không endorse as mechanism)

Nguồn cổ:
- Lingshu "Meridians" (经脉) chapter: "The Great Luo of the
  Spleen, named Dabao: when excess, the entire body is painful
  (全身尽痛); when deficient, all hundred joints are flaccid
  (百节尽皆纵)." This is the defining classical text.
- Zhenjiu Jiayi Jing (Huangfu Mi, ~260 CE): chest pain,
  hypochondriac fullness, difficulty breathing, lateral costal
  pain
- Zhenjiu Dacheng (Yang Jizhou, 1601): whole-body aching,
  generalized weakness, lateral chest pain, shortness of breath

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman, WHO):
- Regulates Qi and Blood of the whole body: generalized body
  ache (fibromyalgia-like presentation), general weakness
- Firms sinews and joints: joint laxity, weakness of extremities
- Benefits the lateral costal region: intercostal neuralgia,
  lateral chest pain, pleurisy
- Unbinds the chest: chest oppression, difficulty breathing, cough

Kinh nghiệm lâm sàng nổi bật:
- SP21 for whole-body aching/weakness is the classical Great Luo
  pattern. In modern practice, this maps loosely to fibromyalgia,
  chronic fatigue syndrome, and post-viral weakness — systemic
  conditions without organ-specific localization. SP21 is
  prescribed when symptoms are "everything aches" or "everything
  is weak" without a specific channel or organ diagnosis.
- SP21 + SP4 (regular Luo): combining both Spleen Luo points for
  comprehensive Luo treatment — local (SP4 for Spleen-Stomach) +
  systemic (SP21 for all vessels).
- SP21 is used INFREQUENTLY in modern clinical practice compared
  to SP6/SP9/SP10, partly due to pneumothorax risk and partly
  because the "Great Luo" concept is theoretical and difficult to
  diagnose reliably.
→ Cross-ref: B01 S3.1

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

**1. Whole-body pain / fibromyalgia — 🔴 Unvalidated**
No systematic review or RCT has examined SP21 for generalized
pain conditions. Acupuncture for fibromyalgia has a growing
evidence base (several Cochrane reviews with low-moderate quality
evidence), but SP21 is not among the commonly studied points in
these protocols (GV20, LI4, LR3, ST36 are more typical). SP21's
classical indication (whole-body pain) has theoretical relevance
to fibromyalgia, but no study has tested this specifically.
Confidence: 🔴 — no controlled evidence.

**2. EFT tapping at "Under Arm" point — 🟡 Plausible (indirect)**
B12-EFT uses SP21 location as the "Under Arm" tapping point.
EFT clinical evidence (Church et al. 2012, 2013; Nelms & Castel
2016) shows significant effects on anxiety, PTSD, and cortisol
reduction. However, EFT uses MULTIPLE tapping points
simultaneously — SP21 is one of 9 points, and its individual
contribution is not isolatable. The EFT mechanism is attributed
to emotional processing (amygdala desensitization, cortisol
modulation) rather than the TCM "Great Luo" concept.
Confidence: 🟡 — EFT has positive evidence as a multi-point
system, but SP21's individual contribution unknown.
→ Cross-ref: B12 S3, S4

**3. Lateral chest wall pain — 🔴 Unvalidated**
No controlled study for SP21 specifically for intercostal
neuralgia or lateral chest pain. Standard acupuncture protocols
for these conditions use local-distal combinations but rarely
highlight SP21 as the key point.
Confidence: 🔴 — no controlled evidence.

### Dynamic acupoint properties

No sensitization study for SP21. The Great Luo's classical
description does not explicitly describe tenderness changes.
Palpation of SP21 for pain is mentioned in some texts as a
diagnostic sign for "Great Luo excess" pattern, but whether
tenderness at SP21 correlates with generalized pain conditions
has not been studied.

The intercostal location presents methodological challenges for
sensitization research — normal deep breathing changes pressure
at the point, and the lateral chest wall is mechanically active
during respiration, confounding tenderness measurements.
→ Cross-ref: C04 S4

### Cơ chế giải thích (BSA framework attribution)

Track A systems at SP21:
- A01 (Peripheral Nervous System): lateral cutaneous branch of
  T6 intercostal nerve. Long thoracic nerve (C5-C7) to serratus
  anterior. T6 provides DIRECT segmental access to the Spleen
  viscerotome (T6-T8) — unlike all medial leg SP points which
  require supraspinal relay.
- A02 (Autonomic Nervous System): T6 sympathetic chain is in
  direct communication — intercostal nerves carry sympathetic
  fibers from the thoracic chain. T6 sympathetic output includes
  splanchnic nerve contributions to the celiac plexus, providing
  a potential pathway to Spleen organ regulation.
- A03 (Fascia-Connective Tissue): Lateral Line (intercostal
  fascia), serratus anterior fascia linking ribcage to scapula.
  Multiple fascial layers at the lateral chest wall.
- A04 (Vascular): intercostal artery/vein at inferior rib border.
  Lateral thoracic artery more superiorly.

**"Great Luo" mechanism question — HOW can a single point govern
all body connections?**

The classical claim (SP21 connects to all 15 Luo vessels, governs
whole-body pain/weakness) describes a NETWORK PROPERTY attributed
to a SINGLE POINT — a conceptual mismatch from an anatomical
perspective. No anatomical structure at SP21 literally connects
to all meridian Luo vessels. Two non-literal interpretations:

(1) **Segmental-splanchnic pathway:** T6 innervation → sympathetic
chain → greater splanchnic nerve → celiac plexus → splenic plexus.
The spleen (organ) is a major lymphoid organ filtering blood and
regulating immune function systemically. If SP21 modulates splenic
function via this direct segmental pathway, the "whole body" effect
could be mediated by systemic immune/inflammatory modulation —
paralleling the vagal anti-inflammatory reflex (Tracey 2002) but
via sympathetic-splanchnic rather than vagal route.

(2) **Lateral Line fascial continuity:** Serratus anterior at SP21
connects ribcage to scapula; the Lateral Line provides fascial
continuity from foot to head via the lateral body. SP21's position
on the Lateral Line could theoretically propagate mechanical
effects along the fascial chain — a local-to-global biomechanical
pathway.

Neither interpretation fully accounts for the classical claim's
scope. The "Great Luo" concept may be better understood as a
TCM DIAGNOSTIC FRAMEWORK for describing systemic conditions
(whole-body pain, generalized weakness) rather than a literal
anatomical connection at SP21.

**SEGMENTAL PARADOX:** SP21 is the ONLY SP channel point with
direct segmental access to the Spleen viscerotome (T6-T8) — yet
it is NOT traditionally used for Spleen-specific conditions
(digestion, blood holding). Its traditional function (whole-body
pain/weakness) is the most NON-organ-specific indication on the
channel. Conversely, the leg points WITHOUT segmental access
(SP3, SP6, SP9 at L3-S3) ARE used for Spleen-specific conditions.
This inverts the expected segmental logic: the closest point
(segmentally) has the broadest function; the farthest points have
the most organ-specific functions.

### An toàn

**⚠️ PNEUMOTHORAX RISK — PRIMARY SAFETY CONCERN**

Location: 6th intercostal space, mid-axillary line. The pleural
cavity at the mid-axillary line extends from the lung apex down
to the 10th intercostal space (costophrenic recess). SP21 at the
6th ICS is well within the PLEURAL ZONE.

Chest wall thickness at the mid-axillary line, 6th ICS:
- Average adult: 15-25 mm
- Thin individuals: as little as 10-15 mm
- Obese individuals: 25-40 mm

Perpendicular insertion of a standard acupuncture needle (25-40
mm) would penetrate the pleural cavity in MOST patients.

Safe needling technique:
- Oblique insertion ONLY, angled along the intercostal space
- Maximum safe depth: 0.3-0.5 cun (8-15 mm)
- Palpate the rib above and below before insertion
- Insert SUPERIOR to the lower rib border to avoid the costal
  groove neurovascular bundle (intercostal artery, vein, nerve
  run at the inferior rib border)

Vascular risk: MODERATE. Intercostal artery in costal groove at
inferior rib border — avoidable with correct technique (insert
superior to lower rib).

Neural risk: LOW-MODERATE. Long thoracic nerve runs on the
surface of serratus anterior — injury causes winged scapula
(serratus anterior paralysis). Rare with acupuncture-depth
needling but reported with deep needling in the lateral chest wall.

Pregnancy: no specific contraindication documented.

Overall safety: **MODERATE-HIGH complication risk.** One of the
higher-risk acupoints on the Spleen channel. Classified among
"dangerous points" (危险穴) in Chinese acupuncture safety
literature alongside LU1, LU2, GB21, and other chest/back points.
Requires experienced practitioner and careful technique assessment.

## SP1 — Ẩn Bạch (隐白, Yǐn Bái)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
Jing-Well (Wood) point of the Spleen channel and **Ke CONTROLLER** point
(Wood controls Earth in the Ke cycle, 木克土). Located at the medial nail
corner of the big toe. LR1 (Dadun) sits at the LATERAL nail corner of the
same toe — two Yin foot channels sharing a single digit, opposite sides,
with different innervation and complementary clinical roles. SP1 is
classically the strongest hemostatic point among the 12 Jing-Well points,
with specific indication for uterine bleeding (崩漏). Emergency
bloodletting and direct moxibustion are the primary techniques.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the medial side of the distal
phalanx of the hallux, at the junction of lines drawn along the medial
border of the nail and the base of the nail, approximately 0.1 cun from
the medial corner of the nail.

Anatomical landmarks: medial nail corner of the big toe. Compare LR1
(lateral nail corner) — same digit, opposite side. SP2 (Dadu) is at
the 1st metatarsophalangeal joint, approximately 3-4 cm proximal. The
red-white skin junction (赤白肉际) begins here and continues through
SP2 and SP3 along the medial foot border.

Needle depth: 1-2 mm shallow puncture, or prick to bleed. Moxibustion
(direct, wheat-grain cones) is the traditional primary technique.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Proper plantar digital nerve of the hallux (medial branch) — from
  the medial plantar nerve (L4-L5), terminal branch of the posterior
  tibial nerve. Supplies plantar-medial sensory innervation at the
  big toe tip.
- Saphenous nerve (L3-L4) — cutaneous contribution on the dorsomedial
  surface of the big toe. At the nail corner, sensory territories from
  both nerves overlap.
- Compare LR1: dorsal digital nerve from deep peroneal nerve (L4-L5).
  Different nerve branch despite adjacent location on same digit.
- Spinal segments: L3-L5
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: medial plantar digital artery (from medial plantar artery,
branch of posterior tibial). Rich subungual capillary plexus at nail bed
— anatomical basis for bloodletting technique.

Tissue: thin skin tightly adherent to underlying tissue, minimal
subcutaneous fat, no muscle belly. Periosteum of distal phalanx is
the floor — avoid direct contact.

Spleen (T6-T8). Classical hemostatic indication operates through

→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Superficial Front Line (SFL) starts at dorsal
toe extensors. SP1 at medial nail corner is at the extreme distal
terminus — not a meaningful myofascial station.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No toe tip data in Kim 2017.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable (digit).

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi partial). Consistent with toe
Well points: LR1 = 2/8, HT9 = 2/8, PC9 = 1/8.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:

  (井主心下满)

## SP2 — Đại Đô (大都, Dà Dū)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
Ying-Spring (Fire) point of the Spleen channel and **TONIFICATION**
point — Fire generates Earth in the Sheng cycle (火生土), making Fire
the mother element of Earth. Located at the medial side of the big toe,
distal to the 1st metatarsophalangeal joint, at the red-white skin
junction. The formal tonification point for Spleen Qi deficiency
(Nan Jing: "for deficiency, tonify the mother"). In practice, one of
the least used SP Five Shu points — overshadowed by SP3 (Yuan-Source,
more proximal and more commonly selected) and SP6/ST36 (dominant
clinical points for Spleen tonification).

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the medial side of the big toe,
in the depression distal and inferior to the 1st metatarsophalangeal
joint, at the junction of the red and white skin (赤白肉际).

Anatomical landmarks: the 1st MTP joint is the prominent bony ball at
the base of the big toe. SP2 is in the depression just distal to this
joint on the medial side. SP1 is more distal (nail corner, ~3-4 cm).
SP3 is more proximal (metatarsal head, ~2 cm). Three SP points in
sequence along the medial big toe / 1st metatarsal, all at the
red-white skin junction (赤白肉际).

Needle depth: 5-10 mm perpendicular. Periosteum of proximal phalanx
nearby — limit depth.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Proper plantar digital nerve of the hallux — from the medial plantar
  nerve (L4-L5), same nerve branch as SP1 but more proximal along its
  course
- Saphenous nerve (L3-L4) — cutaneous contribution on dorsomedial
  surface
- Spinal segments: L3-L5
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: medial plantar digital artery. First dorsal metatarsal artery
branch on dorsal aspect. Great saphenous vein tributaries superficially.

Tissue layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous (slightly thicker than SP1)
- Joint capsule of 1st MTP joint (avoid penetration)
- Flexor hallucis brevis tendon (plantar)
- Periosteum of proximal phalanx / metatarsal head

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Ying-Spring (Fire)** — 2nd of Five Shu on the Spleen
channel. Lingshu: "Where Qi glides is called Spring" (所溜为荥).

Fire on Earth (Spleen) = **TONIFICATION** point. Fire generates Earth
in the Sheng cycle (火生土) — Fire is the MOTHER of Earth. Nan Jing
Difficulty 69: "for deficiency, tonify the mother" (虚则补其母).
SP2 is the theoretical point for tonifying Spleen Qi deficiency.

Tonification point position varies by organ element:

| Organ | Element | Tonification point | Five Shu position |
|---|---|---|---|
| LR | Wood | LR8 (Water) | Sea |
| HT | Fire | HT9 (Wood) | Well |
| PC | Fire | PC9 (Wood) | Well |
| **SP** | **Earth** | **SP2 (Fire)** | **Spring** |
| LU | Metal | LU9 (Earth) | Stream |

Each non-Fire organ's tonification falls at a DIFFERENT Five Shu
position — no two share the same position for this role.

Nan Jing Difficulty 68: "Spring points treat Heat in the body"
(荥主身热) — SP2 classically selected for febrile conditions on
the Spleen channel (dampness-heat patterns).

Yuan-Source: No (SP3)
Luo-Connecting: No (SP4)
Xi-Cleft: No (SP8)

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SP2 Dadu. Ying-Spring of Spleen. Functions:
strengthens Spleen, clears dampness-heat, treats gastric pain,
abdominal distension.

**2. Trigger point:** No significant muscle trigger point at big toe
medial — no muscle belly present.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in hallux valgus (bunion), 1st MTP
arthritis, gout (MTP joint is the classic gout predilection site —
podagra).

**4. Marma point:** Kshipra Marma (foot) at 1st web space — proximal
to SP2. No direct Marma at MTP joint.

**5. Head zone:** L4-L5 dermatome. No Spleen viscerotome overlap.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** SFL at big toe extensors. SP2 at medial MTP
is lateral to the primary SFL track.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data at big toe MTP.

**8. Cupping point:** Not applicable (digit/joint).

**Convergence score: 2/8** (TCM, Ashi partial). Consistent with
foot points distal to ankle.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Nan Jing Difficulty 69: tonification point for Spleen deficiency
  (虚则补其母 → supplement Fire point SP2)
- Nan Jing Difficulty 68: Spring points treat Heat in the body
  (荥主身热) — for febrile Spleen conditions
- Jiayi Jing: fever with aversion to cold, abdominal distension,

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Strengthens Spleen and Stomach — gastric pain, abdominal distension,

- Clears dampness-heat — febrile disease, heaviness, edema

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SP2 individually. The tonification
role is a theoretical Five Element assignment without modern
validation. In practice, Spleen tonification is achieved through
SP6 + ST36 (both with some clinical evidence) rather than through
the formal tonification point SP2.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

LOW risk. Foot location, no major vessels or nerves.

Precautions:

- Caution in active gout at 1st MTP (avoid needling inflamed joint
  tissue — can exacerbate inflammation)

## SP5 — Thương Khâu (商丘, Shāng Qiū)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
Jing-River (Metal) point of the Spleen channel and **SEDATION** point —
Earth generates Metal in the Sheng cycle (土生金), making Metal the
child element of Earth. Located in the depression anterior-inferior to
the medial malleolus. The name 商 (Shāng) corresponds to Metal in
Five Tones theory (宫商角徵羽 = Earth Metal Wood Fire Water), encoding
the point's elemental assignment in its name. Completes the Five Shu
set for the Spleen channel — the 5th complete Yin organ Five Shu set
in the Atlas. At the ankle, SP5 sits within a high-density Three-Yin
convergence zone: LR4 (Zhongfeng, ~0.5-1 cun anterior), KI3 (Taixi,
posterior), and KI6 (Zhaohai, directly below) — four points from three
Yin channels surrounding one bony landmark.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): in the depression distal and
inferior to the medial malleolus, midway between the tuberosity of
the navicular bone and the tip of the medial malleolus.

Anatomical landmarks: the medial malleolus is the prominent bony
landmark on the medial ankle. SP5 lies in the depression just
anterior-inferior to its tip. The navicular tuberosity is palpable
~2 cm distal-inferior — SP5 is midway between these two prominences.

Nearby points (Three-Yin ankle cluster):
- LR4 (Zhongfeng): anterior to medial malleolus, medial to tibialis
  anterior tendon (~0.5-1 cun from SP5)
- KI3 (Taixi): posterior to medial malleolus (between malleolus and
  Achilles tendon)
- KI6 (Zhaohai): directly below the medial malleolus tip
- ST41 (Jiexi): at anterior ankle joint, more lateral

Needle depth: 5-10 mm perpendicular. Thin soft tissue over bony
prominence — periosteal contact common.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Saphenous nerve (L3-L4) — dominant cutaneous nerve of the medial
  ankle. Purely sensory branch of the femoral nerve. This marks the
  TRANSITION from medial plantar nerve territory (SP1-SP2) to
  saphenous territory (SP5 onward through medial leg). SP3 sits at
  the boundary between the two nerve territories.
- No major motor nerve at this superficial location
- Spinal segments: L3-L4
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: medial malleolar artery (branch of anterior tibial artery).
Great saphenous vein passes anterior to the medial malleolus —
visible, palpable, easily avoided.

Tissue layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → thin subcutaneous (bony prominence = minimal soft tissue)
- Inferior extensor retinaculum (fibrous band anchoring ankle tendons)
- Tibialis anterior tendon (anterolateral, not directly at SP5)
- Periosteum of talus/navicular (floor)
No significant muscle belly — tendinous territory.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: **Jing-River (Metal)** — 4th of Five Shu on the Spleen
channel. Lingshu: "Where Qi passes through is called River" (所行为经).

the Sheng cycle (土生金) — Metal is the CHILD of Earth. Nan Jing
Difficulty 69: "for excess, sedate the child" (实则泻其子). SP5 is the
point for draining excess from the Spleen (dampness-accumulation,
dampness-heat patterns).

Nan Jing Difficulty 68: "River points treat cough and chills/fever"
(经主喘咳寒热) — SP5 classically for Spleen-related dyspnea or cough

**Five Shu COMPLETE for SP — 5th Yin organ set:**

## SP7 — Lậu Cốc (漏谷, Lòu Gǔ)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Leaking Valley" — located 6 cun above the medial malleolus on the
posterior border of the medial tibia. No special point category. SP7
sits precisely midway between two of the most clinically important
points on the Spleen channel: SP6 (Sanyinjiao, 3 cun above malleolus)
and SP9 (Yinlingquan, at medial knee). Same neural territory as both
neighbors (saphenous nerve superficial, tibial nerve deep), same
anatomical corridor (posterior medial tibial border), yet SP7 has
minimal independent clinical significance — overshadowed by both
adjacent major points. Primary traditional indications: abdominal
distension, borborygmus, lower limb edema.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the tibial aspect of the leg,
6 cun above the prominence of the medial malleolus, on the posterior
border of the medial aspect of the tibia.

Anatomical landmarks: midway between SP6 (3 cun above malleolus) and
the medial tibial condyle (SP9 at knee level). On the posterior
border of the medial tibial surface — the same anatomical corridor as
SP6 (3 cun below) and SP8 (3 cun above, at 9 cun). The posterior
medial tibial border is a continuous palpable ridge defining the SP
channel's leg segment.

Needle depth: 15-25 mm perpendicular insertion. Similar soft tissue
depth to SP6 — moderate muscle bulk between posterior tibia and
calf muscles.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Saphenous nerve (L3-L4) — primary cutaneous nerve. At 6 cun above
  the malleolus, the saphenous nerve runs superficially along the
  medial tibial border. This is the same nerve accessed at SP5, SP6,
  SP8, and SP9 — five consecutive SP points sharing saphenous
  cutaneous innervation, the longest uninterrupted same-nerve corridor
  on any documented channel.
- Deep: posterior tibial nerve (L4-S3) — deep to the soleus and
  flexor digitorum longus. At SP7 level, the tibial nerve is slightly
  more accessible than at SP6 (where FDL is thicker) but less
  accessible than at SP9 (where muscle thins over tibial condyle).
- Spinal segments: L3-L4 (saphenous) + L4-S3 (tibial, deep)

nerve. Great saphenous vein superficially on the medial leg surface.

Tissue layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (saphenous nerve, great saphenous vein)
- Crural fascia
- Flexor digitorum longus (primary muscle at this depth)
- Tibialis posterior (deepest, against posterior tibia/interosseous
  membrane)
- Posterior tibial nerve and vessels (between FDL and tibialis
  posterior)

Yuan-Source: No (SP3)
Luo-Connecting: No (SP4)
Xi-Cleft: No (SP8)

## SP11 — Cơ Môn (箕门, Jī Mén)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Winnowing Gate" — located on the medial thigh, 6 cun above SP10
(Xuehai), on the line connecting SP10 and SP12 (Chongmen), between
the sartorius and vastus medialis muscles. No special point category.
Positioned directly over the adductor canal (subsartorial canal) —
the anatomical corridor through which the femoral artery, vein, and
saphenous nerve transit from the femoral triangle to the popliteal
fossa. The name 箕 (jī, winnowing basket) likely refers to the
V-shaped groove formed between sartorius and vastus medialis at this
level. Primary traditional indications: urinary conditions, medial
thigh pain.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the medial aspect of the thigh,
6 cun above SP10 (Xuehai), on the line connecting SP10 and SP12.

Anatomical landmarks: SP10 is 2 cun above the medial patella border
on the vastus medialis. Measure 6 cun proximally along the medial
thigh — SP11 is at approximately mid-thigh level. In the groove
between the sartorius (anterior, running obliquely from ASIS to
medial tibia) and the vastus medialis/adductor group (posterior).
With the hip flexed and externally rotated, the sartorius muscle
becomes palpable as an oblique band — SP11 lies just posterior to it.

SP10 (2 cun above patella) → SP11 (8 cun above patella) → SP12
(inguinal groove): three points tracing the medial thigh from distal
to proximal, with escalating vascular risk at each step.

Needle depth: 15-25 mm perpendicular. Adequate muscle bulk between
sartorius and vastus medialis. Deep needling risks approaching the
adductor canal contents.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Anterior femoral cutaneous nerve (L2-L3) — primary cutaneous nerve
  at mid-medial-thigh. This is the neural continuation of the SP10
  transition zone: the saphenous nerve (L3-L4), dominant at SP10 and
  below, has descended into the adductor canal and is no longer the
  primary superficial nerve.
- Saphenous nerve (L3-L4) — now DEEP within the adductor canal,
  beneath the sartorius. Accessible with deep needling but not the
  superficial target.
- Deep: femoral nerve motor branches (L2-L4) — nerve to vastus
  medialis continues from SP10 level. Obturator nerve (L2-L4)
  branches accessible at the posterior border of the adductor
  compartment.
- Spinal segments: L2-L4
→ Cross-ref: A01 S2.3

Vascular: **superficial femoral artery and vein within the adductor
canal** — protected by the sartorius muscle and the vastoadductor
(subsartorial) membrane. At SP11, the femoral vessels are deep and
partially shielded — contrasting with SP12 where the vessels are
SUPERFICIAL in the femoral triangle. Great saphenous vein runs
superficially on the medial thigh.

Tissue layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (anterior femoral cutaneous nerve,
  great saphenous vein)
- Fascia lata
- Sartorius muscle (oblique, forms the roof of the adductor canal)
- Vastoadductor membrane
- Adductor canal contents: femoral artery, femoral vein, saphenous
  nerve, nerve to vastus medialis
- Vastus medialis (anterolateral) / adductor longus-magnus (posterior)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No.
Yuan-Source: No (SP3)
Luo-Connecting: No (SP4)
Xi-Cleft: No (SP8)
No special category designation.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SP11 Jimen. Functions: regulates urination,
clears Damp-Heat from the Lower Jiao.

**2. Trigger point:** Sartorius and vastus medialis trigger points
at mid-thigh level — Dorsher maps the medial mid-thigh region.
Sartorius TrP refers pain to the anterior thigh and medial knee
(consistent with the muscle's oblique trajectory).

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in adductor strain (proximal),
sartorius tendinopathy, medial thigh contusion. Meralgia
paresthetica affects the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (more
lateral) but medial thigh pain may accompany adductor pathology.

**4. Marma point:** **Urvi Marma** (ऊर्वि) — mid-thigh Marma point.
Sira (blood vessel) Marma, 1 angula, vaikalyakara (deformity-causing
if damaged). Overlaps with SP11's mid-thigh location and vascular
significance (femoral vessels in adductor canal beneath).

**5. Head zone:** L2-L3 dermatome. Kidney/ureter referred zone
(T10-L1) is superior — partial overlap with urogenital indications
through lumbar plexus segmental connections.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Line (DFL) — the adductor group
(deep to SP11) is a core DFL component. Sartorius is NOT part of
SFL or DFL — it is an independent oblique muscle bridging hip flexion
and knee medial rotation.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No specific data at mid-medial-
thigh.

**8. Cupping point:** Applicable (sufficient muscle bulk).

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Trigger Point/Ashi, Marma). Urvi
Marma provides the strongest non-TCM correspondence.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: urinary retention, enuresis, inguinal swelling, medial
  thigh pain
- Zhen Jiu Da Cheng: urinary difficulty, genital conditions

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Regulates urination — urinary retention, enuresis, dysuria
- Clears Damp-Heat — inguinal lymphadenopathy, genital itching
- Local: medial thigh pain, numbness

Classical combinations:
- SP11 + SP9 + SP6: urinary disorders (Three-Yin diuresis pathway)
- SP11 + LR10: medial thigh pain (cross-channel local pairing)

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SP11 individually. The adductor canal
region is clinically significant in modern anesthesiology — adductor
canal nerve block targets the saphenous nerve within this canal for
knee surgery analgesia — but this is a different mechanism from
traditional acupuncture at SP11, and SP11 is not studied in this
context.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

MODERATE risk. Femoral vessels within the adductor canal are deep and

Precautions:

- Great saphenous vein superficially — minor hematoma risk
- If pulsation is transmitted through the needle: withdraw and
  reposition laterally
- SAFER than SP12 (vessels here are deep and protected) but requires
  anatomical awareness of the adductor canal trajectory

## SP12 — Xung Môn (冲门, Chōng Mén)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Rushing Gate" — located at the inguinal groove, 3.5 cun lateral to
the anterior midline, on the LATERAL side of the femoral artery. The
name 冲 (chōng, rush/surge) likely refers to the palpable femoral
arterial pulsation at this location — compare LR12 (Jimai, "Urgent
Pulse") on the MEDIAL side of the same vessels. SP12 and LR12 flank
the femoral neurovascular bundle from opposite sides: the medial-
to-lateral arrangement at the inguinal crease is LR12 → femoral
Vein → femoral Artery → femoral Nerve → SP12. Two channels bracketing
one vascular bundle. The inguinal ligament at this level marks the
boundary between lower limb and abdomen — SP12 is where the Spleen
channel transitions from thigh to trunk. HIGH VASCULAR RISK.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the inguinal groove, 3.5 cun
lateral to CV2 (Qugu), lateral to the femoral artery where the
pulsation is palpable.

Anatomical landmarks: palpate the femoral artery pulse at the
inguinal crease — the strong pulsation is the primary landmark. SP12
is on the LATERAL side of this pulsation. Compare: LR12 (2.5 cun
lateral to CV2) is on the MEDIAL side. ST30 (Qichong, 2 cun lateral)
is more medial and slightly superior.

Spatial arrangement at inguinal crease (medial → lateral):
- LR12 (Liver) → femoral Vein → femoral Artery → femoral Nerve →
  SP12 (Spleen)
- Two different Yin channels bracket the same vessels

SP11 is 6 cun distally (mid-thigh) → SP12 at inguinal groove: the
femoral vessels transition from deep-and-protected (adductor canal
at SP11) to SUPERFICIAL-AND-EXPOSED (femoral triangle at SP12).

Needle depth: 10-15 mm perpendicular or oblique LATERALLY (away from
femoral vessels). Shallow insertion mandatory.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- Ilioinguinal nerve (L1) — emerges from external inguinal ring,
  sensory to inguinal skin. Primary cutaneous nerve at SP12's
  location.
- Femoral nerve (L2-L4) — LATERAL to the femoral artery (mnemonic:
  NAVEL = Nerve Artery Vein Empty-space Lymphatics, lateral to
  medial). SP12 is on the same side as the femoral nerve — deep
  needling risks femoral nerve contact.
- Genitofemoral nerve (L1-L2) — femoral branch travels on the
  anterior surface of the femoral artery
- Spinal segments: L1-L4. SP12 marks the transition from lower-limb
  innervation (L2-L4 femoral at SP10-SP11) to trunk innervation (L1
  ilioinguinal). The inguinal ligament corresponds to the
  embryological boundary between limb bud and body wall.
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1

Vascular: **femoral artery immediately medial** — strong pulsation.
**Femoral vein medial to the artery.** Same vessels as LR12 but
approached from the opposite side. **VASCULAR RISK: HIGH — highest
on the Spleen channel.**
→ Cross-ref: A04

Tissue layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue (Camper's and Scarpa's fascia)
- Inguinal ligament / fascia lata
- Iliopsoas muscle (deep lateral)
- Femoral nerve (lateral to artery)
- Pectineus muscle (deep medial)
- Femoral vessels (medial to the point)

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No.
Yuan-Source: No (SP3)
Luo-Connecting: No (SP4)
Xi-Cleft: No (SP8)
No standard category designation.

Some classical sources list SP12 as a meeting point of Spleen with
Liver channel, but this is inconsistently cited. The more standard
Yin Wei Mai intersections on the Spleen channel are SP13, SP15,
and SP16.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SP12 Chongmen. Functions: regulates Qi,
alleviates pain, benefits the Lower Jiao.

**2. Trigger point:** Iliopsoas TrP — refers pain to groin and
anterior thigh. Pectineus TrP — deep groin pain. Both share the
inguinal anatomical region with SP12.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in inguinal hernia, inguinal
lymphadenopathy, groin strain (adductor origin), hip flexor
tendinopathy at the inguinal ligament.

**4. Marma point:** **Lohitaksha Marma** (लोहिताक्ष) — Sira (blood
vessel) Marma at the root of the thigh, defined by the palpable
femoral arterial pulse. The same Marma that corresponds with LR12.
Both SP12 and LR12 fall within Lohitaksha — from opposite sides
of the same vessels, two channels converge on one Marma zone.
→ Cross-ref: LR12 entry (Lohitaksha = strongest Marma-acupoint
correspondence in the Liver thigh group)

**5. Head zone:** L1-L2 dermatome. Approaches kidney/ureter referred
zone (T10-L1). The inguinal region is the segmental boundary between
lower limb (L2+) and trunk (T12-).
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Deep Front Line (DFL) — iliopsoas at SP12
level is the DFL's central corridor transitioning from lower limb
(adductors/pectineus) to trunk (psoas major → diaphragm →
mediastinum). SP12 accesses this critical DFL junction between
limb and trunk components.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No specific data at inguinal
region.

**8. Cupping point:** Contraindicated — vascular risk.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, Trigger Point/Ashi, Marma, Anatomy
Trains). Same convergence level as LR12 — both inguinal points share
Lohitaksha Marma and DFL junction access.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: abdominal pain, hernia (shan qi), lower abdominal
  fullness, urinary retention, uterine prolapse
- Ling Shu: the Spleen channel "ascends the medial thigh, enters
  the abdomen" — SP12 sits at this limb-to-trunk transition

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Regulates Qi and alleviates pain — abdominal pain, hernia
  (inguinal canal proximity provides anatomical basis)
- Benefits the Lower Jiao — urinary retention, genital conditions,
  uterine prolapse
- Local: inguinal pain, groin swelling

Classical combinations:
- SP12 + ST30 + CV3: inguinal hernia (three inguinal points from
  three channels converging)
- SP12 + SP6 + LR3: lower abdominal pain with Liver Qi stagnation
- SP12 + LR12: bilateral inguinal conditions — flanking the vessels
  from both sides. Two Blood-aware channels (Spleen holds Blood,
  Liver stores Blood) meeting at the femoral pulse

Comparison with LR12:
- SP12 (lateral to artery): regulates Qi, general abdominal/hernia
- LR12 (medial to vein): disperses Cold from Liver, genital/hernia

  Qi/transport failure, Liver = Cold/sinew contraction
- LR12 has classical needling prohibition (Su Wen); SP12 does not —

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SP12 individually. Inguinal-region
acupuncture appears in some conservative TCM hernia-management
protocols, but SP12 is not studied in isolation. The proximity to
the femoral nerve has theoretical significance for nerve-stimulation-
based analgesia, but no data exists.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**HIGH risk — highest vascular risk point on the Spleen channel.**

Femoral vessel proximity: femoral artery immediately medial — strong
pulsation palpable. Accidental puncture: hemorrhage, hematoma,
pseudoaneurysm. Same vascular structures as LR12 but approached
from the lateral side.

Mandatory precautions:
- ALWAYS palpate the femoral artery pulse before insertion
- Insert LATERAL to the pulse, angling AWAY from vessels
- Shallow insertion ONLY (10-15 mm maximum)
- If arterial pulsation felt through the needle: withdraw immediately
- Monitor for hematoma post-treatment (apply pressure 3-5 minutes)

Comparison with LR12:
- Both HIGH vascular risk at the inguinal crease
- SP12 lateral approach: closer to femoral NERVE (risk of nerve
  stimulation/injury, sharp radiating pain)
- LR12 medial approach: closer to femoral VEIN (risk of venous
  puncture, hematoma)
- LR12 has classical needling prohibition (Su Wen); SP12 does NOT —
  an asymmetry in safety coding despite equivalent anatomical risk

## SP15 — Đại Hoành (大横, Dà Héng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Great Horizontal" — at the umbilical level, 4 cun lateral to
midline. Meeting point of Spleen and Yin Wei Mai. The name 横
(héng, horizontal) describes the transverse orientation at the
umbilical plane where the channel's vertical trajectory crosses the
body's most reliably identifiable abdominal landmark. T10 intercostal
nerve — the same segmental level as the umbilical dermatome, one of

the MORE clinically used abdominal SP points, with relatively

parallel vertical corridors: CV8 (midline), KI16 (0.5 cun), ST25

palm-widths) lateral. At this level, the lateral border of rectus
abdominis is approximately 2-3 cun from midline — SP15 at 4 cun is
LATERAL to the rectus, in the oblique muscle territory.

Parallel points at umbilical level:
- CV8 Shenque (midline) — moxa only, needle-prohibited
- KI16 Huangshu (0.5 cun lateral)
- ST25 Tianshu (2 cun lateral) — Front-Mu of Large Intestine
- SP15 Daheng (4 cun lateral)

Nerve supply:

  lateral are the primary sensory nerves. T10 = the umbilical

- Spinal segments: T10

branch of deep circumflex iliac artery at the lateral margin.

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

## SP16 — Phúc Ai (腹哀, Fù Āi)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Abdominal Lament" — upper abdomen, 4 cun lateral to midline, 3 cun
above the umbilicus. Meeting point of Spleen and Yin Wei Mai.
Uppermost of the 4 abdominal SP points. T8-T9 intercostal nerve
territory, which PARTIALLY OVERLAPS the Spleen viscerotome (T6-T8)
at T8 — making SP16 the closest SP abdominal point to segmental
correspondence with the Spleen organ. Yet this partial overlap does
not generate stronger organ-specific indications than distant leg
points (SP3, SP6, SP9). Near LR13 (Front-Mu of Spleen, at 11th rib
tip) — both in the upper-lateral abdominal region but from different
channels, with LR13 carrying the diagnostic function that SP16 lacks.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the upper abdomen, 4 cun
lateral to the anterior midline, 3 cun above the center of the
umbilicus.

Anatomical landmarks: at the level of CV12 (Zhongwan, Front-Mu of
Stomach) — SP16 is the lateral counterpart. 3 cun above SP15
(umbilical level). Approaching the costal margin (the inferior rib
border is typically 1-2 cun above SP16 depending on body habitus).

Spatial relationship with LR13 (Zhangmen, Front-Mu of Spleen): SP16
is more anterior (4 cun from midline) while LR13 is more lateral
(at the free end of the 11th rib, variable but typically 8-10 cun
from midline). Both address Spleen-related upper abdominal conditions
but LR13 carries the Front-Mu diagnostic designation.

Needle depth: 10-20 mm oblique or perpendicular. Approaching the
costal margin — verify ribs are not palpable at insertion point.

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- 8th and 9th intercostal nerves (T8-T9) — lateral cutaneous
  branches. T8 PARTIALLY OVERLAPS the Spleen viscerotome (T6-T8).
  This is the closest segmental approach of any SP abdominal point:
  SP13 = T12-L1, SP14 = T11-T12, SP15 = T10, SP16 = T8-T9.
  Ascending through the abdominal group, the segment progressively
  approaches the Spleen organ's visceral referral zone.
- Spinal segments: T8-T9
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A07 S2.2

Vascular: 8th-9th intercostal artery branches. Superior epigastric
artery (continuation of internal thoracic) runs more medially within
the rectus sheath — SP16 at 4 cun lateral is outside this vessel's
typical course.

Tissue layers: same three-layer abdominal wall. At this upper level,
the muscle layers become thinner as they approach their costal
attachments. Deep structures: stomach (central/left), transverse
colon, liver (right side) or spleen (left side) depending on organ
size.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

**Intersection point: meeting of Spleen and Yin Wei Mai**
(足太阴、阴维之会). Third of three Yin Wei Mai intersections on
the SP abdominal segment.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SP16 Fuai. Functions: regulates intestinal Qi,
disperses food accumulation, alleviates upper abdominal pain.

**2. Trigger point:** Upper external oblique and rectus abdominis
TrPs at subcostal level — referred pain to upper abdomen and
epigastrium.

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Tender in gastritis, peptic ulcer
(upper abdominal tenderness), upper abdominal cramping. Right-side
tenderness may overlap with hepatobiliary conditions.

**4. Marma point:** No precise Marma at this upper-lateral
abdominal location. Distant from both Nabhi (umbilical) and Hridaya
(cardiac region).

**5. Head zone:** T8-T9 dermatome. Spleen viscerotome (T6-T8)
PARTIALLY OVERLAPS at T8. Stomach viscerotome (T6-T9) also overlaps.
This is the first SP abdominal point with any visceral referred
pain zone overlap for the Spleen organ.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line — oblique muscles at upper
abdominal level.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Applicable (if sufficiently distant from
costal margin).

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Trigger Point/Ashi, Anatomy
Trains). Uniform convergence profile across the abdominal group.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: upper abdominal pain, indigestion, food accumulation,
  diarrhea, dysentery

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Regulates intestinal Qi — upper abdominal pain, dysentery
- Disperses food accumulation — indigestion, undigested food,
  epigastric fullness
- SP16 is classically used for UPPER abdominal and digestive
  conditions, reflecting its proximity to the stomach/spleen organ
  zone

Classical combinations:
- SP16 + CV12 + ST36: epigastric pain with food stagnation (SP16 at
  the same level as CV12, addressing the same region from lateral)
- SP16 + LR13: harmonize Liver and Spleen at the upper abdominal
  level — two channels treating Spleen-related conditions in the
  same body region

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SP16 individually.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

LOW-MODERATE risk. Upper abdominal position approaches the costal
margin.

Precautions:
- At 3 cun above the umbilicus, the costal margin may be nearby
  (especially in shorter torsos) — palpate for ribs before insertion

- Hepatomegaly (right side) or splenomegaly (left side) may bring
  solid organs closer to the anterior wall — check for organomegaly

- SP16 is the TRANSITION point between purely abdominal risk
  (peritoneal puncture) and thoracic risk (pneumothorax) — SP17
  above is in the 5th intercostal space with definite pneumothorax

## SP17 — Thực Đậu (食窦, Shí Dòu)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Food Hole" — 5th intercostal space, 6 cun lateral to the anterior
midline. First point on the chest wall after the SP channel's
transition from abdomen. The channel SHIFTS 2 cun laterally at the
costal margin (from 4 cun at SP13-SP16 to 6 cun at SP17-SP20),
following the chest wall's lateral flare. T5 intercostal nerve
territory — one segment below the Spleen viscerotome (T6-T8). The
name "Food Hole" (食窦) reflects classical association with food
transport and diaphragm-level digestive disorders, bridging
abdominal and thoracic indications. PNEUMOTHORAX RISK — SP17 is the
first SP point with definite pleural cavity access.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral side of the
chest, in the 5th intercostal space, 6 cun lateral to the anterior
midline.

Anatomical landmarks: locate the nipple (4th ICS in males) — SP17
is one intercostal space below and approximately 2 cun lateral to
the nipple. The costal margin (inferior rib border) is immediately
below: SP17 is in the first full intercostal space above the
abdominal-thoracic transition.

Lateral shift: SP16 (last abdominal point) at 4 cun lateral → SP17
at 6 cun lateral = 2 cun lateral jump. The costal margin angles
laterally, and the SP channel follows this contour from the
abdominal rectus/oblique zone onto the lateral chest wall.

Needle depth: oblique insertion 0.3-0.5 cun (8-15 mm) along the
intercostal space. **PERPENDICULAR DEEP INSERTION CONTRAINDICATED
— pneumothorax risk.**

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- 5th intercostal nerve (T5) — lateral cutaneous branch. T5 is one
  segment below the Spleen viscerotome (T6-T8) and enters the
  cardiac viscerotome (T1-T5) at its lower border.
- Spinal segments: T5
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A07 S2.2

Vascular: 5th intercostal artery in the costal groove along the
inferior border of the 5th rib. Lateral thoracic artery branches
may contribute at this level. Insert needle SUPERIOR to the lower
rib to avoid the neurovascular bundle.

Tissue layers (superficial → deep):
- Skin → subcutaneous tissue
- Serratus anterior (at 6 cun lateral, 5th ICS — serratus
  digitations predominate over pectoralis major at this level)
- External intercostals → internal intercostals → innermost
  intercostals
- Endothoracic fascia → parietal pleura → **PLEURAL CAVITY (LUNG)**

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

No special category. Unlike SP13/SP15/SP16 (Yin Wei Mai
intersections), SP17 has no extraordinary vessel intersection —
the Yin Wei Mai pathway terminated at SP16 on the abdominal wall.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SP17 Shidou (食窦, "Food Hole"). Functions:
regulates Qi, unbinds the chest, benefits the diaphragm, resolves
food stagnation. The name emphasizes digestive function despite
chest wall location. 🟢

**2. Trigger point:** Serratus anterior TrP at 5th rib digitation
— referred pain along the lateral chest wall and to the inferior
angle of the scapula. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Chest wall tenderness at the 5th ICS
in intercostal neuralgia, pleurisy, post-thoracotomy pain. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No precise Marma at this anterolateral chest
location.

**5. Head zone:** T5 dermatome. One segment below Spleen
viscerotome (T6-T8). Overlaps cardiac viscerotome (T1-T5) at its
lower border.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line — serratus anterior and
intercostal fascia at the 5th ICS.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Limited by rib contour. Flash cupping
possible with moderate cup adhesion.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi). Low
convergence, consistent with Tier C.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: chest pain, hypochondriac fullness, inability to
  lie flat, difficulty swallowing
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: chest and lateral costal pain, belching, acid
  reflux

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Unbinds the chest — chest fullness, oppression, difficulty
  breathing
- Regulates Stomach Qi descent — belching, nausea, acid reflux
- Benefits the diaphragm — hiccough, diaphragmatic discomfort
- SP17's indications BRIDGE digestive (food stagnation, belching)
  and respiratory (chest fullness), reflecting the diaphragm-level
  transition between abdominal and thoracic cavities

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SP17 individually.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**HIGH RISK — PNEUMOTHORAX ZONE.**

SP17 is the first SP point with definite pneumothorax risk. SP16
(above costal margin) carries peritoneal puncture risk; SP17
(in the 5th ICS) carries pleural puncture risk — the costal margin
is the BOUNDARY between these two risk domains.

Precautions:
- **Oblique insertion ONLY** — 0.3-0.5 cun (8-15 mm) along the
  intercostal space
- **NEVER perpendicular deep insertion** — lung parenchyma lies
  directly deep to the intercostal muscles
- Chest wall thickness at 6 cun lateral, 5th ICS: typically
  15-30 mm (varies with body habitus). In thin individuals, as
  little as 10-15 mm
- Insert needle SUPERIOR to lower rib to avoid intercostal
  neurovascular bundle (artery, vein, nerve in costal groove)
- In females: breast tissue may extend to the 5th ICS at this
  lateral position — displacement may be needed for accurate
  location
→ Cross-ref: SP21 safety section

anatomical position in the mammary gland zone. T4 overlaps both

chest, in the 4th intercostal space, 6 cun lateral to the anterior
midline.

4th ICS). One intercostal space above SP17. In females, the point
may be within or deep to breast tissue, requiring breast
displacement for accurate needling.

Needle depth: oblique insertion 0.3-0.5 cun (8-15 mm).
**PERPENDICULAR DEEP INSERTION CONTRAINDICATED — pneumothorax
risk.**

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- 4th intercostal nerve (T4) — lateral cutaneous branch. The
  anterior cutaneous branch of T4 innervates the nipple. T4 is

- Spinal segments: T4
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A07 S2.2

chest points. 🟢

anterior TrPs also contribute. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Breast tenderness in mastitis,
premenstrual breast swelling, cyclical mastalgia correlates with
this mammary zone location. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** Stanamuula (स्तनमूल, breast root) — classified
as Sira Marma, 2 angula. Located at the base of the breast.
SP18's position at the 4th ICS near the breast base approximates
this Marma. Injury effects include respiratory difficulty and
cough, matching SP18's traditional respiratory indications. 🟡

**5. Head zone:** T4 dermatome. Nipple level. Cardiac viscerotome
overlap (T1-T5). Lung viscerotome overlap (T3-T5).
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line + Arm Lines — pectoralis major
at this level bridges trunk to shoulder fascial continuity.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Limited by rib contour and breast tissue.

**Convergence score: 4/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi, Marma).

- Zhenjiu Dacheng: chest and lateral costal pain, breast pain,

No systematic review or RCT for SP18 individually.

## SP19 — Hung Hương (胸乡, Xiōng Xiāng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"Chest Village" — 3rd intercostal space, 6 cun lateral to the
anterior midline. T3 intercostal nerve territory. The 3rd ICS
enters the upper chest region where T3 overlaps both the cardiac
viscerotome (T1-T5) and the lung viscerotome (T3-T5). SP19 has
the FEWEST classical indications among SP chest points — a
clinically inconspicuous point positioned between more prominent
neighbors. PNEUMOTHORAX RISK.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral side of the
chest, in the 3rd intercostal space, 6 cun lateral to the anterior
midline.

Anatomical landmarks: one intercostal space above SP18, two above
SP17. At the 3rd ICS, pectoralis major is well developed.

Needle depth: oblique insertion 0.3-0.5 cun (8-15 mm).
**PERPENDICULAR DEEP INSERTION CONTRAINDICATED — pneumothorax
risk.**

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- 3rd intercostal nerve (T3) — lateral cutaneous branch. T3 is a
  key cardiac referred pain segment: cardiac chest pain often
  radiates through T3-T4 to the anterior chest and medial arm.
- Spinal segments: T3
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A07 S2.2

Vascular: 3rd intercostal artery. Insert SUPERIOR to lower rib.

Tissue layers: pectoralis major is the primary superficial muscle
at 6 cun lateral, 3rd ICS. Serratus anterior lies deeper at this
level. External → internal → innermost intercostals → pleura →
**PLEURAL CAVITY (LUNG).**

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

No special category.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SP19 Xiongxiang (胸乡, "Chest Village").
Functions: unbinds the chest, alleviates chest and hypochondriac
pain. Minimal functional specificity. 🟢

**2. Trigger point:** Pectoralis major TrP — sternal/costal fibers
at the 3rd ICS level refer pain to the anterior chest. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Upper chest wall tenderness in
intercostal neuralgia. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No precise Marma at this location.

**5. Head zone:** T3 dermatome. Cardiac viscerotome overlap
(T1-T5). Lung viscerotome overlap (T3-T5). T3 = classic cardiac
referred pain dermatome.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line + Arm Lines — pectoralis major
bridges trunk to shoulder/arm continuity.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Upper chest — possible with smaller cups.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi). Minimal
convergence. Lowest-profile point in the chest group.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: chest pain, fullness of the chest, lateral costal
  pain

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Unbinds the chest — chest pain, fullness, oppression
- Spreads Liver Qi — hypochondriac pain
- SP19 has the FEWEST classical indications among SP chest points,
  reflecting its generic "chest pain" function without the
  diaphragmatic specificity of SP17 or the respiratory specificity
  of SP20

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SP19 individually.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**HIGH RISK — PNEUMOTHORAX ZONE.**

Same precautions as all SP chest points:
- Oblique insertion ONLY — 0.3-0.5 cun (8-15 mm)
- Insert SUPERIOR to lower rib
- NEVER perpendicular deep insertion — lung directly deep
→ Cross-ref: SP21 safety section

## SP20 — Chu Vinh (周荣, Zhōu Róng)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable)**
"All-Round Flourishing" — 2nd intercostal space, 6 cun lateral to
the anterior midline. T2 intercostal nerve territory. The MOST
SUPERIOR point on the SP chest wall (SP17-SP20), and the
second-to-last SP point before SP21. T2's lateral cutaneous branch
is the INTERCOSTOBRACHIAL NERVE, which provides sensory innervation
to the axilla and medial arm — linking the chest wall to the upper
extremity. SP20 at the 2nd ICS is directly inferior to LU1 (Trung
Phủ) at the 1st ICS, also at 6 cun lateral — and LU1 is an
intersection point of both the Lung AND Spleen channels. From SP20,
the channel REVERSES direction: descending 4 intercostal spaces and
shifting laterally to terminate at SP21 (6th ICS, mid-axillary
line), creating a U-SHAPED pathway on the lateral chest.
PNEUMOTHORAX RISK.

### Vị trí giải phẫu

WHO standard location (WPRO 2008): on the lateral side of the
chest, in the 2nd intercostal space, 6 cun lateral to the anterior
midline.

Anatomical landmarks: at the level of the sternal angle (angle of
Louis), a reliable bony landmark at the 2nd rib/2nd ICS level.
One intercostal space above SP19 and one below LU1 (1st ICS, 6 cun
lateral — Lung-Spleen intersection point). The infraclavicular
fossa is directly above.

Spatial relationship with LU1: SP20 (2nd ICS, 6 cun lateral) and
LU1 (1st ICS, 6 cun lateral) are separated by exactly one
intercostal space on the SAME lateral line. LU1 is an intersection
point of the Lung and Spleen channels — the SP channel's ascending
pathway on the chest wall leads directly toward this convergence
point before reversing to SP21.

Needle depth: oblique insertion 0.3-0.5 cun (8-15 mm).
**PERPENDICULAR DEEP INSERTION CONTRAINDICATED — pneumothorax
risk.**

### Cấu trúc mô học tại điểm

Nerve supply:
- 2nd intercostal nerve (T2) — the lateral cutaneous branch of T2
  is the INTERCOSTOBRACHIAL NERVE, which crosses the axilla to
  provide sensory innervation to the medial arm and axillary skin.
  This is the anatomical basis for cardiac pain referral to the
  medial arm (classical "heart attack arm pain" pathway).
- T2 = highest thoracic segment reached by the SP channel on the
  chest wall. Above this (T1), the brachial plexus territory
  begins.
- Spinal segments: T2
→ Cross-ref: A01 S1, A07 S2.2

Vascular: 2nd intercostal artery. The internal thoracic (mammary)
artery runs approximately 1-2 cm from the sternal border — 6 cun
lateral is well beyond its course. The lateral thoracic artery
(branch of axillary artery) descends in this region. Insert
SUPERIOR to lower rib.

Tissue layers: pectoralis major is well developed at the 2nd ICS,
6 cun lateral. Subclavius muscle lies just above (1st ICS level).
Intercostals → pleura → **PLEURAL CAVITY (LUNG).** The lung apex
extends 2-3 cm above the 1st rib — SP20 at the 2nd ICS is
slightly below the apex but overlies lung parenchyma.

### Phân loại huyệt (Point Category Membership)

Five Shu: No. Yuan-Source: No. Luo: No. Xi-Cleft: No.

No special category.

### Cross-tradition mapping

**1. TCM acupoint:** SP20 Zhourong (周荣, "All-Round Flourishing").
Functions: descends Lung Qi, stops cough, unbinds the chest,
transforms phlegm. Strongest respiratory indications among SP chest
points — correlating with upper lung proximity. 🟢

**2. Trigger point:** Pectoralis major TrP — clavicular and upper
costal fibers refer pain to the anterior chest and deltoid
region. 🟡

**3. Ashi correspondence:** Upper chest wall tenderness in
intercostal neuralgia, costochondritis at the upper ribs. 🟡

**4. Marma point:** No precise Marma at this location. Apalapa
Marma (lateral chest) is described more inferiorly.

**5. Head zone:** T2 dermatome. Cardiac viscerotome overlap
(T1-T5). The intercostobrachial nerve (T2) = anatomical basis for
cardiac pain referral to the medial arm — a well-established
clinical sign.
→ Cross-ref: A07 S2.2

**6. Anatomy Trains:** Lateral Line + Arm Lines — pectoralis major
at the upper chest bridges trunk to shoulder/arm fascial
continuity.
→ Cross-ref: A09 S1.3

**7. Neurogenic inflammatory spot:** No data.

**8. Cupping point:** Upper chest — applicable with smaller cups.

**Convergence score: 3/8** (TCM, Trigger Point, Ashi). SP20's most
distinctive feature is the T2 intercostobrachial nerve anatomy
rather than cross-tradition convergence.

### Chỉ định truyền thống

Nguồn cổ:
- Jiayi Jing: cough, chest fullness, difficulty breathing, phlegm
  accumulation
- Zhenjiu Dacheng: cough with excessive phlegm, chest pain,
  oppression

Nguồn hiện đại (Deadman):
- Descends Lung Qi — cough, wheezing, phlegm accumulation
- Unbinds the chest — chest fullness, difficulty breathing
- SP20 has STRONGER RESPIRATORY indications than lower SP chest
  points (SP17-SP19), consistent with its position in the upper
  chest near the lung apex. The indication gradient across SP17-SP20
  tracks anatomy: digestive (SP17, near diaphragm) → mammary
  (SP18) → generic chest (SP19) → respiratory (SP20, near apex)

### Bằng chứng hiện đại

No systematic review or RCT for SP20 individually.

Evidence: 🔴 Unvalidated for all indications.

### An toàn

**HIGH RISK — PNEUMOTHORAX ZONE.**

Same precautions as all SP chest points:
- Oblique insertion ONLY — 0.3-0.5 cun (8-15 mm)
- Insert SUPERIOR to lower rib
- NEVER perpendicular deep insertion — lung directly deep
- At the 2nd ICS, the lung APEX is at or slightly above this
  level (apex extends 2-3 cm above the 1st rib). Pneumothorax
  risk is significant
→ Cross-ref: SP21 safety section

> **Batch 7 Architectural Observations (SP17, SP18, SP19, SP20 —
> Tier C)**
>
> Obs. 31 — CHEST WALL HOMOGENEITY (SECOND HOMOGENEOUS GROUP):
> SP17-SP20 share identical tissue layers: skin → subcutaneous →
> pectoralis major/serratus anterior → external intercostals →
> internal intercostals → innermost intercostals → pleura. The
> ONLY differentiating variable is the intercostal nerve segment
> (T5, T4, T3, T2). This parallels the abdominal group (Obs. 28,
> SP13-SP16) where differentiation was also purely segmental. The
> SP channel has TWO anatomically homogeneous groups — abdominal
> (4 points, T8-T12) and chest (4 points, T2-T5) — flanking the
> costal margin transition. Together, 8 of 21 SP points are
> differentiated solely by spinal segment.
>
> Obs. 32 — LATERAL SHIFT AT COSTAL MARGIN: SP16 (4 cun lateral,
> upper abdomen) → SP17 (6 cun lateral, 5th ICS): the channel
> shifts 2 cun laterally at the abdomen-chest transition. This is
> the ONLY lateral jump in the SP channel pathway — all other
> point-to-point transitions are primarily vertical (ascending)
> or follow continuous medial surface anatomy (leg/thigh). The
> shift reflects the costal margin's angled anatomy: abdominal
> wall muscles (obliques) attach to the costal margin, and the
> chest wall continues above at a more lateral plane.
>
> Obs. 33 — U-SHAPED REVERSAL ON LATERAL CHEST: SP17 (5th ICS) →
> SP18 (4th) → SP19 (3rd) → SP20 (2nd ICS): ASCENDING. Then SP21
> (6th ICS, mid-axillary line): DESCENDING 4 spaces and shifting
> laterally. The channel reaches its HIGHEST thoracic point at
> SP20 (2nd ICS), then REVERSES to terminate at SP21 BELOW its
> own starting point (SP21 at 6th ICS is one space below SP17 at
> 5th ICS). Combined with the lateral shift (6 cun → mid-axillary),
> this creates a U-shaped pathway unique among documented channels.
> Most channels follow a monotonic direction on the trunk. The
> reversal also places SP20 (2nd ICS, 6 cun lateral) directly
> inferior to LU1 (1st ICS, 6 cun lateral) — a Lung-Spleen
> intersection point — before the channel sweeps away to SP21.
>
> Obs. 34 — MAMMARY ZONE OVERLAY: SP18 (4th ICS, nipple level) is
> positioned within the mammary gland zone in females. SP17 (5th
> ICS) may also be partially overlain by breast tissue. SP18 is
> the ONLY SP point with a classical lactation indication
> (insufficient lactation, mastitis) — anatomically logical given
> direct mammary proximity. This is a gender-specific anatomical
> consideration unique to the chest group and the only
> lactation-related indication on any Yin leg meridian. The
> Stanamuula (breast root) Marma correspondence at SP18 provides
> independent cross-tradition support (4/8 convergence vs 3/8 for
> other chest points).
>
> Obs. 35 — CARDIAC VISCEROTOME ENTRY WITHOUT CARDIAC INDICATIONS:
> SP17 (T5) enters the cardiac viscerotome (T1-T5), and all four
> chest points (SP17-SP20) fall within this range. SP20 at T2 is
> where the intercostobrachial nerve provides the classic "heart
> attack arm pain" referral pathway. Yet NONE of SP17-SP20 have
> cardiac-specific indications in classical sources — their
> indications remain digestive (SP17), mammary (SP18), generic
> chest (SP19), and respiratory (SP20). This extends the segmental
> paradox (Obs. 14, 27): the SP channel consistently fails to
> develop organ-specific indications matching its segmental
> position, whether for Spleen (abdominal points), Heart (chest
> points), or Lung (upper chest points). The indication gradient
> across the chest (digestive → mammary → generic → respiratory)
> tracks local anatomical context rather than viscerotome overlap.
>
