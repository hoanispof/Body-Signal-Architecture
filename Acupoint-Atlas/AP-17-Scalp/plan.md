# AP-17 Scalp Acupuncture Microsystem — Child Plan

## 1. Scope

**File:** AP-17-Scalp.md (new file)
**Standard:** WHO/WPRO International Standard Scalp Acupuncture Nomenclature (1984/1991), Chinese national standard GB/T 21709.2-2008 (revised 2021)
**Total entries:** 18 treatment lines/zones + 2 system overview sections
**Phase 1 done:** 0 entries
**Phase 2 remaining:** 18 entries + overview sections

**Why scalp acupuncture is different from both meridian-based and auricular entries:**
- LINE-based, not POINT-based — each entry is a treatment line (1-4.5 cm strip), not a discrete point
- Based on CORTICAL PROJECTION theory — needling over specific cortical areas is hypothesized to affect those cortical functions
- Multiple competing systems: Jiao Shunfa (1971 original), WHO standard (1984/1991), YNSA (Yamamoto 1973), Zhu Mingqing, Fang Yunpeng, Tang Songyan
- No single-point evidence — clinical studies test LINES and PROTOCOLS, not individual points
- Dominant evidence domain: post-stroke rehabilitation (motor, aphasia, dysphagia)
- Template: microsystem template (plan.md §3.2), adapted for LINE entries
- Innervation: trigeminal (V1/V2/V3) + cervical plexus (C2/C3), NO vagal component (unlike auricular)
- Needle technique: subcutaneous threading at 15-30° along galea aponeurotica (subgaleal space), NOT perpendicular point insertion

**Key methodological decisions:**
1. Use WHO standard MS1-MS14 as primary classification (consistent with AP-16 using Chinese national standard)
2. Cross-reference Jiao zones, YNSA points, Zhu zones within each MS entry
3. Document cortical area beneath each line (primary motor cortex, Broca's area, etc.)
4. Overlapping body acupoints (GV, BL, GB, ST, EX-HN endpoints) = mandatory cross-reference
5. Innervation at each line = mandatory field (trigeminal branch vs cervical nerve)
6. Jiao-only zones without WHO MS equivalent = separate entries after MS14
7. YNSA and Zhu = system overview sections (too different in paradigm for line-by-line integration)

## 2. Scalp Anatomy Overview (for zone header)

### 2.1 SCALP Layers (mnemonic)

| Layer | Name | Thickness | Clinical significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | Skin | 3-8 mm | Thickest skin in body. Entry point for needle. |
| C | Connective tissue (dense subcutaneous) | 4-7 mm | Contains major arteries/veins/nerves. Fibrous septa prevent vessel retraction → profuse bleeding if cut. Needle passes THROUGH quickly. |
| A | Aponeurosis (galea aponeurotica) | 1-2 mm | Tough fibrous sheet connecting frontalis↔occipitalis. Distinct tactile "pop" on penetration. |
| L | Loose areolar tissue (subgaleal space) | <1 mm collapsed | **TARGET LAYER for scalp acupuncture.** Minimal resistance, minimal pain, minimal bleeding. Contains emissary veins (infection risk). |
| P | Pericranium (periosteum) | 0.5-1 mm | Adherent to bone. Needle does NOT penetrate. |

**Distance needle tip → cortex:** minimum 8-16 mm (pericranium + skull 5-10 mm + meninges + CSF). Needle CANNOT reach brain — anatomically impossible.

### 2.2 Innervation Map

**Anterior scalp (trigeminal territory):**
- V1 supratrochlear nerve: medial forehead strip (1.7 cm lateral to midline)
- V1 supraorbital nerve: dominant frontal/anterior scalp nerve, extends to lambdoid suture
- V2 zygomaticotemporal nerve: small anterior temple patch
- V3 auriculotemporal nerve: lateral temporal scalp (with superficial temporal artery)

**Posterior scalp (cervical territory):**
- Greater occipital nerve (GON, C2 dorsal ramus): dominant posterior scalp, extends to vertex/coronal suture
- Lesser occipital nerve (LON, C2-C3 ventral rami): lateral posterior scalp, behind ear
- Third occipital nerve (TON, C3): small lower midline occiput
- Great auricular nerve (C2-C3): periauricular (overlaps with AP-16 territory)

**Boundary:** V1↔C2 overlap at vertex (coronal suture region). V3↔LON/great auricular nerve overlap at ear.

### 2.3 Vascular Supply

5 paired arteries ALL in Layer C (connective tissue, ABOVE galea):
- Superficial temporal artery (external carotid) — frontal + parietal branches
- Posterior auricular artery (external carotid) — behind ear
- Occipital artery (external carotid) — posterior scalp, with GON
- Supraorbital artery (internal carotid → ophthalmic) — medial forehead
- Supratrochlear artery (internal carotid → ophthalmic) — central forehead

Needle tip in Layer L (BELOW galea) = separated from major vessels by galea thickness.

### 2.4 Key Mechanism Theories

1. **Cortical projection hypothesis** (Jiao 1971, original): needling OVER a cortical area directly stimulates that area. Problem: "impossible to stimulate cortical functional zones directly" — needle is mechanical, not electromagnetic, separated by skull (Jin 2023, Frontiers in Neuroscience).

2. **Peripheral nerve / trigeminovascular theory** (LEADING current theory, Jin 2023): scalp acupuncture stimulates trigeminal nerve branches → 3 pathways:
   - Antidromic: trigeminal ganglion → CGRP/substance P release → vasodilation → increased CBF
   - Parasympathetic: V afferents → facial nerve → sphenopalatine ganglion → NO → cerebral vasodilation
   - Central: → RVLM → cerebral vasodilation + mean arterial pressure increase

3. **Trigeminal-meninges-CSF pathway** (Wang 2017, Medical Acupuncture): scalp needle → trigeminal → meninges → CSF-contacting neurons → brain parenchyma

4. **Neuroplasticity/BDNF** (Li 2023, Frontiers in Neurology): upregulates BDNF/TrkB → PI3K/AKT + MAPK/ERK → synaptogenesis, neurogenesis, axonal sprouting

5. **Vascular/CBF theory**: transcranial Doppler shows increased MCA velocity; angiogenesis via Wnt/β-Catenin (Yang 2022)

6. **Anti-inflammatory**: suppresses microglial/astrocytic hyperactivation, downregulates TNF-α/IL-6/CRP

**fMRI validation (Zheng 2016, Brain and Cognition):** MS6 middle 2/5 (hand area) specifically modulates contralateral M1 hand area → confirms functional specificity. But activation is MORE WIDESPREAD than simple point-to-point model predicts — involves cingulate, insula, thalamus, DMN.

**Critical assessment (Ye 2023, Alternative Therapies):** Jiao's projection system was borrowed from neurosurgical surface landmarks, not independently derived. >57% of the "19 zones" don't correspond to cortical areas directly beneath. Scientific evidence has not confirmed the original hypothesis.

## 3. Systems Overview

### 3.1 Jiao Shunfa (焦顺发, 1971)

**Origin:** Neurosurgeon in Shanxi province. Mapped neurosurgical cortical projection landmarks onto scalp for acupuncture.
**Zones:** 19 named zones (composite: Jiao's original ~14 + additions by Fang Yunpeng and others)
**Key zones:** Motor Area (运动区), Sensory Area (感觉区), Foot Motor-Sensory Area (足运感区), Speech Areas I/II/III, Chorea-Tremor Control Area, Vision Area, Balance Area, Vertigo-Auditory Area, Praxis Area, Vasomotor Area, Stomach/Thoracic/Genital/Hepatobiliary/Intestinal forehead areas
**Technique:** Rapid twirling (200+ RPM), 15-30° insertion, threading 1-2 inches
**Evidence:** Largest body of research (Chinese-language). Most-used system in Chinese clinical trials.

### 3.2 WHO/ISSA Standard (1984/1991)

**Origin:** WHO-sponsored meeting 1984, unified multiple Chinese systems. Adopted "zoning to define meridians, selecting points on meridians" (以经定区, 循经取穴) principle.
**Lines:** 14 MS codes (MS1-MS14) = 25 actual lines (3 midline + 11 bilateral pairs)
**Key design:** Uses traditional body acupoints as endpoint landmarks (GV20, GB6, GB7, BL3, etc.)
**Standard:** GB/T 21709.2-2008 (revised 2021). 2021 revision introduced text-figure inconsistencies (MS6 start point: GV21 in text vs EX-HN1 in figure).

### 3.3 YNSA — Yamamoto New Scalp Acupuncture (1973)

**Origin:** Dr. Toshikatsu Yamamoto, Japan. Originally 5 basic points.
**Key difference:** Uses DIAGNOSTIC PALPATION (neck/abdomen) to determine which points to treat. Immediate verification: correct needling eliminates diagnostic tenderness.
**Points:** Basic A-I (9 points at forehead/occipital), Ypsilon points (12, one per TCM meridian, at temple), Brain points, Sensorial points, Cranial nerve points (12)
**Paradigm:** Complete somatotopic microsystem. Ipsilateral for pain, contralateral for paralysis.
**Evidence:** YNSA pilot RCT for stroke (Barthel 95 vs 75, PMID 23057482). YNSA for acute LBP double-blind RCT n=80 (Hasegawa 2014, PMID 24316509). SR for pain (BrJP 2018).

### 3.4 Zhu Mingqing (朱明清)

**Origin:** 1970s-1990s China.
**Zones:** 6 zones (17 subdivisions). Named by anatomical location, not cortical function.
**Key difference:** Forceful small-amplitude lifting/thrusting + active movement/massage of affected body part during needling.
**Evidence:** Case reports and series, fewer large RCTs.

### 3.5 Other Systems

- **Fang Yunpeng (方云鹏, 1976):** 7 zones, 21 acupoints. Contributed forehead zones later attributed to "Jiao's 19."
- **Tang Songyan (汤颂延):** San Jiao theory division. Long needle retention. Lifting/thrusting method.
- **Huang Xuelong (黄学龙, 1950s):** Recently identified as EARLIEST founder (Ye 2025/2026). GV-centered approach.
- **Liu's Scalp Acupuncture (USA, 2005):** Holographic microsystem. Sparrow-pecking technique.

- MS14: balance/cerebellum. Cerebellar disorders clinical use.
- Foot Motor-Sensory Area: "most commonly used area in Chinese scalp acupuncture" (Jiao system), lower limb

## 6. Evidence Landscape

### 6.1 Strongest evidence (SR/MA available)

| Condition | Evidence | Key effect sizes |
|---|---|---|
| Post-stroke motor recovery | 30 RCTs MA (Huang 2021) | FMA WMD=15.18 (3mo); Barthel MD=5.79 |
| Post-stroke spasticity | 16 RCTs (Zhang 2024) + 19 RCTs motion-style scalp acupuncture (2025) | MAS MD=−0.56; SMD=−0.73 |
| Post-stroke aphasia | NMA 69 RCTs 5,097 pts (Zhang 2022) | Scalp+tongue+ST SUCRA=97.9% |
| Post-stroke dysphagia | 18 RCTs 1,410 pts (Medicine 2026) | SSA MD=−3.64; OR=0.31 |
| Insomnia | 21 studies 1,606 pts (Liu 2021) | PSQI MD=−1.49 |
| Post-stroke depression | 14 RCTs (Jiang 2023) | Effective rate sig higher |
| Migraine | 8 RCTs 874 pts (2023) | RR=1.24 vs body acu, RR=1.20 vs meds |
| Autism (children) | 14 trials 968 pts (Liu 2019) | CARS MD=−3.08 |
| Vascular dementia | NMA 46 studies 3,731 pts (2022) | MMSE sig improvement |
| Acute LBP (YNSA) | Double-blind RCT n=80 (Hasegawa 2014) | Sig vs sham on pain, function, QoL |
| TMD pain | RCT n=60 (Fernandes 2021) | 83.4% pain reduction |

### 6.2 Evidence quality assessment

- Nearly all studies from China (geographic bias). Vickers 1998: ALL Chinese acu trials report positive results.
- GRADE: predominantly low to very low (JAMA Network Open 2022)
- Blinding: most trials unblinded. Sham scalp acupuncture difficult but achievable (Streitberger needle).
- Reporting quality: CONSORT median 7/13 (Li 2017). 93% high/unclear risk of bias for blinding in Chinese trials vs 47% non-Chinese (Ma 2023).
- Cochrane (Yang 2016): "inadequate evidence to draw conclusions about routine use."
- Publication bias: funnel asymmetry detected in multiple MAs.

### 6.3 Safety

- Structural advantage: skull prevents organ perforation / pneumothorax (body acupuncture risks)
- Minor AEs: scalp soreness/tingling, transient dizziness, minor bleeding/bruising (6.1% in general acu)
- Serious AEs: NONE attributed to scalp acupuncture in systematic reviews
- Contraindications: unclosed fontanelles (infants), cranial defects (post-craniotomy), anticoagulants (OR=4.95 for bleeding), uncontrolled hypertension (>220/120), pacemakers (for EA)
- No serious AEs reported → but under-reporting in Chinese trials limits confidence

**Praxis (C):** Near parietal tubercle. 3 lines at 40°. Apraxia.
