# AP-17 — Scalp Acupuncture Microsystem — Đầu Châm (头针)

> **Status:** Phase 2 COMPLETE — All 20 entries + Review done.
> This file documents 18 treatment lines + 2 system overview sections.
>
> **Microsystem overview:** Scalp acupuncture (头针 tóu zhēn, đầu
> châm) is the second major acupuncture microsystem after auricular
> (AP-16). Jiao Shunfa (焦顺发), a neurosurgeon in Shanxi province,
> developed the first systematic scalp acupuncture approach in 1971,
> mapping neurosurgical cortical projection landmarks onto the scalp
> for acupuncture treatment. Huang Xuelong (黄学龙) may have priority
> (1950s GV-centered approach, Ye 2025/2026), but Jiao's system became
> dominant. WHO/ISSA standardized 14 MS lines (MS1-MS14) in 1984,
> adopting "以经定区, 循经取穴" (zoning by meridians, selecting points
> on meridians) — using traditional body acupoints as line endpoints.
> Chinese national standard GB/T 21709.2-2008 (revised 2021) codified
> these 14 lines.
>
> **What makes scalp acupuncture unique — LINE not POINT:**
>
> Scalp acupuncture treats along LINES (1-4.5 cm strips), not at
> discrete POINTS. The needle is inserted at 15-30° and threaded
> subcutaneously 1-2 inches along the galea aponeurotica (subgaleal
> space, Layer L) — NOT inserted perpendicular at a single point.
> Rapid twirling (200+ RPM, Jiao method) or electroacupuncture during
> stimulation. This differs fundamentally from body acupuncture on the
> scalp (GV20, BL7, GB8 etc. — perpendicular/oblique at single points
> by meridian logic). Same anatomical territory, different paradigm:
> subcutaneous line threading by cortical projection theory vs
> perpendicular point insertion by meridian/channel theory.
>
> **SCALP layers (mnemonic):**
>
> | Layer | Name | Thickness | Significance |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | S | Skin | 3-8 mm | Thickest skin in body. Entry point. |
> | C | Connective tissue (dense) | 4-7 mm | Arteries/veins/nerves. Fibrous septa → profuse bleeding. |
> | A | Aponeurosis (galea) | 1-2 mm | Tough sheet frontalis↔occipitalis. "Pop" on penetration. |
> | L | Loose areolar tissue | <1 mm | **TARGET LAYER.** Minimal pain/bleeding. |
> | P | Pericranium | 0.5-1 mm | Adherent to bone. Not penetrated. |
>
> Needle tip → cortex: 8-16+ mm (pericranium + skull 5-10 mm + meninges
> + CSF). Needle CANNOT reach brain — anatomically impossible.
>
> **Innervation — NO vagal component (unlike AP-16 auricular):**
>
> Anterior scalp (trigeminal territory): V1 supratrochlear nerve
> (medial forehead, ~1.7 cm from midline), V1 supraorbital nerve
> (dominant frontal/anterior, extends to lambdoid suture), V2
> zygomaticotemporal nerve (anterior temple), V3 auriculotemporal
> nerve (lateral temporal scalp). Posterior scalp (cervical territory):
> C2 greater occipital nerve (GON, dominant posterior, extends to
> vertex), C2-C3 lesser occipital nerve (LON, lateral posterior), C3
> third occipital nerve (lower midline occiput). Boundary: V1↔C2
> overlap at vertex (coronal suture). V3↔LON overlap periauricularly.
> → Cross-ref: AP-13 GV20 (V1+C2 dual convergence at vertex),
> AP-16 (ear innervation: ABVN vagal + V3 + C2-C3)
>
> **Key mechanism — trigeminovascular (leading theory, Jin 2023,
> Frontiers in Neuroscience 17:1210537, PMID 37650106):**
>
> Trigeminal branch stimulation → (1) antidromic CGRP/substance P
> release → vasodilation → increased cerebral blood flow; (2)
> parasympathetic via sphenopalatine ganglion → NO → cerebral
> vasodilation; (3) central RVLM pathway → blood pressure modulation.
> Original cortical projection hypothesis (Jiao 1971) critiqued:
> "impossible to stimulate cortical functional zones directly" (Jin
> 2023); >57% of zones don't correspond to underlying cortex (Ye
> 2023, Alternative Therapies, PMID 36302231). fMRI (Zheng 2016,
> Brain and Cognition 105:1-11): MS6 middle 2/5 modulates contralateral
> M1 hand area — confirms some functional specificity, but activation
> more widespread than simple point-to-point model (cingulate, insula,
> thalamus, DMN). Additional mechanisms: neuroplasticity via BDNF/TrkB
> → PI3K/AKT + MAPK/ERK → synaptogenesis (Li 2023, Frontiers in
> Neurology); anti-inflammatory suppression of microglial/astrocytic
> hyperactivation (TNF-α/IL-6/CRP); vascular angiogenesis via
> Wnt/β-Catenin (Yang 2022).
>
> **Evidence landscape — stroke rehabilitation dominates:**
>
> | Condition | Evidence level | Key data |
> |---|---|---|
> | Post-stroke motor | 30 RCTs MA (Huang 2021) | FMA WMD=15.18 (3 months) |
> | Post-stroke spasticity | 16 RCTs (Zhang 2024) | MAS MD=−0.56 |
> | Post-stroke aphasia | NMA 69 RCTs 5,097 pts (Liang 2022) | Scalp+tongue SUCRA=97.9% (Feng 2022) |
> | Post-stroke dysphagia | 18 RCTs 1,410 pts (2026) | SSA MD=−3.64 |
> | Insomnia | 21 studies 1,606 pts (Liu 2021) | PSQI MD=−1.49 |
> | Post-stroke depression | 14 RCTs (Jiang 2023) | Sig. improvement |
> | Migraine | 8 RCTs 874 pts (2023) | RR=1.24 vs body acu |
> | Autism (children) | 14 trials 968 pts (Liu 2019) | CARS MD=−3.08 |
> | Vascular dementia | NMA 46 studies 3,731 pts (2022) | MMSE sig. improved |
>
> Nearly all studies from China (geographic bias). GRADE: predominantly
> low to moderate (JAMA Network Open 2022). Cochrane (Yang 2016):
> "inadequate evidence to draw conclusions about routine use."
>
> **Safety — structural skull advantage:**
> Skull prevents organ perforation / pneumothorax. No serious AEs
> attributed to scalp acupuncture in systematic reviews. Minor AEs:
> scalp soreness, transient dizziness, minor bleeding (6.1% in general
> acupuncture). Contraindications: unclosed fontanelles (infants),
> cranial defects (post-craniotomy), anticoagulants (OR=4.95 for
> bleeding), uncontrolled hypertension (>220/120 mmHg), pacemakers
> (for electroacupuncture).
>
> **Major systems:** Jiao Shunfa (19 zones, 1971) | WHO/ISSA (14 MS
> lines, 1984) | YNSA Yamamoto (basic A-I + Ypsilon + diagnostic
> palpation, 1973) | Zhu Mingqing (6 zones / 17 subdivisions, lifting/thrusting + active
> movement). → YNSA and Zhu overview sections documented at end of file.
>
> **Total entries:** 18 lines (14 WHO MS + 4 Jiao-only) + 2 system
> overviews = 20. Tier: 2A (MS6, MS7) + 7B (incl. YNSA) + 10C + 1 overview (Zhu).

## Frontal Lines (额区) — MS1-MS4

> **Anatomy:** Four parallel lines on the forehead, all 1 cun (~2-3 cm)
> long, running vertically from the anterior hairline downward. Arranged
> medial to lateral: MS1 (midline) → MS2 (1.5 cun lateral) → MS3
> (2.25 cun lateral) → MS4 (~3 cun lateral). All bilateral except
> MS1 (midline only).
>
> **Innervation:** All four lines lie within V1 ophthalmic territory.
> MS1 (medial forehead): supratrochlear nerve (V1). MS2-MS3 (middle
> forehead): medial and lateral branches of the supraorbital nerve
> (V1). MS4 (lateral forehead): lateral supraorbital nerve (V1),
> approaching V2 zygomaticotemporal territory at the lateral margin.
> All → trigeminal ganglion → trigeminal sensory nuclear complex →
> brainstem (NTS, reticular formation, locus coeruleus).
>
> **Vascular:** Supratrochlear artery (medial, MS1 region) and
> supraorbital artery (MS2-MS4) — both branches of the ophthalmic
> artery (internal carotid), running in Layer C above the galea.
> Superficial temporal artery frontal branch approaches the lateral
> forehead near MS4.
>
> **Cortical area beneath:** All four lines overlie the prefrontal
> cortex (frontal bone). Moving medial → lateral:
> - MS1: medial prefrontal cortex / superior frontal gyrus (BA 9/10)
> - MS2: dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (BA 9/46)
> - MS3: middle frontal gyrus, lateral prefrontal (BA 46/10)
> - MS4: lateral frontal, approaching inferior frontal gyrus (BA 10/45)
>
> Note: exact cortical localization beneath each line varies with
> individual skull/brain morphology. These are approximate based on
> standard neurosurgical landmarks.
>
> **Jiao system — visceral organ projection zones:**
> The forehead organ zones were originally developed by Fang Yunpeng
> (方云鹏, 1976), later incorporated into Jiao's "expanded 19 zones."
> Chinese sources are explicit that Jiao borrowed these from Fang's
> system. They map the lateral forehead into vertical strips
> corresponding to visceral organ regulation:
> - MS2 = Thoracic/Chest Cavity Area (胸腔区): cardiopulmonary
> - MS3 = Stomach Area (胃区) with hepatobiliary overlap: GI
> - MS4 = Genital/Reproductive Area (生殖区): urogenital
>
> MS1 (midline) does NOT correspond to a specific Jiao/Fang zone — it
> derives from GV/Du Mai traditions (Tang Songyan, Zhu Mingqing). Some
> textbooks assign "Stomach Area" to MS1, but the original forehead
> organ zones were lateral, not midline.
>
> Cortical projection rationale: prefrontal cortex modulates autonomic
> outflow to visceral organs via vmPFC → hypothalamus → autonomic
> nervous system. However, the forehead zones show the WEAKEST cortical
> correspondence of any Jiao zones (Ye 2023) — the prefrontal cortex
> does not have organ-specific topographic organization comparable to
> the motor/sensory homunculus. For MS4 specifically, the genital
> somatosensory cortex is at the medial paracentral lobule near vertex,
> not at the lateral frontal pole — the clearest anatomical mismatch.
>
> **Needle technique (all forehead lines):**
> Needle: 0.25-0.30 mm diameter × 25-40 mm (28-32 gauge).
> Insertion: at superior endpoint (hairline), directed inferiorly at
> 15-30°, threading subcutaneously into Layer L for ~1 cun (2-3 cm).
> Tactile cue: distinct "pop" penetrating galea (Layer A), then
> minimal resistance in Layer L. Sharp pain = wrong layer (too
> superficial in Layer C or hitting pericranium).
> Stimulation: rapid twirling 200+ RPM (Jiao method), sustained
> 2-3 minutes per rotation bout, repeated 2-3 times during 30-minute
> retention. Alternatively: electroacupuncture at 2-100 Hz, 1-3 mA
> (continuous or dense-disperse wave).
>
> **Clinical significance:** Forehead lines are used predominantly for
> neuropsychiatric and autonomic/visceral regulation rather than the
> motor/sensory functions associated with vertex lines (MS5-MS7). MS1
> is the most clinically important forehead line (Tier B), with
> evidence for post-stroke depression and insomnia. MS2-MS4 (Tier C)
> are typically used in combination with other MS lines for their
> respective organ-zone conditions.

### MS1 — Ngạch Trung Tuyến (额中线, É Zhōng Xiàn) — Middle Line of Forehead

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

Forehead midline treatment line — the most clinically significant of
the four forehead lines. MS1 derives from GV/Du Mai traditions (Tang
Songyan, Zhu Mingqing) rather than Jiao Shunfa's cortical projection
system. Included in scalp acupuncture protocols for post-stroke
depression and insomnia. The medial prefrontal cortex beneath MS1 is
a key node in the default mode network (DMN) and autonomic regulation
circuitry, providing a neuroanatomical basis for its neuropsychiatric
indications. Some Chinese textbooks assign Jiao's "Stomach Area" (胃区)
to this midline position, but the original Jiao/Fang forehead organ
zones were lateral (MS2-MS4 territory).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Chinese standard location (GB/T 21709.2-2008): from GV24 (Shenting,
神庭) downward 1 cun along the midline of the forehead.
Body acupoint endpoint: GV24 (AP-13).
Meridian traversed: Governing Vessel (Du Mai, 督脉).
Direction: superior → inferior (hairline downward).
Length: 1 cun (~2-3 cm). Midline only (unpaired).

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: supratrochlear nerve (V1, ophthalmic division of
trigeminal nerve) — exits at superomedial orbital rim, ascending
medially along the forehead. At midline, contribution from medial
branch of supraorbital nerve (V1). Pathway: V1 → trigeminal ganglion
→ principal sensory nucleus + spinal trigeminal nucleus → brainstem
reticular formation, NTS, locus coeruleus.
Vascular: supratrochlear artery (ophthalmic → internal carotid) in
Layer C, running vertically along the medial forehead.
Cortical area beneath: medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), medial
superior frontal gyrus (Brodmann areas 9/10). The mPFC functions as:
(1) DMN hub — DMN hyperactivity correlates with depression and
insomnia; (2) emotional regulation center — vmPFC → amygdala
inhibitory circuit; (3) autonomic visceral modulation — vmPFC →
hypothalamus → vagal outflow to gastrointestinal tract.
SCALP layer target: loose areolar tissue (Layer L).

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| WHO standard | MS1 额中线 | ✓ Primary standard |
| Jiao Shunfa | No direct Jiao zone (midline ≠ Jiao/Fang lateral zones) | ✗ See note |
| Tang/Zhu tradition | GV/Du Mai midline line | ✓ Origin |
| YNSA | Near basic point A (forehead) | ~ Approximate region |
| Zhu Mingqing | No direct equivalent | — |
| Body acupoint | GV24 Shenting (神庭, AP-13) | ✓ Start point |

Body acupoints along line: GV24 (Shenting, 神庭) at the superior
endpoint — the only standard body acupoint on this 1-cun midline
segment. GV24 is used independently for calming the spirit (安神),
insomnia, and forehead headache — shared indication overlap with MS1.
→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV24

Convergence: MS1 does not map to a specific Jiao/Fang zone (the Jiao
forehead organ zones are lateral, MS2-MS4). Some textbooks assign
"Stomach Area" here but this is likely a later conflation. YNSA basic
point A occupies a broader frontal zone that includes the MS1 region.
GV24's traditional neuropsychiatric indications parallel MS1's modern
clinical applications — the meridian paradigm (GV24 for calming
spirit) and the scalp acupuncture paradigm converge on overlapping
uses at this midline location.

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Official standard indications (GB/T 21709.2): headache (头痛),
dizziness (头晕), red/swollen/painful eyes (目赤肿痛), epilepsy
(癫痫).

Chinese acupuncture textbooks (expanded): mental/psychiatric disorders
(精神失常), insomnia (失眠), neurasthenia (神经衰弱), nasal disease
(鼻病), forehead headache, post-stroke neuropsychiatric sequelae
including depression and cognitive impairment.

Note: some textbooks also list gastrointestinal indications under the
"Stomach Area" designation, but this attribution is uncertain — the
original Jiao/Fang forehead organ zones are lateral (MS2-MS4), not
midline. MS1's indications are primarily neuropsychiatric.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Post-stroke depression (PSD) 🟡 Plausible:**

Jiang et al. 2023 (Medicine, systematic review, 14 RCTs — 10 scalp
acupuncture alone + 4 scalp acupuncture + electroacupuncture): HAMD MD = −2.29
(95% CI [−3.88, −0.70], P = .005) overall vs controls; scalp acupuncture-alone
subgroup MD = −2.75 [−4.84, −0.65]. MS1 is one of the most
frequently selected frontal lines in PSD protocols, typically
combined with MS5 (vertex midline) and/or the Emotion Area.
Individual MS1 contribution cannot be isolated — all trials use
multi-line prescriptions. All Chinese-language trials, high
heterogeneity, low certainty per authors.

Clinical rationale: the mPFC beneath MS1 is a key target in
depression neurocircuitry. TMS and tDCS for depression also target
the left dlPFC/mPFC region — adjacent to the MS1 cortical area.
→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV20 (most-used acupoint for depression in
neuroimaging studies — GV20 is the start point of adjacent MS5)

**Insomnia 🟡 Plausible:**

Liu et al. 2021 (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine,
21 studies, 1,606 patients, 19 with PSQI data): pooled PSQI
MD = −1.96 [−3.21, −0.71], P = .002 (overall); vs drug comparators
specifically MD = −1.49 [−2.05, −0.92], P < .00001. I² = 96% (high
heterogeneity). Protocols used multiple scalp lines/points
(Sishencong, GV20, etc.) — MS1 contribution not isolated. The
mPFC/DMN hub beneath MS1 is consistent with insomnia pathophysiology
— hyperactive DMN during the sleep-wake transition is a recognized
neuroimaging finding in insomnia.

**Gastrointestinal function 🔴 Unvalidated individually:**

No systematic review tests MS1 for GI conditions. Some textbooks
assign Jiao's "Stomach Area" to this midline position, but the
original forehead organ zones were lateral (MS3 territory). Even
granting the attribution, no controlled trial data supports GI
effects at MS1.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

MS1 mechanism operates through prefrontal cortical modulation of
subcortical/brainstem circuits rather than direct cortical stimulation
(anatomically impossible through the skull):

1. Trigeminovascular pathway (primary): V1 supratrochlear stimulation
   at forehead → trigeminal ganglion → brainstem NTS/reticular
   formation → widespread neuromodulation including frontal CBF changes
2. DMN modulation: the mPFC beneath MS1 is a primary DMN node. Scalp
   acupuncture may modulate DMN activity through altered sensory
   processing at the scalp surface → cortical activity changes
   (mechanism unclear — possibly via altered vascular/metabolic
   environment from trigeminovascular CGRP release)
3. Autonomic pathway: vmPFC projects to hypothalamus → autonomic
   outflow to viscera, providing a neural substrate for Jiao's visceral
   organ zone concept

⚡ **Framework note 1 — PREFRONTAL CONVERGENCE AT MS1:** The medial
prefrontal cortex beneath MS1 is the same region targeted by TMS/tDCS
for depression — both scalp acupuncture and non-invasive brain
stimulation approach the mPFC from the scalp surface through different
physical modalities (mechanical needle vs electromagnetic field). The
shared anatomical target despite different stimulation mechanisms
suggests that mPFC accessibility from the forehead surface may be a
common factor. However, the mechanisms are fundamentally different:
TMS induces cortical currents directly through the skull; scalp
acupuncture stimulates peripheral trigeminal nerves at the skin level.
The cortical area is relevant as the downstream target of the
trigeminovascular pathway, not as a directly stimulated structure.

### MS2 — Ngạch Bàng Nhất Tuyến (额旁1线, É Páng Yī Xiàn) — Lateral Line 1 of Forehead

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Chinese standard (GB/T 21709.2-2008): from BL3 (Meichong, 眉冲)
downward 1 cun, parallel to the midline. Located 1.5 cun lateral
to MS1 (bilateral). Body acupoint endpoint: BL3 (AP-07).
Meridian traversed: Bladder channel (Foot Taiyang, 足太阳膀胱经).
Direction: superior → inferior. Length: 1 cun.

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: medial branch of supraorbital nerve (V1).
Vascular: supraorbital artery (ophthalmic → internal carotid) in
Layer C.
Cortical area beneath: anterior prefrontal cortex, edge of medial
orbitofrontal / ventromedial PFC (BA 10/11 border area). No
established viscero-cortical (cardiac/pulmonary) representation at
the frontal pole — cardiorespiratory control maps to insula and
brainstem, not frontal pole. The organ correspondence is a
TCM-theoretical construct, not neuroanatomical.
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| WHO standard | MS2 额旁1线 | ✓ Primary |
| Jiao/Fang Yunpeng | Thoracic/Chest Cavity Area (胸腔区) | ✓ Direct |
| Body acupoint | BL3 Meichong (眉冲, AP-07) | ✓ Start point |

→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL3

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Jiao/Fang Thoracic/Chest Cavity Area: allergic asthma, bronchitis,
angina pectoris, rheumatic heart disease (palpitations, edema,
oliguria), paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia, chest tightness
(胸闷), cough, dyspnea. Cortical projection rationale: the frontal
cortex area beneath MS2 modulates autonomic outflow to thoracic
organs — however, no modern neuroanatomical evidence supports
organ-specific cardiac/pulmonary control at this cortical location.
No dedicated SR/RCT evidence — used almost exclusively in combination
protocols within broader TCM cardiopulmonary treatment.

### MS3 — Ngạch Bàng Nhị Tuyến (额旁2线, É Páng Èr Xiàn) — Lateral Line 2 of Forehead

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Chinese standard (GB/T 21709.2-2008): from GB15 (Toulinqi, 头临泣)
downward 1 cun. Located 2.25 cun lateral to midline (bilateral).
Body acupoint endpoint: GB15 (AP-11).
Meridian traversed: Gallbladder channel (Foot Shaoyang, 足少阳胆经).
Direction: superior → inferior. Length: 1 cun.

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: lateral branch of supraorbital nerve (V1).
Vascular: supraorbital artery lateral branches in Layer C.
Cortical area beneath: lateral prefrontal cortex, middle frontal
gyrus (Brodmann areas 46/10).
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| WHO standard | MS3 额旁2线 | ✓ Primary |
| Jiao/Fang Yunpeng | Stomach Area (胃区) with hepatobiliary overlap | ✓ Direct |
| Body acupoint | GB15 Toulinqi (头临泣, AP-11) | ✓ Start point |

→ Cross-ref: AP-11 GB15

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Jiao/Fang Stomach Area: acute/chronic gastritis, gastric and duodenal
ulcer pain, epigastric distress. Also right-upper-quadrant pain from
hepatobiliary disease — the hepatobiliary indication is folded into
MS3's indication list through referred pain reasoning, not as a
separate Jiao zone. Chinese textbooks: gastrointestinal complaints,
lateral headache, eye conditions (blurred vision — Gallbladder
channel pathology). The positioning on the Gallbladder meridian
creates a double-system convergence: cortical projection logic
(Jiao/Fang Stomach Area) and meridian theory (GB channel for
liver/gallbladder) at this location.

### MS4 — Ngạch Bàng Tam Tuyến (额旁3线, É Páng Sān Xiàn) — Lateral Line 3 of Forehead

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Chinese standard (GB/T 21709.2-2008): from a point 0.75 cun medial
to ST8 (Touwei, 头维), downward 1 cun. Located approximately 3 cun
lateral to midline (bilateral). This line's start point does NOT
coincide with a named body acupoint — it is defined by its distance
from ST8.
Nearest body acupoint: ST8 (AP-03), 0.75 cun lateral to the line.
Meridian traversed: between Gallbladder and Stomach channels.
Direction: superior → inferior. Length: 1 cun.

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: lateral supraorbital nerve (V1), with possible
contribution from zygomaticotemporal nerve (V2) at the lateral margin
— this is the V1↔V2 transition zone on the lateral forehead.
Vascular: superficial temporal artery frontal branch (external carotid)
approaches this area in Layer C. Important vessel awareness — more
lateral position increases proximity to the superficial temporal artery
compared to MS1-MS3.
Cortical area beneath: lateral frontal cortex, approaching inferior
frontal gyrus (Brodmann areas 10/45).
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| WHO standard | MS4 额旁3线 | ✓ Primary |
| Jiao/Fang Yunpeng | Genital/Reproductive Area (生殖区) | ✓ Direct |
| Body acupoint (near) | ST8 Touwei (头维, AP-03, 0.75 cun lateral) | ~ Near, not on line |

→ Cross-ref: AP-03 ST8

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Jiao/Fang Genital/Reproductive Area: functional uterine bleeding
(功能性子宫出血), impotence (阳痿), spermatorrhea (遗精), uterine
prolapse (子宫脱垂), urinary frequency/urgency, dysmenorrhea (痛经),
irregular menstruation (月经不调), enuresis (遗尿). Commonly combined
with the Foot Motor-Sensory Area for uterine prolapse.

Cortical projection rationale: the lateral frontal cortex is proposed
to modulate autonomic outflow to pelvic organs. However, this is the
CLEAREST anatomical mismatch of the four forehead lines — the genital
somatosensory cortex is at the medial paracentral lobule near the
vertex (BA 4/3/1/2 medial), not at the lateral frontal pole. The
organ correspondence is a TCM-theoretical construct. No dedicated
SR/RCT evidence — used mainly within post-stroke urinary
incontinence combination protocols.

## Vertex Lines (顶区) — MS5-MS9

> **Anatomy:** The vertex covers the crown of the skull at the junction
> of frontal and parietal bones (sagittal + coronal sutures). MS5-MS7
> cover the most clinically important territory in scalp acupuncture:
> MS5 (midline, SMA/premotor), MS6 (anterior oblique, primary motor
> cortex), MS7 (posterior oblique, primary somatosensory cortex).
>
> **Innervation:** V1 supraorbital + C2 greater occipital convergence
> at the vertex (documented at GV20, AP-13 Framework notes 1-2:
> cranial nerve architecture + PVN→oxytocin pathway). As the oblique
> lines descend toward the temporal region, innervation transitions
> from V1/C2 through V2 zygomaticotemporal to V3 auriculotemporal.
> MS7 (more posterior) has greater C2 contribution than MS6 (more
> anterior) at the vertex segment.
> → Cross-ref: AP-13 GV20 (V1+C2 convergence, PVN→oxytocin)
>
> **Cortical areas beneath:**
> - MS5 (midline, anterior): SMA (BA 6) + anterior paracentral lobule
>   boundary — lower limb motor planning, gait initiation, bladder
>   control
> - MS6 (anterior oblique): precentral gyrus = primary motor cortex
>   (M1, BA 4), from paracentral lobule (leg) to lateral sulcus (face)
> - MS7 (posterior oblique): postcentral gyrus = primary somatosensory
>   cortex (S1, BA 3/1/2), same somatotopic arrangement
>
> **Somatotopic organization (MS6 and MS7):**
> Both MS6 and MS7 follow the Penfield homunculus — each divided into
> 3 functional zones totaling 5 units:
> - Upper 1/5: contralateral lower limb + trunk
> - Middle 2/5: contralateral upper limb (hand — fMRI confirmed for
>   MS6, Zheng 2016)
> - Lower 2/5: contralateral face (+ motor speech on dominant
>   hemisphere for MS6; + trigeminal neuralgia/TMD for MS7)
>
> **MS6+MS7 = core prescription:**
> Apriori analysis (Wang et al. 2022, 35 RCTs, PMC9389219):
> MS7→MS6 confidence = 95.2% (when MS7 is used, MS6 is co-selected
> 95.2% of the time). Six-location core combination: GV20, GV24,
> MS6(ipsilateral to lesion), MS7(ipsilateral), parietal region,
> Yintang.
>
> **Needle technique (vertex lines):**
> Same general parameters as forehead lines (see Frontal region intro).
> Additional: segment-specific needling for focal indications (e.g.,
> middle 2/5 only for hand function). Interactive/dynamic technique
> (motion-style scalp acupuncture): patient performs active motor/sensory exercises during needle
> retention — evidence shows this outperforms static needling.

### MS5 — Đỉnh Trung Tuyến (顶中线, Dǐng Zhōng Xiàn) — Middle Line of Vertex

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

Vertex midline treatment line running from GV20 (Baihui) ANTERIORLY
to GV21 (Qianding) along the sagittal suture. Partially overlaps
with but is distinct from Jiao's Foot Motor-Sensory Area (足运感区,
which is 1 cm lateral to the midline). Used for lower limb
motor/sensory deficits and cortical urinary dysfunction. The
supplementary motor area (SMA) beneath MS5 is involved in lower
limb motor planning, gait initiation, and cortical bladder control.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Chinese standard location (GB/T 21709.2): from GV20 (Baihui, 百会)
to GV21 (Qianding, 前顶), along the midline (Du Mai).
Direction: GV20 → GV21 = ANTERIOR (toward forehead). GV21 is 1.5 cun
anterior to GV20. Note: GV19 (Houding, "Back Vertex") is posterior.
Length: 1.5 cun (~3.5-4.5 cm). Midline only (unpaired).
Body acupoint endpoints: GV20 + GV21 (both AP-13).
Meridian traversed: Governing Vessel (Du Mai).

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: V1 supraorbital nerve + C2 greater occipital nerve.
GV20 sits at the exact V1/C2 boundary (AP-13: dual cranial nerve
convergence). The line extends anteriorly toward GV21 into more V1
territory.
Vascular: parietal emissary veins near sagittal suture (connect scalp
drainage to superior sagittal sinus). Occipital artery branches.
Cortical area beneath: MRI localization (Shen 2011, PMC3135375) shows
GV20 is 22.7 ± 2.2 mm ANTERIOR to the central sulcus — placing it
over the SMA (BA 6) and anterior paracentral lobule boundary. MS5
extends further anterior toward GV21, thus overlying primarily SMA
and premotor cortex, NOT the paracentral lobule proper (which lies
POSTERIOR to GV20, in the GV20→GV19 direction).
SCALP layer target: loose areolar tissue (Layer L).

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| WHO standard | MS5 顶中线 | ✓ Primary standard |
| Jiao Shunfa | Foot Motor-Sensory Area (足运感区) | ~ Adjacent, not identical |
| Body acupoints | GV20 + GV21 (AP-13) | ✓ Both endpoints |

Jiao's Foot Motor-Sensory Area: 1 cm LATERAL to midline, 3 cm long,
bilateral. MS5 is ON the midline. Both target lower limb function,
but Foot Motor-Sensory Area (lateral) overlies the paracentral lobule (medial M1/S1
for leg) while MS5 (midline) overlies the interhemispheric fissure
and SMA. Adjacent but anatomically distinct.
→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV20 (V1+C2 convergence, PVN→oxytocin pathway,
Framework notes 1-3)

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Official standard: contralateral lower limb paralysis (对侧下肢瘫痪),
numbness, weakness. Cortical urinary frequency/incontinence (皮层性
尿频/尿失禁), childhood enuresis (小儿遗尿), rectal prolapse (脱肛).

Chinese textbooks: post-stroke lower limb hemiplegia, post-stroke
urinary dysfunction, dizziness (头晕), vertigo.

Bladder indications are consistent with SMA anatomy — the cortical
micturition center is adjacent to the SMA in the medial frontal lobe.
Cortical lesions here produce urge incontinence.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**ADHD (children) 🔴 Limited evidence:**

He et al. 2014 (Zhongguo Zhen Jiu 34(12):1179-83, PMID 25876346):
RCT, n=100 children. EEG biofeedback + scalp acupuncture (MS5 + MS8
+ GV20 + EX-HN1) vs biofeedback alone. Effective rate 91.5% vs
83.3%; greater improvement in Wechsler (FIQ), Conners (CIH), and
θ/β EEG ratio. MS5 contribution cannot be isolated from the
multi-point protocol.

**Post-stroke lower limb motor 🔴 Not MS5-specific:**

Yang et al. 2025 (PLOS ONE, PMID 40333934, 12 trials, 1,318
patients): FMA-L SMD = −0.48, BBS SMD = −0.86. Multiple scalp lines
used — MS5 not analyzed separately.

**Post-stroke urinary incontinence 🔴 Not MS5-specific:**

Standard indication but no dedicated RCT/SR isolates MS5. Mixed
head+body point protocols in scattered clinical studies. Evidence gap.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

V1+C2 convergent pathway documented at GV20 (AP-13): supraorbital +
greater occipital → trigeminocervical complex → brainstem (NTS, LC,
DRN, PVN). GV20 EA specifically activates PVN→oxytocin (Gong 2026,
retrograde tracing + receptor blockade confirmation).

SMA modulation: the trigeminovascular pathway may modulate SMA
function through altered cerebral blood flow and neuroplasticity,
providing a plausible but unproven mechanism for both motor (lower
limb planning, gait) and bladder (cortical micturition center)
indications.

**Kỹ thuật kích thích:**

Needle: 0.25-0.30 mm × 25-40 mm. Insertion at GV20, directed
anteriorly toward GV21 at 15-30°, threading into Layer L for 1.5 cun.
Infant/young child caution: unclosed anterior fontanelle near GV21 —
needling contraindicated until fontanelle closure (~18-24 months).
Stimulation: rapid twirling 200+ RPM (Jiao method), 1-2 min × 2-3
bouts, 20-30 min retention. EA: 2-100 Hz, 1-3 mA.

### MS6 — Đỉnh Nhiếp Tiền Tà Tuyến (顶颞前斜线, Dǐng Niè Qián Xié Xiàn) — Anterior Oblique Line of Vertex-Temporal

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**

The MOST studied scalp acupuncture line and the cornerstone of stroke
motor rehabilitation. Corresponds to Jiao Shunfa's Motor Area (运动区)
— the first zone Jiao defined in 1971, and the best-validated zone in
his system. MS6 overlies the precentral gyrus (primary motor cortex,
M1). Apriori analysis identifies MS6 as the #1 most-used scalp
acupuncture zone; when MS7 (sensory line) is used, MS6 is co-selected
95.2% of the time (Wang 2022, PMC9389219). The most heavily evidenced
line in scalp acupuncture: 30 RCTs for post-stroke motor recovery
(Huang 2021), 16 RCTs for spasticity (Zhang 2024), 19 RCTs for motion-style scalp acupuncture
(2025), and the ONLY scalp line with fMRI confirmation of
segment-specific cortical modulation (Zheng 2016).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Chinese standard location (GB/T 21709.2-2021): from GV21 (Qianding,
前顶) obliquely to GB6 (Xuanli, 悬厘).

Start point controversy: the 2008 standard had a text-figure
discrepancy — text specified GV21 but figure depicted anterior
Sishencong (EX-HN1, 1 cun anterior to GV20). The 2021 revision and
2023 WFAS standard adopted GV21 as the official start point. However,
a 2024 critical analysis (Li & Zhang, Acupuncture Research
2024;49(5):544-550) argues this resolution remains disputed.
Positional difference: GV21 is 0.5 cun (~1-1.5 cm) anterior to
EX-HN1 — using GV21 places the start slightly closer to the forehead.

Body acupoint endpoints: GV21 (AP-13) → GB6 (AP-11).
Direction: vertex → temporal, oblique (roughly parallel to coronal
suture). Length: approximately 18 cm in adults. Bilateral.

**Somatotopic division (3 zones, 5 units — Jiao Motor Area):**

| Zone | Fraction | Body region (contralateral) | Cortical area |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper | 1/5 | Lower limb + trunk | Paracentral lobule (medial M1, BA 4) |
| Middle | 2/5 | Upper limb (hand) | Lateral precentral gyrus (M1, BA 4) |
| Lower | 2/5 | Face + motor speech (dominant hemisphere) | Inferior precentral gyrus + operculum (BA 4/6/44) |

Mirrors the Penfield motor homunculus: foot/leg medial (vertex) →
hand/arm lateral → face/tongue inferolateral (near lateral sulcus).
fMRI (Zheng 2016) confirmed: only stimulation at the hand segment
modulated the contralateral M1 hand area — foot and face segments did
NOT (see Mechanism section).

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply — gradient along the line:
- Vertex (near GV21): V1 supraorbital + supratrochlear, with C2 GON
  contribution at V1/C2 boundary
- Mid-segment: V1 → V2 zygomaticotemporal transition
- Temporal (near GB6): V3 auriculotemporal nerve

MS6 stimulates afferents from all three trigeminal divisions
(V1→V2→V3) — the broadest trigeminovascular input of any single
scalp line.

Vascular: superficial temporal artery (parietal branch) in Layer C,
particularly at the temporal segment near GB6.
Cortical area beneath: precentral gyrus (M1, BA 4) throughout. SMA
(BA 6) near vertex. Premotor cortex (BA 6) mid-segment. Frontal
operculum/Broca's area (BA 44) at inferior end (dominant hemisphere).
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| WHO standard | MS6 顶颞前斜线 | ✓ Primary standard |
| Jiao Shunfa | Motor Area (运动区, founding zone 1971) | ✓ Direct |
| YNSA | Brain Points (motor cortex concept) | ~ Conceptual, no 1:1 map |
| Zhu Mingqing | 顶颞带 (Vertex-Temporal Band) | ✓ Same territory |
| Body acupoints | GV21 (AP-13) → GB6 (AP-11) | ✓ Endpoints |

Body acupoints along line: BL6 (Chengguang), BL7 (Tongtian), GB4
(Hanyan), GB5 (Xuanlu) lie in or near the line's path — these are
treated independently by meridian logic, not as scalp acupuncture
targets.

Convergence: MS6 is the BEST-VALIDATED zone in Jiao's system — even
the Ye 2023 critique acknowledged motor/sensory areas as among the
better-supported zones. Zheng 2016 provides segment-level fMRI
validation unique in the Atlas.

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Per somatotopic segment:
- **Upper 1/5:** contralateral lower limb paralysis, post-stroke leg
  hemiplegia, paraplegia (partial)
- **Middle 2/5:** contralateral upper limb paralysis, arm/hand motor
  weakness, fine motor dysfunction
- **Lower 2/5 (dominant hemisphere):** motor aphasia (Broca-type,
  dysarthria, drooling, central facial palsy)
- **Lower 2/5 (non-dominant):** central facial palsy only

Chinese textbooks: post-stroke hemiplegia (偏瘫, primary indication),
Parkinson's disease (motor area + tremor-control area), cerebral palsy
(pediatric protocols), traumatic brain injury motor recovery.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Post-stroke motor recovery (hemiparesis) 🟡 Plausible:**

Huang et al. 2021 (Frontiers in Neurology, PMC8695983, 30 RCTs,
1,043 patients, China 2007-2019): MS6+MS7 contralateral to lesion:
- 1-month course (10 RCTs): FMA MD = +10.3 (95% CI 7.43-12.63,
  P < .01, I² = 78%)
- 3-month course (4 RCTs): FMA MD = +15.18 (95% CI 8.06-22.31,
  P < .01, I² = 83%) — longer course = larger effect
- Shoulder-hand syndrome: OR = 0.39 (95% CI 0.16-0.94, significant)
- AEs: dizziness/local redness 6.67% (scalp acupuncture) vs 8.89% (control)
- Quality: low (Cochrane RoB 2.0); only 1 sham-controlled; publication
  bias detected

**Post-stroke spasticity 🟡 Plausible:**

Zhang et al. 2024 (Medicine, PMC10906645, 16 RCTs, 1,249 patients):
- MAS (12 studies, n=874): MD = −0.56 (95% CI −0.75 to −0.37,
  P < .00001); sensitivity-adjusted −0.60 (I² = 39%)
- FMA (13 studies, n=980): MD = +5.86 (95% CI 3.26-8.46, P < .0001);
  subgroup 4-8 weeks: MD = +14.02
- Modified Barthel Index (12 studies, n=920): MD = +5.79 (95% CI
  4.73-6.84, P < .00001)
- Effective rate (5 studies, n=505): RR = 1.25 (95% CI 1.16-1.36)
- Safety: 8 punctate bleeding + 2 hematomas (resolved), 2 studies
  zero AEs

**Motion-Style Scalp Acupuncture 🟡 Plausible:**

motion-style scalp acupuncture 2025 SR (ScienceDirect, 19 RCTs, n=1,648): motion-style scalp acupuncture = concurrent
MS6 needling + active motor rehabilitation during retention. MAS
outcomes consistently favor motion-style scalp acupuncture > static scalp acupuncture.

Mechanistic study (Zhang 2025, Brain and Behavior, PMC12657263): MCAO
rat model n=132, MS6 needling (GV21→GB6, 200 RPM, + treadmill
10 m/min during retention):
- Laser speckle imaging: significantly higher regional CBF recovery
  (time × group P < .001)
- 5-HT2AR upregulation → spinal KCC2 reactivation → restored
  inhibitory GABAergic tone → reduced spasticity
- Antagonist reversed benefit, agonist mimicked — causal pathway
  confirmed
- MAS significantly lower in motion-style scalp acupuncture vs static scalp acupuncture at days 9-10 (P < .05)

**Interactive dynamic scalp acupuncture (clinical) 🟡 Plausible:**

Three RCTs consistently show needling + simultaneous movement >
static needling:
- Upper limb (PMID 26790201): FMA significantly greater with
  interactive protocol
- Lower limb multicenter (PMID 34913147, n=231): gait/motor improved
- fMRI (PMC12381643, 2025): enhanced bilateral basal ganglia
  functional connectivity

**Post-stroke aphasia (lower 2/5) 🔴 Unvalidated for MS6:**

No dedicated RCT/MA isolates the lower 2/5 segment. The 69 RCTs
aphasia NMA (Zhang 2022) pools multiple scalp lines — MS6's lower
segment not analyzed separately. Evidence gap.

**Non-stroke (PD, cerebral palsy) 🔴 Not MS6-specific:**

Motor area scalp acupuncture is used in PD data-mining analyses and
pediatric CP protocols (PMC6945653), but MS6-specific contribution
is not isolated.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

**1. Segment-specific cortical modulation (fMRI):**

Zheng et al. 2016 (Brain and Cognition 105:1-11, PMID 26799679): scalp acupuncture
at middle 2/5 of MS6 (hand segment) during motor imagery and motor
execution:
- INHIBITED ipsilateral hand-representation area (needled side)
- RELEASED/DISINHIBITED contralateral hand area
- Foot and mouth segment controls did NOT produce this effect —
  confirming segment-level specificity
- Additional: increased superior parietal lobule activation
  (sensorimotor integration)

This "hemisphere-crossing release" (ipsilateral inhibition +
contralateral disinhibition) may represent the neural substrate for
motor recovery in hemiplegia — suppressing the intact hemisphere's
compensatory over-activity while disinhibiting the lesioned
hemisphere's motor output.

**2. Trigeminovascular pathway:**

MS6 stimulates V1→V2→V3 afferents along its length (see zone
header mechanism section). Antidromic CGRP release → local
vasodilation → increased regional CBF. Confirmed by motion-style scalp acupuncture laser
speckle imaging (significantly higher CBF recovery).

**3. BDNF/neuroplasticity:**

Scalp acupuncture (motor area territory) upregulates BDNF/TrkB →
PI3K/AKT + ERK/CREB → synaptic proteins (SYN, PSD-95) →
synaptogenesis (GV24+GV20 EA rat MCAO studies). Not confirmed
MS6-specifically but extrapolated from same cortical territory.

**4. motion-style scalp acupuncture mechanism — activity-dependent neuroplasticity:**

Concurrent motor activation during needling enhances
activity-dependent neuroplasticity: MS6 needling creates a "primed"
cortical state (enhanced CBF, altered cortical excitability) that
is then shaped by volitional movement. Specific pathway: MS6 →
5-HT2AR upregulation → spinal KCC2 reactivation → restored
inhibitory tone. This represents TECHNIQUE-SPECIFIC mechanism:
same line, same needling, different outcomes depending on whether
the patient simultaneously moves.

⚡ **Framework note 2 — SEGMENT-SPECIFIC CORTICAL MODULATION:** MS6
is the first acupoint/line in the Atlas with fMRI evidence of
WITHIN-LINE somatotopic specificity: hand segment stimulation
modulates contralateral M1 hand area, but foot and mouth segments do
NOT (Zheng 2016). This is higher-resolution specificity than any prior
finding — not just point-specific but SEGMENT-specific within a single
line. The hemisphere-crossing "release" (ipsilateral inhibition +
contralateral disinhibition) may be the neural substrate for how scalp
acupuncture facilitates motor recovery in hemiplegia. Extends ⚡1
(prefrontal convergence) into motor cortex territory.

⚡ **Framework note 3 — MOTION-STYLE SCALP ACUPUNCTURE / ACTIVITY-DEPENDENT
NEUROPLASTICITY:** Interactive dynamic scalp acupuncture (concurrent needling +
active movement) outperforms static needling across 3 RCTs + fMRI
+ rat mechanistic study. motion-style scalp acupuncture 2025 identifies a specific causal
pathway: MS6 → CBF augmentation + 5-HT2AR → spinal KCC2 → restored
GABAergic tone. This is the first TECHNIQUE-SPECIFIC mechanism in
the Atlas: same anatomical line, same needling parameters, different
outcomes based on concurrent motor activity. Implication: scalp
acupuncture may create a "neuroplasticity window" maximally exploited
by simultaneous motor training — consistent with general
activity-dependent plasticity principles but now evidenced for a
specific scalp acupuncture line.

**Kỹ thuật kích thích:**

Needle: 0.25-0.30 mm × 25-50 mm (longer needles for full-length).
Insertion at GV21 (or EX-HN1), directed obliquely toward GB6 at
15-30°, threading into Layer L.
Segment-specific needling: for focal indications, only the relevant
segment — e.g., middle 2/5 for hand function, lower 2/5 for aphasia.
Stimulation: rapid twirling 200+ RPM (Jiao method), 1-2 min × 2-3
bouts, 20-30 min retention.
Interactive/dynamic (motion-style scalp acupuncture): patient performs active motor exercises
of the affected limb during needle retention + stimulation. Evidence
supports this over static needling.
EA: 2-100 Hz, 1-3 mA, continuous or dense-disperse wave.
Depth: 1-4 cm. CRITICAL: must remain subgaleal (Layer L). Excessive
depth in anticoagulated patients risks vascular injury — 4 reported
intracranial hemorrhage cases across the literature when needling
was inappropriately deep (Permanente Journal review).

**An toàn:**

Shared scalp acupuncture safety profile (see zone header). MS6-
specific: the temporal segment (near GB6) approaches the superficial
temporal artery in Layer C — shallow insertion and vessel awareness
required. Anticoagulated patients: increased bleeding risk (OR=4.95),
4 reported intracranial hemorrhage cases from inappropriately deep
needling. Mandatory subgaleal technique.

### MS7 — Đỉnh Nhiếp Hậu Tà Tuyến (顶颞后斜线, Dǐng Niè Hòu Xié Xiàn) — Posterior Oblique Line of Vertex-Temporal

**Evidence tier: A (Deep Analysis)**

The second most important scalp acupuncture line, running parallel to
and 1.5 cm/cun posterior to MS6 (Motor Line). Corresponds to Jiao's
Sensory Area (感觉区). MS7 overlies the postcentral gyrus (primary
somatosensory cortex, S1). Used primarily for sensory recovery
(numbness, paresthesia) and pain conditions (TMD, trigeminal
neuralgia, CRPS, phantom limb pain). MS6+MS7 = the "core
prescription" — co-selected 95.2% of the time in stroke trials.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Chinese standard location: from GV20 (Baihui, 百会) obliquely to GB7
(Qubin, 曲鬓).
Direction: vertex → temporal, oblique, parallel and posterior to MS6.
Body acupoint endpoints: GV20 (AP-13) → GB7 (AP-11). Bilateral.

Offset from MS6: 1.5 cm (Jiao's original) or 1.5 cun (some WHO
interpretations) posterior. The discrepancy likely reflects Jiao's
original system using fixed metric distances vs WHO's proportional
cun — flagged as needing primary WHO/ISSA 1984 document verification.

**Somatotopic division (3 zones, 5 units — Jiao Sensory Area):**

| Zone | Fraction | Body region (contralateral) | Cortical area |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper | 1/5 | Lower limb + trunk + head | Medial postcentral gyrus (S1, BA 3/1/2) |
| Middle | 2/5 | Upper limb | Lateral postcentral gyrus (S1) |
| Lower | 2/5 | Face | Inferior postcentral gyrus (near lateral sulcus) |

Mirrors MS6's motor somatotopy exactly: foot/leg medial → hand/arm
lateral → face inferolateral. Shifted posteriorly onto the postcentral
gyrus.

Upper 1/5 indications are broader than MS6's (includes occipital pain,
dizziness, tinnitus) — consistent with the medial parietal cortex's
role in multisensory integration. Lower 2/5 targets trigeminal
neuralgia and TMD — consistent with the face area of S1.

S1 Brodmann areas: BA 3a (proprioceptive), BA 3b (primary tactile),
BA 1 (texture/integration), BA 2 (higher-order proprioceptive).

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply — innervation gradient:
- Vertex (GV20): C2 GON + V1 supraorbital boundary. MS7 starts at
  GV20 (V1/C2 watershed) while MS6 starts at GV21 (0.5-1.5 cun more
  anterior, deeper V1). MS7's upper segment thus has GREATER C2
  contribution — an anatomically meaningful distinction.
- Temporal (GB7): V3 auriculotemporal nerve.

MS7 innervation gradient: C2/V1 → V3. Compared to MS6: V1 → V2 → V3.
The greater C2 component may enhance trigeminocervical complex (TCC)
activation — relevant to migraine/cervicogenic headache mechanisms
(Bartsch & Goadsby 2003).

Vascular: superficial temporal artery branches near GB7 in Layer C.
Cortical area beneath: postcentral gyrus (S1, BA 3/1/2) throughout.
Secondary somatosensory cortex (S2) in parietal operculum near
inferior end.
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| WHO standard | MS7 顶颞后斜线 | ✓ Primary standard |
| Jiao Shunfa | Sensory Area (感觉区) | ✓ Direct |
| YNSA | No direct mapping (YNSA Sensory Points = special senses) | ✗ Different paradigm |
| Zhu Mingqing | No confirmed correspondence | — |
| Body acupoints | GV20 (AP-13) → GB7 (AP-11) | ✓ Endpoints |

YNSA note: YNSA's "Sensory Points" target special sense organs (eyes,
ears, nose, mouth) — functionally different from MS7's body-surface
somatosensory strip. No structural correspondence found. Independent
theoretical frameworks.

→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV20 (start point, V1+C2, PVN→oxytocin)

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Per somatotopic segment:
- **Upper 1/5:** contralateral leg/back numbness, paresthesia,
  occipital pain, dizziness, tinnitus
- **Middle 2/5:** contralateral arm/hand numbness, pain, paresthesia
- **Lower 2/5:** contralateral facial numbness, trigeminal neuralgia
  (三叉神经痛), toothache, TMD/temporomandibular pain

Chinese textbooks: post-stroke sensory loss (偏身感觉障碍), phantom
limb pain, CRPS, cortical sensory deficits. Combined with MS6 for
patients with both motor and sensory deficits (the standard approach).

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**TMD pain 🟡 Plausible:**

Peixoto et al. 2021 (Pain Medicine 22(4):905-914, PMID 33561277):
RCT n=60 (4 arms × 15: counseling, splint, scalp acupuncture, manual
therapy). Scalp acupuncture: MS6+MS7 lower 2/5 (face zones,
bilateral) + GV20, GV21, GV22, GV24. 8 sessions, 40 min, 2×/week:
- Pain: VAS 4.4→0.73 = **83.4% reduction** after 8 sessions
- Immediate relief in 94.2% of sessions with baseline pain
- 30-day follow-up: partial regression to VAS 2.46 (44% sustained)
- Sleep/QoL: scalp acupuncture did NOT improve sleep (splint and
  manual therapy did)
- Combined MS6+MS7 > either alone for sleep (35.3% vs 19.2% vs 13.8%)

Note: previously cited as "Fernandes 2021" in child plan — corrected
to Peixoto et al. 2021.

**CRPS 🔴 Case-level evidence:**

Hommer 2012 (Military Medicine 177(10):1231): 2 soldiers, upper-limb

- Pain VAS/NRS >80% improvement

**Post-stroke sensory recovery 🔴 Insufficient MS7-specific data:**

2025 SR/MA (PMC11841453, 57 RCTs, 4,794 patients): scalp acupuncture
used in only 7/57 trials, NOT analyzed as separate subgroup. Overall
acupuncture: effective rate RR=1.21, sensory score MD=5.37, VAS pain
MD=−1.44. MS7-specific data cannot be extracted. Genuine evidence gap.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

MS7 shares the trigeminovascular pathway with MS6 (see zone header
and MS6 mechanism). Key mechanistic differences:

**1. Target cortex: S1 (sensory input) vs M1 (motor output):**

MS7 overlies S1 — the primary cortex for body-surface sensation.
Therapeutic hypothesis: needling over S1 modulates AFFERENT processing
(incoming sensory information) rather than EFFERENT motor commands.
Consistent with use for sensory recovery and pain modulation.

**2. Greater C2 contribution → enhanced TCC activation:**

MS7's more posterior start (GV20) gives greater C2 GON input than
MS6 (GV21). C2 afferents converge with trigeminal at the
trigeminocervical complex — the same convergence underlying migraine
(Bartsch & Goadsby 2003). Enhanced TCC activation may contribute to
MS7's pain-modulation effects (head/neck pain, trigeminal neuralgia).

**3. No MS7-specific fMRI (evidence gap):**

Unlike MS6 (Zheng 2016 segment-specific fMRI), no published study
isolates MS7 and measures S1 BOLD activation. General scalp-vs-limb
acupuncture fMRI (PMID 19922250) showed greater contralateral
postcentral gyrus activation with scalp acupuncture, but was not
MS7-specific. This is a significant gap — MS7 lacks the
neuroimaging validation that MS6 has.

**4. Pain mechanism — not DNIC:**

MS7 is sometimes hypothesized to work via DNIC (counter-irritation).
However, Schliessbach 2012 (PMC3132481) found acupuncture at low
pain intensity did NOT produce reliable DNIC effects. MS7's pain
modulation more likely involves cortical somatosensory processing
via the trigeminovascular pathway + potential reorganization of
central sensitization patterns at S1 level.

⚡ **Framework note 4 — MOTOR-SENSORY ASYMMETRY IN EVIDENCE:** MS6
(motor) has segment-specific fMRI validation (Zheng 2016) but MS7
(sensory) does not — despite being anatomically parallel, equally
important clinically, and routinely used together. This creates an
asymmetric evidence profile: the motor cortex modulation hypothesis
has neuroimaging support, while the somatosensory cortex modulation
hypothesis remains anatomically plausible but unconfirmed by imaging.
The asymmetry likely reflects research priorities (motor recovery is
a more measurable outcome than sensory recovery in stroke) rather
than a genuine mechanistic difference. This parallels the broader
Atlas pattern where motor-related evidence (ST36 GI motility, LI4
pain gate) consistently outweighs sensory-related evidence.

**Kỹ thuật kích thích:**

Same as MS6 (see MS6 Kỹ thuật section). Insertion at GV20, directed
obliquely toward GB7 at 15-30°, Layer L. Segment-specific needling
for focal indications. Interactive/dynamic technique recommended
(concurrent sensory rehabilitation during retention). EA: 2-100 Hz.

## Temporal Lines (颞区) — MS10-MS11

> **Anatomy:** Two lines on the temporal scalp, overlying the
> squamous temporal bone and greater wing of the sphenoid bone.
> Both follow the Gallbladder meridian curvature, running superior
> to inferior. The temporal bone is the THINNEST skull region
> (~3.5 mm at the pterion) — but subcutaneous threading at 15-30°
> keeps the needle in Layer L, parallel to the bone surface.
> Intracranial penetration is anatomically impossible with standard
> technique.
>
> **Relationship to MS6/MS7:** MS6 (Motor Line) terminates at GB6
> — the SAME endpoint as MS10. MS7 (Sensory Line) terminates at
> GB7 — the SAME endpoint as MS11. The oblique vertex-temporal
> lines thus TRANSITION INTO the dedicated temporal lines at
> shared endpoints. The somatotopic gradient of MS6/MS7 (leg→arm→
> face/speech from vertex to temporal) culminates in the cortical
> areas covered by MS10 (language production) and MS11
> (auditory/vestibular processing).
>
> **Innervation:** V2/V3 transition zone. MS10 (anterior temporal):
> V2 zygomaticotemporal (anterior portion) → V3 auriculotemporal
> (primary supply). MS11 (posterior temporal): V3 auriculotemporal
> primarily, with C2 GON/LON overlap at the superior end (GB8). A
> direct nerve-to-nerve interconnection between the V3
> auriculotemporal and C2 lesser occipital nerves is documented
> precisely in the MS11 territory (~3 mm above ear apex, PMID
> 35795503).
>
> **Vascular:** Superficial temporal artery (STA) and branches —
> dominant vessels, running in Layer C alongside the V3
> auriculotemporal nerve. PALPATE the arterial pulse before
> insertion. Below the skull: the middle meningeal artery (MMA)
> runs in the epidural space — NOT an acupuncture risk (separated
> by full skull thickness).
>
> **Cortical areas beneath:**
> - MS10 (anterior temporal): perisylvian language network —
>   inferior frontal gyrus / Broca's area (BA 44/45) on dominant
>   hemisphere → superior temporal gyrus (BA 22). Language
>   production cortex.
> - MS11 (posterior temporal): superior temporal gyrus (BA 22,
>   auditory association cortex / Wernicke's area on dominant
>   hemisphere). Primary auditory cortex (Heschl's gyrus, BA 41/42)
>   and parieto-insular vestibular cortex (PIVC) are DEEP in the
>   lateral sulcus — not directly on the lateral surface.
>
> **Jiao zones in temporal region:**
> - Speech I area (语言一区): = lower 2/5 of the Motor Area (MS6
>   inferior segment), NOT MS10. But MS10 is co-prescribed with
>   MS6 lower 2/5 for motor aphasia (shared GB6 endpoint).
> - Speech III area (语言三区): posterior half of the
>   Vertigo-Auditory area, roughly MS11 territory. For sensory
>   (receptive) aphasia on dominant hemisphere.
> - Vertigo-Auditory area (眩晕听觉区): 4 cm horizontal line,
>   centered 1.5 cm above ear apex → partial overlap with MS11
>   (which runs vertically; they cross above the ear).
>
> **Needle technique (temporal lines):**
> Maintain strict 15-30° angle (thin temporal bone — never
> perpendicular). Palpate for STA before insertion. Threading
> direction: superior → inferior for both MS10 and MS11.
>
> **Clinical significance:** MS10 is the primary line for
> post-stroke motor aphasia (co-prescribed with MS6 lower 2/5) —
> scalp + tongue acupuncture + speech training is the most
> effective combination (SUCRA 97.9%, Feng 2022). MS11 is the
> primary line for tinnitus (20 RCTs SR, Chen & Jing 2025),
> vertigo, and auditory disorders. Together, they cover the two
> major temporal lobe functions: language and hearing/balance.

### MS10 — Nhiếp Tiền Tuyến (颞前线, Niè Qián Xiàn) — Anterior Temporal Line

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

Anterior temporal line on the dominant hemisphere — the primary
scalp acupuncture line for post-stroke motor aphasia, co-prescribed
with MS6 lower 2/5. MS10 overlies the perisylvian language network
at the frontotemporal junction: inferior frontal gyrus / Broca's
area (BA 44/45) transitioning to superior temporal gyrus (BA 22).
Critically, MS10 is NOT identical to Jiao's Speech I area —
Speech I overlaps the lower 2/5 of the Motor Area (MS6), while
MS10 is a separate, adjacent line sharing the GB6 endpoint. The
GB/T 21709.2-2021 standard recommends MS6 lower 2/5 + MS10
together for motor aphasia. The strongest evidence comes from the
triple combination: scalp + tongue acupuncture + speech-language
training (SUCRA 97.9%, Feng 2022).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Chinese standard (GB/T 21709.2-2008): from GB4 (Hanyan, 颔厌)
to GB6 (Xuanli, 悬厘), along the curve of the temporal scalp.
GB4 is at the junction of the upper 1/4 and lower 3/4 of the
ST8-GB7 arc (anterior temporal, near the pterion). GB6 is at the
junction of the lower 1/4 and upper 3/4 of the same arc
(bilateral).
Body acupoint endpoints: GB4 + GB6 (both AP-11).
Meridian traversed: Gallbladder channel (Foot Shaoyang, 足少阳胆经).
Direction: superior → inferior. Length: approximately 2-3 cm.

Note: GB6 is ALSO the inferior endpoint of MS6 (Motor Line). This
shared endpoint creates anatomical continuity between the oblique
vertex-temporal Motor Line and the dedicated anterior temporal line.

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: V2 zygomaticotemporal nerve (anterior portion near
GB4) transitioning to V3 auriculotemporal nerve (primary supply
for the bulk of the line). V2/V3 transition zone. The
auriculotemporal nerve courses closely with the superficial
temporal artery.
Vascular: STA parietal branch in Layer C. Palpate before insertion.
Cortical area beneath: on the dominant (left) hemisphere —
perisylvian language network at the frontotemporal junction.
Pars opercularis (BA 44) and pars triangularis (BA 45) of the
inferior frontal gyrus = Broca's area territory, transitioning to
superior temporal gyrus (BA 22). The insula lies deep to the
lateral sulcus, NOT directly beneath the scalp surface. On the
non-dominant hemisphere: homologous cortex for prosody and
emotional communication, not a primary speech area.
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

| WHO standard | MS10 颞前线 | ✓ Primary |
| Jiao | Speech I area (语言一区) | ≈ Adjacent — Speech I = lower 2/5 of MS6, NOT MS10. Co-prescribed for motor aphasia. |
| Body acupoints | GB4 Hanyan + GB6 Xuanli (颔厌 + 悬厘, AP-11) | ✓ Endpoints |

on the dominant hemisphere — the PRIMARY indication. Also: oral
and facial motor dysfunction (口面功能障碍).
Jiao indications: motor (Broca's) aphasia — non-fluent, effortful

hemisphere (left for >95% right-handed individuals).
Chinese textbooks: combine MS10 with MS6 lower 2/5 for Broca's
aphasia; add tongue acupuncture (sublingual points) for combined
peripheral-central speech rehabilitation.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Post-stroke motor aphasia 🟡 Plausible — strong NMA trend but
combination protocols prevent MS10-specific attribution:**

Two major NMAs establish the aphasia evidence landscape:

(1) Liang et al. 2022 (Brain Sciences, PMID 36291216): 69 RCTs,
5,097 patients. 14 acupuncture modalities compared. Scalp
acupuncture ALONE: SUCRA = 17.8% (LOWEST among all modalities).
Tongue acupuncture alone: SUCRA = 86%. Body + scalp acupuncture:
OR = 3.28 (1.51-7.13) vs speech-language training.

(2) Feng et al. 2022 (Frontiers in Neurology, PMID 36619913):
29 RCTs, 2,082 patients (MOTOR aphasia only, Bayesian NMA).
Scalp + tongue acupuncture + speech training (STA+ST):
SUCRA = 97.9%, OR = 8.30 (95% CrI 3.87-17.33) vs speech training.
Scalp acupuncture + speech training: SUCRA = 44.3%, OR = 2.95.

Note: the "SUCRA 97.9%" in the zone header evidence table is from
Feng 2022 (29 RCTs, motor aphasia), not from a single 69-RCT
study. The attribution to "Zhang 2022" in the zone header may
require correction in the Review phase.

**Liu 2021 — MS10-specific RCT (BMJ Open, PMID 34489271):**

203 patients with Broca's aphasia, community-based, 13 Chinese
health centers. Protocol: MS6 lower 40% + MS10 + speech-language
therapy. 0.30 mm × 40 mm needle, 10-20° insertion, rapid twirling
180-300 RPM. 30 min/day, 5 days/week, 4 weeks.
- BDAE grade: 1.78→2.96 (intervention) vs 1.44→1.98 (control)
  Difference: 0.65 (95% CI 0.43-0.86), P < 0.001
- CRRCAE: 286→517 vs 105→239, difference 182.47 (P < 0.001)
- Cost-effective: intervention cost LESS (4,002 vs 4,324 EUR)
  ICER = 495 EUR per BDAE grade gained.

**Neuroimaging — Li 2025 fNIRS (PMID 39892714):**

64 patients, assessor-blinded RCT. fNIRS confirmed scalp
acupuncture in the Speech I / MS10 territory modulated cortical
activation in the left pars opercularis (BA 44, Broca's area),
left frontopolar area, left fusiform gyrus, and right superior
temporal gyrus. WAB scores improved (all subscales P < 0.001).

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

**1. Trigeminovascular pathway at V2/V3 territory:**

V2 zygomaticotemporal and V3 auriculotemporal afferents from MS10
→ trigeminal ganglion → antidromic CGRP vasodilation increasing
CBF in the perisylvian cortex (Broca's area territory). Same
three-pathway mechanism as vertex lines (see zone header).

**2. Cortical activation (fNIRS confirmed):**

Li 2025 directly confirms left BA 44 activation. Li 2022
neuroimaging SR (PMID 35494482, 16 studies) found enhanced Broca's
area activation and intensified frontoparietal/DMN connectivity
with scalp acupuncture for aphasia. MS10 was used in 10% of those
studies — insufficient for MS10-specific attribution, but confirms
perisylvian activation from temporal scalp acupuncture.

**3. Perisylvian network modulation (not point-specific):**

MS6 lower 2/5 (motor speech cortex via M1 face area) + MS10
(frontotemporal junction / Broca's territory) provides
comprehensive dorsal and ventral language stream coverage. Adding
tongue acupuncture provides peripheral speech-motor stimulation —
the triple combination dramatically exceeds any single modality.

⚡ **Framework note 5 — MULTI-MODAL SYNERGY EXCEEDS SINGLE-MODALITY
CORTICAL STIMULATION:** Scalp acupuncture alone for aphasia:
SUCRA = 17.8% (Liang 2022, 69 RCTs) — the LOWEST ranking among
all 14 modalities tested. Scalp + tongue acupuncture + speech
training: SUCRA = 97.9% (Feng 2022, 29 RCTs). The ~80-point
SUCRA gap demonstrates that central cortical stimulation (scalp
acupuncture) is dramatically more effective when combined with
peripheral effector stimulation (tongue acupuncture → hypoglossal/
lingual nerve at speech muscles) and active cognitive
rehabilitation (speech-language therapy). This parallels ⚡3 (motion-style scalp acupuncture)
in the motor domain: concurrent movement during motor-line needling
outperforms static retention. The principle appears general across
scalp acupuncture: cortical "priming" amplifies concurrent targeted
rehabilitation, rather than acting as a standalone treatment.

**Kỹ thuật kích thích:**

Insertion at GB4 (superior), directed inferiorly toward GB6 at
15-30° (10-20° per Liu 2021), subcutaneous into Layer L. 0.30 mm
× 40 mm needle. Rapid twirling 180-300 RPM. Palpate for STA.
EA: 2-100 Hz if used. 30 min retention, 2-3 stimulation bouts.
For motor aphasia: combine with MS6 lower 2/5 on dominant
hemisphere + tongue acupuncture (sublingual) + active
speech-language exercises during retention (interactive technique).

**An toàn:**

General scalp acupuncture safety applies (see zone header).
Temporal-specific: palpate for STA before insertion (runs with
auriculotemporal nerve in Layer C). Temporal bone ~3.5 mm at
pterion — subcutaneous technique (15-30°, parallel to bone)
prevents intracranial risk. MMA in epidural space = NOT an
acupuncture risk.

| Jiao | Vertigo-Auditory Area (眩晕听觉区) | Partial overlap — MS11 vertical, Jiao area horizontal; they cross above the ear |
| Jiao | Speech III area (语言三区, dominant) | Partial overlap — Speech III = posterior half of Vertigo-Auditory area |
| Body acupoints | GB8 Shuaigu + GB7 Qubin (率谷 + 曲鬓, AP-11) | ✓ Endpoints |

The Jiao Vertigo-Auditory area is a 4 cm horizontal line centered
1.5 cm above the ear apex. MS11 runs approximately vertically from
GB8 (~3.5 cm above ear apex) downward to GB7 (at ear apex level).
The two systems cross in the posterior temporal region — overlapping
target area, different line orientation. The Vertigo-Auditory area
is one of the more anatomically plausible Jiao zones (Ye 2023)
since it does overlie auditory association cortex (BA 22).
On the dominant hemisphere, the posterior portion overlaps Speech
III area — for sensory (receptive) aphasia. This dual indication
reflects the anatomical proximity of auditory cortex and Wernicke's
area on the superior temporal gyrus.
→ Cross-ref: AP-11 GB8, GB7; MS7 (shared GB7 endpoint); MS14
(cerebellar Balance Area — complementary for vestibular disorders)

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Standard indications (GB/T 21709.2): vertigo (眩晕), tinnitus
(耳鸣), hearing loss (耳聋), auditory hallucinations (幻听).
Jiao Vertigo-Auditory area: Ménière's disease, dizziness,
tinnitus, hearing impairment. On the dominant hemisphere (Speech
III overlap): sensory/receptive aphasia (语义性失语).
Chinese textbooks: cerebellar infarction vertigo (combined with
MS14 Balance Area), post-stroke auditory processing deficits.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Tinnitus 🟡 Plausible — dedicated scalp acupuncture SR:**

Chen & Jing 2025 (Complementary Therapies in Medicine, PMID
39828220): 20 RCTs, 1,430 patients — the ONLY systematic review
specifically on scalp acupuncture for tinnitus. Compared scalp
acupuncture vs body acupuncture (8 RCTs), sham (4 RCTs), no
treatment (1 RCT), and medications (7 RCTs). Outcome measures:
clinical effective rate, THI, VAS, TEQ. Full effect sizes
unavailable (paywall). This is the key evidence anchor for MS11 —
one of the few non-stroke SRs in the scalp acupuncture literature.

fMRI support: Wei et al. 2022 (Frontiers in Neuroscience, PMID
35153654): 17 chronic tinnitus patients. Acupuncture including
GB8 (MS11 territory) decreased dynamic functional connectivity
(DFC) temporal variability between PCC and angular gyrus
(T = −8.96, P < 0.00001). Reduced tinnitus scores correlated with
decreased connectivity variability. Note: used GB8 as a body
acupoint (perpendicular), not MS11 line threading (subcutaneous).

**Vertigo / vestibular disorders 🔴 Insufficient MS11-specific
data:**

Li et al. 2022 (PMID 35800002): 20 RCTs, 1,541 patients,
acupuncture for posterior circulation infarction vertigo. Basilar
artery flow SMD = 0.58 (0.40-0.76), clinical effective rate
RR = 1.22 (1.15-1.29). But predominantly BODY acupuncture (GB20,
GV20, BL10) — scalp acupuncture MS11 not specifically analyzed.

Cerebellar infarction study (100 cases): electroacupuncture at
BOTH the Balance Area (MS14 region) AND Vertigo-Auditory area
(MS11 region) — improved brain blood circulation and vertigo
outcomes vs body acupuncture group. The most directly relevant
study for MS11 vestibular application.

Ménière's disease: Tang et al. 2024 (PMID 39722819): 6 RCTs, 494
patients. RR = 1.20, DHI MD = 6.94, PTA MD = 6.57 dB. But body
acupuncture — no included RCTs used scalp acupuncture.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

**1. Trigeminovascular pathway at V3/C2 territory:**

V3 auriculotemporal afferents from MS11 → trigeminal ganglion →
antidromic CGRP vasodilation in the temporal lobe vascular
territory (MCA temporal branches) → increased CBF to auditory and
vestibular cortex. C2 GON/LON contribution at GB8 →
trigeminocervical complex → additional brainstem modulation pathway.

**2. Trigeminal-vestibulocochlear reflex:**

V3 territory has specific connections to the vestibulocochlear
system. Neurogenic inflammation via the trigeminal-vestibulocochlear
reflex contributes to vestibular migraine and other vestibular
symptoms. Stimulating V3 afferents in the MS11 territory may
modulate this reflex — a mechanism beyond simple CBF effects.

**3. MS11 vs MS14 — differential vestibular mechanism:**

MS11 targets cortical vestibular/auditory processing (temporal
cortex → perceptual integration), while MS14 targets cerebellar
balance processing (coordination, postural stability, motor
learning). Combined MS11 + MS14 addresses both perceptual (cortical)
and motor (cerebellar) components of vestibular disorders.

**4. Cortical depth caveat:**

Primary auditory cortex (BA 41/42) and PIVC are in the lateral
sulcus, not on the exposed surface. MS11 directly overlies only
the auditory ASSOCIATION cortex (BA 22). The trigeminovascular CBF
enhancement in the MCA territory may reach deeper structures, but
direct cortical stimulation through the skull is not the mechanism
(Liu 2023, Ye 2023).

**Kỹ thuật kích thích:**

Insertion at GB8 (superior), directed DOWNWARD toward GB7 at
15-30°, subcutaneous threading into Layer L. 0.30 mm × 25-40 mm
needle. Rapid twirling 200+ RPM. Palpate for STA (artery
superficial to nerve in ~80% of individuals). EA: 2-100 Hz if used.
30 min retention.
For complex vestibular: combine with MS14 (Balance Area,
BL9→BL10) — addresses both cortical and cerebellar pathways.
For sensory aphasia (dominant hemisphere): combine with MS7 lower
2/5 + Speech III area coverage.

**An toàn:**

General scalp acupuncture safety applies (see zone header).
Temporal-specific: palpate for STA before insertion. Squamous
temporal bone ~3.5 mm — subcutaneous technique (15-30°, parallel
to bone) prevents intracranial penetration. Apply gentle pressure
after removal (STA proximity). MMA in epidural space = NOT an
acupuncture risk.

## Occipital Lines (枕区) — MS12-MS14

> **Anatomy:** Three lines on the occipital scalp, overlying the
> occipital bone. The occipital region spans two critical structures
> separated by the tentorium cerebelli: the occipital lobe (primary
> visual cortex) ABOVE and the cerebellum (balance, coordination,
> motor learning) BELOW. MS12 and MS13 (upper occipital) overlie
> visual cortex. MS14 (lower occipital) descends toward cerebellar
> territory.
>
> **Innervation:** Entirely C2/C3 cervical — NO trigeminal
> contribution at this posterior location. The greater occipital
> nerve (GON, C2 dorsal ramus) is dominant, ascending from the
> suboccipital region to supply most of the posterior scalp. The
> third occipital nerve (TON, C3) supplies the lower midline
> occiput. The lesser occipital nerve (LON, C2-C3) supplies the
> lateral posterior scalp behind the ear.
> → Different mechanism pathway from vertex/temporal lines:
> C2/C3 → trigeminocervical complex (C1-C3 converge with
> trigeminal) → brainstem, not directly via trigeminal ganglion.
>
> **Vascular:** Occipital artery (external carotid branch) in
> Layer C, running with the GON. Emissary veins connecting scalp
> drainage to the transverse sinus.
>
> **Cortical areas beneath:**
> - MS12 + MS13 (upper): primary visual cortex (V1, BA 17) and
>   visual association cortex (V2/V3, BA 18/19) — the calcarine
>   fissure and surrounding occipital cortex
> - MS14 (lower): transition zone from occipital cortex to
>   cerebellum — the tentorium lies approximately at the level of
>   the external occipital protuberance
>
> **Jiao zones in occipital region:**
> - Vision Area (视区): overlies occipital cortex above the
>   tentorium → MS12 + MS13 territory
> - Balance Area (平衡区): overlies cerebellum below the tentorium
>   → MS14 territory
>
> **Needle technique (occipital lines):**
> GON lies in Layer C — potential for nerve irritation during
> insertion. Occipital bone is thick (6-8 mm at the protuberance)
> — intracranial risk minimal. At the LOWER occipital region (near
> MS14 inferior endpoint), maintain strict subcutaneous technique
> as the foramen magnum is approached.
>
> **Clinical significance:** MS12/MS13 target visual cortex for
> cortical visual disorders. MS14 targets cerebellar territory for
> balance and coordination — complementary to MS11 (cortical
> vestibular, temporal region). MS14 is the only Tier B occipital
> entry.

### MS12 — Chẩm Thượng Chính Trung Tuyến (枕上正中线, Zhěn Shàng Zhèng Zhōng Xiàn) — Upper-Middle Line of Occiput

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

Occipital midline treatment line overlying the primary visual
cortex (V1, BA 17) — the midline component of the Jiao Vision
Area (视区). Targets the calcarine fissure region where the visual
cortex processes central visual field information. Used for
cortical visual disorders (cortical blindness, visual field
deficits after occipital stroke).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Chinese standard (GB/T 21709.2-2008): from GV18 (Qiangjian, 强间)
to GV17 (Naohu, 脑户), along the midline.
Body acupoint endpoints: GV18 + GV17 (both AP-13).
Meridian traversed: Governing Vessel (Du Mai, 督脉).
Direction: superior → inferior. Length: 1.5 cun. Midline only
(unpaired).

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: C2 greater occipital nerve (GON). NO trigeminal at
this posterior location.
Vascular: occipital artery branches (external carotid) in Layer C,
running with the GON. Emissary veins near the external occipital
protuberance.
Cortical area beneath: primary visual cortex (V1, BA 17) at the
calcarine fissure, and visual association cortex (V2, BA 18). This
is cerebral cortex ABOVE the tentorium cerebelli — not cerebellum.
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| WHO standard | MS12 枕上正中线 | ✓ Primary |
| Jiao | Vision Area (视区), midline portion | ✓ Direct |
| Body acupoints | GV18 Qiangjian + GV17 Naohu (强间 + 脑户, AP-13) | ✓ Endpoints |

→ Cross-ref: AP-13 GV17, GV18

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Standard indications (GB/T 21709.2): cortical visual disorders
(皮层性视力障碍). Jiao Vision Area: cortical blindness, visual field
deficits, blurred vision. Chinese textbooks: post-stroke occipital
lobe visual impairment, dizziness with visual symptoms, occipital
headache. No dedicated SR/RCT evidence for MS12 alone — used in
combination with MS13 for post-stroke visual recovery protocols.

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

Paramedian occipital line 0.5 cun lateral to MS12 — the lateral
component of the Jiao Vision Area. Overlies the lateral visual
cortex, processing peripheral visual field information. Bilateral
— one line on each side, flanking MS12.

relationship to MS12 midline.
Nearest body acupoint: BL8 Luoque (络却, AP-07) at 1.5 cun
lateral — further lateral than MS13 (0.5 cun lateral).
Direction: superior → inferior. Length: 1.5 cun. Bilateral.

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: C2 greater occipital nerve (GON). At 0.5 cun lateral,
still firmly in GON territory. NO trigeminal.
Vascular: occipital artery branches in Layer C.
Cortical area beneath: visual association cortex (V2/V3, BA 18/19).
The lateral occipital cortex processes peripheral visual fields and
higher-order visual processing (motion, form, color).
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL8 (nearby but not exact endpoint)

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Standard indications (GB/T 21709.2): cortical visual disorders
(same as MS12). Jiao Vision Area: cortical blindness, visual field
deficits. Chinese textbooks: bilateral MS13 + midline MS12 =
"three-line protocol" for occipital visual rehabilitation. MS13
specifically targets PERIPHERAL visual field loss (lateral occipital
cortex processes peripheral vision). No dedicated SR/RCT evidence
— always used with MS12 in combined vision protocols.

### MS14 — Chẩm Hạ Bàng Tuyến (枕下旁线, Zhěn Xià Páng Xiàn) — Lower-Lateral Line of Occiput

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

Lower occipital line spanning the tentorium cerebelli transition
zone — the boundary between the occipital lobe (visual cortex)
above and the cerebellum (balance/coordination) below. MS14 is
the ONLY MS line that predominantly overlies infratentorial
structures (cerebellum). BL9 (superior endpoint) sits at the level
of the external occipital protuberance, which corresponds
internally to the confluence of sinuses (torcular Herophili) /
tentorium attachment — the exact supratentorial-infratentorial
boundary. BL10 (inferior endpoint) is at the occipitocervical
junction, clearly in cerebellar territory. Corresponds to Jiao's
Balance Area (平衡区). Complementary to MS11 (cortical vestibular
perception, temporal) — MS14 targets cerebellar balance
coordination while MS11 targets cortical vestibular processing.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Chinese standard (GB/T 21709.2-2008): from BL9 (Yuzhen, 玉枕)
to BL10 (Tianzhu, 天柱). BL9 is at 1.3 cun lateral to GV17, at
the level of the external occipital protuberance. BL10 is at 1.3
cun lateral to GV15, 0.5 cun above the posterior hairline, at the
occipitocervical junction on the lateral side of the trapezius
(bilateral).
Body acupoint endpoints: BL9 + BL10 (both AP-07).
Meridian traversed: Bladder channel (Foot Taiyang, 足太阳膀胱经).
Direction: superior → inferior (MUST be needled top-down — one of
only two Jiao zones with this directional requirement). Length:
2 cun (~4-5 cm).

Note: BL10 is a DANGER POINT for body acupuncture (deep
perpendicular needling in a medial-superior direction risks the
medulla oblongata). For MS14 scalp acupuncture, the needle enters
at BL9 (on thick occipital bone) and threads DOWNWARD
subcutaneously — remaining in Layer L on the bone surface. The
body acupuncture danger does NOT apply to the scalp threading
technique.

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: C2 greater occipital nerve (GON, dorsal ramus of C2)
— dominant. C3 third occipital nerve (TON) at the lower portion
(BL10 region). NO trigeminal contribution — MS14 is entirely in
C2/C3 cervical territory, unlike all other MS lines which include
at least some trigeminal input.
The GON emerges from the suboccipital triangle (between C1 and C2),
pierces trapezius aponeurosis at the superior nuchal line —
directly in the MS14 territory.
Vascular: occipital artery (external carotid branch) runs with the
GON in Layer C.
Structures beneath:
- Upper portion (BL9): TRANSITION ZONE at the tentorium level. The
  external occipital protuberance = internal occipital protuberance
  = confluence of sinuses where superior sagittal sinus, straight
  sinus, and transverse sinuses meet. Occipital lobe visual cortex
  (BA 17/18) above the tentorium; cerebellar superior surface below.
- Lower portion (toward BL10): posterior fossa — cerebellar
  hemispheres (neocerebellum). At BL10 level, the medulla oblongata
  is deep but separated by occipital bone + meninges + CSF.
SCALP layer target: Layer L. Occipital bone is thick (6-8 mm at the
protuberance) — subcutaneous needle cannot reach intracranial
structures.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| WHO standard | MS14 枕下旁线 | ✓ Primary |
| Jiao | Balance Area (平衡区) | ≈ Substantial overlap, slight lateral offset (Jiao: 3.5 cm lateral to EOP; MS14: ~2.6 cm lateral) |
| Body acupoints | BL9 Yuzhen + BL10 Tianzhu (玉枕 + 天柱, AP-07) | ✓ Endpoints |

Jiao Balance Area: starts 3.5 cm lateral to the external occipital
protuberance, running 4 cm inferiorly. Must be needled top-down
(one of only two Jiao zones with this requirement). The Balance
Area is ~1 cm more lateral than MS14, but there is substantial
overlap in posterior fossa coverage. In clinical practice, MS14 and
the Balance Area are treated as functionally equivalent.
→ Cross-ref: AP-07 BL9, BL10; MS11 (cortical vestibular —
complementary); MS12/MS13 (visual cortex — supratentorial, adjacent
but different indication)

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Standard indications (GB/T 21709.2): balance disorders (平衡障碍),
cerebellar dysfunction, gait disturbance.
Jiao Balance Area: disequilibrium, cerebellar atrophy, cerebellar
stroke, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease (postural
instability), ataxia, traumatic brain injury-related balance loss.
Chinese textbooks: post-stroke gait rehabilitation (combined with
MS6 upper 1/5 and Foot Motor-Sensory Area), coordination disorders,
vestibular-cerebellar vertigo. Needled BILATERALLY for balance.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Post-stroke balance/gait 🟡 Plausible — part of combination
protocols:**

Scalp acupuncture + suspension training for stroke balance (PMID
34936265): RCT using bilateral MS14 region + parietal midline +
MS6 upper 1/5. Berg Balance Scale improved. Combination superior
to either modality alone.

Cerebellar infarction vertigo (100 cases): electroacupuncture at
Balance Area (MS14 region) + Vertigo-Auditory area (MS11 region)
— 90% effective rate vs 68% conventional body acupuncture. Improved
brain blood circulation. (Also documented in MS11 entry.)

BL9-BL10 threading for spastic cerebral palsy (PMID 21793388):
n=120 RCT, used the exact MS14 trajectory (4 needles threaded
subcutaneously BL9→BL10). Rationale: superficial projection of the
pyramidal decussation. 3 months daily treatment. Outcomes: modified
Ashworth (spasticity), GMFM-88 (gross motor function). Note: used
for motor/spasticity outcomes rather than balance.

**Parkinson's disease 🔴 Insufficient MS14-specific data:**

Oh et al. 2025 (Journal of Acupuncture Research): SR of scalp
acupuncture for Parkinson's, 15 RCTs, 991 patients. UPDRS total:
MD = 7.61 (6.69-8.53, P < 0.00001) for scalp acupuncture + Western medicine.
However, Balance Area used in ONLY 1/15 studies — most used
Chorea-Tremor Control Area (10 studies) and Motor Area (5 studies).
The Parkinson's indication for MS14 is traditional rather than
evidence-based.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

**1. C2/C3 → TCC pathway (NOT direct trigeminovascular):**

MS14 is entirely in C2/C3 cervical territory — NO trigeminal. The
mechanism pathway differs from vertex/temporal lines: C2/C3
GON/TON afferents → dorsal horn C2-C3 → trigeminocervical complex
(TCC, where C1-C3 converge with trigeminal) → (1) antidromic
CGRP/SP release in POSTERIOR meningeal vessels → vasodilation →
increased posterior fossa CBF; (2) parasympathetic activation via
facial nerve → cerebral vasodilation; (3) RVLM central pathway.

**2. Posterior circulation specificity:**

Liu 2023 (PMID 37650106): acupuncture at GB20 (C2-C3 territory,
near BL10) specifically enhances POSTERIOR circulation blood flow
(vertebral and basilar arteries) but NOT middle cerebral arteries.
Li 2022 (PMID 35800002): vertebrobasilar flow improvement —
basilar artery SMD = 0.58, vertebral arteries SMD = 0.44-0.48.
Improved vertebrobasilar flow = improved cerebellar perfusion →
improved cerebellar function. The cerebellum is supplied entirely
by posterior circulation (superior/inferior cerebellar arteries
from vertebral/basilar system).

**3. Occipital nerve → cerebellar blood flow:**

Regional CBF studies after occipital nerve modulation (from
headache literature) demonstrate blood flow changes in the
cerebellum — C2/C3 stimulation does affect cerebellar perfusion
via TCC convergence with trigeminal-mediated cerebrovascular
regulation.

⚡ **Framework note 6 — POSTERIOR CIRCULATION SPECIFICITY — CERVICAL
VS TRIGEMINAL VASCULAR TARGETS:** MS14 is the only MS line with
purely C2/C3 innervation (no trigeminal). C2/C3 stimulation
enhances POSTERIOR circulation (vertebrobasilar) flow but NOT
middle cerebral artery flow (Liu 2023). In contrast, V1/V2
vertex/temporal stimulation enhances MCA flow to motor/sensory/
language cortex. The innervation territory determines the vascular
territory modulated: anterior scalp (V1/V2/V3 trigeminal) →
anterior/middle cerebral artery territory → cortical function;
posterior scalp (C2/C3 cervical) → posterior cerebral/cerebellar
artery territory → cerebellar function. This topographic
correspondence between scalp nerve territory and cerebrovascular
territory provides the mechanistic basis for why different scalp
regions treat different neurological conditions — and explains why
MS14 specifically targets cerebellar (balance/coordination)
rather than cortical (motor/sensory/language) function.

**Kỹ thuật kích thích:**

Insertion at BL9 (superior), directed DOWNWARD toward BL10 at
15-30°, subcutaneous threading into Layer L. 0.30 mm × 25-40 mm
needle. MUST be needled top-down (Jiao Balance Area directional
requirement). Rapid twirling 200+ RPM. EA: 2-100 Hz if used.
Bilateral — always needle both sides for balance disorders.
For complex vestibular: combine with MS11 (Vertigo-Auditory,
cortical vestibular) — addresses both cerebellar and cortical
vestibular pathways.
For lower limb rehabilitation: combine with Foot Motor-Sensory Area + MS6 upper
1/5 + MS5 (lower limb cortical representation).

**An toàn:**

BL10 is a DANGER POINT for body acupuncture — deep needling in a
medial-superior direction risks the medulla oblongata. For MS14
scalp acupuncture, the needle threads DOWNWARD from BL9 on thick
occipital bone (6-8 mm) — the threading direction and skull
thickness prevent intracranial penetration. The vertebral artery
is deep (~3-4 cm) in the suboccipital triangle — unreachable by
subcutaneous needle. The cisterna magna is separated by the full
skull thickness + meninges + CSF. GON crosses the superior nuchal
line in MS14 territory — minor nerve irritation possible, but
acupuncture at the GON is actually a TREATMENT for occipital
neuralgia. General scalp acupuncture safety applies (see zone
header).

## Jiao-Only Zones (焦氏独立区)

> **Why separate:** The four zones below exist in Jiao Shunfa's
> original scalp acupuncture system but were NOT assigned WHO
> standard MS codes (MS1-MS14). They are defined relative to other
> Jiao zones — particularly the Motor Area and Sensory Area (= MS6
> and MS7) — using anatomical landmarks rather than traditional body
> acupoints as endpoints. Some partially overlap with WHO MS lines
> (Foot Motor-Sensory Area ≈ MS5; Chorea-Tremor ≈ MS8).
>
> **Template adaptation:** The "Vị trí trên microsystem map" uses
> Jiao's original descriptions relative to the Motor Area or
> midline, not WHO body acupoint endpoints.

### Foot Motor-Sensory Area — Túc Vận Cảm Khu (足运感区, Zú Yùn Gǎn Qū)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

Jiao-only zone overlying the paracentral lobule — the medial
continuation of the motor and sensory cortex at the
interhemispheric fissure margin. The paracentral lobule contains
primary motor cortex (BA 4) and somatosensory cortex (BA 3/1/2)
for the contralateral foot, leg, and perineal region, PLUS the
cortical micturition center for bladder control. This dual
function (motor/sensory + sphincter) gives the Foot Motor-Sensory Area the
broadest indication range of any single Jiao zone: lower limb
paralysis, cortical urinary incontinence, enuresis, rectal
prolapse, and urogenital disorders. Some Chinese textbooks describe
it as "the most commonly used area in Chinese scalp acupuncture"
— supported by its uniquely broad indications and routine bilateral
needling. Located 1 cm lateral to the midline at the vertex —
between MS5 (midline, SMA) and MS8 (1.5 cun lateral). No WHO MS
equivalent.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Jiao description: 1 cm lateral to the midline on both sides of the
vertex, starting at approximately the midpoint of the
anterior-posterior midline (≈ GV20/Baihui level), extending 3 cm
posteriorly parallel to the midline (some English sources: 4 cm).
WHO landmarks: approximately 1 cm lateral to GV20, running
posteriorly. Between MS5 (0 cm lateral) and BL7/MS8 (1.5 cun =
~3.5 cm lateral).
Bilateral — one line on each side. Both sides for
bladder/urogenital/systemic conditions; contralateral for
unilateral limb conditions.
No WHO standard MS code.

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: V1 supraorbital nerve + C2 greater occipital nerve
(V1/C2 transition zone at vertex — same convergence as GV20,
AP-13 Framework notes 1-2).
Vascular: parietal emissary veins, occipital artery branches in
Layer C. The anterior cerebral artery (ACA) supplies the
paracentral lobule internally.
Cortical area beneath — paracentral lobule:
- BA 4 (primary motor cortex): motor execution for contralateral
  foot, leg, perineal muscles
- BA 3/1/2 (primary somatosensory cortex): sensation from
  contralateral lower limb below knee, distension signals from
  bladder and rectum
- BA 6 (SMA): motor planning, at the anterior margin
- Cortical micturition center: the medial frontal cortex provides
  INHIBITORY control over the pontine micturition center
  (Barrington's nucleus). Lesions → release of voiding reflex →
  incontinence. ACA strokes characteristically produce BOTH leg
  weakness AND urinary incontinence — both from paracentral lobule.
The Foot Motor-Sensory Area is among the Jiao zones with GOOD cortical
correspondence (Ye 2023) — the paracentral lobule genuinely lies
beneath the vertex 1 cm lateral to midline.
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| Jiao | Foot Motor-Sensory Area (足运感区) | ✓ Primary |
| WHO standard | No MS equivalent | Between MS5 (midline) and MS8 (1.5 cun lateral) |
| Relationship to MS5 | MS5 → SMA (motor planning); Foot Motor-Sensory Area → primary M1+S1 (execution) | Complementary |
| Relationship to MS6 upper 1/5 | MS6 → lateral convexity; Foot Motor-Sensory Area → medial surface | Same somatotopic target, different cortical surface |

→ Cross-ref: MS5 (adjacent medial), MS8 (lateral), MS6 upper 1/5

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Jiao indications (broadest of all zones):
- Lower limb: contralateral foot/leg paralysis, pain, numbness.
  Combined with MS6 upper 1/5: "如下肢瘫痪，可选下肢运动区配足运感区."
- Bladder/sphincter: cortical urinary incontinence (皮层性尿频),
  nocturia, enuresis, cortical urinary difficulty. BILATERAL
  needling required.
- Urogenital: uterine prolapse, rectal prolapse, erectile
  dysfunction.
- Systemic: allergic colitis/diarrhea, diabetes-related
  polydipsia/polyuria — less well-supported anatomically.

**Bằng chứng hiện đại:**

**Post-stroke urinary incontinence 🟡 Plausible — paracentral
lobule rationale supported, but Foot Motor-Sensory Area not isolated:**

2025 SR/MA (PMID 40433622): 36 RCTs, 2,796 patients. Acupuncture
+ rehabilitation for post-stroke urinary incontinence. RR = 1.23
(1.19-1.28, P < 0.001); ICIQ-SF WMD = −2.40; 24h frequency
WMD = −1.40; residual urine WMD = −20.84. The review explicitly
states: "acupuncture at relevant points on the head improves
regulation of urination by the cortical center (paracentral
lobule)." Most used points: GV20, BL32, RN3 (mixed body + scalp).
No RCT isolates Foot Motor-Sensory Area specifically.

2023 SR/MA (PMID 38046590): 15 RCTs, 1,414 patients. EA for
post-stroke urinary incontinence. Maximum cystometric capacity
MD = 43.23 (28.86-57.60, P < 0.00001). Mechanism: EA "activates
functional reorganization in peri-infarct area" and "downregulates
c-fos in pontine micturition center."

**Lower limb motor recovery 🔴 Insufficient Foot Motor-Sensory Area-specific:**

Zhang 2024 (PMID 38428878): 16 RCTs, 1,249 patients. Scalp acu
+ rehabilitation for post-stroke spasticity. FMA MD = 5.86.
But scalp zones not specified by subgroup. Apriori analysis (PMID
35992903) of scalp acupuncture combinations did not list Foot Motor-Sensory Area
— studies use WHO MS codes, not Jiao zone names.

**Cơ chế giải thích:**

**1. V1/C2 → TCC → ACA territory CBF:**

Same V1/C2 convergence as GV20 (AP-13). The paracentral lobule
is in ACA territory — vertex scalp stimulation may enhance ACA
perfusion through the trigeminovascular pathway. 54 animal studies
(PMID 24496233) confirm GV20-area scalp acupuncture improves
infarct volume and neurological function.

**2. Bladder mechanism — cortical inhibitory control:**

The cortical micturition center provides tonic INHIBITION of the
pontine micturition center. ACA stroke → loss of inhibition →
incontinence. Scalp acupuncture may restore this cortical
inhibitory control through CBF enhancement and neuroplasticity.
Bilateral needling may be necessary because bilateral cortical
input is required for full bladder inhibitory control — unilateral
lesions may not cause incontinence, bilateral lesions do.

**3. Good cortical correspondence:**

Foot Motor-Sensory Area is among the Jiao zones with good anatomical match (Ye
2023) — paracentral lobule genuinely lies 1 cm lateral to midline
at the vertex (MRI confirmed, Shen 2011).

**4. No Foot Motor-Sensory Area-specific neuroimaging:**

No fMRI/fNIRS study isolates Foot Motor-Sensory Area needling and measures
paracentral lobule activation. Mechanism extrapolated from GV20
and general scalp acupuncture studies.

**Kỹ thuật kích thích:**

Insertion at the anterior end (≈ GV20 level, 1 cm lateral),
directed posteriorly at 15-30°, subcutaneous threading into
Layer L. 0.30 mm × 25-40 mm needle. Rapid twirling 200+ RPM.
Can be needled anterior-to-posterior or vice versa (anterior-to-
posterior described as easier in Chinese textbooks).
Bilateral for bladder/urogenital/systemic conditions.
Contralateral for unilateral limb conditions.
For lower limb paralysis: combine with MS6 upper 1/5
(contralateral) + MS5 (midline SMA).
For cortical urinary incontinence: bilateral Foot Motor-Sensory Area + GV20 +
body acupoints BL32, CV3 (sacral/pelvic).

**An toàn:**

General scalp acupuncture safety applies. Vertex 1 cm lateral to
midline is on thick parietal bone — no special safety concerns.
Parietal emissary veins near the sagittal suture may cause minor
bleeding.

### Chorea-Tremor Control Area — Vũ Đạo Chấn Chiến Khống Chế Khu (舞蹈震颤控制区, Wǔdǎo Zhènchàn Kòngzhì Qū)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

Jiao-only zone overlying premotor cortex, 1.5 cm anterior to
the upper segment of the Motor Area (MS6). Named for movement
disorders from cortico-basal ganglia circuit dysfunction: chorea,
tremor, dystonia. The premotor cortex beneath has reciprocal
connections with the basal ganglia (putamen, caudate, globus
pallidus) via the cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical loop.
Partially overlaps with MS8 (Vertex Lateral Line 1).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Jiao description: 1.5 cm anterior to and parallel with the upper
segment of the Motor Area (MS6 upper 1/5, leg/trunk region).
Length: 3 cm. Direction: parallel to the Motor Area upper segment,
approximately parasagittal.
WHO landmarks: approximately 1-1.5 cun lateral to midline,
anterior to BL7 — overlapping with MS8 anterior portion.
Bilateral — always needled on both sides.
No WHO standard MS code.

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: V1 supraorbital nerve + C2 GON (V1/C2 transition
zone at vertex, same as MS5/MS8).
Cortical area beneath: premotor cortex (BA 6) — motor planning,
sequencing, INDIRECT pathway of movement control. Major
projections to the striatum (caudate + putamen) = cortical input
to the basal ganglia circuit. Dysfunction → hypokinetic
(Parkinson's) or hyperkinetic (chorea, tremor, dystonia) disorders.
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| Jiao | Chorea-Tremor Control Area (舞蹈震颤控制区) | ✓ Primary |
| WHO standard | No MS equivalent | MS8 partial overlap (anterior segment) |
| YNSA | No direct correspondence | — |

→ Cross-ref: MS8 (partial overlap documented)

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Jiao indications: chorea (舞蹈病, Huntington's, Sydenham's),
tremor (震颤, resting and intention), Parkinson's disease,
dystonia. Chinese textbooks: involuntary movements of any type,
contralateral upper/lower limb. Combined with MS6 for movement
disorders with concomitant motor weakness.
No SR/RCT evidence for the Chorea-Tremor Area alone. Parkinson's
protocols typically use MS5 + MS6 + this zone + body acupoints
GB34/LR3 — limited to case series and small uncontrolled studies.

### Vasomotor Area — Huyết Quản Thư Súc Khu (血管舒缩区, Xuèguǎn Shūsuō Qū)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

Jiao-only zone on the anterior vertex, overlying frontal cortex
involved in autonomic cardiovascular regulation. The most anterior
of the Jiao vertex zones — 4-4.5 cm anterior to the upper Motor
Area. Named for vasomotor regulation (hypertension, edema). The
anatomical rationale is weakest of all Jiao zones — cardiovascular
autonomic control maps primarily to the insular cortex and
brainstem, not the lateral frontal surface.

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Jiao description: starting from the midpoint of the Chorea-Tremor
Control Area (i.e., ~4-4.5 cm anterior to the upper Motor Area),
extending 4 cm superiorly, parallel to the midline.
WHO landmarks: approximately on the frontal bone anterior to the
coronal suture, 1-1.5 cun lateral to midline — similar territory
to MS3-MS4 but more superior.
Bilateral — needled on both sides.
No WHO standard MS code.

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: V1 supraorbital nerve (frontal region).
Cortical area beneath: dorsolateral/medial prefrontal cortex
(BA 9/10/46). This frontal territory has descending projections to
hypothalamus and brainstem autonomic centers (NTS, RVLM) involved
in cardiovascular regulation. However, cortical cardiovascular
control is mediated primarily by the INSULAR cortex and anterior
cingulate (deep structures), not the lateral frontal surface.
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| Jiao | Vasomotor Area (血管舒缩区) | ✓ Primary |
| WHO standard | No MS equivalent | Approximate MS3-MS4 vicinity |
| YNSA | No direct correspondence | — |

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Jiao indications: hypertension (高血压), cortical edema (皮层性水肿),
vasomotor dysfunction. Chinese textbooks: also Raynaud's phenomenon,
peripheral vascular disease. No SR/RCT evidence. Used rarely in
modern practice — hypertension protocols typically use GV20 +
auricular points + body acupuncture (LR3, LI11) instead. Weakest
anatomical rationale of all Jiao zones.

### Praxis Area — Vận Dụng Khu (运用区, Yùnyòng Qū)

**Evidence tier: C (Minimum Viable Entry)**

Jiao-only zone near the parietal tubercle (顶骨结节), overlying
the supramarginal gyrus (BA 40) — the cortical area for motor
planning and skilled purposeful movement (praxis). Unique among
all scalp acupuncture zones in having a THREE-LINE configuration
at 40° angles from each other, rather than a single line.
Damage to the dominant hemisphere supramarginal gyrus causes
ideomotor apraxia (inability to perform learned movements on
command despite intact motor and sensory function).

**Vị trí trên microsystem map:**

Jiao description: three lines, each 3 cm long, radiating at 40°
angles from each other near the parietal tubercle. The center of
the three-line configuration is at the parietal eminence — the
most prominent point on the lateral parietal bone.
WHO landmarks: approximately at the junction of the upper 2/5 and
lower 3/5 of MS6, on the parietal bone.
Bilateral — typically needled on the DOMINANT hemisphere (left for
right-handed individuals) for apraxia; non-dominant for spatial
neglect.
No WHO standard MS code.

**Giải phẫu tại đường châm:**

Nerve supply: V1 supraorbital nerve (lateral extension) or V2
zygomaticotemporal, depending on exact lateral position. Possible
C2 GON contribution at more posterior portions.
Cortical area beneath: supramarginal gyrus (BA 40) — inferior
parietal lobule. Functions:
- Dominant hemisphere: motor planning, praxis, phonological
  processing
- Non-dominant hemisphere: spatial awareness, visuospatial
  attention (damage → hemispatial neglect syndrome)
Adjacent to the angular gyrus (BA 39, Speech II area / Wernicke's
territory) — proximity reflects the functional coupling of motor
planning and language comprehension in the inferior parietal lobule.
SCALP layer target: Layer L.

**Cross-system mapping:**

| System | Designation | Correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| Jiao | Praxis Area (运用区) | ✓ Primary |
| WHO standard | No MS equivalent | Near MS6/MS7 junction area |
| Jiao | Speech II area (语言二区) | Adjacent — both use parietal tubercle as landmark |

The Praxis Area and Speech II share the parietal tubercle landmark.
Speech II extends inferiorly (overlapping MS9 territory), while
the Praxis Area radiates in three directions. This overlap reflects
the anatomical proximity of language comprehension (BA 39) and
motor planning (BA 40) in the inferior parietal lobule.
→ Cross-ref: MS9 (Speech II partial overlap)

**Chỉ định truyền thống:**

Jiao indications: apraxia (运动不能), ideomotor apraxia (意念运动性
失用). Chinese textbooks: dominant hemisphere — post-stroke apraxia,
motor planning deficits. Non-dominant hemisphere — hemispatial
neglect, visuospatial processing deficits. No SR/RCT evidence.
The three-line configuration is unique but clinically rarely used
— most post-stroke protocols focus on MS6/MS7 motor-sensory
recovery rather than praxis-specific rehabilitation.

## Alternative Scalp Acupuncture Systems — YNSA and Zhu

> Two major non-Chinese-origin or non-Jiao scalp acupuncture
> systems have independent clinical traditions and evidence bases.
> YNSA (Yamamoto New Scalp Acupuncture, Japan, 1973) is a
> somatotopic microsystem with palpation-based diagnosis —
> paradigmatically closer to auricular acupuncture (AP-16) than
> to Chinese cortical-projection scalp acupuncture. Zhu Mingqing's
> system (China/USA, 1970s-1990s) retains Chinese meridian
> landmarks but replaces Jiao's rapid twirling with small-amplitude
> lifting/thrusting, extends needle retention from minutes to hours
> or days, and integrates active body movement (Daoyin) during
> treatment. Neither system is included in the WHO/ISSA MS1-MS14
> standard nomenclature.

### YNSA — Yamamoto New Scalp Acupuncture (山元式新頭針療法, 1973)

**Evidence tier: B (Standard Entry)**

Somatotopic microsystem developed by Dr. Toshikatsu Yamamoto
(1929-2026), a Japanese surgeon trained in Tokyo, New Jersey,
and Cologne, who first presented his system at the Ryodoraku
Congress, Osaka, 1973 — two years after Jiao Shunfa's first
publication. Yamamoto's system developed independently from
Chinese scalp acupuncture and uses a fundamentally different
theoretical basis: whole-body somatotopic representation on
scalp zones (comparable to auricular microsystem in AP-16),
NOT cortical projection mapping. The defining feature is
**diagnostic palpation** — practitioners palpate specific neck
and abdominal diagnostic points to determine which scalp points
to treat, with real-time verification by re-palpation after
needling. "With rare exceptions, YNSA points are not the same
acupoints" as Chinese scalp acupuncture lines. Used
internationally (Japan, Germany, Brazil, Australia, USA) but
NOT included in the WHO/ISSA standard.

#### Point system

YNSA comprises 5 point categories, each with Yin (frontal)
and Yang (occipital/posterior) mirror locations:

**Basic Points A-I** — the primary treatment points, located
near the anterior hairline (Yin aspect) and superior nuchal
line (Yang aspect):

| Point | Somatotopic area | Yin location (frontal) |
|---|---|---|
| A (A1-7) | Cervical spine C1-C8 | 0.5 cun lateral to metopic suture, at hairline |
| B-1 | Shoulder (AC/GH joint) | 1.2 cun lateral to metopic suture |
| B-2 | Foot | Posterior/lateral to B-1 |
| C-1 | Upper extremity (shoulder→elbow→wrist) | 2.0 cun lateral, angled 30° toward nose |
| C-2 | Lower extremity (hip→knee→ankle) | Same zone as C-1, mirrored arrangement |
| D (D1-6) | Lumbar spine + sacrum (L1-S2) | At zygomatic process level, anterior to ear |
| E (E1-12) | Thoracic spine T1-T12 | Above eyebrows, supraorbital region |
| F | Sciatic nerve | Mastoid process at tragus height (Yang only) |
| G (G1-3) | Knee (medial/patella/lateral) | Near D line anteriorly |
| H, I | Supplementary lumbar | Used only combined with D, not alone |

All basic points have mirrored Yang locations on the occipital
scalp / nuchal ridge. Side selection: **ipsilateral** for pain
and musculoskeletal conditions; **contralateral** for central
paresis/hemiplegia and brain-related conditions.

**Ypsilon Points (Y1-Y12)** — 12 points in the temporal region,
each representing one TCM organ/meridian. Selected via diagnostic
palpation of corresponding neck points, NOT by symptom-based
protocol:

| Y code | Organ | Y code | Organ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Y1 | Small Intestine | Y7 | Gallbladder |
| Y2 | Triple Heater | Y8 | Kidney |
| Y3 | Large Intestine | Y9 | Urinary Bladder |
| Y4 | Stomach | Y10 | Lung |
| Y5 | Liver | Y11 | Pericardium |
| Y6 | Spleen/Pancreas | Y12 | Heart |

**Additional categories:**
- Brain points: cranial to the A-point line, treated
  contralaterally for central/brain-related conditions.
- Sensory points: on the forehead for eye, nose, mouth, ear.
- Cranial nerve points (~12): frontal scalp extending dorsally
  from Yin A-point toward GV20, 6-8 cm, for neurologic and
  orthopaedic disorders.
- Special points: tinnitus/vertigo 4-point combination near ear
  root; Broca's aphasia point (right temporal near KI Ypsilon);
  Wernicke's aphasia point (right temporal between ST and SP
  Ypsilon); TMJ point.

#### Diagnostic palpation — YNSA's defining feature

Three-step procedure:
1. **Sidedness**: bilateral compression of LI4 (Hegu) — the
   more tender side is treated first.
2. **Yin/Yang phase**: palpate Liver neck diagnostic point on
   the SCM — firmness determines whether to treat Yin (frontal)
   or Yang (occipital) scalp points.
3. **Point verification**: one hand palpates the positive neck
   tender point while the other contacts the corresponding scalp
   point — correct identification confirmed when neck tenderness
   diminishes or disappears.

12 neck diagnostic points are distributed on the SCM and
trapezius, each corresponding to one organ/meridian (e.g., Liver
at thyroid-cartilage-level SCM, Heart at superior SCM belly,
Kidney in the triangle between SCM heads, Lung adjacent to
thyroid cartilage). Abdominal diagnosis provides supplementary
information for Ypsilon and cranial nerve point selection.

This closed-loop feedback (diagnose → treat → re-palpate →
verify) distinguishes YNSA from all Chinese scalp acupuncture
systems, which select points by protocol/indication without
comparable real-time somatic verification.
→ Cross-ref: AP-16 auricular (Nogier's RAC/VAS diagnostic
method shares the concept of somatic verification but uses
radial pulse, not neck/abdominal palpation)

#### Technique comparison

| Parameter | YNSA | Chinese scalp acupuncture (Jiao/WHO) |
|---|---|---|
| Needle | Short, thin (~0.30 × 30 mm) | Longer (25-75 mm) |
| Insertion | Piqué at ~70°, then thread at ~15° | Near-horizontal 15-30° |
| Stimulation | Minimal or none — verified by palpation | Rapid twirling 200+ RPM or electroacupuncture |
| Retention | ~20 minutes | 30 min to several hours |
| Diagnosis-guided | Yes — neck/abdominal palpation | No — protocol-based |
| Side selection | Ipsilateral (pain), contralateral (paresis) | Generally contralateral |

#### Evidence summary

| Study | Design | Key findings | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stroke pilot (PMID 23057482) | Assessor-blinded RCT, inpatient rehab, 2-year follow-up | Barthel 95±4 (YNSA) vs 75±4 (control) | 🟡 Pilot only |
| Acute LBP (Hasegawa 2014, PMID 24316509) | Double-blind RCT, n=80, sham-controlled | Statistically sig. secondary outcomes (function, SF-36, analgesic use p=0.004); primary VAS endpoint NOT clinically significant (≥2 cm) | 🟡 Mixed |
| Pain SR (Artioli 2018, BrJP) | Systematic review | Only 3 eligible trials; poor quality; "promising but insufficient" | 🔴 Very limited |
| Migraine RCT (JACM 2014) | n=80, YNSA vs traditional acupuncture | Both equally effective | 🟡 Non-inferior |
| fMRI sham-controlled (PMID 20923941) | n=17 ischemic stroke | Genuine YNSA: motor/premotor/SMA activation vs sham | 🟡 Mechanistic |

**Evidence gap vs Chinese scalp acupuncture:** YNSA has a markedly smaller
evidence base. The pain SR (2018) found only 3 eligible trials
total. The stroke evidence rests on a single pilot RCT. By
contrast, Chinese scalp acupuncture has 30+ RCTs for stroke motor recovery
alone, 69+ for aphasia, and multiple systematic reviews/
meta-analyses. Recent Japanese studies (2021, 2025) show
improving rigor but the quantity gap remains substantial.

### Zhu Mingqing Scalp Acupuncture (朱明清头针, 1970s)

Zhu Mingqing graduated in the first cohort of the Acupuncture
and Moxibustion Department, Shanghai College of Traditional
Chinese Medicine (1964), and began practice at the affiliated
Shuguang Hospital. During the Cultural Revolution, while serving
as sole village doctor, he had a breakthrough treating an acute
stroke patient — after scalp needling combined with Daoyin (导引,
guided movement/breathing), a previously paralyzed patient stood
unassisted. This event seeded his systematic integration of
active body movement with scalp acupuncture.

Career: Medical Director (Guiyang TCM Hospital), Research
Director (Beijing Research Institute of Acupuncture and
Traumatology). First mainland Chinese doctor invited to Taiwan
by the Red Cross (1987). Acupuncturist of the Year, International
Congress of Chinese Medicine and Qi Gong (1990). Emigrated to
the USA 1989; founded the Chinese Scalp Acupuncture Center of
the U.S.A. in San Francisco (1991). Published *Zhu's Scalp
Acupuncture* (8 Dragons Publishing, 1992, ISBN 9627328162).
Contributed to WHO's International Standardization of Scalp
Acupuncture effort.

#### Zone system — 6 zones, 17 subdivisions

All zone names derive from anatomical scalp regions and meridian
landmarks — NOT from cortical function (contrast with Jiao's
"Motor Area," "Sensory Area"). Zones are organized along the
Governing Vessel (GV/Du), Bladder (BL), and Gallbladder (GB)
meridian pathways.

**Three primary zones (11 subdivisions):**

| Zone | Path | Subdivisions | ≈ WHO MS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eding (额顶, forehead-vertex) | ~0.5 cun anterior to GV24 → GV20, 1 cun wide, 5 cun long | 1: head/neck; 2: chest (GV24-22); 3: middle burner (GV22-21); 4: lower burner + limbs (GV21-20) | MS1 + MS5 midline |
| Dingzhen (顶枕, vertex-occiput) | 0.5 cun either side of GV20 → GV17, 1 cun wide | 1: neck (GV20); 2: C7-T10; 3: T10-L5; 4: sacrum/coccyx (GV17, rarely used — painful) | MS5 + MS12 midline |
| Dingnie (顶颞, vertex-temple) | GV21 → ~0.5 cun anterior to ST8, 1 cun wide, diagonal | 1: lower limbs; 2: upper limbs; 3: head (near ST8, rarely used) | ≈ MS6 oblique |

**Three secondary zones (6 subdivisions):**

| Zone | Path | Subdivisions | ≈ WHO MS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Epang (额旁, forehead-side) | Near GB15 and GB13-ST8 | 1: 0.5 cun either side of GB15 (acute middle burner); 2: midpoint GB13-ST8 (acute lower burner) | ≈ MS3-MS4 |
| Dingjie (顶界, vertex-adjacent) | Adjacent to GV20 | Front: GV20→BL7 (hip/inguinal); Back: GV20→BL8 (upper trapezius) | ≈ MS8 vicinity |
| Nie (颞, temple) | Temporal region | Nieqian (前颞): GB4-GB6 (shaoyang disorders, migraine); Niehou (后颞): GB9-TB20 (ear disease) | MS10 + MS11 |

**Note on zone counting:** Secondary sources cite "8 zones" or
"9 zones" — the exact count depends on whether primary and
secondary zones are tallied separately, and whether some
subdivisions are promoted to zone status. The 6-zone /
17-subdivision breakdown above follows the most granular
published source (Dharmananda/ITM Online). A 2023 scoping review
(JAMS, PMID 37885251) independently counts 19 total areas for
Zhu's system using a different reference standard.

#### Technique — Daoyin-integrated scalp acupuncture

| Parameter | Zhu Mingqing | Jiao Shunfa |
|---|---|---|
| Manipulation | Forceful small-amplitude lift-thrust (≤0.1 cun movement): "Jinqi" (进气, thrust — neurological) and "Chouqi" (抽气, lift — pain) | Rapid twirling 200-300 RPM |
| Needle retention | **Long**: minimum 4 hours, up to 2 days — patients sent home with needles in situ | Short: removed after manipulation |
| Body engagement | **Active movement/massage** of affected body part during needling; ambulation for lower-limb cases; breathing/qigong (Daoyin) integration | Not integral to original method |
| Needle gauge | 32-34 gauge, ~1 cun (30 mm), 15-25° | 26-30 gauge (suited to fast twirling) |
| Session pattern | 1-2 min manipulation, 10-15 min intervals, total 2-3 hours; taper daily → alternate days → twice weekly | Rapid stimulation bursts |
| Special formations | "Crossing technique" (2 needles at right angles, severe pain); "Arrow formation" (3 parallel needles, chronic/deficiency) | — |

**Key innovations:**
1. Concurrent active body movement during needle retention —
   the system's signature contribution and its most influential
   innovation. This principle was independently validated by
   motion-style scalp acupuncture (Motion-Style Scalp Acupuncture) clinical research
   decades later.
   → Cross-ref: MS6 (⚡3 motion-style scalp acupuncture mechanism — 19 RCTs, 2025)
2. Extended multi-hour to multi-day needle retention
3. Small-amplitude lift-thrust replacing rapid twirling (less
   operator fatigue, less patient discomfort)
4. Zone naming by anatomical/meridian landmarks rather than
   hypothesized cortical function — a more modest theoretical
   claim than Jiao's

#### Evidence

- **Long-stay method RCT** (2023, Neuropsychiatric Disease and
  Treatment, DOI 10.2147/NDT.S413205): 24-hour retention superior
  to conventional 30-minute retention on FMA, MMT, ADL, Barthel
  Index, and NIHSS for acute ischemic stroke motor dysfunction.
- Broader scalp acupuncture meta-analyses (not Zhu-specific):
  6/8 RCTs positive for neurological deficit, but effect may be
  overstated due to low methodological quality.
- Evidence base is primarily case series and clinical experience
  literature — fewer controlled trials than Jiao/WHO standard.

**Current status:** Actively taught internationally. Lineage:
Moyee Siu (designated successor 2014, San Jose clinic); Dr.
James C. Lu (studied under Zhu 2006-2020, "ZSA Seminar"
training). Multiple continuing-education vendors offer courses
(2024-2025).

## Critical Assessment of the Cortical Projection Hypothesis

> Jiao Shunfa's 1971 hypothesis — that needling scalp zones
> overlying specific cortical areas directly stimulates those
> areas — remains the stated theoretical basis of Chinese scalp
> acupuncture and the WHO/ISSA standard. Multiple independent
> analyses have challenged this hypothesis on anatomical,
> mechanistic, and historical grounds.

#### Zone correspondence analysis (Ye et al. 2023)

Ye, Fan et al. (2023, Alternative Therapies in Health and
Medicine 29(7):242-251, PMID 36302231) mapped Jiao's 19
stimulation zones against modern cortical functional-area
cartography. Key finding: **>57% of the 19 zones do not
correspond to the cortical functional areas directly beneath
them.**

Non-corresponding zones (11 of 19):
- 5 forehead zones (Chest, Stomach, Genital, Hepatobiliary,
  Intestinal — originally Fang Yunpeng's, not Jiao's)
- Nasopharyngeal-mouth-tongue zone
- Madness-control zone
- Spirit-emotional zone
- Balance zone (→ confirmed: MS14 overlies cerebellum, not
  vestibular cortex)
- Chorea-Tremor control zone (→ confirmed: premotor BA 6, not
  basal ganglia)
- Vasomotor zone (→ confirmed: weakest anatomical rationale)

Core methodological flaw identified: Jiao used neurosurgical
cranial-positioning landmarks to define zone boundaries but did
NOT systematically verify that each zone actually overlies the
corresponding gyrus. Because gyri are irregular curved strips,
zones drawn from fixed skull landmarks "can't represent the
projections of the gyrus' irregular strips and bands." The 19
zones are characterized as "a mixture of Jiao's speculation...
and other authors' clinical experiences."

**Independent corroboration (2023 scoping review):** JAMS, PMID
37885251, examined scalp acupuncture systems against a reference
standard (SIANSA). Results: Jiao's system — 16 areas, 11
anatomically matching (31% mismatch). Zhu's system — 19 areas,
13 matching (32% mismatch). Merged WHO standard ("Chinese scalp acupuncture")
— 19 areas, 12/12 matching. Standardization improved
correspondence relative to individual systems.

#### Mechanism critique (Jin et al. 2023)

Jin GY et al. (2023, Frontiers in Neuroscience 17:1210537, PMID
37650106) articulated the mechanistic impossibility of cortical
projection:

"The stimulation of scalp acupuncture [SA] is mechanical rather than transcranial
electromagnetic, making it **impossible to stimulate the cortical
functional zones directly below the scalp.**"

"The cranial or occipital nerves innervating the scalp have no
such segmental connections with the cerebral cortex."

The needle sits in subgaleal loose areolar tissue (Layer L),
separated from cortex by pericranium + skull (5-10 mm) +
meninges + CSF = minimum 8-16 mm. Mechanical stimulation
cannot traverse this barrier. The alternative mechanism: trigeminal
nerve-mediated modulation of cerebral blood flow — "the efficacy
of scalp acupuncture [SA] in treating brain diseases, especially ischemic [stroke],
is mostly achieved by stimulating the nerves innervating the
scalp, specifically the trigeminal nerve, to improve CBF."
→ Cross-ref: Zone header trigeminovascular mechanism
→ Cross-ref: ⚡2 segment-specific modulation, ⚡6 posterior
circulation specificity — both demonstrate that peripheral nerve
territory (not cortical projection) determines therapeutic effect

#### Historical reframing — Huang Xuelong (黄学龙, 1877-1962)

Ye, Fan et al. (2025/2026, Medical Acupuncture, DOI
10.1177/19336586251361931) identified Huang Xuelong as the
EARLIEST founder of scalp acupuncture zone therapy — predating
both Fang Yunpeng (1970) and Jiao Shunfa (1971) by approximately
two decades.

Huang, a traditional Qing dynasty scholar who became an
acupuncture expert, introduced the conceptual link between scalp
and cerebral cortex as early as 1935, drawing partly from
cortical anatomy and partly from Ivan Pavlov's neuro-reflexology.
He developed scalp zone therapy in the 1950s in Jiangsu and
Zhejiang provinces. Core doctrine: "the 14 Channels and the
brain are a sole system: when divided, they are demonstrated as
14 Channels, and when combined, the Channels are all unified in
the brain."

Huang's approach was **GV/Du Mai-centered** — scalp acupoints
organized along the Governing Vessel with a longitudinal
stimulating line centered at Baihui (GV20). This is a
channel/meridian-theory model, not an isolated cortical-zone
model. If accepted, this historical reframing displaces Jiao
from the "founder" position and re-roots the field's origin in
meridian/channel theory rather than cortical projection theory —
directly complementary to the anatomical critique above.

The prior academic debate was two-way (Fang 1970 vs Jiao 1971).
Ye/Fan reframe this as a three-way priority question with Huang's
1950s practice (and 1935 conceptual antecedent) predating both
by ~20 years.

#### Current consensus

Cortical projection theory as originally framed is **increasingly
regarded as anatomically untenable** among researchers studying
scalp acupuncture mechanisms. The emerging consensus favors
**peripheral cranial/trigeminal nerve stimulation → autonomic/
vascular effects on cerebral blood flow** as the primary
mechanism.

However, this remains a growing but still minority critical
literature. Most clinical practice and trials continue to use
Jiao-derived zone nomenclature without adjudicating the mechanism
question. No official revision of the WHO/ISSA MS1-MS14 standard
has occurred. The WHO MS nomenclature retains its clinical utility
regardless of whether the original theoretical justification is
correct — the LINES work clinically even if the REASON they work
differs from Jiao's hypothesis.
